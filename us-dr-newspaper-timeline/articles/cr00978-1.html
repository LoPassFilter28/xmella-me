The Evening Star published an analysis attributing cross-border tensions between Haiti and the Dominican Republic to acute hunger and agricultural underproduction resulting from Haiti's dense population. The writer argued that food shortages drove impoverished Haitians across the frontier to steal livestock and farm animals. In turn, isolated Dominican homesteaders strongly resented these cattle thefts and feared for their lives and families.

El Evening Star publicó un análisis que atribuía las tensiones fronterizas entre Haití y la República Dominicana al hambre aguda y al déficit de producción agrícola causados por la alta densidad demográfica haitiana. El autor sostenía que la escasez de alimentos empujaba a haitianos desposeídos a cruzar la frontera para robar ganado y animales de labranza. Por su parte, los campesinos dominicanos de zonas aisladas resentían profundamente esos robos pecuarios y temían por la integridad de sus familias y vidas.

Considerably greater production would be possible in Haiti, despite the fact that the soil has been treated villainously ever since the French were expelled, if it were not for a racial trait which is characteristic of the island. Well does the Haitian peasant know this weakness of his fellow-countrymen. Of what use for him—or, more accurately, for his wife—to work hard and produce an abundance if some one else is to reap the benefit? In Haiti, the prudent peasant never allows fruit to ripen on his trees—if he does, his neighbors, on the first dark night that comes along, will pick it for him. Thus the situation in Haiti is a combination of dense population with underproduction of food. When such a situation comes into being in any country it means trouble—trouble within the country and trouble for the neighbors without. Hunger Basic Cause. In a word, it is hunger, and not just a propensity for vicious thievery, that drives the poverty-stricken Haitians to commit depredations. But the isolated white homesteader in Santo Domingo, who sees his cattle driven off and slaughtered by roving bands from Haiti, does not stop to ponder on the economic forces which in their functioning make the Haitian a marauder and himself a victim.
