The West Virginia Digest profiled newly elected Haitian President Élie Lescot, outlining his earlier service as Haiti's Envoy Extraordinary and Minister Plenipotentiary to Ciudad Trujillo. In that post, Lescot helped negotiate the settlement of the border dispute between Haiti and the Dominican Republic, earning the Dominican decoration of Juan Pablo Duarte. He subsequently served as a delegate to the Buenos Aires peace conference before becoming Haitian Minister to the United States in 1937.

El West Virginia Digest perfiló la biografía del recién electo mandatario haitiano Élie Lescot, reseñando su desempeño diplomático anterior como enviado extraordinario y ministro plenipotenciario de Haití en Ciudad Trujillo. En aquella misión, Lescot facilitó las gestiones conciliatorias del pleito limítrofe entre Haití y la República Dominicana, lo que le valió la condecoración dominicana de la Orden de Juan Pablo Duarte. Con posterioridad asistió como delegado a la conferencia de paz de Buenos Aires antes de asumir como ministro plenipotenciario de Haití ante los Estados Unidos en 1937.

the early age of twenty-seven he was elected to Legislative Assembly where he served with distinction for three years. At the close of this experience he entered the judiciary finally winning promotion to the cabinet portfolio of Secretary of Public Education and Agriculture in the administration of President Borno. President Vincent made him Secretary of Justice and of the Interior and later sent him to Ciudad Trujillo, the Dominican Republic, as Envoy Extraordinary and Minister Plenipotentiary of the Haitian government. He settled the vexing boundary dispute between Haiti and Santo Domingo, receiving from the Dominican government the cross of the "Gran Oficial del Orden de Merito de Juan Pablo Duarte." President Vincent bestowed upon him the title of "Grand officier de l'Ordre Honneur et Merite" for achievements as Secretary of the Interior. He also was among the Haitian delegates to the Inter-American Conference for the Maintenance of Peace at Buenos Aires. In April, 1937, M. Lescot presented his credentials to President F. D. Roosevelt, as Haitian minister to the United States, succeeding M. [Albert?] Blanchet. His activities in the United States have been unprecedented in promoting and cementing better economic relations between his country and the United States as well as increasing the exchange of cultural amenities, and a practical program of exchange of college students for the purpose of a reciprocal assimilation of language and customs.
