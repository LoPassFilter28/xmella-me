THE REPUBLIC OF SAN DOMINGO.

The Dominican Republic has arisen! No more will she be the prey to the great mother of republics. Should the Republic of Washington now turn her back, or refuse to unite the lot of this loyal American Republic, the white race will soon be extinguished in the Antilles. It was a cruel mistake in President Lincoln to receive Geffrard to his arms, and at the same moment reject the alliance of the Dominican Republic, offering us as a sacrifice to the dark race, as if no race but the negroes had the sympathy of the United States.

Only yesterday the mulatto came in from Petit Trou, on the coast of Hayti, with a stolen steamer, to stir up again the blacks of Jamaica, and now a new attempt of the negroes of Jacmel to exterminate the mixed bloods "between two fires"—mulattoes and whites.

President Geffrard, of Hayti, is a great and good patriot. All the negroes here have joined the color-line, and, after exterminating the mulattoes and quadroons, try to reduce the whites to the level of the Dominican blacks. It is a war of races; were it not that French, English, and Spanish vessels of war have arrived, Jacmel had been a port of Hayti. These naval vessels are to protect the citizens that have power to protect or avenge the citizens of their respective nations, and keep in check the Haytian throne by the insurgent negroes.

The President of the Dominican Republic will be elected on the 24th instant, and the choice seems to be between Pedro Valverde and General Cabral. Don Pedro Valverde is a noble soldier, and a finished gentleman of pure white stock. He is of the "Yankee party," and is openly pledged to sustain the course of policy inaugurated in June and July. This is to open the Dominican gold mines, mahogany forests, and pearl fisheries to all immigrants who have the means and disposition to develop the vast natural resources of the Republic, under a general colonization law. Don Pedro Valverde also would give President Geffrard no peace until the ports of Hayti were thrown open, and neutral ports where the elements of the two republics meet; which free ports should be placed under the commercial control of white agents or men who represent Dominican Republic. Free trade and a neutral line, if Hayti will accept it, would, he thinks, preserve it from obliteration.

General Cabral will go further than that with Hayti if he is elected, and if the United States Government refuses to co-operate and join hands with us. Cabral has a slight tinge of Indian blood in his system, and he may be inclined to enter into a union with Hayti so close as to blur the distinct line which separates them from each other. He has most intimate relations with the European Powers; while Don Pedro Valverde is a pure American or Yankee in his feelings, and we should be desolated if, again, Don Pedro Valverde is rejected. The foreign element in the government he will have created here in trying to conquer us will be met by a force of 15,000 men on the border to defend a neutral border line of division, with free ports on each end to keep up the peace, and preserve the Dominican Republic. If Spain insists on keeping Santo Domingo, or a pecuniary indemnity for the immense amount of property which she would have lost if your Government at Washington had simply recognized the existence of the Dominican Republic, the San Domingo question would be settled.

The election of Cabral will give a more European character to the government of San Domingo; and as to the white element everywhere in the Antilles, it may lead to the total extinction of the white race in the Antilles.

CARLOS MELLA.
