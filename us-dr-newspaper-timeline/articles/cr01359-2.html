The Daily Worker reported that following the marine withdrawal, Dominican President Vásquez assumed dictatorial powers that facilitated American corporate control of over 300,000 acres of sugar plantations. The article cited Charles G. Dawes's 1928 mission to structure the Dominican budget for repayments to the National City Bank under the customs treaty. It further claimed that High Commissioner Russell in Haiti feared the Dominican rebellion would inspire Haitian peasants to revolt against President Borno's administration.

El Daily Worker denunció que, tras la retirada de los marines, el presidente dominicano Vásquez asumió facultades dictatoriales que facilitaron el control corporativo estadounidense sobre más de 300 000 acres de plantaciones azucareras. El artículo citó la misión de Charles G. Dawes en 1928 para estructurar el presupuesto dominicano de cara a los reembolsos al National City Bank bajo el tratado aduanero. Asimismo, afirmó que el alto comisionado Russell en Haití temía que la rebelión dominicana incitara a los campesinos haitianos a alzarse contra el gobierno del presidente Borno.

The marines withdrew in 1925, and Vasquez began to take more and more dictatorial powers. His government aided the American imperialists to exploit the masses. The peasants have been deprived of their land and are forced to work for the American sugar centrals at starvation wages and under feudal conditions. The American sugar corporations own over 300,000 acres of Santo Domingo properties, and have an investment of over $30,000,000 in the island. The masses are mainly Negro, Indian and Spanish peasants. In 1928 Charles G. Dawes went to Santo Domingo to work out a financial budget for the government in order to facilitate its payments to the National City Bank. Under the treaty with the United States, forced on the masses by marine intervention, a U. S. customs collector was appointed. When the recent revolution broke out Santo Domingo [illegible] High Commissioner Russell in Haiti, which borders on Santo Domingo, expressed the fear that the mass uprising there would give encouragement to the Haitian peasants who are seething with revolt against U. S. imperialism and the Borno puppet government. Recently the United States Steel Corporation made a contract under which it declared it would invest $200,000,000 in iron ore mines in the state of Minas Gerais, Brazil. There is an election campaign now going on with the Conservative party backing British imperialism, and the so-called Liberal party getting the support of American imperialism. [illegible]
