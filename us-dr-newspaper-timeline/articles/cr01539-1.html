The Evening Star reported that Representative Hamilton Fish denied being questioned by the Internal Revenue Bureau regarding a $25,000 credit he received from Dominican dictator Rafael L. Trujillo in 1939. Fish stated that he acted solely as an agent in oil speculations, explaining that $12,500 was lost and the remaining $12,500 was returned to Trujillo. Fish added that tax officials had advised him the agency transaction did not need to be declared on his income tax return.

El Evening Star informó que el legislador Hamilton Fish desmintió haber sido interrogado por la Oficina de Rentas Internas en torno a una partida de 25.000 dólares transferida por el dictador dominicano Rafael L. Trujillo en 1939. Fish alegó que se limitó a desempeñarse como comisionista intermediario en especulaciones petrolíferas, precisando que se esfumaron 12.500 dólares y se reembolsó a Trujillo el restante de 12.500. Fish arguyó asimismo que las autoridades impositivas le dictaminaron que dicha intermediación no requería ser asentada en su balance tributario anual.

renomination in President Roosevelt's home district, today flatly declared that he had never been questioned by any representative of the Internal Revenue Bureau regarding a $25,000 credit he received from Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo, dictator of the Dominican Republic, in 1939. Mr. Fish discussed over the long distance telephone, from his office at 111 Broadway, New York, the copyrighted account of his dealings with Gen. Trujillo published in the Washington Post today. "There are four points," said Mr. Fish, "in connection with this matter. "First, no representative of the Internal Revenue Bureau has approached me to discuss this credit of $25,000. "Second, I was acting merely as an agent in the matter, in connection with some oil speculations. "Third, there was a total loss of $12,500, and the other $12,500 I returned to Gen. Trujillo. Consulted Bureau on Tax Returns. "Fourth, I wrote a letter to the Internal Revenue Bureau, after consulting two men in that bureau who informed me there was no reason for taking up the matter with the bureau, setting forth the details of the transaction. This was written after my campaign for renomination got under way, comparatively recently. "As a matter of fact, I consulted representatives of the bureau about this credit before I filed my income tax for 1939 and was told that as I was merely an agent, I did not have to Include a statement of the credit in my return." Secretary of the Treasury Morgenthau declined comment on the Fish matter at a press conference
