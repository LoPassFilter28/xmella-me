The Evening Star reported that Dominican Ambassador Emilio Garcia Godoy presented the Order of Christopher Columbus to Admiral Richard E. Byrd and Maj. Banks at the Dominican Embassy. Godoy referred to Byrd as "today's Columbus" during the ceremony conferring the high Dominican decoration. Byrd, currently stationed in Washington between missions to the Pacific, noted he would defer future exploration plans until after the war.

El Evening Star informó que el embajador dominicano Emilio García Godoy condecoró con la Orden de Cristóbal Colón al almirante Richard E. Byrd y al mayor Banks en la sede de la embajada dominicana. García Godoy enalteció a Byrd calificándolo del «Colón de nuestros días» durante la ceremonia de imposición de la presea nacional. Byrd, comisionado provisionalmente en Washington en el intermedio de sus expediciones por el Pacífico, manifestó que postergaría sus exploraciones polares futuras hasta el desenlace de la conflagración bélica.

Admiral Byrd, a soft-spoken gray-haired man, fingered the red and silver decoration, almost as big as a fist, and seemed embarrassed when Ambassador Godoy called him "today's Columbus." He was reluctant to talk about his trips to the Antarctic regions—trips that exceeded the dreams of adventure of most small boys. Asked if he planned another exploration trip in the future, Admiral Byrd smiled and said: "I haven't thought much about that for the near future—I'll wait until the war is over." He is now stationed in Washington between trips to the Pacific theater, in connection with aerial warfare, and recently was decorated by President Roosevelt with the Legion of Merit for a secret war mission in the Pacific. Maj. Banks, formerly on the staff of the late Lt. Gen. Lesley J. McNair, now is serving with the War Finance Division of the Treasury Department. He is the author of several books, four of which have been translated into Spanish, and has announced plans to write a movie scenario on the life of Admiral Byrd. The Order of Christopher Columbus is one of the highest awarded by the Dominican Republic, and those winning it usually have some romantic connection to Columbus, or exploration, Dominican officials said.
