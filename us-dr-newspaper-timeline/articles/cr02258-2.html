The Key West Citizen reported that Fidel Castro shifted strategy to radio propaganda and clandestine support to undermine the neighboring Caribbean republics. In the Dominican Republic, local Communists reportedly aligned with remnants of the Trujillo regime to oppose Rafael Bonnelly's transitional government.

The Key West Citizen informó que Fidel Castro varió de estrategia hacia la propaganda radiofónica y el apoyo clandestino para socavar a las repúblicas vecinas del Caribe. En la República Dominicana, comunistas locales se habrían aliado según informes con remanentes del régimen trujillista para oponerse al gobierno de transición de Rafael Bonnelly.

Castro agents have made considerable headway in tiny Haiti. An invasion attempt in August, 1959, was unsuccessful, when members of the Cuban army were captured by troops of President Duvalier. Cuban agents have now embarked upon a campaign of terror in this primitive little country. They have seized upon wretched living conditions to win some converts. When invasion attempts against the neighboring Dominican Republic failed, Castro changed his tactics. Regular radio broadcasts, beamed to the Dominican Republic by the Dominican Liberation Movement, foment discontent. Dominican Communists have joined with some followers of late dictator Trujillo in efforts to overthrow the moderate government of Rafael [Bonnelly?]. U.S. Congressman Victor Anfuso, a Dominican visitor earlier this year, calls the Castro threat very real. Other countries to be threatened by Castro-financed revolution are Peru, Guatemala, Nicaragua, Panama, Colombia and Paraguay.
