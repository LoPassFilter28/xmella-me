The Key West Citizen reported details of Rafael Trujillo's assassination, describing how gunmen ambushed his car on an oceanfront highway outside the capital. Reports identified former army commander General Juan Tomás Díaz as the leader of the seven-man ambush team that killed the dictator and wounded his chauffeur.

The Key West Citizen dio cuenta de pormenores sobre el asesinato de Rafael Trujillo, describiendo cómo hombres armados emboscaron su automóvil en una autopista costera en las afueras de la capital. Los informes identificaron al excomandante del ejército, general Juan Tomás Díaz, como el líder del comando de emboscada de siete hombres que abatió al dictador e hirió a su chofer.

A Dominican newsman reported by telephone from Ciudad Trujillo—the capital city the dictator renamed in his own honor—that Trujillo was ambushed by gunmen as he rode along an ocean front highway outside the capital. Another car forced his to halt. Several shots were fired from a “handgun.” He was believed to have died instantly. His chauffeur was wounded. Trujillo was armed but “probably had no time to defend himself.” A crack shot, the dictator once boasted, “The man who takes a potshot at me had better be sure of his aim. Otherwise he'll be a dead man." Other sources said seven persons in an automobile made the attack, that two of the assailants were caught or killed and the other five were presumed to have got away. NBC correspondent John Hlavacek reported from Ciudad Trujillo that the leader of the band was Gen. Juan Tomas Diaz, who was believed to have had a personal grudge against Trujillo. Dominican exiles in Mexico City said Diaz was a career soldier whose sister and her husband fled to Brazil last year. But Diaz reportedly continued as army commander of the country’s central zone, at least until last January.
