The Provisional Government is probably in possession of the capital today, although there was no possibility of receiving any news from the island for several weeks to come, owing to the want of direct communication with it. When he left Puerto Plata the revolution had assumed such a form as to give him confidence in the success of the uprising, and the American Consul at the Santo Domingo capital had, even at that time, stated that the Baez dynasty was completely doomed. Mr. Passalaigue was very reticent as to the intentions of the United States, merely stating that, as a native of South Carolina, he chose, at this crisis, the country of his birth. He is deeply interested in the success of the new Government of the Dominican Republic, and he has received documents of importance from American Consuls to be presented to important officials in this country.

THE NEW COMMANDER.

Gen. Gonzales, who seems destined to play a prominent part in the political history of Santo Domingo, is a young man only about 30 years of age. He has for some time been before the eyes of the people as General of the Republic. He is of small figure, but of good presence and pleasing appearance. He is about 5 feet 8 inches high; his complexion is fair, and dark side whiskers and mustache give it an almost pallid appearance. He has fine eyes and a broad though not high forehead.

THE SAMANA BAY COMPANY.

An official of the Samana Bay Company was questioned by a reporter of THE TRIBUNE relative to the effect that the revolution in Santo Domingo would have on the concession of Samana Bay. He stated that he did not believe it would have any injurious effects. C. S. Passalaigue, the accredited agent of the revolutionary party, who recently came to this city, has called at the office of the Company and assured them that there is no feeling of hostility felt toward the Samana Bay Company; on the contrary, they were [desirous] to aid by all legitimate means the development of the country.
