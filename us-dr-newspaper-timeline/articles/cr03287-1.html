The East Hartford Gazette reviewed major news highlights of late 1946, noting that the Dominican Republic suffered severe losses from a violent earthquake and tidal wave in August. The retrospective also cited international incidents including the desertion of the steamship American Farmer and the Yugoslav shootdown of two American airplanes. Additional domestic events covered labor strikes, price controls, and the anniversary of the U.S. Army Air Forces.

El East Hartford Gazette presentó una recopilación de los sucesos más descollantes de finales de 1946, destacando que la República Dominicana sufrió desastrosas pérdidas a consecuencia del terremoto y maremoto registrados en agosto. La síntesis informativa rememoró también episodios internacionales como el abandono del vapor American Farmer y el derribo por fuerzas yugoslavas de dos aeronaves militares estadounidenses. En el ámbito interno, recapituló jornadas de huelga obrera, controversias por los controles de precios y el aniversario de las Fuerzas Aéreas del Ejército norteamericano.

AUGUST. August 2nd was the 39th birthday of the United States Army Air Forces. On August 4th Protestant leaders from eight nations, gathered in England, favored joint action with the Catholics on world problems. Athens, Tennessee, saw ex-GIs drive the sheriff elect out of town and seize control of the government. The Dominican Republic suffered great loss in a tidal wave and violent earthquake. The steamship American Farmer was deserted at sea and became a problem for international litigation. Channing Pollock, famous playwright, died August 18. The Yugoslavs shoot down two American planes. The State Department sent violent protest. Severe rioting occurred in Calcutta. The Norwalk, Connecticut, schools teachers go on strike for wage relief. President Truman vacationed in Bermuda. New England scientists from M. I. T. studied cosmic rays at 40,000 feet altitude. Fifteen German spies were arrested in the American zone of occupation. Those were some of the August headlines and then SEPTEMBER. Price control went on a spree on such items as canned fruits and vegetables and many other items. The truck strike was hitting New York. An Army jet plane did 578 miles per hour at the Cleveland races. Marshal Tito apologized to the United States for the shooting down of our planes. A musicians' strike silenced the dining rooms of the New York hotels. The Queen Mary arrived on September 4th with art treasures, valued at seven million dollars, for exhibition in the U. S. A.
