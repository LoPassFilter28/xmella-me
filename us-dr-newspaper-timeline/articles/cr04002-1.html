The Evening Star reported that foreign ministers meeting in San José debated Venezuelan demands for Rio Treaty sanctions against the Dominican Republic over the attempted assassination of President Rómulo Betancourt. Dominican Foreign Minister Porfirio Herrera denied complicity and promised exculpatory evidence, while the United States and several South American nations urged caution regarding economic sanctions.

El Evening Star informó que los ministros de Relaciones Exteriores reunidos en San José debatieron las exigencias venezolanas de sanciones bajo el Tratado de Río contra la República Dominicana por el intento de magnicidio contra el presidente Rómulo Betancourt. El canciller dominicano Porfirio Herrera negó cualquier complicidad y prometió pruebas exculpatorias, mientras que Estados Unidos y varias naciones sudamericanas instaron a la cautela respecto a las sanciones económicas.

A sharp split seemed to be developing among other foreign ministers on what to do about the Venezuelan-Dominican dispute, in which Venezuela has charged the regime of Dominican Dictator Raphael Trujillo with masterminding an assassination attempt on Venezuelan President Romulo Betancourt. '47 Sanctions Involved Diplomatic informants said Venezuela had ready a draft Resolution asking to invoke all sanctions in the 1947 Rio Treaty short of armed intervention. The treaty says the ministers, by a two-thirds vote, can suspend diplomatic relations and impose communications bans, but this has never happened. Dominican Foreign Minister Porfirio Herrera denied the Venezuelan charges and said in a news conference late yesterday he had some surprise testimony and proof of his own to submit. Venezuela’s call for sanctions is supported in varying degrees by a substantial number of Latin-American nations, in cluding those which have broken off relations with the Dominican Republic. But a number of others, including Uruguay, Chile, El Salvador, and the United States, are advising a more cautious approach. U.
