Independence, Recognition, and Samaná SchemesIndependencia, Reconocimiento y Proyectos de Samaná · 1844–1861

On February 27, 1844, Los Trinitarios proclaimed Dominican independence. American press coverage surged with reports on the new republic, repeated Haitian invasion threats, diplomatic recognition appeals, and southern schemes to secure Samaná Bay as a naval base.El 27 de febrero de 1844, Los Trinitarios proclamaron la independencia. La prensa estadounidense cubrió la nueva república, las invasiones haitianas, llamamientos de reconocimiento diplomático y proyectos para adquirir la Bahía de Samaná.

—The Dominican Republic and our Government have received a new humiliation from England. When that Power, in conjunction with France, sent a fleet to St. Domingo, to order the Baez-Jimenes treaty to be canceled, it also insisted, in behalf of the negroes, that the black population should be represented in the Cabinet or Senate. In obedience to this behest, an educated negro, named Bobadilla, was nominated to the Council of State. Rather than have any difficulty, President Santana acceded to this; and sent private orders to our Commercial Agent at St. Domingo to write to Washington that there was a new one to which England would not object; and this President Santana would be very glad to annul or negotiate a treaty. This precious Anglo-African mutilation of the original treaty is now in the hands of the President, and will probably be submitted to the Senate about the 8th or 9th of May.
