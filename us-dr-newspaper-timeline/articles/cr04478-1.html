THE LIBERTY PARTY ORGANIZING A CONSTITUTION—

ELECTIONS ORDERED—SIEGE OF SAN DOMINGO CITY

—TWO WAR VESSELS AND A LARGE MORTAR WANTED

—BAEZ’S FRIENDS FALLING OFF—THE AMERICAN

SCHOONER CHARLES HILL.

The schooner City Belle, Captain Pendleton, arrived at this port yesterday from Port au Plata, with news dated at that place on 29th of October.

The authors of the revolution of the liberty party in the Dominican republic are fast organizing a new government very different from that of Senor Baez.

The election for the deputies of the nation was to take place on the 10th of November, and the members were to meet at the town of Moca on the 1st of December.

General Santana and several other valiant Generals were besieging the city of St. Domingo with a force of 8,000 men. The besiegers are very moderate in their fire against the city, as it was momentarily expected that Baez would be forced to capitulate. An expedition of 2,000 men strong were marching against Samana, the only place holding for Baez.

Business in Port au Plata and Santiago continued quite safe and easy, although all the military in the province of Cibao were under arms.

The Cibao government wish to purchase two small clipper schooners of 150 to 180 tons, to blockade the port of St. Domingo; vessels that are staunch and strong, that will be able to carry a twenty-four pounder upon a pivot, besides small guns of smaller calibre. There being no vessels in port suitable, any American speculator might find a good offer from the government, if quickly attended to, in Port au Plata.

News had reached Port au Plata that many of the first families had lately left the city of St. Domingo, besides natives that Baez has sent away.

The people and foreign residents were very much astonished that the American government did not send a vessel of war to St. Domingo, to demand satisfaction in the case of the American schooner Charles Hill, which was seized by Baez (valued at $10,000), and as yet not claimed by the American government. The American Consul at St. Domingo had sent all the documents relative to this affair in May last to Washington.

Fighting men, friends to the republic, would be well received. Arms and ammunition were in good demand, and they were in want of an 18-inch mortar, for which the highest rates would be paid.
