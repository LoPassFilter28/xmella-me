The April 12, 1860, New York Herald reported that foreign governments—specifically France, England, and Spain—disapproved of the growing American presence and influence in the Dominican Republic. Despite this opposition, the newspaper stated that the Dominican government welcomes white men of character and enterprise and will not back down from its policies favoring American settlement. Additionally, the excerpt noted that a proposed treaty from Hayti was respectfully declined, as the Dominicans prefer American immigration.La edición del 12 de abril de 1860 de The New York Herald informó que gobiernos extranjeros —en concreto Francia, Inglaterra y España— desaprobaban la creciente presencia e influencia estadounidense en la República Dominicana. A pesar de dicha oposición, el periódico señaló que el gobierno dominicano acogía a hombres blancos de carácter e iniciativa y no retrocedería en sus políticas favorables al establecimiento estadounidense. Además, el extracto apuntó que se rechazó respetuosamente una propuesta de tratado proveniente de Haití, puesto que los dominicanos preferían la inmigración estadounidense.

Yague River—Health of President Santana, &c.Río Yaque —Salud del Presidente Santana, etc.

After a period of repose, succeeding their grand coup in forcing the Dominican government to recognise and redeem the paper issued by the late nigger chief Baez for his own personal aggrandizement, the foreign consuls have come out of their holes and are again sneaking around the administration, breathing hatred from their hearts and cowardly insinuations from their lips against the American element now settling amongst us. Sneaking is the term to apply to such conduct. They bring no direct charges, and have no arguments to offer against the present policy of the government favoring American emigration, but content themselves with observing that their governments—France, England and Spain—cannot look with favor upon a possible predominance of Yankee pluck in such exceedingly suggestive propinquity to their respective Majesties’ possessions. Merodear es el término a aplicar a tal conducta. No traen cargos directos, y no tienen argumentos que ofrecer contra la política presente del gobierno favoreciendo la emigración estadounidense, pero se contentan con observar que sus gobiernos —Francia, Inglaterra y España— no pueden mirar con favor una posible predominancia del coraje yanqui en tal propincuidad sumamente sugestiva a las posesiones de sus respectivas Majestades.
