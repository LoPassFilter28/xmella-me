The Evening Star reported that the United States concluded a ten-year bilateral pact with the Dominican Republic authorizing guided missile monitoring stations to close the observation gap between the Bahamas and Puerto Rico. Under the agreement, unarmed test missiles launched from Florida would fly over Dominican coastal waters rather than over the mainland. U.S. military personnel operating the tracking sites were capped at 300 individuals, expandable up to 500 under exceptional circumstances.

El Evening Star informó que Estados Unidos concertó un pacto bilateral de diez años con la República Dominicana que autorizaba estaciones de rastreo de misiles guiados para cerrar la brecha de observación entre las Bahamas y Puerto Rico. En virtud del acuerdo, los misiles de prueba desarmados lanzados desde Florida sobrevolarían aguas costeras dominicanas en lugar de territorio continental. El personal militar estadounidense que operaría los puestos de seguimiento tenía un límite máximo de 300 efectivos, ampliable hasta 500 en circunstancias excepcionales.

1949 talked in terms of control and monitoring stations over the first 500 miles of a course that would eventually extend 3,000 miles into the South Atlantic. Will Close 500-Mile Gap. Stations authorized on five islands of the Bahamas group pro vided a 500-mile stretch of close control. The stations to be built in the Dominican Republic will close the 500-mile gap from the Bahamas to Puerto Rico. The Air Force already has control points in Puerto Rico, about 1,000 miles from the launching base at Cocoa on the east coast of Florida. The present range of Air Force guided missiles is a secret, but officials are now talking about extending the range to send pilot less bombers as far as 6,000 miles into the South Atlantic. Will Run 10 Years. The agreement with the Do minican Republic will run for 10 years and may thereafter be cancelled or extended by either party. It provides that sites and “other installations,” not detailed, may be set up to acquire test data and [illegible] to maintain continuous control over guided missiles The agreement provides that the missiles are to be flown over waters under Dominican juris diction, not over the mainland.
