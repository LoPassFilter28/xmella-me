The Evening Star reported that the Secretary of State instructed the American consul in Santo Domingo to demand the revocation of a Dominican decree seizing a bridge across the Ozama River owned by American contractor Nat McKay. McKay built the bridge under a twenty-year concession, and the United States intervened after Dominican authorities held him liable for damages caused when a river scow struck the structure.

El Evening Star informó que el secretario de Estado instruyó al cónsul estadounidense en Santo Domingo para que exigiera la revocación de un decreto dominicano que incautaba un puente sobre el río Ozama perteneciente al contratista estadounidense Nat McKay. McKay construyó el puente al amparo de una concesión de veinte años, y Estados Unidos intervino después de que las autoridades dominicanas lo responsabilizaran de los daños causados al chocar una barcaza fluvial contra la estructura.

A Bridge in San Domingo. The Secretary of State has sent a telegram to the United States consul at Santo Domingo, instructing him to request the cancellation of a decree by the Dominican government involving the seizure of a bridge over the Ozama river at Santo Domingo, built by Mr. Nat. McKay, a citizen of the United States, under a twenty years' exclusive concession. The bridge was built three years ago at an expense of [illegible] and was recently damaged by a scow running into it. Mr. McKay has been held responsible for the injury to the scow in the sum of $3,000, and has protested against the judgment, inasmuch as the bridge was the principal sufferer by the collision, which, moreover, he claims was due entirely to the mismanagement of the scow. Work of Chinese Forgers. Latest advices from China tell of the uttering by Chinese forgers on the Island of Java of [illegible] of Java banknotes. In order to get the notes accepted, forged notary acceptances were placed on them. Many prominent Chinese merchants have been placed under arrest. [illegible]
