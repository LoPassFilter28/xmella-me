The Sun reported that an English company was replacing an American syndicate in managing Dominican customs duties and railroad concessions to secure foreign loan payments. The article traced the concession back to an earlier multimillion-dollar loan by the Amsterdam firm Westendorp & Co., which had financed a railroad from Puerto Plata to the interior. It noted that customs revenues subsequently declined due to poor crops, administrative mismanagement, and widespread smuggling.

The Sun informó que una compañía inglesa estaba sustituyendo a un consorcio estadounidense en la administración de las aduanas y concesiones ferroviarias dominicanas para asegurar el pago de empréstitos extranjeros. El artículo remontó el origen de la concesión a un préstamo multimillonario previo de la firma de Ámsterdam Westendorp & Co., la cual había financiado un ferrocarril desde Puerto Plata hacia el interior. Señaló además que las rentas aduaneras disminuyeron con posterioridad debido a malas cosechas, mala gestión administrativa y un contrabando generalizado.

character not unusual; and, in fact, the English company is practically taking the place of an American syndicate that received, several years ago, a like control of the import and export duties, to cover a loan. The railroad concessions and control which the American syndicate has hitherto enjoyed also pass to its British successor, the latter paying to the former whatever the Dominican Government still owes it on account of its loan. We may further point out that the original holder of these customs privileges was the Amsterdam banking firm of Westendorp Co., which made a loan of several million dollars to San Domingo eight or nine years ago. It was stipulated that "Westendorp Co. should appoint their own agents in collecting the duties, that these duties were not to be altered without their consent, and that Holland should be the arbiter in disputes between them and San Domingo." The Amsterdam firm thereupon floated its bonds, and of a total amount of $6,600,000, American capitalists, we think, took about $2,800,000. San Domingo, with the proceeds, cancelled some old debts and began work on the railroad running from Puerto Plata to the interior. But hard times came, with poor crops, affecting the exports, and there was also, it is said, mismanagement at the ports and no little smuggling, affecting the imports.
