The Salt Lake Tribune reviewed the origin of the 1905 Dominican modus vivendi, crediting former American Minister Thomas C. Dawson with devising the administrative revenue plan. Put into effect on April 1, 1905, the agreement placed customs collection under American officials who distributed funds between the government and foreign creditors. The article observed that civil conflicts ceased almost immediately once revolutionists were unable to seize customs houses and revenues.

The Salt Lake Tribune examinó el origen del modus vivendi dominicano de 1905, reconociendo al exministro estadounidense Thomas C. Dawson la concepción del plan administrativo de recaudación. Puesto en vigor el 1 de abril de 1905, el acuerdo confió el cobro aduanero a funcionarios estadounidenses que distribuían los fondos entre el gobierno y los acreedores extranjeros. El artículo observó que las guerras civiles cesaron casi de inmediato una vez que los revolucionarios se vieron impedidos de apoderarse de las aduanas y de sus caudales.

It became evident that the creditors would be willing to wait indefinitely for actual payment providing they could be assured that the revenues would be honestly collected and their proportion placed in secure hands. Thomas O. Dawson, who has since been minister to Panama and who has recently been appointed minister resident by the department of state, was then minister to Santo Domingo. He it was who stepped into the breach and evolved the plan that has since been worked out with such success. He it was who laid the groundwork for the temporary solution. Accordingly, the Dominican government submitted the draft of a proposed modus vivendi which, after some modifications, was later accepted by the president of the United States, and, by decree of the Dominican government, put into effect April 1, 1905. It provided simply that impartial and competent Americans should collect the customs duties, pay [illegible] per cent to the Dominican government and deposit the remainder in a trust fund to be distributed later among the creditors in proportion to their just claims. The good effects of this arrangement were immediately manifest. Serious civil disturbances ceased. [illegible] The practical impossibility of securing possession of the Customhouses and so of the revenues of the country discouraged would-be revolutionists.
