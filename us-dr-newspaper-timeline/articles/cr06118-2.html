New-York daily tribune — 1858-07-01

The *New-York daily tribune* reported that the cultivation of tobacco had stimulated industry in Santiago, a city with a fine climate and a larger population than its southern rival, which the prevailing party in the late civil war—led by Pedro Santana—sought to make the seat of government. Historically, the territory acknowledged Spanish authority until declaring independence as the Republic of Spanish Haiti in 1821, though the inhabitants soon united with the western Haitian Republic under President Boyer. Following the fall of Boyer twenty-one years later, the people of San Domingo seized the opportunity to regain their independence.

El *New-York daily tribune* dio cuenta de que el laboreo del tabaco fomentó la actividad productiva en Santiago, plaza favorecida por su clima benigno y mayor vecindario que su émula del sur, la cual la facción triunfante en la pasada discordia civil —conducida por Pedro Santana— procuraba designar capital del Estado. En lo tocante a su pasado, la comarca reconoció la potestad hispana hasta proclamar su emancipación como Estado Independiente del Haití Español en 1821, incorporándose en breve los naturales a la República haitiana bajo el presidente Boyer. Tras el derrocamiento de Boyer veintiún años después, el pueblo dominicano aprovechó la coyuntura para reconquistar su libertad política.

Republic. The cultivation of tobacco, introduced some years airce into this part of the island, has given oceasion to a certain degree of industry and aud ita veghborhood. Santiago, which by virtue of ite elevation possesses a fize climate, can boast quite a number of baudeome new buildings. Its population is groater than thet of ite southera rival, end it is the policy of the prevailing party ia the late civil war to make it the seat of governwent. Tbe leader in this movemect is Pedro Santana,
