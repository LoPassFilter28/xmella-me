Senator Morton's reply to Sumner: the commission could not negotiate, Haiti itself assured Minister Bassett it would not interfere with the Dominicans' free will, and the United States merely warned Haiti - which had given Cabral passage and war vessels - not to make war during the negotiations.

La respuesta del senador Morton a Sumner: la comisión no podía negociar, Haití mismo aseguró al ministro Bassett que no interferiría con la libre voluntad de los dominicanos, y Estados Unidos solo advirtió a Haití - que había dado a Cabral paso y buques de guerra - que no hiciera la guerra durante las negociaciones.

Mr. Morton then had read by the Secretary a dispatch to United States Minister Bassett, from the Haytien Secretary of Foreign Affairs, assuring him that Hayti had no disposition or desire to interfere with the free will of the Dominicans. As a further reply to Mr. Sumner, he reminded the Senator, as evidence, that the Administration had not proposed to take any immediate action upon annexation; that Commission had no authority to negotiate a treaty, and that no one went with them who could take a single step in that direction. The Haytien Government, in violation of the law of nations, had given free passage across its territory to Cabral, and had furnished him with war vessels to attack Dominica, and the United States had merely notified Hayti that while our negotiations were pending with Dominica we would not allow him to make war on the island. This the United States had an undoubted right to do, because our Government had information of organizations on foot in Hayti to defeat annexation. No one need tell him (Morton) that the Senator from Massachusetts was a friend of liberty or the colored man when he so industriously spent his time in an effort to put the Democratic party in power, for to destroy the President is to destroy the great party of which he is the head (applause), and we know the terrible effect upon the liberties of this country if the Democratic party comes into power.
