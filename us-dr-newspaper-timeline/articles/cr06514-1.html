Accounts from the Dominican Republic to the 7th of December have been received in Havana, by which it appears that Baez and his party are hard pressed by the liberator, Santana. So complete is said to be the demoralization which prevails among them, and so hopeless their position, that official steps have been taken to procure peace. The English, French, and Spanish Consuls met in the camp of Santana to arrange the terms on which the belligerents could lay down their arms, but have hitherto failed to satisfy Santana, who insists upon Baez reducing to writing the proposition for the capitulation of San Domingo. There is little doubt that the latter will be obliged eventually to accede to this demand, as the party of Santana are rapidly gaining successes and adherents in all parts of the country. Tan completa se dice ser la desmoralización que prevalece entre ellos, y tan desesperada su posición, que se han tomado pasos oficiales para procurar la paz. Los Cónsules inglés, francés y español se reunieron en el campo de Santana para arreglar los términos en que los beligerantes podrían deponer las armas, pero hasta aquí han fracasado en satisfacer a Santana, quien insiste en que Báez reduzca a escrito la proposición para la capitulación de Santo Domingo.
