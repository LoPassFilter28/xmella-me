Passage summaryResumen del pasaje

The *Washington Daily News* addressed reports from Puerto Rican exiles that General Rafael Trujillo had negotiated with Germany to lease Samaná Bay as a submarine base, dismissing the claims as improbable. The writer argued that Trujillo sought friendly relations with the United States and would view leasing the bay to Nazi Germany as an act of hostility. The piece asserted that in any emergency, Trujillo would likely grant the U.S. Navy use of Samaná Bay and other Dominican ports.

El *Washington Daily News* abordó las versiones de exiliados puertorriqueños que sostenían que el general Rafael Trujillo había negociado con Alemania el arrendamiento de la bahía de Samaná como apostadero para submarinos, catalogando las afirmaciones de improbables. El editorialista razonó que Trujillo procuraba cultivar lazos cordiales con Estados Unidos y consideraría la cesión de la bahía a la Alemania nazi como una provocación hostil. El artículo aseguraba que, ante cualquier contingencia, era más probable que Trujillo concediera a la Armada estadounidense el uso de la bahía de Samaná y de otros puertos dominicanos.

ANENT SAMANA BAY

Now, once more anent Samana Bay.

The other day it was reported that

Gen. Trujillo had negotiated with Ger

many for the lease of that strategic

body of water as a submarine base.

The report emanated from Puerto Rico,

where there are a number of exiled

enemies of the Generalissimo. It does

seem that in view of the fact the

General desires only the friendliest re

lations with this country, he would be

smarter than to enter into any such

negotiations. Especially when he had

so recently entertained a number of

prominent Americans in his own house.

The lease of the bay to the Nazis

would not only be an unfriendly act

to the United States, but a hostile one.

And, also in view of this same friend

liness, it is certain that at any emer

gency the General would permit the

United States Navy the use of Samana

Bay or any other Dominican port, as a

submarine base or any other military

faculty.

These aren’t direct quotes from the

Generalissimo, but they are very defi

nitely the impression received during

our visit. The General knows that we

are friendly to his country, but he also

knows that we expect him to play ball

with us.
