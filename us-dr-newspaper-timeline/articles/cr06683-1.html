1849-06-20 · Raleigh, N.C. · 1844-1861 Independence, recognition, Samana schemes

IMPORTANT FROM ST. DOMINGO.

SourceFuente: The North-Carolina standard, Raleigh, N.C., 1849-06-20
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85042147/1849-06-20/ed-1/?sp=3

By the arrival of the schooner J. D. Lindsay, Captain Hathaway, from St. Domingo City, which place she left on the 25th ult., we learn that another revolution had broken out, and that the army and navy had declared in favor of General Santa Anna as President.

Hernandes, the President, met the troops on the outskirts of the city, and declared to them that he was still their commander. General Santa Anna then withdrew from the army, and retired to his plantation, after which the troops disbanded; but they not being satisfied, again requested Santa Anna to head them, which he complied with, and immediately marched for the city. Previous to his march, the city of San Carlos, was burnt by order of President Hernandes, with a view of preventing the troops taking possession of it.

It was supposed that St. Domingo City would fall into the hands of Gen. Santa Anna, and that he would be declared President of the Dominican government.

New York Herald.
