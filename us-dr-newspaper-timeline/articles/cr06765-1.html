The Evening Star published a statement praising President Trujillo for directing the rebuilding of Santo Domingo following its destruction, modernizing the city without incurring new debt or defaulting on bonded interest payments. The report highlighted extensive nationwide public works, including new roads, schools, hospitals, Latin America's largest suspension bridge, and a nearly completed $3,000,000 artificial port at Trujillo City. It added that despite public efforts to rename the capital in his honor, Trujillo had initially opposed the proposal.

El Evening Star publicó una declaración en la que elogiaba al presidente Trujillo por dirigir la reconstrucción de Santo Domingo tras su destrucción, modernizando la urbe sin contraer nueva deuda ni suspender el pago de intereses sobre los bonos. El informe destacó amplias obras públicas en todo el territorio nacional, como nuevas carreteras, escuelas, hospitales, el puente colgante más grande de América Latina y un puerto artificial de 3.000.000 de dólares a punto de concluirse en Ciudad Trujillo. Añadió que, pese a las gestiones públicas para rebautizar la capital en su honor, Trujillo se había opuesto inicialmente a dicha propuesta.

President Trujillo, the statement says: "With a will that brought comment from the entire world, the 39-year-old Chief Executive personally directed the task of saving the impoverished population from starvation and epidemics. and then of constructing a new city to replace the old one. Rapidly new streets, buildings, homes and parks appeared, until Santo Domingo became a new metropolis in gleaming white, far surpassing the city it had replaced. "Without defaulting or postponing Interest payments on bonded indebtedness. and without incurring new indebtedness. President Trujillo conducted a vast program of public works throughout the nation, financed entirely from current revenue. New highways were laid into every corner of the country; great bridges were swung across rivers, among them the largest suspension bridge in Latin America; new schools, hospitals, libraries. were erected everywhere. Agricultural development mounted to new all-time highs and industry leaped from a handful of manufacturing establishments to more than 1,200. After his re-election in 1934. President Trujillo began an even more impressive public works program, the climax of which is the creation of an artificial port at Trujillo City, now nearly completed. At a cost of $3,000,000 this new port permits for the first time actual entry to the docks of the harbor.
