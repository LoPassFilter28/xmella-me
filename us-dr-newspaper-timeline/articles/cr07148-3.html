The Springfield Weekly Republican reported that Rafael Estrella Ureña assumed the provisional presidency of the Dominican Republic after negotiating with Fort Ozama commander General Rafael Trujillo. Outgoing President Horacio Vásquez requested legislative permission to travel abroad due to ill health, while Trujillo declined consideration for the presidency. U.S. Minister Charles B. Curtis, who had insisted on constitutional continuity, expressed satisfaction with the arrangement as rebel forces secured political control.

El Springfield Weekly Republican informó que Rafael Estrella Ureña asumió la presidencia provisional de la República Dominicana tras negociar con el comandante de la Fortaleza Ozama, general Rafael Trujillo. El presidente saliente Horacio Vásquez solicitó autorización legislativa para viajar al extranjero por motivos de salud, mientras que Trujillo declinó postularse a la presidencia. El ministro estadounidense Charles B. Curtis, quien había insistido en mantener la continuidad constitucional, expresó su conformidad con el acuerdo mientras las fuerzas rebeldes consolidaban el control político.

Vasquez Wishes to Travel At the same time President Vasquez asked the National Assembly to permit him to leave the republic and to stay in a foreign country. He gave ill health as his reason for making such a request before his term of office was over. Estrella Urena, who is a lawyer by profession, led the insurgent troops into Santo Domingo two days ago, and carried on negotiations with Gen. Rafael Trujillo, loyal commander of Fort Ozama. He was minister of justice and instruction in the Dominican cabinet in 1928, but resigned six months later and was appointed minister to France. Trujillo Declines Candidacy Gen. Trujillo declined to be considered as a candidate for the provisional presidency, though his name had been prominently mentioned in connection with it. Charles B. Curtis, American minister to the republic, who today informed the insurgents that the American government would not recognize a de facto government, apparently found the appointment of Estrella Urena satisfactory. With the departure of President Vasquez and the appointment of [a?] provisional president chosen from the ranks of the insurgents themselves, the political effervescence of the past week will probably die down. The insurgents' advance on Santo Domingo and their seizure of power in the north was practically bloodless, and no violent enmities seem to have been aroused by their action.
