An Associated Press dispatch from Savannah, Georgia, dated November 9 reports that Commander Meltz of the United States gunboat Newport received orders to coal at once and sail for Puerto Plata. The coaling was rushed, a gun was fired at 7:30 to summon crew members on shore leave, and at 9 in the morning the vessel steamed down the river to sea; Commander Meltz refused to discuss his orders, but said they were urgent.

Un despacho de Associated Press desde Savannah, Georgia, fechado el 9 de noviembre informa que el comandante Meltz, del cañonero Newport de los Estados Unidos, recibió órdenes de cargar carbón de inmediato y zarpar hacia Puerto Plata. La carga de carbón se hizo a toda prisa, se disparó un cañonazo a las 7:30 para llamar a los tripulantes con permiso en tierra, y a las 9 de la mañana el buque bajó por el río hacia el mar; el comandante Meltz se negó a hablar de sus órdenes, pero dijo que eran urgentes.

(By the Associated Press.) Savannah, Ga., Nov. 9. — Commander Meltz, of the United States gunboat Newport, received orders to coal at once and sail for Puerto Plata. The coaling was rushed and at 7:30 o'clock, the Newport fired a gun to summon such members of her crew as had received shore leave. At 9 a. m. the vessel steamed down the river proceeding immediately to sea. Commander Meltz refused to discuss his orders, but said they were "urgent." Ready to Negotiate Canal Treaty. (By the Associated Press.) Washington, Nov. 9. — The State Department has been informed by an agent of the Panama Canal Company that the provisional government of Panama has designated a commission of three members, one of whom is [Federico?] [Boyd?], a member of the Junta, who will leave Panama tomorrow for Washington, to begin immediately the negotiations of a new canal treaty. The commission, it is stated, is clothed with full powers to conclude the instrument. The Protest of Colombia. (By the Associated Press.) Washington, Nov. 9. Secretary Hay today placed in the hands of the President the Colombian protest against the course pursued by the Washington government on the isthmus and its attitude toward the new government of Panama.
