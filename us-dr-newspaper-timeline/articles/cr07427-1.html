Revelations Made by Harry J. Overman, Brother of Senator From South Carolina.

Inside information as to the influences at work on the appointment of American representatives in the Dominican government was brought out yesterday at the first session of the Sullivan hearing here.

Harry J. Overman, file clerk in the office of the Secretary of the Senate and a brother of Senator Lea S. Overman of North Carolina, testified concerning his efforts to obtain appointment as deputy receiver of customs at Santo Domingo under the treaty relations between the Dominican republic and the United States. He declared that he had been urged to apply for the place by a man named Gold, who, he said, suggested that William C. Beer would be a good person to support him for the place. Beer, he said he was told by Gold, could influence Secretary of State Bryan through John H. Grey, of Wilmington, Del.

Md. Overman testified that inasmuch as his own brother was unwilling to use his position to further his candidacy he was compelled to do much of the work himself. He said that he had met Mr. Beer at a hotel in Washington in company with Minister Sullivan.

John E. Osborne, First Assistant Secretary of State, stated that prior to the appointment of Mr. Sullivan, he had gone to Haiti and Santo Domingo on a secret mission for the Government. While there, he stated, he had warned the Dominican government against increasing its indebtedness.

Regarding the Sullivan case, Secretary Osborne said that he knew practically nothing except that he had received personal letters from Mr. Vick, criticising the conduct of the minister.

Others who were present were Joseph P. Tumulty, Secretary to the President; Assistant Secretary of State, Philips; Assistant Secretary of War Breckenridge, and Brigadier General McIntyre, chief of the Bureau of Insular Affairs, War Department.

At the outset of today's session Charles H. Strong, attorney, conducting the examination for Comimssioner Phelan, announced that the entire scope of the investigation was to inquire into the fitness, competence, and conduct of Minister Sullivan.

“The Commissioner intends,” he said, “to run down any legitimate clue bearing on this case without fear or favor for anybody in or out of public office. He means to sift the case to the bottom. Necessarily, inasmuch as the lines upon which this investigation is being conducted are wholly different from court procedure, much hearsay testimony, or even gossip, must creep into the evidence. But as a result of this hearsay evidence we have already been able to unearth things of the utmost importance.”
