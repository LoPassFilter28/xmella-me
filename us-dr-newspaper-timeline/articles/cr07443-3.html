The representatives of the Samana Bay Company, however, had protested against the action of the Dominican Government, and he (Gov. Fabens), with Capt. Samuels, had returned to the North in order that they might lay the matter before their directors, and subsequently before the Government. Dr. Howe, with his wife, Mrs. Julia Ward Howe, would remain at Samana, acting as Acting Governor, to protect the interests of the Company. When the Commissioners left Santo Domingo the Government had dispatched a force to take possession of the Company’s property, and as the Tybee was getting under way the Dominican steamer was also on her way to Samana. Although these events caused a great deal of discussion in Santo Domingo, he did not deem it probable that any popular tumult would occur. Gov. Fabens further said that while he could not say precisely what action the Company would take, he thought it very probable that they would call upon the United States Government for protection of its citizens, and demand that the matter be referred to arbitration. The Company had leased the Island of Levantados in Samana Bay to the United States, as a coaling station, on such terms as the United States deemed proper and equitable.

Paul N. Spofford, another of the directors, said that he did not care to connect himself with the expression of any views as yet. The Samana Bay Company would have a meeting this week and discuss the situation. He understood that the abrogation of the treaty was largely due to British influence, backed by the promised support of the British Government, the loan and full equipment of a vessel of war and the loan of an amount amounting to several millions of dollars to the Dominican Government to be repaid when convenient, without interest. He did not believe, however, that the failure of the Hartmont (English) loan, which had been the cause of some diplomatic and other correspondence between the Dominican Government, was responsible for the abrogation of the treaty.

Another director said that the Dominican Government had some time since granted concessions to the Company, and another to the Samana Surveying Company. The Samana Bay Company had not absorbed the last-mentioned Companies, and any defaults of either of the last-mentioned Company would not vitiate its grants.
