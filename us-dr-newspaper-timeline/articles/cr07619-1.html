The South Bend News-Times reviewed U.S. financial interventions in Latin America, noting Theodore Roosevelt's treaty appointing an American customs receiver for the Dominican Republic to prevent European debt intervention. It reported that U.S. forces landed in 1914 after unauthorized debt increases and withdrew in 1924 under an agreement retaining an American financial adviser. The article also summarized similar fiscal receivership and military intervention arrangements in Haiti.

El South Bend News-Times examinó las intervenciones financieras de Estados Unidos en América Latina, destacando el tratado de Theodore Roosevelt que designó a un receptor general de aduanas estadounidense para la República Dominicana a fin de evitar la intervención europea por cobro de deudas. Señaló que fuerzas estadounidenses desembarcaron en 1914 tras incrementos no autorizados de la deuda y se retiraron en 1924 bajo un acuerdo que conservaba un consejero financiero estadounidense. El artículo también resumió disposiciones semejantes de intervención militar y control fiscal aduanero en Haití.

Santo Domingo (Dominican Republic) To forestall European intervention because the country was not repaying its loans, Roosevelt made a treaty allowing the United States to appoint an American official as general receiver of customs for the republic. Several years later, in 1914, the country threatened to increase its debts without American consent and the United States sent in military forces. These were withdrawn in 1924 under an agreement allowing an American "adviser" to assist the government in managing its finances. Haiti The treaty of 1915 allows the United States to "nominate" a general receiver of customs and a financial adviser, and specifies the disposition of government revenues. Marines were landed in 1915 to support these American officials. Colombia Private American financial experts have revised the country's fiscal system and set up a new national bank. Guatemala There have been rumors that the country was seeking a private American financial adviser. In 1913 Great Britain threatened to send a warship if the government continued to misappropriate coffee duties which had been set aside as security for a British loan.
