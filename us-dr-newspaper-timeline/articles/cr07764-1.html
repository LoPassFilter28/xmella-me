A letter to the editor attacks Secretary of State Seward's shifting plans for St. Domingo, arguing that whether the form is intervention, a protectorate or annexation, the result would be enormous wealth for certain private individuals. The writer says President Baez, entrenched in his capital with civil wars to look after and exiles including Cabral returning, proposes through Seward to transfer to the United States the parts of his country over which he has no control.

Una carta al editor ataca los cambiantes planes del secretario de Estado Seward para Santo Domingo, argumentando que, ya sea bajo la forma de intervención, de protectorado o de anexión, el resultado sería una riqueza enorme para ciertos particulares. El autor dice que el presidente Báez, atrincherado en su capital con guerras civiles que atender y exiliados como Cabral de regreso, propone por medio de Seward transferir a los Estados Unidos las partes de su país sobre las que no tiene ningún control.

[illegible] THE DOMINICAN QUESTION To the Editor of The Tribune SIR: The plans of the Secretary of State in regard to St. Domingo are many and various, but all tend in the same direction. When one form of action is ignominiously defeated in the House, he has substantially the same thing (at least so far as the interests of private parties are concerned) proposed in another shape. It makes little difference to him whether it is intervention, a protectorate," or annexation," which finally succeeds. Either will give enormous wealth to certain private individuals for whom Mr. Seward's benevolent nature seems to be painfully excited. I mean no reflection upon the persons who have secured Mr. Seward's very active assistance. They have a right to find their fortunes where they can, and if Mr. Seward chooses to prostitute his office to their service, they would be more than "Reward and Weed men," if they refused to profit by it. But he has a simple public duty to perform, and has no right to know or to promote any interests but those of his country. I do not profess to be specially informed on this subject.
