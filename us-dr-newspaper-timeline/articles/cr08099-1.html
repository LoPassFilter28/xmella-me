A Tribune correspondent describes the San Domingo expedition's voyage to Samana Bay: charming weather for two days, then stormy seas from the latitude of Hatteras onward, until land was sighted on the morning of January 24. Rounding Cape Cabron and entering the bay, the party saw the roofs of Santa Barbara de Samana, palm tops, green hills and small farm patches, and dropped anchor in the outer harbor at two o'clock that afternoon.

Un corresponsal del Tribune describe el viaje de la expedición a Santo Domingo hasta la bahía de Samaná: tiempo encantador durante dos días y luego mares tormentosos desde la latitud de Hatteras en adelante, hasta que se avistó tierra en la mañana del 24 de enero. Al doblar el Cabo Cabrón y entrar en la bahía, el grupo vio los techos de Santa Bárbara de Samaná, las copas de las palmas, las colinas verdes y pequeños pedazos de tierra cultivada, y echó el ancla en el puerto exterior a las dos de esa tarde.

A [letter?] in the New York Tribune giving an account of the San Domingo expedition, in addition to what we published yesterday: THE [illegible] [illegible] THE ARRIVAL AT SAMANA BAY THE BAY AND ITS SURROUNDINGS. Writing in the Bay of Samana, on January 27, the Tribune correspondent says: We had charming weather for the first two days out cold, indeed, but bright and clear, with fresh and favoring winds, and every prospect of a pleasant voyage. But toward evening of the second day, in the rough latitude of Hatteras, our troubles began, and from that time until we neared our destination we experienced abundant discomfort and many stormy seas, though without any specially severe test of the seagoing qualities of the frigate. Using generally both steam and sails, we have made a moderately quick passage; but the behavior of the vessel under steam is far from satisfactory, and the machinery is found to be full of serious defects. As a sailer, however, the ship is pronounced faultless. We had been out of sight of land six days and a half, when on the morning of the 24th we heard from the look-out in the maintop the welcome cry of "Land ho!" and the passengers rushed upon deck. Thick clouds hung heavily over a dark line about two points off our starboard bow, hardly discernible at first; but as we came nearer we gradually traced the outline of the mountain range which stretched westward to the mainland from Cape Cabron at the extremity of the peninsula of Samana.
