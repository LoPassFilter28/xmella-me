A correspondent at Samana describes landing with the commissioners and inspecting the town's public buildings, including its Catholic church, a Methodist church being built, and its schools, where the textbooks are all in English and published in Philadelphia. He also describes the barefoot, half-clad troops guarding the jail, their varied knives, swords and muskets, and a village where more than half of the eighty houses are in some way devoted to trade.

Un corresponsal en Samaná describe el desembarco con los comisionados y la inspección de los edificios públicos del pueblo, incluidos su iglesia católica, una iglesia metodista en construcción y sus escuelas, donde los libros de texto están todos en inglés y publicados en Filadelfia. También describe a las tropas descalzas y semivestidas que guardan la cárcel, con sus variados cuchillos, espadas y mosquetes, y un pueblo donde más de la mitad de las ochenta casas se dedican de algún modo al comercio.

some time was passed in pleasant conversation over the news from home and from the fleet. Later in the day the commissioners landed for a visit to the local authorities, and these latter distinguished officials hastened to return the compliment. It was not until the morning of the 25th that the rest of the party went ashore. SCENES AT SAMANA. After giving the description of the town, already published by us, the correspondent continues: We pushed our way through the lazy, grinning, good-natured crowd, and made an inspection of the public buildings of Samana. It did not take us long, for there are none at all except two churches and a calaboose and, perhaps, I should add a couple of school houses. The Catholic church is a rude structure, much larger than the inhabitants seem to require, and full of the oddest images dressed in quaint, not to say grotesque, habiliments. We tried to converse with the priest, but he spoke none of the three languages at the command of our party, so we gave it up. We afterwards learned that he was an Italian. A Methodist church is building under the auspices of the English Mission Society, and the British flag is displayed on the unfinished edifice as well as on the residence of the minister. The Rev. Joseph James is a colored man of ordinary intelligence, and has a congregation including about three hundred communicants, mostly descendants of the colored colony sent here from Philadelphia in [illegible].
