A Herald correspondent interviews an imprisoned former governor who says he took up arms because he wanted liberty of expression in the vote concerning the sale of Samana. He insists the people were dragooned into voting for the Baez measure and would have voted against it if the pressure had been taken off, that none of the money paid by the Americans would benefit the country but would go into the pockets of Baez and his associates, and that the government has done nothing for the people toward Monte Cristo who have had no rain in two years.

Un corresponsal del Herald entrevista a un exgobernador encarcelado que dice que tomó las armas porque quería libertad de expresión en la votación sobre la venta de Samaná. Insiste en que la gente fue obligada a votar por la medida de Báez y habría votado en contra si se le hubiera quitado la presión, que nada del dinero pagado por los estadounidenses beneficiaría al país sino que iría a los bolsillos de Báez y sus asociados, y que el gobierno no ha hecho nada por la gente de hacia Monte Cristi que lleva dos años sin lluvia.

English, without being perfect, was at least plain. I entered into conversation with the old man. I inquired what was the motive of his going into [illegible] acts of hostility to the government, and he answered that he was anxious to have liberty of expression in the vote concerning the Samana sale. Had he succeeded in capturing Puerto Plata the sense of the people would have been freely given on the question of renting Samana Bay to the Americans. They would not have been dragooned to vote for the Baez measure, and if the pressure had been taken off a majority would have been found against it. Every man who voted did so in dread of the consequences of recording himself in opposition to the government wishes. Only one or two had the temerity to say "no," but they were mere lads and their conduct was overlooked, he was OPPOSED TO THE BAEZ ADMINISTRATION because he believed it was composed of a set of thieves, and Baez was the biggest thief of the lot. The country would never receive any benefit from the money paid by the Americans. It would all go into the pockets of Baez and his associates, but principally of Baez. He should like to know where all the money went that Baez got from time to time to carry on the government. There was none of it expended for the good of the country, and the soldiers were half starving. Baez not only sold the Republic, but he has also cheated the Americans, and before long they will find that they had the worst of the bargain. Cabral may be over the border any day,
