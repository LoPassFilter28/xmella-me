The Springfield Weekly Republican reported that the implementation of the Dominican customs receivership awaited President Morales concluding agreements with European creditor nations. The Secretary of War prepared to nominate customs collectors, with President Roosevelt recommending William E. Gould of Baltimore, a linguist and financial editor, for a collectorship. It was also anticipated that George R. Colton, formerly an American revenue collector in the Philippines, would be appointed to assist in administering the Dominican customs service.

El Springfield Weekly Republican informó que la implementación de la administración de aduanas dominicanas dependía de que el presidente Morales concluyera acuerdos con las naciones acreedoras europeas. El secretario de Guerra se preparaba para nombrar a los recaudadores de aduanas, habiendo recomendado el presidente Roosevelt a William E. Gould, de Baltimore, lingüista y editor financiero, para una recaudaduría. Asimismo, se preveía la designación de George R. Colton, antiguo recaudador de rentas estadounidense en Filipinas, para colaborar en la administración del servicio de aduanas dominicano.

It is said that the next step in the. execution of the department’s plans relative to San Domingo cannot be taken until President Morales has formally concluded the arrangement with the foreign creditor powers, two of whom up to Tuesday had not answered. When the president has secured the necessary signatures to his agreement, and has so informed Washington, the secretary of war will nominate to President Morales the person who is to act as chief collector, and perhaps the subcollectors at the minor ports, though it is possible the designation of these will be left to the chief collector. William E. Gould to be Collector. The president has decided to recommend the selection of William E. Gould of Baltimore as one of the collectors of customs at San Domingo. It has not yet been determined whether Mr Gould shall have general charge of the collections at all ports or whether other persons will be appointed. The details are still under consideration. It is stated that in addition to Mr Gould it is quite likely that G. R. Colton, formerly collector of internal revenue at Iloilo, Philippine islands, will be named as one of the collectors. Mr Gould is a New Yorker, though he has lived at Baltimore for some time past. He is a graduate of Johns Hopkins, and has made a special study of the Romance languages. At present he is doing financial editorial work on the staff of the Evening Herald of Washington.
