The Evening Star reported on deliberations at the Japanese peace treaty conference in San Francisco. Representatives from Mexico, the Dominican Republic, Chile, and Bolivia endorsed the proposed treaty and pledged their signatures, with Soviet delegate Andrei Gromyko standing alone in opposition. The Soviet Union introduced sweeping amendments demanding the withdrawal of U.S. forces, demilitarization, and great-power veto rights.

El Evening Star informó sobre las deliberaciones en la conferencia del tratado de paz con Japón celebrada en San Francisco. Representantes de México, la República Dominicana, Chile y Bolivia respaldaron el proyecto de tratado y comprometieron sus firmas, quedando el delegado soviético Andréi Gromyko como el único opositor. La Unión Soviética presentó enmiendas de gran alcance en las que exigía la retirada de las fuerzas de EE. UU., la desmilitarización y derechos de veto para las grandes potencias.

Mr. Gromyko’s amendments would (1) compel the withdrawal of all American troops from Japan 90 days after the treaty went into force, (2) prevent any mili tary alliance by Japan “against any power” which fought against Japan in World War II, and (3) require demilitarization of certain Japanese coastal areas. Russia also would get a veto over the treaty along with other great powers. The Soviet amendments further would (1) take the Ryukyu and Bonin Islands from United States administration and give them to Japan, (2) legally secure Russia’s 1 own hold on the Kurile Islands and South Sakhalin and (3) pro vide for reparations from Japan to nations which Japanese forces occupied in the war.
