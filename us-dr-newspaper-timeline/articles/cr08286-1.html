LATER FROM ST. DOMINGO.

SourceFuente: The daily national Whig, Washington, D.C., 1849-06-13
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82014405/1849-06-13/ed-1/?sp=2
sugar_trade_economyrevolution_unrest

—By the arrival of the schooner J. B. Lindsay, Capt. Hattaway, from St. Domingo city, which place she left on the 24th ult., we learn that another revolution had broken out, and that the army and navy had declared in favor of Gen. Santa Anna as President. Hernannes, the President, met the troops on the outskirts of the city, and declared to them that he was still their commander. Gen. Santa Anna then withdrew from the army, and retired to his plantation, after which the troops disbanded, but they, not being satisfied, again requested Santa Anna to head them, which he complied with, and immediately marched for the city. Previous to his march, the troops had sent in a guard in advance to President Hernannes, with a view of preventing his property being destroyed, &c.

It was reported that St. Domingo city would fall into the hands of Gen. Santa Anna, and that he would be declared President of the Dominican Government.
