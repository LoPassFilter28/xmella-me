Passage summaryResumen del pasaje

The *Maryland women's news* published Horace G. Knowles's account of the 1916 American intervention in the Dominican Republic, alleging that President Wilson secretly deployed naval forces to seize the nation without congressional approval or a war declaration. Warships landed marines under covering guns, after which American authorities presented President Francisco Henriquez y Carvajal with a treaty subordinating national sovereignty. When Henriquez refused the terms, the American naval commander initiated full military subjugation of the republic.

El *Maryland women's news* publicó el relato de Horace G. Knowles sobre la intervención estadounidense de 1916 en la República Dominicana, alegando que el presidente Wilson desplegó secretamente fuerzas navales para apoderarse de la nación sin aprobación del Congreso ni declaración de guerra. Buques de guerra desembarcaron a infantes de marina bajo el fuego de cobertura de su artillería, tras lo cual las autoridades estadounidenses presentaron al presidente Francisco Henríquez y Carvajal un tratado que subordinaba la soberanía nacional. Al rechazar Henríquez las condiciones, el comandante naval estadounidense inició el pleno sometimiento militar de la república.

The following is a condensed summary of the case as presented by

Mr. Knowles:

The Invasion.

In May of 1916, when the Dominican Republic was at peace with the

world, while it was a party to two existing treaties with the United States

and in direct violation to one of them, and without the slightest attempt

to appeal to or use diplomatic means or submitting any question in dispute

to The Hague tribunal, President Wilson ordered a part of the United

States Navy, without any notice to the Dominican Government, to go

secretly to Santo Domingo to seize the government and proceed to subju

gate the people. This was done without consulting or informing Con

gress, without a declaration of war.

American battleships entered the roadstead of Santo Domingo City,

and under the cover of long range guns landed a force of marines and

took possession. This was a paramount act of war. A little later the

United States presented to the Dominican president, Francisco Henriquez

y Carvajal, a treaty which completely destroyed the liberty, independence

and sovereignty of his nation, putting it under the absolute control of

the United States. It was an exact duplicate of the treaty which had just

been forced upon the helpless Haitian government by guns and bayonets.
