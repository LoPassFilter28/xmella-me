A feature on Santo Domingo under President Morales says the government pays out money daily at Santiago, La Vega, Moca, Puerto Plata and other towns to maintain peace, while recalcitrant citizens and even suspects are imprisoned, banished or summarily shot. It argues that export duties ruin the sugar planter and discourage production, even though tobacco from the Cibao is shipped to Germany and cocoa, coffee, wax, honey and gold washed from mountain streams all leave the country.

Un artículo sobre Santo Domingo bajo el presidente Morales dice que el gobierno reparte dinero a diario en Santiago, La Vega, Moca, Puerto Plata y otros pueblos para mantener la paz, mientras los ciudadanos recalcitrantes e incluso los sospechosos son encarcelados, desterrados o fusilados sumariamente. Sostiene que los derechos de exportación arruinan al productor de azúcar y desaniman la producción, aun cuando el tabaco del Cibao se envía a Alemania y el cacao, el café, la cera, la miel y el oro lavado de los arroyos de las montañas salen del país.

At Santiago the present government of Morales PAYS $300 DAILY OR A MONTHLY [illegible], DISTRIBUTED TO ITS VAGABOND POPULATION, INCLUDING GENERALS galore, to maintain [peace?]. At La Vega it pays $200 daily for the same purpose, and at Moca, Puerto Plata and each of the other important towns, approximately as much or more, while all recalcitrant citizens and even suspects are thrown into prison, banished or summarily shot. Life, liberty and the right to enjoy the usufructs of one's own property have no guarantees, In a country where almost anything in the way of an agricultural product will grow almost spontaneously from the soil, and hogs and cattle roam wild at will through the forests and over the plains, scarcely anyone has the courage to do anything. The lone negro in his hut by the wayside will tell you that he has nothing to eat, because the revolution comes along And Takes or Destroys all he has; the otherwise prosperous sugar planter tells you that his industry is ruined by the export duty which the government slaps upon his product before it is allowed to leave the country, and so with the production of cocoa, a source of untold wealth, and anything else which the government finds leaving the country, and on which an export duty can possibly be charged.
