The Norwich bulletin recounted the chronic political instability and recurring civil wars in Santo Domingo that left the republic heavily indebted to foreign creditors. When foreign warships threatened bombardment to enforce debt claims, Dominican authorities entered into an agreement with the United States in 1905 to take over the collection of customs. Under this treaty, American authorities restructured the nation's tariffs to tax luxuries and guaranteed payments to satisfy European claims.

El Norwich bulletin rememoró la crónica inestabilidad política y las constantes guerras civiles en Santo Domingo que abocaron a la república a un pesado endeudamiento con acreedores extranjeros. Al amenazar cañoneros extranjeros con bombardear los puertos para hacer efectivas sus reclamaciones, las autoridades dominicanas celebraron un acuerdo con los Estados Unidos en 1905 para confiarles la recaudación aduanera. Bajo este convenio, los funcionarios estadounidenses modificaron los aranceles nacionales gravando los artículos de lujo y aseguraron pagos para saldar los reclamos europeos.

bulletin. "Revolution had followed revolution almost with clocklike regularity. There were assassinations, there were betrayals, there were conspiracies, there were wars within and wars without — war with Haiti over boundary questions and civil war over the control of the government. Debts were piled up without thought of the day of payment or even provisions for meeting the interest charges. "So long and so steady was the orgy of revolution, speculation, debt making and interest-dodging that the nation's credit grew worse than that of its individual citizens. Finally, the day of reckoning came. Foreign warships approached the ports whose harbors had given refuge to the great discoverer Columbus and whose capital city contains what undoubtedly are his ashes, and demanded that the claims of their subjects be satisfied — claims for money advanced, claims for property wantonly destroyed — and they demanded it at the point of big naval guns. "In a predicament from which they were powerless to extricate themselves, the Dominicans were ready enough to negotiate a treaty turning over the control of the country's customs to the United States, if, in return therefor, the United States would protect them from angry European creditors and rejuvenate their treasury. "And so it was that in [illegible] the United States undertook to serve as treasurer of Santo Domingo and to vouch for her debts. The tariff was quickly revised, the American authorities urging that duties be laid on luxuries rather than upon necessities.
