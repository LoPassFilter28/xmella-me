The Indianapolis Journal reported from Haiti regarding proposed constitutional changes concerning foreign property rights. Haitian Foreign Minister Brutus St. Victor denied claims that the government intended to repeal Article 6 of the constitution, which prohibited alien ownership of real estate, stating the public was not prepared for such reform. St. Victor also declined to disclose specific government plans to address the rising premium on paper currency.

The Indianapolis Journal informó desde Haití sobre las propuestas de reforma constitucional relativas a los derechos de propiedad de los extranjeros. El ministro de Asuntos Exteriores haitiano, Brutus St. Victor, desmintió las versiones de que el gobierno pretendiera derogar el artículo 6 de la constitución, que prohibía a los extranjeros la propiedad de bienes inmuebles, afirmando que la opinión pública no estaba preparada para tal cambio. St. Victor declinó asimismo revelar planes gubernamentales específicos para hacer frente al creciente sobreprecio del papel moneda.

A Haitian deputy, who professes to be well informed on all political matters, said yesterday that the government was certain to favor the elimination of Article 6 from the Constitution at the session of the Chambers, which will probably begin this week. The correspondent of the Associated Press derived from Brutus St. Victor, the secretary of state for foreign relations, to-day, an entirely different view of the parliamentary programme. Mr. St. Victor denied categorically that the government contemplated any project for the removal of the prohibition of alien ownership of real property. Such a step, he said, if ever taken, must be yet a very great way off. "The people," he continued, "are not ready to accept a change. There must be a gradual preparation of public sentiment before the government could even suggest it. There is, no doubt, such an opinion favorable to it, but it has as yet manifested only chiefly in the cities, and it must be remembered that the mass of the Haitian population is rural, and that it is strongly wedded to national traditions."
