The Richmond Dispatch published an account from a former American diplomat concerning an unfinished protocol submitted by Dominican President Ulises Heureaux as the basis for a new treaty. After verbally briefing Secretary Sherman in Washington, the diplomat decided to disclose the details following the acceptance of his resignation. He explained that Heureaux had requested his visit to Santo Domingo in March to receive the proposed terms and related memoranda.

THE PROTOCOL. "In answer to your direct question, and with a knowledge of the deep interest you manifest in everything affecting the foreign relations of the United States, I will give you an outline of the unfinished protocol submitted to me by General Heureaux, President of the Dominican republic, for presentation to my government as the basis of a new treaty between the two countries. On my arrival in Washington I made a verbal report to Secretary Sherman, because I had not arranged the memoranda submitted by President Heureaux, and hastily taken in a note-book on the eve of my departure, and the Secretary, who appeared interested in the matter, suggested that I go home, and after I had recruited my strength, make him a formal report as to the proposed convention and the general conditions in Haiti and San Domingo. Now, inasmuch as the acceptance of my resignation, tendered the [illegible] of March, has released me from the obligations of my position, I deem it at least permissible to give you for the press the information you desire, although I can only surmise the source from which you acquired a knowledge of the matter in question.
