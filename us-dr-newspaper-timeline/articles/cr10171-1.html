THE PRESIDENT IN FAVOR OF FREE SPEECH—

AMICAL RELATIONS WITH HAYTI—CLAIMS FOR

INDEMNITY AGAINST THE UNITED STATES.

SANTO DOMINGO, March 22.—The yacht Tarolinta of the New-York Yacht Club arrived here on the 19th inst. from Porto Rico, having on board Mr. Kent, her owner, and a party of friends who have been making a tour of the islands. Yesterday they called on President Gonzales, who returned the visit in the afternoon, accompanied by two of the ministers and other dignitaries. The President in a speech on the yacht said he was determined to try the experiment of free speech, a free press, and as much liberty as existed in the United States, even though he should perish in the attempt. No one had been exiled but Baez and a few of his family, and they were at liberty to return here now if they wished. The Tarolinta left here this morning for Kingston, Jamaica, from which place she will proceed to Havana, and thence home.

President Gonzales, accompanied by three of his ministers, his staff and some friends, returned a few days since from the province of Azua, where he had a conference with President Domingue of Hayti. The meeting was near the boundary line, and, by preconcert, took place on February 27, the anniversary of the separation of the two Republics in 1844, which is another proof of the entente cordiale now existing between the two Governments and people of the island. The treaty of peace, amity, extradition, reciprocity of trade guarantees to each other that neither any part nor the whole of the territory of either shall be alienated, has been ratified and exchanged.

It is stated that this Government is now being hard pressed by about 2,000 of its citizens, victims of Baez’s action, while under the immediate protection of the United States ships of war, to prepare and present a claim on the United States Government for losses and injuries to the Government and people arising therefrom. The foundation of this claim has been taken from the speech of Senator Sumner, of March 27, 1871, on violations of international law and usurpations of war powers. The Government intends, it is said, to present a claim for $5,000,000, but has not yet decided how or by whom they will present it, having no diplomatic representative in Washington. The argument which it is said the Dominican Government will submit in support of this claim recites the facts in connection with Baez’s accession to power, his attempt to dispose of the country, and the lease of Samana; declares that he was maintained in power by the United States troops, and was compelled to fly when they ceased to support him, and that, while occupying the Presidency, protected by United States ships, he plundered the Treasury of a large portion of its revenue, and ill-treated the citizens; alludes to many other facts in regard to the relations between the United States and Santo Domingo which have occupied attention during several years, and asserts that the acts of the United States Government were in violation of international law, and that it is responsible for the losses and injuries occasioned by the protection of Baez.
