After a border clash near Comendador, the Dominican government is hurrying troops and reinforcements on the American steamer Cherokee, while the Haitian minister has advised his government to withdraw its defeated troops. At San Domingo on December 27, President Ramón Cáceres said his latest advices looked more favorable for a friendly settlement of the boundary dispute with Haiti: he did not wish a conflict, but demanded that his country's rights be respected, and work continued on the government railway intended ultimately to connect the two capitals.

Tras un choque fronterizo cerca de Comendador, el gobierno dominicano está enviando tropas y refuerzos con premura en el vapor estadounidense Cherokee, mientras el ministro haitiano ha aconsejado a su gobierno retirar sus tropas derrotadas. En Santo Domingo, el 27 de diciembre, el presidente Ramón Cáceres dijo que sus últimas noticias parecían más favorables para un arreglo amistoso de la disputa limítrofe con Haití: no deseaba un conflicto, pero exigía que se respetaran los derechos de su país, y continuaban los trabajos del ferrocarril gubernamental destinado a conectar con el tiempo ambas capitales.

Dominican troops near Comendador. The Dominican government is hurrying troops and reinforcements on the American steamer Cherokee, and the Haytiena minister has advised his government to withdraw its defeated troops. The countries are having a dispute over the boundary line. MAY BE SETTLED. Associated Press. San Domingo. Dec. 27.—President Ramon Caceres, in an interview today, said that his latest advices looked more favorable for a friendly settlement of the boundary dispute with Hayti. The president does not wish a conflict with the neighboring republic, but demands that his country’s rights be respected. The rush to exchange unsatisfactory presents for ones that the recipient considers more essential to his or her happiness.
