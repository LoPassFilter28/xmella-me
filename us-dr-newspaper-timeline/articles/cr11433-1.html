A Herald letter from aboard the frigate Tennessee, off Port-au-Prince on March 1871, describes the Santo Domingo Commission's return journey, with the steamer Nantasket arriving from Puerto Plata bringing Professor White and the parties who had rendezvoused there after traversing the country. The commissioners are shown quarreling over the journey home: Ben Wade wants to make the most direct course home now that their business is done and their report almost completed, while Dr. Howe and Professor White outvote him, to the general dissatisfaction of everyone on board, and the letter doubts the three will even settle on a joint report.

Una carta del Herald desde a bordo de la fragata Tennessee, frente a Puerto Príncipe en marzo de 1871, describe el viaje de regreso de la Comisión de Santo Domingo, con la llegada del vapor Nantasket desde Puerto Plata trayendo al profesor White y a los grupos que se habían reunido allí tras recorrer el país. Los comisionados aparecen discutiendo sobre el regreso: Ben Wade quiere tomar el rumbo más directo a casa ahora que su trabajo está hecho y su informe casi terminado, mientras el doctor Howe y el profesor White lo superan en votos, para disgusto general de todos a bordo, y la carta duda que los tres lleguen siquiera a acordar un informe conjunto.

Correspondence With Cabral. UNITED STATES FRIGATE Tennessee, Off Port au Prince, Hayti, March [illegible], 1871. [illegible] delay—Incomprehensible delay! Kept at anchor in this delectable harbor, where filth and stench, both in the streets and in the miserable government, alike breed rottenness and disease. At three o'clock yesterday the steamer Nautasket steamed into this harbor, having left Puerto Plata the morning before. Professor White determined not to await the arrival of the Tybee, which might not reach there for several days, so, collecting together all the scattered parties who had been traversing the country and who rendezvoused at Puerto Plata, they sped around to join the Tennessee. Her arrival caused the greatest delight among our party, who had become heartily tired of this unsocial and unsightly town; and as we have only been waiting for her to come we looked forward to our quickly leaving for home with certainty. The Haytiens, who have looked with great suspicion upon the Tennessee, deeming her object to be hostile to their petty government, are now no doubt doubly alarmed at the presence in their harbor of another American man-of-war. Dr. Howe, Secretary Burton, Fred Douglass and son, with several others, were taking dinner at Minister Bassett's when she arrived, and from his residence, which is located on the mountain side, about a mile and a half distant from the city, she could be distinctly seen coming into the harbor.
