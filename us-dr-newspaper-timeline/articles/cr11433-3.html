In a letter from his headquarters dated March 5, 1871, General Jose Maria Cabral tells the United States Commissioners, Wade, White and Howe, that he has no power to enter into any negotiations on the annexation of his country, that he has protested against it and that his people have been protesting with arms in their hands for more than a year. He invites the Commissioners to visit the people and his headquarters, but White and Howe reply that a visit is not convenient for the present, while Mr. Wade declines to sign the reply; the correspondent adds that Cabral in fact holds only a small strip of mountainous land along the Neyba River.

En una carta desde su cuartel general fechada el 5 de marzo de 1871, el general José María Cabral dice a los comisionados de los Estados Unidos, Wade, White y Howe, que no tiene poder para entrar en ninguna negociación sobre la anexión de su país, que ha protestado contra ella y que su pueblo lleva más de un año protestando con las armas en la mano. Invita a los comisionados a visitar al pueblo y su cuartel general, pero White y Howe responden que la visita no es conveniente por el momento, mientras el señor Wade se niega a firmar la respuesta; el corresponsal añade que Cabral en realidad solo ocupa una pequeña franja de tierra montañosa a lo largo del río Neyba.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS, [illegible], March [illegible], 1871. To Señors Benjamin B. Wade, Andrew D. White and Samuel G. Howe, members of the Commission of the United States: GENTLEMEN: I have read the contents of your communication, dated on the 21st of the past month, with an additional note dated on the 28th of the same month in Azua, and have duly countered them. But not having any power to enter into any class of negotiations, or to treat in any way as to the annexation of my country, and having protested in the month of March past in behalf of the people and the army which I have the honor to govern, it appears to me altogether useless to be interviewed as to the subject which you propose to me. If you have instructions from your government to study the opinions and desires of the Dominican people upon these particulars, according to the expressions in your communications, the best way in which you can fulfil your instructions is to pass through and visit the people, and in this way you will be able to assure yourselves better of that which I have already said in my communication of the 28th of February. And it will appear that we have not alone protested in uniting against the annexation, but that it will be shown to be a fact that for more than a year we have been protesting against annexation with arms in our hands.
