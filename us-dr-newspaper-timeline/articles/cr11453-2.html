On the 13th of February, 1869, Gen. N. P. Banks, Chairman of Committee of Foreign Affairs, addressed a note to the Hon. Wm. H. Seward, Secretary of State, of which the following is a copy:

HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES, COMMITTEE OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS, WASHINGTON, D. C., Feb. 13, 1869.

MY DEAR SIR: It would very much aid the considerations of the San Domingo question, now before the Committee of Foreign Affairs, if a complete official statement could be made of the entire amount of the debt and condition of the Republic. The subject has come so suddenly before us that we have not had time to establish the facts, except from such information as we are receiving from the agents of the Republic. Mr. Fabens informs me that he is about to leave for San Domingo with a report, and I think it would in all respects be advantageous to the Government and to the Committee if the Department would suggest sending him to San Domingo for that purpose.

It ought to be definitely ascertained, also, if any transfers of territory or perpetual or other franchises to private individuals or corporations have been granted, or are contemplated by the Government of San Domingo, as such transfers, if made or anticipated, would endanger the project of annexation, even after it had been approved by Congress. It is important to the settlement of the question that we have, from such information as we have, all the facts. I am, very truly, yours, N. P. BANKS.

To the HON. WILLIAM H. SEWARD, Secretary of State.

Pursuant to the request of the Chairman of Foreign Affairs, Mr. Seward dispatched Mr. Fabens to San Domingo with the view of obtaining all such information as was suggested in the letter of Gen. Banks. Especially was he directed to look into the condition of the debt of the Republic. Fabens remained two days in San Domingo City, and immediately returned to Washington, bringing the statement that the debt of San Domingo was only $600,000 (or, per statement of the Secretary of State of San Domingo, $589,327 01). This was all the information to communicate to the Secretary of State on the subject of the debt of San Domingo. Fabens was himself at that time supposed to be interested in the Hartmont loan.

Fabens returned to Washington about the 1st of April; and we find Baez, his Ministers, and the Consulting Committee of the Senate passing a law on the 19th day of May following. The contract for the loan was made. It was first printed in THE TRIBUNE'S private correspondence of September, 1869, nearly five months after it had been made, and has only quite recently become known, as that journal has only a privileged circulation. The following is a statement of this contract, in which the text is quoted as far as it relates to the Samana Bay:

The Government of the Dominican Republic, desiring to facilitate the public debt, a view of developing its public works, as well as the national commerce and industry, has entered into this contract with Edward Herzberg Hartmont, of London, Consul-General of the Republic, and Messrs. J. Lorimer Graham and Joseph W. Curiel, Minister of the Treasury, fully authorized to that effect, and in accordance with the proposal of the house of Hartmont & Co. of London, and all other houses which may be associated in said loan.

ARTICLE 1—Names the amount of the loan £420,000.

ART. 2—After providing for the payment of the sinking fund (which is finally to pay up and cancel this loan), the Dominican Government binds itself to pay to the contractors of this loan the sum of £58,908, as a total annual amount, payable by half-yearly installments, respectively on the 15th of December and June of each year, which loan is to run for at least 25 years. The first of these payments shall be made on the 15th day of December following the emission of the loan.

ART. 3. States that the bonds shall be represented by coupons, and the conditions on which subscriptions are to be opened in London or elsewhere.

ART. 4. States that the contractors shall fix the nominal amount of the bonds, the interest thereon, and the amount of the sinking fund, in such a way that they shall not exceed the annual payment stipulated.

ART. 5. States how or when the Dominican Government & Co. shall decide. Stipulates that the bonds are to be signed by the Minister of the Treasury and countersigned by the Consul-General of the Dominican Republic, and payable at London, Paris, and whatever points Hartmont & Co. may decide.

ART. 6. The cost of printing the bonds and the general expenses of the loan are to be paid by Hartmont & Co.

ART. 7. Stipulates that Hartmont & Co. are to pay a commission of 5 per cent on the nominal amount of the loan within the last six months of the current year.

ART. 8. To keep the first six months of the interest for the next payment.

ART. 9. The regular payment of the sums necessary to the settlement of the interest and sinking fund is guaranteed by the whole of the revenues of the Republic; and as a special and first guarantee for the payment of there is accorded to the holders of the bonds of this loan the first mortgage on the customs of the Republic. At every city, the State of San Domingo having no other debt.

ART. 10. Safeguards the customs of the Republic at San Domingo City and Porto Plata, which amount annually to $500,000.

ART. 11 Authorizes Hartmont & Co. to appoint agents at the custom-houses to see that the total amount of the required sum is received.

ART. 12. Bonds which are to be paid off in consequence of drawings are to be paid at par, and the list of the numbers drawn is to be published, and the interest on the bonds so drawn is to cease from the day of the drawing. The contractors are to be authorized to receive at each Custom-House to the extent of the sum said list represents.

ART. 13. The tariff rates on exportation and importation shall not be reduced during the period of the contractors of the loan.

ART. 14. As a supplementary guaranty the Dominican Government authorizes in favor of the takers of this loan a first mortgage upon the coal mines and woods belonging to the State in the Peninsula of Samana, in the province of the same name, as also upon the duties which shall come into the Treasury of the Republic for the guano from the Isle of Alta Vela. The working of these mines and the export of the woods and guano shall have been already secured to Hartmont & Graham, under special contracts, and of the surplus of which they are authorized to take the amount which may be necessary to secure the account of these rights of controlling guano and woods, and as a supplementary and special security to the holders of these bonds, which sums are to be applied to the payment of the interest, and to and the sinking fund of the loan.

ART. 15. The Dominican Government engages to pay the banking-house charged with the payment of the bonds a commission of 1 per cent upon the total amount fixed for the payment of the interest and sinking fund.

ART. 16. The contractors are authorized to retain the funds necessary for the payment of the interest and sinking fund of the first year.

ART. 17. The bonds are not to be taxed.

ART. 18. The par of the English pound is 25 francs. The contract may be assigned. RICARDO CURIEL.

SANTO DOMINGO, May 1, 1869. EDWARD H. HARTMONT.

The signatures of President Baez and the Cabinet are also attached in approval of the above.

ANOTHER CONTRACT ENTERED INTO BETWEEN THE SAME PARTIES.

ARTICLE 1 Concedes £100,000 to Hartmont & Co. to pay them for their services.

ART. 2. Secures Hartmont & Co. this sum, even should they negotiate only £100,000, but in this case only £5,000 commission is to be paid. RICARDO CURIEL,

SANTO DOMINGO, May 1, 1869. EDWARD H. HARTMONT.

Approved as above.

The contracts above given were made and signed about a month after Fabens had been down to San Domingo, and were concluded in a quiet, secret manner, so that the people of San Domingo knew nothing of what was transpiring. Of the £50,000 that were to have been paid down on the 1st of July, 1869, only £38,000 was in fact received by Baez, because of certain successes of the revolutionary party, which prevented Hartmont from further payments. This appears by the report of the Treasury Minister of the Republic. The remaining £12,000 have been paid since, but no public statement has appeared showing who received the money. It is known, however, that a considerable portion went into Fabens's pocket. The contract for the loan is still in full force.

In this connection we quote the following paragraph from the London Times of Jan. 13:

"Respecting the San Domingo 6 per cent loan for £420,000, which was recently introduced by Messrs. Peter Lawson & Son, the following new arrangements have been made: 1. A royalty of £1 per ton on all guano and 5 per cent on all other minerals from the Samana Bay and Island, and as the quantity is expected to reach 60,000 tons per annum, it will provide £60,000 per annum for interest and sinking fund; viz., £50,000, may thus be secured by the United States. 2. Messrs. Spofford, Tileston & Co., of New-York, owners of a line of steamers between New-York and the ports of San Domingo, have been appointed agents to collect the royalty for the benefit of the bondholders, and the collection thus passing into the hands of a powerful American firm, the contract offers a much more satisfactory security than before."

Now, let it be borne in mind that Peter Lawson & Son are the assignees of this same loan, the features of which have been heretofore detailed. By the terms of the contract signed 1st of May, 1869, it will be seen that all the coal mines and woods of Samana are pledged as a security for the loan, as well as the guano-beds of Alta Vela; so that, in fact, should the Samana lease be ratified by the Senate, the United States Government would have to assume this liability of £420,000, or be deprived, for 25 years at least, of the use of the mortgaged property.
