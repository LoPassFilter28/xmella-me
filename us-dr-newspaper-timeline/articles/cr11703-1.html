The Evening Star published an editorial urging the United States to adopt stronger measures against Haitian dictator François Duvalier, suggesting economic boycotts and clandestine support for anti-Duvalier insurgents. The commentary argued that Washington's official reluctance to intervene disregarded past precedents like Guatemala and the Bay of Pigs, warning that inaction could allow Communist influence to expand next to Cuba.

El Evening Star publicó un editorial instando a Estados Unidos a adoptar medidas más enérgicas contra el dictador haitiano François Duvalier, sugiriendo boicots económicos y respaldo clandestino a los insurgentes antiduvalieristas. El artículo sostenía que la renuencia oficial de Washington a intervenir ignoraba precedentes pasados como Guatemala y Bahía de Cochinos, advirtiendo que la inacción podría permitir la expansión de la influencia comunista junto a Cuba.

Obviously we take the position nowadays that armed intervention cannot be undertaken in this hemisphere, barring a spectacular threat to our security like the presence somewhere of Soviet rockets. There are ways, however, of accomplishing these things. For one thing, as we pointed out the other day, there is economic pressure. Our purchases from Haiti are not extensive. But, for instance, could we not get along without Haitian coffee while Duvalier remains in power? Duvalier could be toppled, as he should be, by the Haitians themselves— relatively few of them. The men to topple him are there and, sooner or later, one of them will organize the job. They need guns, grenades and explosives. If the United States remains a spectator, growling only when its na tionals seem to be menaced, the next leader of Haiti will owe us even less than the present one. If the State Department now vir tuously argues that we cannot meddle in the affairs of neighboring countries, it has a short memory that any survivor of the Bay of Pigs or of the CIA-sponsored revolt against Jacobo Arbenz of Guatemala could refresh. It also should have heeded these promptings of reticence before our national prestige was conspicuously committed to the abortive effort last month.
