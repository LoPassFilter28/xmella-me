The New Britain Herald reported on extensive infrastructure modernization in Haiti under President Borno, including more than 1,000 miles of paved roads, new schools, hospitals, and lighthouses. A concrete highway connecting Port-au-Prince with Santo Domingo had reached the border on the Haitian side, promising to reduce travel time between the capitals from days to hours once the Dominican section was completed. The report also highlighted telephone network expansion, tariff revisions to promote trade with the United States, and the continuing presence of 800 American marines.

El New Britain Herald informó sobre la vasta modernización de infraestructura en Haití bajo el presidente Borno, con más de 1 000 millas de carreteras pavimentadas, nuevas escuelas, hospitales y faros. Una carretera de hormigón que comunicaba Puerto Príncipe con Santo Domingo había alcanzado la frontera del lado haitiano, prometiendo reducir el tiempo de viaje entre ambas capitales de días a horas una vez concluido el tramo dominicano. El despacho destacó asimismo la ampliación de la red telefónica, modificaciones arancelarias para estimular el comercio con Estados Unidos y la continua presencia de 800 infantes de marina estadounidenses.

In the last ten years, more than 1,000 miles of paved roads have been laid, replacing highways that had deteriorated until they were little more than trails. A concrete highway connecting Port-au-Prince, the Haitian Capital, and Santo Domingo, capital city of the Dominican Republic, has been completed by the Haitians up to the border. When the Dominican portion is completed, the trip between the two capitals may be done in hours, where formerly it took days. This progress has been particularly notable during the administration of the present chief executive, President Borno, said a report in the booklet made by the Haitian minister of public works. His administration also has seen the construction of fourteen schools, thirteen hospitals and eleven lighthouses; installation of an automatic telephone system, and material progress in irrigation and canals. Since 1921, there has been an increase of 350 per cent in the number of telephones in use and the telegraph system has been augmented and improved. In order to foster trade between the United States and Haiti, the present Haitian Import Customs Tariff is being revised. Some eight hundred American Marines still remain in Haiti, completing their work of reorganizing the native constabulary.
