The Saint Paul Globe published a letter comparing the proposed annexation of Hawaii to President Grant's unsuccessful effort to annex Santo Domingo in 1869 and 1870. The writer recalled that despite strategic arguments, joint resolution efforts, and a favorable commission report, the annexation treaty failed in the Senate. The author argued that past fears of foreign powers absorbing Santo Domingo proved unfounded and hoped Hawaii would avoid annexation.

HISTORY REPEATS. Similarity in Hawaiian and Santo Dominican Cases. To the Editor of the Globe. About thirty years ago an attempt was made by the Grant administration to annex Santo Domingo, the second largest island in the West Indies, which ingloriously failed, as I hope the present administration will fail in its attempt to annex the Hawaiian islands. Then, as now, a treaty had been signed for annexation between President Grant and the president of the Santo Domingo republic on Sept. 9, 1869, which treaty was rejected by the senate. In December, 1870, inspired by the president's annual message, another attempt was made to annex said island by passing a joint resolution, which then, as it is now claimed, could be accomplished by a majority vote. This was met and it was held that no territory could be annexed without a treaty which must be ratified by a two-thirds vote of the senate. Finally, a commission of visiting statesmen was appointed, who, in due time, reported favorably, but the whole thing was abandoned in the long run, and no "foreign power" has thus far expressed any desire to absorb that republic. Then, as now, it was strongly urged how indispensable that island was to us for military and naval reasons. Then, as now, certain great patriots were much distressed lest some foreign power would absorb that island and its dusky inhabitants.
