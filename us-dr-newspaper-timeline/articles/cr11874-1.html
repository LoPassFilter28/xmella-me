The Evening Star reported that the Organization of American States referred the dispute between Haiti and the Dominican Republic over Col. Astrel Roland's subversive broadcasts to a peace commission, which advised direct bilateral negotiations. Haitian diplomats stated the broadcasts caused widespread unrest that necessitated declaring a state of siege to maintain public order. While Dominican authorities defended the broadcasts under free speech on a private station, Haitian officials contended the station was controlled by President Trujillo's brother.

El Evening Star informó que la Organización de los Estados Americanos remitió a una comisión de paz la disputa suscitada entre Haití y la República Dominicana por las transmisiones sediciosas del coronel Astrel Roland, encomendando dicho organismo la apertura de negociaciones directas de cancillería. El cuerpo diplomático haitiano adujo que las emisiones agitaron graves conmociones que forzaron a la promulgación del estado de sitio para restablecer la paz pública. Si bien las autoridades dominicanas escudaron las transmisiones amparándose en la libre expresión ejercida en una emisora particular, los voceros haitianos arguyeron que la referida estación estaba supeditada al hermano del gobernante Trujillo.

The council politely referred the matter to "the Inter American Commission on Methods for the Peaceful Solution of Conflicts.” Whereupon the gentlemen of this imposing committee suggested that the two governments themselves get together and iron out their differences. It is explained that the matter was turned over to this small committee because the all-important council feels it should keep itself free to deal with what one official called "larger and more far reaching questions.”. Meantime Col. Roland's radio campaign has continued. In fact, Haitian diplomats insist that the broadcasts have created such unrest and commotion among the illiterate and ignorant masses that the government has been compelled to impose a state of siege in order to prevent serious disorders. At any rate, the OAS has been called upon once again to take action. Issue Over Ownership The Dominican government insists that the radio station over which Roland speaks is a privately owned station and that, after all, the Dominican government must respect freedom of speech. According to the Haitians, the station is owned by President Trujillo's brother. In any case, any one acquainted with the complete government domination of every institution and activity within the Dominican nation knows that, if Col. Roland's campaign did not enjoy the official sanction of President Trujillo, the broadcasts would not and could not continue.
