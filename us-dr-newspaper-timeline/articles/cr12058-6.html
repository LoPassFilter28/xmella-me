In Senate debate over the Samana Bay lease, Senator Harlan confirmed that under the lease the United States was in possession of Samana Bay and the American flag was floating there, and that the lease had been made under an appropriation by Congress with a provision for renewal. He argued that the government in possession was simply declaring it would defend that possession if anyone tried to wrest it away, and he attacked the long speeches made against the Grant administration over the affair.

En el debate del Senado sobre el arrendamiento de la bahía de Samaná, el senador Harlan confirmó que, en virtud del arrendamiento, los Estados Unidos estaban en posesión de la bahía de Samaná y la bandera americana ondeaba allí, y que el arrendamiento se había hecho al amparo de una asignación del Congreso con una disposición para su renovación. Sostuvo que el gobierno en posesión simplemente declaraba que defendería esa posesión si alguien intentaba arrebatársela, y atacó los largos discursos pronunciados contra la administración de Grant por este asunto.

There are enough in our diplomatic history to silence the Senator if he should call the truth of my statement in question. Mr. Morton. I ask the Senator if he does not know that under that lease we are now in possession of Samana bay? Mr. Harlan. Certainly and the American flag is floating there. Mr. Morton. And has been ever since the treaty was made. Mr. Harlan. And the President has been defending our possessions in that island, as it is his right and duty to do. Mr. Sumner. Have they Mr. Sherman. That lease was made in pursuance of an appropriation by congress. Mr. Conkling. Certainly with a provision for renewal. Mr. Harlan. I am much obliged to the honorable Senators. Here was a lease made in pursuance of of a law covering an appropriation to pay the rent, and the American Government, in possession under that lease, defends that possession, or, rather declares that it will defend it if its rights shall be invaded. That is all—that it will defend this if any one, either within the Dominican Republic or from abroad, should be hardy enough to attempt to wrest it from our hands. I have consumed more time, Mr. President, than I intended. I shall occupy but a very few moments more, and that by recalling attention to the purpose for which these resolutions have been introduced, and the object these Senators hope to achieve by the delivery of their elaborate and carefully prepared speeches, as stated by themselves.
