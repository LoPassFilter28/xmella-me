The Indianapolis Journal reported that insurgent forces led by Generals Morales and Villanueva were advancing on Puerto Plata and Santiago de los Caballeros against Dominican President Heureaux. The report profiled revolutionary leader General Juan Isidro Jimenes, an established merchant with business houses in Europe and the Caribbean who had organized the movement from New York. It noted that Jimenes enjoyed wide popularity and had previously resided in Paris to escape Heureaux's hostility toward political rivals.

El Indianapolis Journal informó que fuerzas insurgentes encabezadas por los generales Morales y Villanueva avanzaban sobre Puerto Plata y Santiago de los Caballeros contra el presidente dominicano Heureaux. El informe trazó un perfil del líder revolucionario general Juan Isidro Jimenes, un consolidado comerciante con casas comerciales en Europa y el Caribe que había organizado el movimiento desde Nueva York. Señalaba que Jimenes gozaba de amplia popularidad y que previamente había residido en París para escapar de la hostilidad de Heureaux hacia sus rivales políticos.

Morales and Villanueva, they are marching on Puerto Plata, while another detachment is moving against Santiago de los Caballeros, on the Yaque river east of this place. Gen. Garcia, another Dominican leader, is said to be taking part in the revolution, and it is expected there will be a rising at Santo Domingo, the capital of the Dominican republic. General Jiminez is a merchant who has business houses here at Monte Cristo, at Puerto Plata and at Hamburg. In his youth he served in the army and for many years he has been very popular in Santo Domingo. President Heurreaux, according to a common saying among the Dominicans, has killed off all the prominent men on whom he could lay hands and who might be rivals. The President's hatred for General Jiminez is especially marked, but the latter has kept out of his reach, residing chiefly in Paris. About two months ago General Jiminez left Cape Haitien, ostensibly for Europe, via Port-au-Prince, but it is said he did not go any further than New York, and it is believed that while in that city he perfected the arrangements for the revolution in Santo Domingo over which, if successful, the great majority of the Dominicans for the time being at least would rejoice. General Jiminez is fifty-five years of age and of a mild temperament. He has refused several times to be a candidate for the presidency of Santo Domingo.
