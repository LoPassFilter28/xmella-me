The Waterbury Democrat reported that attorney Donald Richberg was representing President Trujillo in the diplomatic conflict over the reported massacre of roughly 8,000 Haitians. The report alleged that Trujillo's concession to conciliation was merely an evasive maneuver engineered by counsel regarding whether he had previously refused to negotiate. These diplomatic delays, including an envoy's refusal to travel to Washington, reportedly caused frustration within the U.S. State Department and the Pan-American Union.

El Waterbury Democrat informó que el abogado Donald Richberg representaba al presidente Trujillo en el conflicto diplomático suscitado por la masacre de unos 8.000 haitianos. La crónica señalaba que la avenencia de Trujillo al proceso de conciliación fue una simple maniobra dilatoria urdida por sus asesores jurídicos en torno a su previa negativa a negociar. Estas demoras diplomáticas, incluida la negativa de un enviado a trasladarse a Washington, provocaron según los informes gran frustración en el seno del Departamento de Estado de EE. UU. y de la Unión Panamericana.

Haitian Massacre At present Richberg Is representing the Dominican president in his dispute with Haiti, resulting from the massacre of about 8.000 Haitians in the most horrible manner. At a given signal, reputedly given by Trujillo himself, these Haitians were driven into the sea, chopped up with machetes, some drenched with gasoline and ignited, still living. For some time Trujillo refused to conciliate the dispute. After much pressure by the United States, he finally issued a spectacular announcement that he would do so. But when the official acceptance arrived it showed that he merely accepted conciliation of whether or not he previously had refused to conciliate. Which meant that nothing minus nothing left nothing. Some people suspected Don Richberg's astute legal mind of trumping up this run-around. At any rate Trujillo appointed Richberg and the Dominican minister to Cuba as his representatives to conciliate the question of whether he had previously refused to conciliate. But the envoy in Cuba refuses to come to Washington because of a cold, although seen nightly in Havana's night clubs. All this is getting on the nerves of the State Department and the entire Pan-American Union. Latest inner circle suggestion is to make Don Richberg settle the whole blithering mess as a test of his judicial qualities for the Supreme Court.
