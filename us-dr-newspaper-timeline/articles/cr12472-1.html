Eyewitness reports from Matanzas, on the north coast, said a tidal wave from Sunday's earthquake swept as much as a mile inland, catching even a party fleeing by automobile, and left only eight of the town's 300 houses standing. Residents had taken refuge in trees and high places, and reports from Matanzas and Nagua said frightened inhabitants in the forests were suffering from hunger, with authorities considering dropping food from airplanes, as wrecked communications made the damage across more than 11 towns hard to assess.

Testimonios desde Matanzas, en la costa norte, decían que un maremoto provocado por el terremoto del domingo penetró hasta una milla tierra adentro, alcanzó incluso a un grupo que huía en automóvil y dejó en pie solo ocho de las 300 casas del pueblo. Los vecinos se habían refugiado en árboles y lugares altos, y los informes de Matanzas y Nagua decían que habitantes asustados en los bosques padecían hambre, mientras las autoridades consideraban lanzar alimentos desde aviones y las comunicaciones destruidas dificultaban evaluar los daños en más de 11 pueblos.

Eye witness reports received today from Matanzas on the North Coast, said a tidal wave swept inland as much as a mile so swiftly that it caught a party fleeing in an automobile. The eyewitness said the residents had been forewarned and had taken refuge in trees and high places. Only eight of the 300 houses in Matanzas were reported left standing. Wrecked telegraph and communication facilities made it difficult to get a clear picture of the damage wrought by the quake which apparently centered in the deepest point in the Atlantic ocean, approximately 50 miles off the east coast of the Dominican Republic. Reports from the Matanzas and Nagua regions said that frightened inhabitants had taken refuge in the forests and were suffering from hunger. Authorities were considering dropping food from airplanes. Meanwhile apprehension gripped the Republic as new tremors shook the ground here yesterday and blacked out radio communications for several hours. MERCHANTS WILL MEET THIS NIGHT A meeting of all Juneau merchants will be held in the Legion Dugout at 8 o'clock tonight to discuss issues of vital importance and to complete plans for a merchants organization. David Scoll, attorney for the Territory in the current hearings on transportation, will speak to the group.
