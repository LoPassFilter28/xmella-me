Our St. Domingo correspondent makes a statement of numerous outrages committed on the property and rights of citizens of the United States domiciled in the Dominican republic by the Spanish military forces during their invasion of that country. Many Americans are settled in St. Domingo, engaged in various important and extensive agricultural, manufacturing and mining enterprises; and our correspondent states that American individuals and companies, which are all sleeping unworked because the United States would not tell Spain to stand off from this magnificent field of enterprise. Looking over a mountain of pure rock salt, one of those wonders of nature which is worth a trip across the ocean to see, which is owned by a New York company, I believe; and I have seen slabs of marble extracted out like marble, in immense blocks, without flaw or stain, like marble, and they would be welcomed around that beautiful, healthy country, there are rich cotton and coffee lands vacant, or held by American owners, enough to supply fifty thousand of your contraband negroes with homes, and they would be welcomed here by black and white.
