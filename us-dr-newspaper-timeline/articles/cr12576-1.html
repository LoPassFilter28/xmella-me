But it is not a matter of much importance after all, and the neglectful envoy had seen too much of Santo Domingo to treat its diplomacy seriously.

Mr. Baez proposes to President Grant to establish a protectorate over Santo Domingo, which shall guard the Republic against the direct attacks of Hayti, as well as the more insidious assaults upon its life set on foot among rebellious Dominicans by Haytien influences. It was this consideration which dictated the annexation movement, and when that failed the same purposes informed the negotiations which resulted in the cession of Samana Bay to the company called by its name. That enterprise has apparently not furnished the protection which the Baez Government needs, and the President, in this despairing appeal, acknowledges himself at the end of his resources. He makes an appeal to our national jealousy by including correspondence in regard to a project of a treaty drawn up between the representatives of Hayti and Santo Domingo in Paris, one clause of which binds both Republics never to alienate or mortgage any portion of their territory to any foreign power. This treaty was promptly disavowed and rejected by Baez, who sends it to us as a proof of the machinations of European Powers to prevent our acquiring a foothold in the Caribbean Sea.
