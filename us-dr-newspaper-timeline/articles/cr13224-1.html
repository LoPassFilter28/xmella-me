The Portland Daily Press reported that the United States government intervened to protect the property of American citizen Nat McKay, whose toll bridge in Santo Domingo was seized by the Dominican government following a collision with a scow. American Consul Archibald Grimké was instructed to file a strong protest after Dominican courts dismissed McKay's damage claims and ordered the structure sold to satisfy court costs.

The Portland Daily Press informó que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos intervino para proteger la propiedad del ciudadano estadounidense Nat McKay, cuyo puente de peaje en Santo Domingo fue incautado por el gobierno dominicano tras una colisión con una barcaza. Se instruyó al cónsul estadounidense Archibald Grimké para que presentara una enérgica protesta luego de que los tribunales dominicanos desestimaran las reclamaciones por daños y perjuicios de McKay y ordenaran la venta de la estructura para cubrir las costas judiciales.

Last year a scow ran into the bridge, and caused $3,000 damage, for which Mr. McKay's agents sued in the local court. In the lower court he obtained judgment, but the case went against him on appeals to the highest judicial authority of the republic, and $3,000 was awarded the government for injury to the scow and costs of court. Mr. McKay declares this contrary to law and evidence and everything else. The judgment of the court was promptly supplemented by a government decree seizing the bridge and offering it for sale to pay the costs. Against this, according to Mr. McKay, United States Consul Grimke at San Domingo has been instructed to protest most vigorously. It is learned at the State Department that this action has been or will be taken to prevent illegal injury being done the property of any American in Santo Domingo. All details of the affair, according to custom, are withheld. Major General Nelson A. Miles, commanding the army, today appeared before the Senate committee on coast defenses. He stated he had examined Senator Squires's bill and approved its provisions. It was absolutely necessary, he said, that appropriations be made for the protection of our sea coast cities.
