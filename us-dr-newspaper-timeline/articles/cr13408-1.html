Independence, Recognition, and Samaná SchemesIndependencia, Reconocimiento y Proyectos de Samaná · 1844–1861

On February 27, 1844, Los Trinitarios proclaimed Dominican independence. American press coverage surged with reports on the new republic, repeated Haitian invasion threats, diplomatic recognition appeals, and southern schemes to secure Samaná Bay as a naval base.El 27 de febrero de 1844, Los Trinitarios proclamaron la independencia. La prensa estadounidense cubrió la nueva república, las invasiones haitianas, llamamientos de reconocimiento diplomático y proyectos para adquirir la Bahía de Samaná.

  • Late from the City of St. Domingo. · June 15, 184915 de junio de 1849

The schooner Mary and Elizabeth arrived here yesterday afternoon from Mariagone, St. Domingo, which place she left the 7th instant. Capt. Smith informs us that President Soulouque had, after a severe battle, taken from the Dominicans the towns of Las Matas and St. John, in which a great many of the Dominicans were killed and taken prisoners. Many of them escaped, abandoning five large pieces of artillery and a 6-pounder.

The above attack took place on the morning of the 18th March. Gens. Bobo, Vincent and Michel were left in possession of the captured towns, and the President with his troops left on the 19th for Port au Prince.

Three hundred troops from Jeremie passed through Mariagone on the 2d inst., on their way to Port au Prince to join the President. Men were gathering from all directions to join the President, who would shortly make another attack on the Dominicans.—N. Y. Tribune.
