The Evening Star reported that Rafael Trujillo announced his retirement to his country estate and was named head of the Dominican delegation to the United Nations General Assembly. Observers viewed the reshuffle—which included reports that President Héctor Trujillo would take an ambassadorship in Europe—as a maneuver to ease inter-American pressure regarding the assassination attempt against Venezuelan President Rómulo Betancourt.

Rafael Trujillo only this week announced he was leaving his office in the National Palace here and withdrawing to his country estate. He had previously given up the jobs of head of the Dominican Party and chief of the armed forces. Government sources said he will go to New York before the Assembly begins its three month 15th annual session September 20. They said he is to head the Dominican delegation because the Assembly will deal with questions of vital importance to Latin America’s future. He headed it once before, in early 1953, after turning over the presidency to his brother. Hector May Be Envoy There were unofficial reports that Hector Trujillo would become Ambassador to some country in Europe. Speculation was that the Trujillos had been moved from center stage to quiet criticism abroad and lessen the likelihood of inter-American action on Venezuela's charges that the Trujillos planned the bomb explosion June 24 that burned its President, Romulo Betancourt. Western Hemisphere Foreign Ministers will discuss this charge when they meet August 16 in San Jose, Costa Rica.
