CAPTAIN GREENO'S STORY. A Timely Rescue When Threatened by Dominican Desperadoes. [illegible] According to the story told by Captain W. [illegible] Greeno, who formerly commanded the Nova Scotian brig Martha J. Brady, and who with his wife, mate, and six seamen arrived on the steamer Saginaw yesterday, the protection of American men-of-war is needed somewhere else than in Hayti. Captain Greeno left Annapolis, Nova Scotia, in the Martha J. Brady, bound for the West Indies. On the night of October 28th the brig was blown ashore on the southeast coast of San Domingo at a place called San Luis Petit Trou. The vessel had struck a reef and lay in a very dangerous position, so Captain Greeno got his wife and crew into the boat and succeeded in getting ashore. They had only the clothes on their backs and a few articles and shipstores they could lay their hands on. Next morning, after passing a night exposed to the elements, Captain Greeno saw that it would be possible to reach the stranded brig and obtain from it the [illegible] of the crew, the sails, and other property. A boat went over to her and succeeded in getting a quantity of useful things. On its return and while the Captain was engaged in putting up a tent to shield the party somewhat from the rays of the tropical sun, a band of ferocious-looking Dominicans, armed to the teeth, descended on the camp.
