ST. DOMINGO.

LUPERON DECLARED A PIRATE—FAVORABLE REPORTS OF BAEZ.

ST. DOMINGO, June 20, via HAVANA, June 29.—The Dominican Senate has declared Gen. Luperon and the crew of the steamer Telegrafo pirates. The people of Puerto Plata and Samana refused to receive Luperon, who has issued a proclamation of rebellion.

The Dominican troops remain faithful to President Baez. The Boletin Official reports that the Provinces of Cibas, Seybro, and Samana are tranquil. More troops are needed, but large numbers of citizens are offering their services to defend the frontier.

BOMBARDMENT OF PUERTO PLATTE—FAILURE OF THE ATTACK.

We are in receipt of advices from Puerto Platte to the 8th inst. Gen. Luperon, who is acting in concert with ex-President Cabral for the overthrow of the Baez government, had carried out the threat made by him some time back, of bombarding Puerto Platte. He went from St. Marc in the steamer Telegrafo (formerly the blockade runner Little Bee), which he now owns, appearing off the harbor of Puerto Platte on the 2d inst. The steamer immediately opened fire upon the town, and kept it up in an intermittent sort of way for six days, but she was at so great a distance from the town, and the shots were so badly aimed that no damage was done to the place, no property destroyed, and no lives lost. Owing to the position taken up by the Telegrafo the forts could not possibly reply to her fire, and as she maintained that position during the whole of the bombardment, the Dominicans had no chance of getting a shot at her. On the morning of the 8th inst. she ceased firing and shortly afterward steamed away in a westerly direction—it was supposed for St. Marc. Luperon had expected that his arrival before Puerto Platte in this vessel would have been the signal for a revolutionary uprising in the town and surrounding country, but in this he was disappointed.

Intelligence had been received at Puerto Platte that Cabral had commenced active military operations in the south-western part of the Dominican territory. It was understood that Luperon should operate in the north, while Cabral should confine himself to the south, it being hoped that by thus compelling Baez to divide his forces a more easy conquest would be gained over him by the revolutionary chiefs.

Trade was brisk at Puerto Platte, and American provisions were plentiful, especially flour. American lumber was also in good supply.
