The New-York Tribune reported that the Dominican modus vivendi proved financially beneficial, resulting in $3,000,000 deposited in New York, increased revenues, four U.S.-built coastguard cutters, and reduced smuggling. Following Secretary Root's revision of the pending treaty, an agreement was reached between Finance Minister Federico Velasquez and foreign creditors to reduce claims by 50 percent for cash settlement. A New York banking house agreed to advance funds against $20,000,000 in fifty-year 5 percent bonds to consolidate Dominican debts.

The New-York Tribune informó que el modus vivendi dominicano resultó económicamente provechoso, acumulando 3.000.000 de dólares depositados en Nueva York, un alza en los ingresos, cuatro guardacostas construidos en los Estados Unidos y la reducción del contrabando. Tras la revisión del tratado pendiente por el secretario Root, el ministro de Hacienda Federico Velásquez y los acreedores extranjeros alcanzaron un acuerdo para reducir las reclamaciones en un 50 por ciento mediante pago al contado. Una casa bancaria de Nueva York acordó adelantar fondos contra 20.000.000 de dólares en bonos a cincuenta años con un interés del 5 por ciento para consolidar las deudas dominicanas.

The modus vivendi, however, resulted beneficially, as far as the little republic's finances were concerned. Not only did it lead to the deposit in New York of [illegible], which will now be placed to the credit of the Dominican government, but that government has had more available money during the period of its operation than at any other time in its history. This state of affairs resulted from a more economical administration, the suppression of smuggling and the encouragement of business. The Dominican government also finds itself in possession of four fine coastguard cutters, built in the United States, forming an effective force for the protection of the customs revenues and avail able for all public uses. The substance of the modus vivendi, in the shape of a treaty, was pending before the United States Senate when Secretary Root assumed office. Recognizing the force of the objections which developed to its ratification, he set about recasting the document, and on February 13 last the treaty which has just been ratified was submitted to the Senate. The way had been paved by an agreement between Federico Velasquez, the Dominican Minister of Finance, and the creditors of the republic whereby the latter agreed to a 80 per cent reduction in their claims if these were settled in cash.
