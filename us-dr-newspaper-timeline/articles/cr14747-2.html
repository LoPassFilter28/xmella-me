The New-York Tribune reported that the U.S. Secret Service arrested Giordani in New York, uncovering his role as the financial agent of a Haitian revolutionary movement under General Fermin. Giordani had previously contracted the printing of 1,500,000 "Deux Gourdes" banknotes, half of which had been stored in a New York warehouse after an earlier failed uprising. Federal authorities intervened to prevent the remaining currency from being shipped to finance active insurgent forces in Haiti.

Giordani había contratado previamente la impresión de 1.500.000 billetes de «Deux Gourdes», la mitad de los cuales se hallaban almacenados en un depósito neoyorquino tras un fracasado levantamiento anterior. Las autoridades federales intervinieron para impedir que el resto del papel moneda fuera despachado para financiar a fuerzas insurgentes activas en Haití.

According to such facts as can be learned apart from the formal wording of the complaint, Giordani has for five years resided for the greater portion of the time in New York as the financial representative of the Haytian revolutionary movement, and several years ago, after presenting proper authorization, had printed by the Hamilton Bank Note Company £1,500,000 of beautifully engraved and “yellow backed” “Doux Gourdes” bills of the proposed new Haytian government.

At the time one-half of this currency was sent to Hayti to pay off the insurgent troops then in the field, serving under General Fermin. But before the golden bills arrived in the southern republic the revolutionary movement had met with failure, and. according to information in the possession of the federal authorities, the consignment of revolutionary currency was burned upon its arrival. Since that time the other half of the money has reposed quietly in a New York warehouse. The recent Haytian insurrection, it is asserted, has brought into existence again the self-appointed Haytian government behind the currency, and it is said arrangements had been perfected to forward the remaining bills to Hayti to pay off insurgent troops now in the field.
