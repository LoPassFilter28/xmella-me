A Suspended Law.Una ley suspendida.

Since 1861 that statute of the United States known as the "Guano act" has been a dead letter, so far as the State Department is concerned. No American citizen, we believe, when he desired to avail himself of the benefit of its provisions, has ever been allowed to invoke them against unlawful interference with his business by any other power.Desde 1861 ese estatuto de los Estados Unidos conocido como la «ley del Guano» ha sido letra muerta, en lo que respecta al Departamento de Estado. Ningún ciudadano estadounidense, creemos, cuando deseó valerse del beneficio de sus disposiciones, ha sido jamás permitido invocarlas contra la interferencia ilegal con su negocio por cualquier otra potencia.

In order to encourage and build up an extensive and valuable domestic trade, Congress declared that whenever a citizen of the United States should discover guano on any derelict island or key, beyond the maritime jurisdiction of any other nation, he might raise the flag of the United States over it, and proceed to dig and ship the guano to this country under the laws of the coastwise trade. Such islands became by reason of such occupation appendages of the United States, subject to its jurisdiction, and entitled to its protection. They were registered in the Treasury Department, upon certain proofs of the facts, and the Treasury certificate was a conclusive and final adjudication in favor of the parties making the discovery, and adventuring their capital and labor in the business into which they were expressly invited by the public policy of the nation.A fin de fomentar y construir un extenso y valioso comercio doméstico, el Congreso declaró que cuando un ciudadano de los Estados Unidos descubriera guano en cualquier isla o cayo abandonado, más allá de la jurisdicción marítima de cualquier otra nación, podría izar la bandera de los Estados Unidos sobre él, y proceder a excavar y embarcar el guano a este país bajo las leyes del comercio de cabotaje. Tales islas se convirtieron por razón de tal ocupación en apéndices de los Estados Unidos, sujetas a su jurisdicción, y con derecho a su protección. Fueron registradas en el Departamento del Tesoro, mediante ciertas pruebas de los hechos, y el certificado del Tesoro era una adjudicación concluyente y final en favor de las partes que hacían el descubrimiento, y que aventuraban su capital y trabajo en el negocio al que fueron expresamente invitadas por la política pública de la nación.

The "Guano act" was plain. Several times its provisions were submitted to the Attorney-General, and received each time the same interpretation, both as to the municipal law and the public law of the world. In a case with Venezuela it was diplomatically enforced. In a case with Hayti it was enforced by the navy, under instructions of the State Department. But Mr. SEWARD, for reasons of his own, chose to disregard it. American citizens had discovered a rich deposit of guano upon a barren and derelict islet lying in the high seas off the coast of San Domingo. They occupied it peacefully for seven months, had complied with all the conditions of the law, and established a large business. At the end of that time an armed Dominican vessel called, tore down the flag, and carried the men away prisoners. There never was a more flagrant insult to a nation, or a more indefensible outrage upon the rights of our private citizens. But Mr. SEWARD declined to seek any redress, and from that day to this there has been no instance in which the Guano act has been successfully invoked against a foreign aggressor. It appears that a case almost exactly similar to the above has been recently brought to the attention of the State Department. This time it is Mexico which sends an armed force to tear down the American flag, and carry off American citizens engaged in lawful business, in which they were by statute specially promised the protection of their country. Will Mr. FRELINGHUYSEN follow the remarkable precedent of 1861? Will he follow those set by Gen. CASS, and enforce the law not merely as he finds it in every writer from VATTEL to WHEATON, but as it is written in plain English on the statute book of the United States? If the newspaper reports of the facts is correct, the case is too clear for any dispute, and the people will be glad to see some evidence of old-time spirit and vigor in our dealings with this class of questions.La «ley del Guano» era clara. Varias veces sus disposiciones fueron sometidas al Fiscal General, y recibieron cada vez la misma interpretación, tanto en cuanto al derecho municipal como al derecho público del mundo. En un caso con Venezuela fue diplomáticamente aplicada. En un caso con Haití fue aplicada por la marina, bajo instrucciones del Departamento de Estado. Pero el Sr. Seward, por razones propias, eligió desconocerla. Ciudadanos estadounidenses habían descubierto un rico depósito de guano en un islote estéril y abandonado situado en alta mar frente a la costa de Santo Domingo. Lo ocuparon pacíficamente por siete meses, habían cumplido con todas las condiciones de la ley, y establecido un gran negocio. Al final de ese tiempo un buque armado dominicano llamó, derribó la bandera, y se llevó a los hombres prisioneros. Nunca hubo un insulto más flagrante a una nación, ni un ultraje más indefendible sobre los derechos de nuestros ciudadanos privados. Pero el Sr. Seward declinó buscar redress alguno, y desde ese día hasta hoy no ha habido ningún caso en el que la ley del Guano haya sido invocada con éxito contra un agresor extranjero.

The Sun. New York [N.Y.], May 28, 1882. Page 4.
