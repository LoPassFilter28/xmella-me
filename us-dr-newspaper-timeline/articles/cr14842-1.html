The Birmingham Age-Herald reported that Haiti dispatched armed forces to halt Dominican road construction between Pedernales and Lake Fondo, which the Dominican government condemned as a hostile act and met with defensive troop shipments. American Minister Russell indicated that reports of direct armed conflict had been denied, while officials in Washington considered deploying a small gunboat to observe the situation. President Caceres stated that he sought an amicable diplomatic resolution while firmly asserting Dominican rights.

The Birmingham Age-Herald informó que Haití despachó fuerzas armadas para detener la construcción dominicana de una carretera entre Pedernales y el lago del Fondo, lo que el gobierno dominicano condenó como un acto de hostilidad y respondió con el envío defensivo de tropas. El ministro estadounidense Russell indicó que los informes sobre un conflicto armado directo habían sido desmentidos, mientras en Washington las autoridades sopesaban enviar un pequeño cañonero para observar la situación. El presidente Cáceres declaró que procuraba una salida diplomática amistosa al tiempo que hacía valer con firmeza los derechos dominicanos.

American Minister Russell stated that while news of the conflict had reached Santo Domingo it was promptly denied, though the Haytien government is admitted to have sent out armed forces to compel the Dominicans to stop work on the road between Pedernales and Lake Fondo. The Dominican government which is building the road insists that it does not cross Haytien territory and that even if it did the question was one that should be settled by diplomatic representations. The action of Hayti in sending an armed force against peaceful road builders is, therefore, regarded as a clearly hostile act. This the Dominican authorities regard as sufficient to warrant them in taking prompt defensive measures, wherefore it is sending troops and a Dominican gunboat loaded with stores and material to Pedernales which is on the border lines between the two countries on the south coast of the island. The difficulty is not regarded here as serious but it is not unlikely that one of the little United States gunboats on the Central American coast will be sent to observe developments. Dispute May Be Settled San Domingo, Republic of San Domingo, December 27.—President Ramon Caceres, in an interview today said that his latest advices looked more favorable for the friendly settlement of the boundary suit with Hayti.
