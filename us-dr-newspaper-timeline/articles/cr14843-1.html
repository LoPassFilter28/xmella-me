Dwelt especially upon the expenditure, by General Babcock, on San DomingoSe detuvo especialmente en el desembolso, por el general Babcock, en Santo Domingo

Eureka Daily Sentinel. Eureka, Nev., October 27, 1877. Page 2. View the originalVer el original

According to the newspaper, General Babcock expended a large sum from the Secret Service Fund regarding San Domingo, leading to a misunderstanding between President Grant and Senator Sumner, where the President intended to discuss the treaty for the acquisition of the Dominican Republic while Sumner believed they were discussing the expenditure. Sumner assured the President he was a Republican supporter and intended to examine the papers to sustain the expenditure, while Major Poore's statement purportedly resolves historical doubts regarding the cause of the difficulty between Grant and Sumner.Según el periódico, el general Babcock desembolsó una cuantiosa suma del fondo de servicios secretos en relación con Santo Domingo, lo que suscitó un malentendido entre el presidente Grant y el senador Sumner: mientras el presidente se proponía tratar el tratado para la anexión de la República Dominicana, Sumner entendió que deliberaban sobre dicha erogación. Sumner aseguró al presidente su lealtad republicana y su disposición a revisar los documentos para respaldar el desembolso, mientras que las declaraciones del mayor Poore esclarecen supuestamente las dudas históricas sobre el origen de las desavenencias entre Grant y Sumner.

WASHINGTON, Oct. 26.WASHINGTON, 26 de octubre.

Major Ben. Perley Poore, who was Senator Sumner’s guest at the time of his estrangement from President Grant, has explained the causes of the trouble. He says the removal of James M. Ashley, ex-Congressman from Ohio, from the Governorship of Montana, and the appointment of Colonel Potts, which had just happened, was the subject of table conversation, and a letter from Ashley, protesting against the change, had been read, when the President was announced. He was received, shown Ashley’s letter, and became immediately excited, denouncing Ashley as a worthless mischief-maker. The next topic discussed was the San Domingo treaty. Mr. Sumner dwelt especially upon the expenditure, by General Babcock, on San Domingo, of a large sum of money taken from the Secret Service Fund, for the purpose of promoting intercourse with the West India Islands, which Seward had prevailed on Thad. Stevens to have inserted in the Appropriation bill during the war, and the President impressed Sumner with the idea that he feared an attack in Congress for that expenditure.
