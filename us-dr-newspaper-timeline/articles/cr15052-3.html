From St. Domingo.

—We learn from Capt. Wilson of the brig Republic, just arrived from Port au Prince, that on the 12th of March the Haytien man of war Herard, in the schr. La Granada, had been seen off the South side of the Island, five miles off, by five gun boats. The whole coast was guarded by vessels of war, to prevent the ex-President and his adherents from landing. The government called out all the militia, and the whole of that part of the Island belonging to the Haytien government was under martial law. Several of the adherents of Herard had been taken and shot, and there was a great excitement throughout the Island. Coffee was very scarce and high on account of all the country men having been taken for soldiers, and no persons left to bring the Coffee to market. The markets were glutted with American produce.

Although numerous arrests were made in different parts of the Republic, among whom were some of the journalists, there was no general sympathy for Herard, who was viewed as an ambitious usurper, and by all parties of government, that his attempts to revolutionize the country would prove abortive.

It was rumored at Port Republican, that a revolution had taken place at St. Domingo, which had resulted in the expulsion of Gen. Santana from the Presidency, and in the elevation to that office of Major Moru, a mulatto of splendid character, and the respectable family of the Alvares of Zagnate (Saint Domingo.) If true, this change of government in the Dominican Republic would be the triumph of an enlightened policy over military rule, and demagogism personified in Santana and Bobadilla.
