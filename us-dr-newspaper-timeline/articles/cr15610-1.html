The Morning Call reviewed early United States diplomatic and naval missions to the Dominican Republic, including Lieutenant David D. Porter's 1846 survey of Dominican finances and Samaná Bay's strategic harbors. The paper also recalled the 1851 joint mission of Robert M. Walsh alongside British and French commissioners to negotiate an end to Haitian military incursions against the Dominican Republic.

The Morning Call examinó las primeras misiones diplomáticas y navales de los Estados Unidos en la República Dominicana, incluida la inspección efectuada en 1846 por el teniente David D. Porter sobre las finanzas dominicanas y los puertos estratégicos de la bahía de Samaná. El diario también recordó la misión conjunta de 1851 de Robert M. Walsh junto con comisionados británicos y franceses para negociar el cese de las incursiones militares haitianas contra la República Dominicana.

On his return to Washington, Porter made a full report in duplicate, one for the State and the other for the Navy Department, but both of these papers disappeared from the departments prior to the breaking out of the Civil War in 1861. The report was highly favorable in tone, commending the excellent harbor at Samana Bay, and pointing to its strategical advantages as a naval station. In 1851 President Fillmore and Secretary of State Webster commissioned Robert M. Walsh as special agent of the Government to Hayti and San Domingo to co-operate with the British and French commissioners in bringing about a cessation of the warfare that the Haytians had been waging on the peacefully inclined and numerically inferior San Dominicans. The United States steamer Saranac conveyed Commissioner Walsh to Port-au-Prince, where he met the French and English commissioners, and during a stay of some weeks had two interviews with Emperor Faustin I. With the French and English commissioners he then went to San Domingo and reported the results of their negotiations at Hayti to President Baez of the Dominican republic.
