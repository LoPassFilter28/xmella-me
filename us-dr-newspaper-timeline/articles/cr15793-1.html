The Imperial Valley Press reported that a political commentator named Gamero advocated for a U.S. foreign policy of non-recognition toward Latin American dictatorships to encourage democratic development. Gamero stated that alongside Argentina, several regional governments exhibited fascist tendencies, specifically naming Brazil, Paraguay, Honduras, Nicaragua, the Dominican Republic, and Guatemala.

El Imperial Valley Press informó que el analista político Gamero propuso una política exterior de Estados Unidos basada en el no reconocimiento hacia las dictaduras latinoamericanas como medio para propiciar transformaciones democráticas. Gamero sostuvo que, además de Argentina, múltiples regímenes de la región presentaban marcados tintes fascistas, señalando expresamente los casos de Brasil, Paraguay, Honduras, Nicaragua, la República Dominicana y Guatemala.

(UP) Roy P Arnold, Los Angeles optical technician and ex-member of the German-American Bund, testified at the mass sedition trial that a strike of produce farmers around New York City was to have been the signal for the bund's abortive plans to stage a "putsch" and install its own "strong man in America. Arnold, who joined the bund as a result of his friendship with Hans Diebel, one of the 29 defendants in the trial, said Diebel told him of the signal for the uprising after he made "repeated" requests. He testified that two other defendants in the trial— Evangelist Gerald P Winrod and Silver Shirt Organizer William Dudley Pelley were the bund's candidates for the role of "strong man" in this coun[illegible] Arnold told the jury that Diebel had said that he intended to "get himself five Jews before he was stopped" if the planned putsch became "too hot." In his testimony, Arnold said Diebel told him the bund was "especially anxious to cultivate the sympathy and following of the armed forces and law enforcement officers, especially the state militias," presumably in connection with its "putsch" plans.
