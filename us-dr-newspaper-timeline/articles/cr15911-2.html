The Evening Star published an analytical article examining Dominican political history through the writings of Américo Lugo and Francisco J. Peynado during the 1916 crisis. The piece argued that Dominican national instability resulted not only from domestic shortcomings but also from foreign intervention and the restrictive 1907 financial convention imposed by Washington.

El Evening Star publicó un artículo analítico que examinaba la historia política dominicana a través de los escritos de Américo Lugo y Francisco J. Peynado durante la crisis de 1916. El texto sostenía que la inestabilidad nacional dominicana obedecía no solo a deficiencias internas sino también a la intervención extranjera y a la restrictiva convención financiera de 1907 impuesta por Washington.

The Dominican State reflects what, it can, the variable will of the popular masses; in no way a public desire that does not exist here. “The Dominican people are not a nation because they have no awareness of what constitutes one, because their political activities have not become sufficiently generalized. Not being a nation, the State that pretends to represent this, is not a true State.” To this negative conclusion arrived the sated hope of the great Dominican when nearing his 46th birthday. He had had no opportunity to observe in his country one single moment of national sanity. About this same time in 1916, Don Francisco J. Peynado also delivered his famous speech at the Floral Games. He was then travelling along the same pathways of desperation and skepticism that moved Lugo's soul. It was not then possible, in that mournful year, to be able to give a satisfied smile regarding the future of the Republic. Every one was bad and no one could feel happy. However, we frankly believe that both Lugo's and Peynado's theses were incomplete. We Dominicans have never been solely responsible for the national incapacity of our country. Foreigners are as much to blame as we ourselves. In 1916, we were paying for, apart from our administrative errors, the niggardliness and the absolute lack of spirit of international co-operation by which the Government of Washington negotiated the 1907 Pact.
