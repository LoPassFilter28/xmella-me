The Evening Star reported that Manolo Castro, head of the Cuban Sports Commission, pleaded not guilty in federal court in Jacksonville to charges of illegally exporting weapons and explosives intended for an uprising against Dominican President Rafael Trujillo. Castro alleged that Trujillo's personal hostility was behind his indictment. Co-defendants Chester L. Miller and Percy H. Cyr also pleaded not guilty, while Hollis B. Smith entered a guilty plea to purchasing the munitions.

El Evening Star informó que Manolo Castro, comisionado nacional de Deportes de Cuba, se declaró inocente ante el tribunal federal de distrito en Jacksonville de las imputaciones por tráfico clandestino de pertrechos bélicos y explosivos destinados a una insurrección armada contra el mandatario dominicano Rafael Trujillo. Castro alegó que su encausamiento respondía al encono personal que Trujillo le profesaba. Los coacusados Chester L. Miller y Percy H. Cyr sostuvieron igualmente su no culpabilidad, mientras que Hollis B. Smith admitió formalmente los cargos de adquisición ilícita de armas.

Cuban Blames Trujillo For Arms Plot Charges By the Associated Press JACKSONVILLE, Fla., Dec. 9.— Manolo Castro, director of the Cuban Sports Commission, charged yesterday that enmity of Rafael Trujillo was responsible for his implication in an alleged plot to export arms to Cuba for use in an unsuccessful revolt against the Dominican Republic President last summer. Castro, who came to Jacksonville for arraignment on an indictment charging illegal exportation of arms and high explosives from the United States, pleaded not guilty before Federal Judge Louie W. Strum. The case was set for trial January 5. Not guilty pleas also were entered by Chester L. Miller and Percy H. Cyr, both airplane pilots of Hasbrouck Heights, N. J. Hollis B. Smith, Palisades Park, N. J.. who is alleged to have purchased the arms, pleaded guilty. Smith was permitted to remain under bond pending disposition of the charges against the other defendants. Bert W. Beatty, 58, Dies BLUEFIELD, W. Va., Dec. 9 (UP).— Bert W. Beatty, 58, Norfolk Western Railway conductor for the last 30 years, died in a hospital here yesterday from a heart ailment. He was a native of McDonald Mills, Va.
