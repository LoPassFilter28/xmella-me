The Dominican Consulship.

The Hon. H. C. C. Astwood, United States Consul to San Domingo, arrived in New York last Monday, and proceeded immediately to Washington.

In the New York Sun of Tuesday, we find a communication from San Domingo City, the closing paragraph of which, bearing upon the Dominican Consulship, is here given:

H. C. C. Astwood, the United States Consul, who is an experienced consul, and the most eminent and very popular with the American residents here, has left for New York. Mr. Astwood's success in negotiating and settling American claims against the Dominican Government leads people here to hope that his efforts with his own people on behalf of his country here may prove fruitful.

Mr. Astwood succeeded in having the repudiated claims of Messrs. Remington of New York, Wanamaker & Brown of Philadelphia, and others, recognized. It was he who obtained an indemnity of $33,000 for the widow of Platt, the American carpenter, who was shot by the Government troops here in June last. The underwriters of New York, Philadelphia and Boston, in recognition of his services in breaking up and preventing numerous frauds attempted upon them, have together with Messrs. Clyde of New York and many others, petitioned the President, praying that he be continued in office. His knowledge of the language and people renders him almost indispensable to the Americans here. When the immense amount of American capital invested in sugar plantations and otherwise is considered, it is important that a suitable representative of the United States Government should be here.

It is very gratifying to note this high endorsement of Mr. Astwood's diplomatic labors. We have no doubt that President Cleveland will recognize in Mr. Astwood a capable officer of the diplomatic service, and grant the petition of those Americans who have business relations with San Domingo to reappoint Mr. Astwood.

We understand that Mr. Astwood is not anxious to retain the post, but allows his name to go before the President for consideration at the earnest solicitation of the parties above cited, who recognize in him a public servant whose place it would not be very easy to supply.

We trust the President will reappoint Mr. Astwood United States Consul to San Domingo.

The Sun's correspondent states that the affairs of San Domingo are in a very unsettled condition, and anticipates that there will be an upheavel consequent upon the Presidential succession.
