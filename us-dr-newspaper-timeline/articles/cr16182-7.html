In a letter from Azua, W. G. Temple asks Commissioner B. F. Wade to withhold consent to a proposed expedition and to ask President Baez to forbid the gentlemen's passage through his lines. The correspondence adds that a new plan had been devised since arriving at Azua: Dr. Howe questioned prisoners and deserters from Cabral's force and, with Baez's consent, sent one prisoner back to the mountains with a letter to the revolutionary chieftain requesting a conference on Haitian territory, the messenger to receive three Spanish doubloons on his return.

En una carta desde Azua, W. G. Temple pide al comisionado B. F. Wade que niegue su consentimiento a una expedición propuesta y que solicite al presidente Báez que prohíba el paso de esos caballeros a través de sus líneas. La correspondencia añade que desde la llegada a Azua se había ideado un nuevo plan: el Dr. Howe interrogó a prisioneros y desertores de la fuerza de Cabral y, con el consentimiento de Báez, envió a uno de los prisioneros de vuelta a las montañas con una carta para el jefe revolucionario solicitando una conferencia en territorio haitiano, y el mensajero recibiría tres doblones españoles a su regreso.

Since we arrived at Azua, however, a new plan of communication with the insurgents has been devised. Dr. Howe has interrogated a number of prisoners and deserters from Cabral's force, and, with the consent of President Baez has sent one of the prisoners back to the mountains with a letter to the revolutionary Chieftain requesting a conference on Haytien territory. The messenger, on returning with an answer, is to receive three Spanish doubloons ([illegible]). We are confident that he will succeed. Wade and the [illegible], San Domingo Correspondence New York [Sun?]. The rage of Wade is fearful to behold. He has turned himself into a volcano of profanity. He asserts that Grant has sent him down here with a pack of damned fools who, now that their work is done, want to sport around the West India islands and scoop up all the snakes and lizards. He swears and sweats, and sweats and swears in the hot sun until one could almost imagine that his great soul was just ready to jump out of his body. The fact is Ben, wants to go home. His work is done, and he don't want to loaf around any longer.
