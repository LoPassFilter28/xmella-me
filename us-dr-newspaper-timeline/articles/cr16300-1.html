The Presbyterian of the South reported that Captain Knapp established a military government in Santo Domingo following the overthrow of President Jiminez and Washington's refusal to recognize Frederico Enriquez. Under Knapp's proclamation, the military administration guaranteed monthly loan payments to the Guaranty Trust Company of New York and the payment of salaries for Dominican officials. Secretary Daniels noted that the 1,800 marines would enforce order without infringing upon Dominican civil rights or regular judicial processes.

El Presbyterian of the South informó que el capitán Knapp constituyó un gobierno militar en Santo Domingo tras la deposición del presidente Jimenes y el desconocimiento de Washington hacia Francisco Henríquez y Carvajal (citado como Frederico Enriquez). Mediante la proclama de Knapp, la administración castrense garantizaba los abonos mensuales del empréstito a la Guaranty Trust Company de Nueva York y la cancelación de sueldos a los servidores públicos dominicanos. El secretario Daniels indicó que los 1.800 soldados de infantería de marina mantendrían el orden sin quebrantar las garantías cívicas dominicanas ni el curso procesal ordinario.

Under the military government put into effect by Captain Knapp's proclamation, Captain Knapp will act as military governor and [while acting?] not only will provide for the payment of $100,000 a month to the fiscal agent of the loan, the Guaranty Trust Company, of New York, and interest on any other existing foreign obligations, but also will see that the salaries of Dominican government officials and expenses of the government are paid. Although no publicity had been given to the government's intentions, Captain Knapp's action was the result of plans carefully worked out by State and Navy Department officials after conferences with Minister Russell, who was recalled from Santo Domingo for the purpose, and consideration of full reports from the naval officers on the scene. Captain Knapp, recently sent to relieve Rear-Admiral Pond in Dominican waters, carried with him complete instructions for his task. The necessity for a drastic step has been considered since the overthrow several months ago of President Jiminez by General Arias, a strongly antiAmerican revolutionist. With Arias in control, the Dominican Assembly elected Frederico Enriquez president, but the United States refused to recognize the new government. Secretary Daniels explained that the form of rule set by Captain Knapp did not contemplate any infringement of the civil rights or processes of the native government.
