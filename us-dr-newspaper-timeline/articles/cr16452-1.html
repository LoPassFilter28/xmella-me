The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union reported that U.S. Minister James M. Sullivan and Consul Charles W. Hathaway departed to inform Dominican insurgents that Washington firmly opposed their rebellion against President Bordas. Sullivan was instructed to protect American-supervised customs houses, supported by the cruiser Des Moines, which arrived at Puerto Plata ready to land marines. The excerpt also detailed the U.S. Senate's passage of the Underwood tariff bill.

WASHINGTON, Sept. 9.—James M. Sullivan, the new American minister to Santo Domingo, and Charles W. Hathaway, consul at Puerto Plata, left Washington late today for Puerto Plata to tell the Dominican revolutionists there in emphatic terms that the United States has no sympathy with their effort to overthrow President Borda's government. They received final instructions at the state department just before their departure. Mr. Sullivan will take steps to protect the customs houses in the revolutionary zone, which like all those in the island republic, are operated under American supervision. The cruiser Des Moines arrived at Puerto Plata late today and should it be necessary there will be no hesitancy in the landing of marines, which besides guarding the customs houses would afford protection to the American consulate and all American interests.
