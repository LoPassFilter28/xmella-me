The Twice-a-Week Courier-Journal, commenting on public sentimentThe Twice-a-Week Courier-Journal, al comentar el sentimiento público

The Twice-a-Week Courier-Journal, commenting on public sentiment in Santo Domingo after Heureaux's death, remarks that people in the United States have wondered to hear that a man named Ramon—whose surname is garbled in the print as "Caoares" and "Cocorss"—who killed Heureaux in cold blood and cast his body into the street where a mob mutilated it, has become a popular idol and was called to serve as Minister of War. That wonder lessens, the writer suggests, given the admiration fellow citizens accord someone who rid the country of its tyrant, adding that he carries his honors modestly and has just resigned from the Vasques cabinet citing his own unfitness for executive office. At the same time, residents of Santo Domingo, chiefly foreigners, contend that only an iron hand like Heureaux's can govern what they call a thoughtless and revolutionary people who yield only to force.The Twice-a-Week Courier-Journal, al comentar el sentimiento público en Santo Domingo tras la muerte de Heureaux, señala que en Estados Unidos ha causado asombro saber que un hombre llamado Ramon —cuyo apellido aparece distorsionado en el impreso como «Caoares» y «Cocorss»—, quien mató a Heureaux a sangre fría y arrojó su cuerpo a la calle donde una turba lo mutiló, se ha convertido en ídolo popular y fue llamado a ocupar la cartera de Ministro de Guerra. Ese asombro se atenúa, sugiere el autor, ante la admiración que sus conciudadanos profesan a quien libró al país de su tirano, añadiendo que lleva sus honores con modestia y acaba de renunciar al gabinete de Vasques alegando su propia falta de aptitud para un cargo ejecutivo. Al mismo tiempo, residentes de Santo Domingo, principalmente extranjeros, sostienen que solo una mano de hierro como la de Heureaux puede gobernar a lo que califican de pueblo irreflexivo y revolucionario que únicamente se doblega ante la fuerza.

The Twice-A-Week Courier-Journal. Louisville, Ky., November 15, 1899.
