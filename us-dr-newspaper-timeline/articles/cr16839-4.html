From St. Domingo we learn that there is a prospect of a misunderstanding between the Spanish authorities and the Haytians, relative to the question of the boundary line. Spain is determined to have the old limits established; but the Haytians are inclined to resist this. Both parties are in unjust and illegal claim. The Spanish forces are concentrating at Agua, and it is expected that they will attack the Haytians unless they do as they are bid. The Spaniards say that if urged to arms they they will compel the Haytians to give up the line claimed and to pay all expenses. The Madrid correspondent of the Independence Belge, writing on the 28th ult., states that a royal decree, signed by the Queen of Spain, has created the famous Santana, who sold St. Domingo to the Crown, a Grandee of Spain and "Marquis de las Carreras," this being the name of the place where the Haytians and Dominicans met in combat some years ago.
