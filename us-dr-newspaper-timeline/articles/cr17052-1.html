The New-York Tribune recounted political maneuvers during President Buenaventura Báez's historical efforts to annex the Dominican Republic to the United States. The account recalled how Báez jailed political opponents to guarantee passage of the treaty until an American politician persuaded him that a small dissenting vote would look less suspicious. It also detailed an earlier attempt to lease Samana Bay surveyed by Captain George B. McClellan, which collapsed when British and French warships arrived to oppose American acquisition.

El New-York Tribune relató las maniobras políticas durante las gestiones históricas del presidente Buenaventura Báez para anexionar la República Dominicana a los Estados Unidos. La crónica recordó cómo Báez encarceló a opositores políticos para asegurar la aprobación del tratado hasta que un político estadounidense lo convenció de que una pequeña votación disidente resultaría menos sospechosa. Detalló asimismo una tentativa anterior para arrendar la bahía de Samaná, inspeccionada por el capitán George B. McClellan, la cual fracasó al llegar buques de guerra británicos y franceses para oponerse a la adquisición estadounidense.

end therewith he declared the conference At an end

Sure enough, the opposition failed to appear at the reassembling of the Congress, and soon the news leaked out that all five men had been thrown into jail

When the report reached the ears of a prominent New-York politician who was at the Dominican Capitol, and who was greatly interested in the treaty, he went at once to Baez and remonstrated:

“Jail methods will ruin the whole business,” he said. “This thing must be done on the square, or it will fall through.” Baez at first shrugged his shoulders, and when he did speak he only said: “But you want it unanimous, don't you? “Not at all. A unanimous vote always creates suspicion. A small minority vote gives a far better impression.” “All right.” said Baez. “You can have that. Anything you want you can have in San Domingo.” Baez summoned several representatives, and there was a hurried conference. The treaty was passed with a “small minority.” Twenty years before the failure of this annexation scheme this government made a treaty with San Domingo to rent in perpetuity the great sheltered Bay of Samana. The father of the present Mayor of New-York accompanied the commission sent to negotiate for the territory, and Captain McClellan, as he was known then, made a survey of that part of the Dominican coast. Just before the treaty could be ratified, however, news of its purpose leaked out. England and France both took alarm, and dispatched warships to Samana Bay. At the sight of European cannon the San Domingo statesmen forgot all about the treaty.
