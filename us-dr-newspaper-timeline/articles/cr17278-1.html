The Sentinel-Record reported that the Dominican Republic was descending into chaos with four concurrent revolutions, leaving President Bordas unable to suppress insurgents despite maintaining power through extra-constitutional decrees. Fighting disrupted multiple regions, including Azua, where the national navy destroyed the custom house, and La Romana, where American sugar estates requested protection. With Customs Collector Vick resigned and Minister Sullivan returning to the United States, Washington faced mounting pressure to intervene directly.

The Sentinel-Record informó que la República Dominicana caía en el caos ante cuatro revoluciones simultáneas, hallándose el presidente Bordas imposibilitado de someter a los rebeldes pese a gobernar mediante decretos extraconstitucionales. Los combates desestabilizaban numerosas zonas, entre ellas Azua, donde la marina de guerra nacional destruyó la aduana, y La Romana, donde centrales azucareros estadounidenses demandaban resguardo. Con la renuncia del recaudador aduanero Vick y el regreso del ministro Sullivan a los Estados Unidos, Washington afrontaba una creciente presión para intervenir en forma directa.

With four separate revolutions raging and the American minister returning home after several months of fruitless efforts to restore peace, the little republic of San Domingo is causing the Washington government grave anxiety. The United States is morally bound to maintain order there under the semi-protectorate created by treaty. Today's reports showed no improvement. For many months President Bordas, obliged to maintain his office by extra-official decrees and partial elections, has been leading his troops unsuccessfully against rebellious generals in the north. Meanwhile revolutions have broken out in other sections at Azua, where the government navy found it necessary to destroy the customs house, along the line of railway between Sanchez and Samana Bay, at Monte Cristi and La Romana in the southeast. In this last neighborhood there are extensive American plantations, agents for which appeared at the state department today looking for protection of their property. A conviction is growing here that only through powerful outside representations and probably that of the United States, can chaotic conditions be terminated. The revolutionists appear unable to overcome the Bordas government, but Bordas has not been able to defeat his enemies.
