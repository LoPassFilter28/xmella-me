I have not ascertained satisfactorily what is the situation here, but I will briefly state my belief on the subject.

President Baez, as a statesman and patriot, sincerely desires the annexation of Dominica to America. Delay in accomplishing this is attended by financial trouble, anxiety and risk of insurrection. There is a very rich and large extent of valley country on the western frontier of Dominica, watered by the affluents of the Artibonite, containing some small towns, bearing Spanish names, but of which the Dominicans have not had the complete possession. It is here that Cabral operates with a small predatory party, which President Baez believes is procured and supported by Hayti. Cabral is now at San Juan, say forty miles east of the boundary line between Dominica and Hayti, according to the treaty between Spain and France, with his followers, variable in numbers, but always small, whence he makes raids and retreats as occasion requires. His last operation, noised about as a movement to defeat annexation, was very insignificant and amounted to a few persons killed, wounded and captured by his followers. At Azua and at the posts on several roads leading westward from it the Dominicans have some 1,000 or 1,500 troops. These seem to be in position for defence against incursion from without or insurrection from within. They appear to form the main organization of troops, though there are some troops here and in other places. Though the ordinary expenses of the military, which is the chief administration, are estimated at about $7,000 monthly, the revenue is so small and the paper money of the country is of so little value that the employés of the government have been a long time unpaid, and measures are now proposed looking to treating the paper money like our Continental currency and the French assignats, and providing for receiving some real money for duties wherewith to carry on the government.
