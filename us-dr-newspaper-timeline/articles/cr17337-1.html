The Omaha Daily Bee reported that Haiti and the Dominican Republic settled their diplomatic disputes after Haitian President Hyppolite apologized and offered indemnity for border raids linked to General Luperón. Presidents Heureaux and Hyppolite formed an alliance barring political exiles from organizing in either nation and pledged to coordinate against foreign aggression. The agreement also stipulated that neither republic would cede naval bases like Samaná Bay or Môle Saint-Nicolas to foreign powers without mutual consent.

El Omaha Daily Bee informó que Haití y la República Dominicana resolvieron sus disputas diplomáticas luego de que el presidente haitiano Hyppolite se disculpara y ofreciera una indemnización por las incursiones fronterizas vinculadas al general Luperón. Los presidentes Heureaux y Hyppolite conformaron una alianza que prohibía a los exiliados políticos organizarse en cualquiera de las dos naciones y se comprometieron a coordinar acciones contra agresiones extranjeras. El acuerdo estipulaba además que ninguna de las dos repúblicas cedería bases navales como la bahía de Samaná o Môle Saint-Nicolas a potencias extranjeras sin consentimiento mutuo.

between the two republics were settled. On behalf of Hayti President Hippolyto formally apologized for the encouragement Hayti had given General Luperon in his raid over the Santo Domingo border. He promised to pay indemnity. President Heureaux was satisfied with this apology and promise of indemnity. He has issued orders for the withdrawal of his troops from the border line. Traffic is thus reopened. An alliance was entered into between the two republics by which they agree to deny the right of refuge in either republic to political exiles from the other. Heureaux also enters into alliance to operate with Hayti on all questions of foreign interferences. President Heureaux and the war ship El Presidente have gone to the city of Santo Domingo. Hippolyto's forces that were massed about Cape Haytien have started on their return to Port-au-Prince. Hippolyte, his cabinet and his family will remain at Cape Haytien and return the visit of Heureaux in August. The German man-of-war [Gneisenau?] has sailed for Cuba. The United States ship Kearsarge is the only foreign war vessel now in these waters. Under the agreement between Presidents Hippolyto and Heureaux all hope of Europe securing either Samana Bay or the Mole St. Nicholas as a coaling station is lost. The treaty has been revised, and under the terms the presidents of the republics are bound not to cede either port without the consent of the other.
