The Ogden standard-examiner reported Secretary of State Colby's statement that the United States hoped to withdraw from Haiti as its mission to stabilize the republic neared completion. Colby explained that the 1915 intervention was undertaken after decades of revolutions and the mob murder of President Vilbrun Guillaume Sam in the French legation prompted French troops to land. He asserted that the United States intervened to restore order and protect foreign interests under the Monroe Doctrine before European powers could take aggressive military action.

El Ogden standard-examiner informó sobre la declaración del secretario de Estado Colby en la que manifestó que Estados Unidos esperaba retirarse de Haití a medida que se acercaba a su fin la misión de estabilizar la república. Colby explicó que la intervención de 1915 se llevó a cabo luego de décadas de revoluciones y de que el asesinato tumultuario del presidente Vilbrun Guillaume Sam en la legación francesa provocara el desembarco de tropas francesas. Afirmó que Estados Unidos intervino para restablecer el orden y proteger los intereses extranjeros bajo la Doctrina Monroe antes de que las potencias europeas pudieran emprender acciones militares agresivas.

The work which the United States undertook to do is nearing completion and upon its completion this government hopes to withdraw and leave the administration of the island to the unaided efforts of the Haitien people. BEGINNING OF TROUBLE. "As is well known, for many years prior to 1916, Haiti was the scene of many revolutions and chronic disorders. The revolutions were sometimes accompanied by wholesale massacres and always by serious fatalities. Public authority had broken down and the people in the rural districts were frequently reduced to starvation. The culmination of these conditions came with the murder of President V. G. Sam, who was dragged by a mob from the French legation, where he had taken refuge. This grave affront to the rights of the French government resulted in the landing of French troops in Port Au Prince. At this time Haiti's European creditors were pressing for payment of the foreign debts and the fear was generally entertained that murder of the Haitian president would prompt an aggressive program on the part of some European government which would not only challenge the Monroe doctrine, but arraign the United States for a failure to perform duties which it had expressly avowed as a part of the Monroe doctrine. These duties have nowhere found more explicit statement than in the addresses and messages of the late President Roosevelt. PROMISES TO [illegible].
