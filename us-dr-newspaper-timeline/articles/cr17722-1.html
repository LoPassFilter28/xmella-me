The Evening Star reported that an OAS five-nation fact-finding committee departed Washington to conduct on-site investigations in Haiti, Cuba, the Dominican Republic, and Guatemala. The inquiry aimed to defuse regional crises ignited by Rafael Trujillo's war-power request and a reported conspiracy to assassinate Haitian President Dumarsais Estime. The investigating mission comprised delegates from the United States, Uruguay, Colombia, Bolivia, and Ecuador operating under the Rio Treaty.

El Evening Star informó que una comisión investigadora de la OEA integrada por cinco naciones partió de Washington para realizar indagaciones in situ en Haití, Cuba, la República Dominicana y Guatemala. Las pesquisas buscaban desactivar las crisis regionales encendidas por la solicitud de poderes de guerra de Rafael Trujillo y una supuesta conspiración para asesinar al presidente haitiano Dumarsais Estimé. La misión investigadora estaba compuesta por delegados de Estados Unidos, Uruguay, Colombia, Bolivia y Ecuador que operaban al amparo del Tratado de Río.

authorized inter-American pacts.” The five nation fact finding committee was scheduled to leave National Airport today for on-the spot investigations in Haiti, which has been feuding with the Dominican Republic; Cuba, which also has been involved in charges and counter-charges hurled across the Caribbean; the Dominican Republic, which is decidedly at odds with the other two countries, and finally, Guatemala. The current feuding stems from action in December of the Dominican congress granting Gen. Trujillo special powers to declare war on any country harboring plotters suspected of conspiring against the republic. The powers, requested by Gen. Trujillo, came after he charged that: new preparations were under way in Cuba for an invasion of his country. Cuba denied the allegations. Rio Treaty Invoked. On December 19, Haiti announced it had broken up a plot aimed at assassinating President Dumarsais Estime and other officials. The Haitians said the plot Included an invasion of Haiti by the Dominican Republic. As charges mounted between the two countries, the council of the Organization of American States invoked the Rio treaty, a Latin American mutual defense pact similar to the North Atlantic Pact, and decided to get to the bottom of the feuding in the Caribbean. The fact-finding group is made up of representatives from the United States, Uruguay, Colombia, Bolivia and Ecuador.
