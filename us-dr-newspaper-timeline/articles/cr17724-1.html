A feature describes Higuey as one of the principal inland cities of the Dominican Republic, a city of some 2,000 souls in the least settled eastern half of the country, center of a considerable primitive trade. It is the site of the most famous shrine on the island, the shrine of the Virgin of Alta Gracia, visited by devotees from every part of the country and housed in a church built in the old Spanish architecture.

Un reportaje describe Higüey como una de las principales ciudades del interior de la República Dominicana, una ciudad de unas 2.000 almas en la mitad oriental menos poblada del país, centro de un considerable comercio primitivo. Es el sitio del santuario más famoso de la isla, el santuario de la Virgen de la Altagracia, visitado por devotos de todas partes del país y alojado en una iglesia construida en la antigua arquitectura española.

[Give?] a little attention to his nearest friend. PEDDLER VISITS AN AMERICAN CAMP. American soldiers buying odds and ends from a travelling peddler near their training camp in France. LOADING UP FOR THE HUN. Sailors in port loading munitions aboard an American [illegible], preparatory to leaving for the war zone. Little-Known Dominican City. Higuey is one of the principal inland cities of the Dominican Republic. Like its native land, it is quaint, historic, isolated, immensely rich in natural resources, almost poor in actual fact, and practically unknown to the world at large. It lies in the eastern half of the republic, which is the least settled, the least developed and the least known. It is a city of some 2,000 souls, the center of a considerable primitive trade and the site of the most famous shrine on the island. This latter is the shrine of the Virgin of Alta Gracia, which is visited by devotees from every part of the country. The church which houses the shrine is built in the old Spanish architecture, the quaint and enduring style which has stamped its features on the buildings of a continent, from Patagonia to Colorado. The Situation. "In regard to the relative condition of the city and the county, the former takes the same stand as the Teuton." "How so?" "It wants peace with annexation."
