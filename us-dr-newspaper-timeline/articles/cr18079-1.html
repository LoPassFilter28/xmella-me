The paper reports that free-port shopping plans are spreading in the Caribbean, with Haiti and the Dominican Republic, in addition to Jamaica, among the islands most active in expanding them. A Pan American flight route from Miami is described as touching Port-au-Prince, Montego Bay and Kingston, Ciudad Trujillo and San Juan, making it possible to cover them all on a single air route.

El diario informa que los planes de compras en puertos francos se están extendiendo por el Caribe, con Haití y la República Dominicana, además de Jamaica, entre las islas más activas en expandirlos. Se describe una ruta aérea de Pan American desde Miami que toca Puerto Príncipe, Montego Bay y Kingston, Ciudad Trujillo y San Juan, lo que permite recorrerlas todas en una sola ruta aérea.

COAST-TO-COAST [illegible] CARIBBEAN ATLANTIC LINES [illegible] UNITED TRAVEL AGENCY [illegible] idea is spreading and, by next winter, it may be possible to cut a bargain-hunting swath by air from Miami to Puerto Rico. Islands most active with [free-port?] plans—in addition to Jamaica which is expanding them—are Haiti and the Dominican Republic. And it will be possible to cover them all on a single air route, dropping off for a day or a week along the bargain Riviera. The feasibility of such a trip was demonstrated recently when I took off from Miami at 9 a.m. on Pan American flight No. 431, touched down at Port au Prince, then Montego Bay and Kingston, Ciudad Trujillo and flew on to San Juan. Wherever the bargain islands appear, it is possible to plan even now on taking a Pan Am flight, for the line that pioneered the region now blankets the area and touches virtually every major point. Check on Prices If the trip had a motive beyond the actual enjoyment of islands that have a particular appeal to me, it was to resolve any doubts that free-port prices are what Jamaica says they are. I asked to be convinced, and was. Sam Levy, the well-traveled and debonair glad-hander who manages Kingston’s Myrtle Bank
