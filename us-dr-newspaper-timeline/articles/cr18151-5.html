A Washington article traces the money behind the Samana lease to a West India fund obtained by Thaddeus Stevens, saying Seward took $100,000 of it on his West Indies trip and General Babcock took the balance of $150,000, with which he leased the Bay of Samana. With that fund now exhausted, the article says, Senators friendly to the administration asked Representative Dawes to bring in a bill appropriating $150,000 for the payment now due to Fabens, but Dawes refused because there is no law for it, and Senator Sumner is quoted as saying the whole proceeding is without a shadow of law.

Un artículo de Washington atribuye el dinero del arrendamiento de Samaná a un fondo para las Indias Occidentales obtenido por Thaddeus Stevens, y dice que Seward se llevó 100.000 dólares de ese fondo en su viaje a las Indias Occidentales y que el general Babcock se llevó el resto, 150.000 dólares, con los que arrendó la bahía de Samaná. Agotado ya ese fondo, dice el artículo, senadores afines a la administración pidieron al representante Dawes que presentara un proyecto para asignar 150.000 dólares para el pago que ahora se debe a Fabens, pero Dawes se negó porque no existe ley que lo autorice, y se cita al senador Sumner diciendo que todo el procedimiento carece de la menor base legal.

appropriated, and soon after Seward, with his son and Admiral Porter, started for the West Indies. They took with them $100,000 out of Thad's West India fund, and spent the most of it. As is well known, this trip did not result in anything. When General Babcock went to St. Domingo he took the balance of this Seward -Stevens fund, 150,000, with which he leased the Bay of Samana. This fund is now exhausted, so that Envoy Fabens will not be able to collect his rent from that source. It is stated that a couple of Senators, friends of the administration, visited Mr. Dawes, chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations, and requested him to bring in a bill appropriating [illegible] for the payment now due. Dawes, who is a very careful fellow, and who does not seem to be aware of the existence of the "extra article" of the treaty, positively refused to comply with the request, for the reason, as he alleged, that there is no law for it. He told the Senators that he could only make appropriations which are authorized by law. Should Mr. Dawes adhere to his determination it is difficult to see where Mr. Fabens will get his rent. It might be taken out of the State Department Secret Service fund, but of late years this fund has been limited. Last year it was cut down from [illegible] to [illegible]. This year Judge Kelsey, who has charge of the Diplomatic and Consular appropriation bill, says he intends to keep it down to $100,000. This will not be sufficient for the payment of Mr. Fabens little bill. Senator Sumner
