The Richmond Virginian reported that war appeared imminent between Haiti and the Dominican Republic amid revolutions in both countries, following the assassination of Haiti's President Leconte and rebel capture of the border town Dajabon. Santo Domingo prepared to declare war after hundreds of Haitians allegedly assisted Dominican insurgents, massing 4,000 troops along the border. The region also faced severe agricultural drought and famine, while civil strife escalated concurrently in Nicaragua.

The Richmond Virginian informó que la guerra parecía inminente entre Haití y la República Dominicana en medio de revoluciones en ambos países, tras la muerte del presidente haitiano Leconte y la toma por los rebeldes de la localidad fronteriza de Dajabón. Santo Domingo se preparaba para declarar la guerra luego de que cientos de haitianos presuntamente auxiliaran a los insurgentes dominicanos, concentrando 4.000 soldados a lo largo de la frontera. La región padecía además una severa sequía agrícola y hambruna, mientras la guerra civil se intensificaba a la par en Nicaragua.

[Latin?] Minor Victories. In Nicaragua, General Mena's forces are reported to have gained a number of minor victories in the interior and to be marching on Managua. They have captured Leon and a portion of the railway leading to the center of the republic and are pillaging and burning estates as they march. Much fear is felt for Americans and their interests in the interior, and Minister [Weitzel?] has been asked to guard the United States property as much as he can with the 100 men from the scout cruiser Annapolis until the arrival of the 350 marines who are expected on Sunday. It is expected war will be declared between Haiti and Santo Domingo within a few hours, advices from there tonight indicating an [open?] breach between the countries, which diplomacy appears unable to heal. The situation is complicated by the fact that both republics are in the throes of revolutions, that in Haiti having manifested itself principally through the assassination of President [Leconte?], and that in Domingo showing through the capture of Dajabon, a frontier town. Because the Haitians aided the Dominican rebels to take the city, Santo Domingo probably will declare war on the negro republic. Four thousand Dominican troops are massed at Ouanaminthe, ready to invade Haiti. Threatened With Famine. In addition, all Central America is threatened with a famine, resulting from the drought and failure of the corn crop.
