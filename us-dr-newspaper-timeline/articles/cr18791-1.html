Independence, Recognition, and Samaná SchemesIndependencia, Reconocimiento y Proyectos de Samaná · 1844–1861

On February 27, 1844, Los Trinitarios proclaimed Dominican independence. American press coverage surged with reports on the new republic, repeated Haitian invasion threats, diplomatic recognition appeals, and southern schemes to secure Samaná Bay as a naval base.El 27 de febrero de 1844, Los Trinitarios proclamaron la independencia. La prensa estadounidense cubrió la nueva república, las invasiones haitianas, llamamientos de reconocimiento diplomático y proyectos para adquirir la Bahía de Samaná.

We learn from Capt. Hathaway, of schr. L. B. Lindsey, from St. Domingo City, up to the 12th ult., that a revolution had broken out there. The troops and the navy had declared in favor of Gen. Santa Anna for President. The President, Hernannes, met the troops on the outskirts of the city, and declared that he was still their commanding officer. Gen. Santa Anna instantly withdrew from the army, and went to his plantation. The troops then disbanded. Not being satisfied, they again requested Santa Anna to head them, which he did, and immediately marched for the city. Previous to his march, and by the orders of President Hernannes, the city of Don Carlos was burnt, to prevent the army from marching into it. It was supposed that the city would eventually fall into the hands of the troops, and that Santa Anna would be declared President of the Dominican Government.—N. Y. Jour. of Com.

Still Later.

Ey Dates from St. Domingo to June Ist state, that

the city is much excited concerning the action of the

President about sacrificing the country to the Hay

tiens; he and his generals having deserted the army

and many strong posts.

The Haytiens made for the city, but Santa Ana

drove them back. On his returning to the city, he

was refused an entrance by Jimines, who had made

several arrests of men and women, and also attempted

to arrest Mr. Elliot, the American Consul.

After some parley, Santa Ana determined to lay

siege and take the town, and his ordering a surrender

being refused, he was getting ready to commence op

erations. The Foreign Consuls have gone on board

vessels of war, and the women and children and

Congress had gone outside.
