PORT AU PRINCE, March 7.—The commission reached here the second of March, from Azua. All are in excellent health. They will wait here for Commissioner White, who is expected here by the 10th, bringing with him two scientific expeditions, under Professors Blake and Ward, that crossed before him. The Tennessee will then start immediately for home, touching at Santiago de Cuba and Key West for coal. The questions of debt and disputed boundary are the most important since the last dispatches. The Haytiens are bitterly opposed to annexation. The commissioners, however, were received civilly. Cabral's strength consists almost wholly of Haytien support. The information that the Tennessee had been reported missing first reached the party here. The statement of Gen. Banks that she was unseaworthy was received with great indignation, and denounced as heartlessly cruel. She is one of the strongest and best ships in the navy, with a select company of efficient officers, who expect now, unless delayed by the Nantasket, to reach home by the 25th of March, or, at the farthest, the 1st of April. A delay of one week from this cause is possible, and if the ship is not heard from for two weeks it should not cause anxiety. Mail from the commission leaves for New York to-day by the steamer Port au Prince.
