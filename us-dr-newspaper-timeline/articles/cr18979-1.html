The National Tribune reported that European nations lodged diplomatic protests against the United States-Dominican reciprocity treaty, demanding identical trade privileges under existing most-favored-nation pacts. The Dominican government defended the treaty, explaining that special concessions were necessary because the United States accounted for the majority of Dominican trade and provided essential markets for its sugar, coffee, and hides. Facing persistent European pressure, President Heureaux appealed to Washington for diplomatic backing.

El National Tribune informó que varias naciones europeas presentaron protestas diplomáticas contra el tratado de reciprocidad entre los Estados Unidos y la República Dominicana, exigiendo privilegios comerciales idénticos en virtud de los pactos existentes de nación más favorecida. El gobierno dominicano defendió el tratado explicando que las concesiones especiales eran necesarias debido a que Estados Unidos representaba la mayor parte del comercio dominicano y ofrecía mercados fundamentales para su azúcar, café y cueros. Ante la persistente presión europea, el presidente Heureaux apeló a Washington en busca de respaldo diplomático.

The story of the persecution of the "Black Republic" by the European powers mentioned — for "persecution" the State Department officials call it — is an interesting one, and dates back one year, when negotiations looking to the ratification of the reciprocity treaty between that country and the United States were concluded. The European powers mentioned above followed these negotiations with a jealous eye, as they were the only countries outside of the United States which had any considerable commerce with the island, and it was very manifest that the adoption of such a favorable treaty would give our country a practical monopoly of San Domingo's import trade. These Nations at first protested individually, and subsequently united in a joint protest, and argued that under their respective treaties with San Domingo each was to be placed on a similar footing to that enjoyed by any other Nation with whom the Republic might form a treaty. To this the Dominican Government replied that the only exception was in favor of the United States, and this was due to the fact that over one-half of their trade was with this country, and that their refusal to give the United States these advantages would shut off their market with us for coffee, sugar, and hides, their principal exportations.
