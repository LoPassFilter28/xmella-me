THE DOMINICAN TROUBLE.

The little Republic of Santo Domingo has been having a very lively time of it lately over the reciprocity treaty which it recently concluded with the United States. By the terms of that treaty certain American products are admitted into the Dominican ports free of duty, or at a greatly reduced rate, in consideration of the free admission of Dominican sugar into the United States. This arrangement was very distasteful to the European Powers, and especially to Germany, which has a large trade with the island. Germany at once demanded that her products should be admitted on the same terms as those of the United States, under the 'most favored nation' clause of her treaty with the Dominican Republic. This President Henreaux refused to do, on the ground that the concessions to the United States were not free, but were for a valuable consideration. Germany then threatened to retaliate by placing a heavy tariff upon their tobacco. This threat is the more effective, because Germany is the principal consumer of the tobacco raised on that island, and it added new embarrassment to President Henreaux's position, as the two Provinces in which tobacco is grown showed signs of discontent and mutterings of revolution were heard. There the matter stands at present. After all, this tempest in a teapot over the concessions granted by the Dominican Government to the United States is likely to result in a victory for the latter, as the Dominican Government has shown a firm front, and the United States is prepared to back it up.
