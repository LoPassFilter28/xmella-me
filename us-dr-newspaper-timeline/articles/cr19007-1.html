The Evening Star reported on regional political polarization pitting Cuba, Guatemala, and Costa Rica against the authoritarian regimes of Rafael Trujillo in the Dominican Republic and Anastasio Somoza in Nicaragua. While the United States maintained a policy of non-intervention, diplomats debated proposals by Panama's Ricardo J. Alfaro urging the Organization of American States to deny recognition to regimes seizing power through coups. Observers emphasized that enduring democratic stability in the Caribbean would require addressing deep-seated socioeconomic grievances.

Alfaro, quien instaba a la Organización de los Estados Americanos a negar el reconocimiento a los regímenes que tomaran el poder mediante golpes de Estado. Los observadores enfatizaron que una estabilidad democrática duradera en el Caribe requeriría abordar profundos reclamos socioeconómicos.

Calling themselves “champions of democracy,” Cuba, Guatemala and Costa Rica are reported to have aligned themselves against the “dictatorships” of Gen. Trujillo in the Dominican Republic and Gen. Somoza, Nicaragua's Minister of Defense. The United States, despite obvious interest in the Caribbean, has adopted a “hands off” policy in recent years. Within the State Department, however, there has been some support for a formula proposed by former President Ricardo J. Alfaro of Panama, who is his nation's U. N. representative. Dr. Alfaro has urged that the Organization of American States withhold recognition from any government which comes to power as a result of a military coup. Recognition would be given only after a free and fair election to determine public preference. The ambitions of political and military leaders, the intervention of Central American countries in the internal affairs of their neighbors and some Communist Influence are factors in the constant turmoil. But more pressing are the social and economic factors that have led to revolt. The United States has taken the lead in bettering conditions in the Caribbean. Some progress has been reported already. But observers see little hope for stability and for a real trial of democratic principles until social and economic measures succeed.
