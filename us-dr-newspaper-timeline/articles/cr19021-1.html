The Salt Lake Tribune discussed political reactions following the assassination of Dominican President Ramon Caceres, noting his past role in killing dictator Ulises Heureaux in 1899. Officials recalled Caceres's rise through cabinet and vice-presidential posts to the presidency in 1905, expressing concern that rival politicians might seize on his death to provoke unrest. The War Department noted that the assassination disrupted an exceptional four-year period of peace in a nation previously plagued by annual revolutions.

The Salt Lake Tribune analizó las reacciones políticas tras el magnicidio del presidente dominicano Ramón Cáceres, recordando su participación en la muerte del dictador Ulises Heureaux en 1899. Los funcionarios rememoraron el ascenso de Cáceres a través de carteras ministeriales y la vicepresidencia hasta alcanzar la presidencia en 1905, expresando inquietud de que políticos rivales aprovecharan su deceso para provocar alteraciones. El Departamento de Guerra advirtió que el magnicidio truncó un excepcional período de cuatro años de paz en una nación anteriormente asolada por revoluciones anuales.

Is now at his home near this city, he has arranged to leave tomorrow for Havana, whence he will cross Cuba to Guantanamo. If no commercial steamer Is at hand, he probably will be transported to San Domingo by one of the gunboats now at Guantanamo. President Caceres's stormy career is recalled by officials here who are familiar with the peculiar Dominican temperament. 'It is remembered that the president himself was the slayer of the redoubtable old dictator, Hereaux, in [illegible], which he alleged was in self-defense. It is likewise known that relatives of the former president always cherished a desire that the death of their kinsman be avenged. It is pointed out that, irrespective of the cause of the assassination, serious complications may arise if ambitious politicians seize upon the disturbed condition of the country to further their own ends. President Caceres's political career may be said to have been begun when he killed President Hereaux in [illegible]. He was a partisan of General Jiminez and the death of President Hereaux made possible the succession of Jiminez to the presidency. Caceres became minister of war in the Jiminez cabinet, subsequently was made vice president, under Morales and succeeded Morales as president in December, [illegible]. Peace for Four Years. It is pointed out at the war department that this is the first serious trouble that has occurred in Santo Domingo in four years, whereas the history of the country showed an average of at least one revolution per annum.
