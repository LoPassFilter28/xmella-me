Independence, Recognition, and Samaná SchemesIndependencia, Reconocimiento y Proyectos de Samaná · 1844–1861

On February 27, 1844, Los Trinitarios proclaimed Dominican independence. American press coverage surged with reports on the new republic, repeated Haitian invasion threats, diplomatic recognition appeals, and southern schemes to secure Samaná Bay as a naval base.El 27 de febrero de 1844, Los Trinitarios proclamaron la independencia. La prensa estadounidense cubrió la nueva república, las invasiones haitianas, llamamientos de reconocimiento diplomático y proyectos para adquirir la Bahía de Samaná.

The first of the series appeared in the Intelligencer of Thursday last; the second is given to-day.

THE REVOLUTION IN ST. DOMINGO.

A vessel arrived at New York from Turk’s Island brings some interesting details of the progress of the revolution in St. Domingo. The advices from Port-au-Platte are to the 17th ultimo. The revolution is said to be drawing rapidly to a close. With the exception of the city of St. Domingo, the whole of the Republic has declared in favor of the new order of things. Baez was still in the capital, but it was thought he could not hold out much longer. The revolutionary party have established a provisional government at Santiago, of which Gen. Jose Desiderio Valverde is President. The new government has issued a decree for the election of a convention to frame a new constitution. The revolutionists have also issued a decree opening all the ports of the Republic to the commerce of all nations. The revolutionary army is said to be well organized and well equipped. The people are said to be enthusiastic in favor of the revolution.
