The Evening Star reported that Haitian President Sténio Vincent estimated 8,000 Haitians were assassinated in the Dominican Republic, maintaining that the border dispute had already been settled by treaty two years prior. Vincent asserted that the Dominican government should have used standard diplomatic channels to repatriate undesirable foreigners, rather than resorting to violence without warning. While Vincent requested mediation from the United States, Cuba, and Mexico, President Trujillo expressed surprise at the appeal, pointing to existing bilateral accords.

El Evening Star informó que el presidente haitiano Sténio Vincent estimó en 8.000 los haitianos asesinados en la República Dominicana, sosteniendo que la disputa de límites ya había quedado resuelta mediante tratado hacía dos años. Vincent aseveró que el gobierno dominicano debió recurrir a los canales diplomáticos habituales para repatriar a los extranjeros indeseables en lugar de acudir a la violencia sin previo aviso. Mientras Vincent solicitaba la mediación de Estados Unidos, Cuba y México, el presidente Trujillo expresó sorpresa ante dicha apelación señalando la vigencia de los acuerdos bilaterales.

President Vincent asserted that the border question which once existed had been settled, largely at the initiative of the Haitian government two years ago when a treaty was signed by the two nations. He said there had been no border question since then. Usual Channels Open. "If the Dominican government wanted to expel foreigners they believed for one reason or another undesirable. they could have used the usual channels, the same as Cuba did when it repatriated thousands of Haitians from Cuba recently," President Vincent said. "I vow that this government has never received the slightest indications that Haitians, many of whom resided in the Dominican Republic for many -years, suddenly had become undesirable, until I heard of the assassinations." President Vincent's estimate of 8.000 slain was higher even than reports circulating on the streets of this sultry little mountain capital, where the populace is clamoring for strong measures in dealing with the Dominican Republic. The United States State Department on November 13 announced it had received a request from President Vincent for the United States to offer its good offices, together with Cuba and Mexico, toward seeking a solution of the Haitian-Dominican controversy. When President Rafael Leonidas Trujillo of the Dominican Republic learned of President Vincent's action, he expressed surprise that the United States, Cuba and Mexico had been asked to mediate. He mentioned a diplomatic accord which exists between the two governments.
