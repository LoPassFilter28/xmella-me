SAN DOMINGO SUGAR.

Judge Coxe Decides that it Cannot be Brought In Free of Duty.

Judge Coxe of the United States Court decided yesterday in favor of the Government the suit of Joseph Netherclift & Co., one of a number of similar suits, in which San Domingo sugar importers seek to recover customs duties aggregating many hundred thousand dollars which their counsel argue was levied in violation of treaty obligations and of the tariff act of 1883. The treaty made with San Domingo in 1867 said that "no higher or other duties shall be imposed on the importations into the United States of any articles, the growth, produce, or manufacture of San Domingo, than are or shall be payable in like articles, the growth of any foreign country."

The treaty with Hawaii of 1876 made sugar free, and it was claimed that San Domingo sugar ought also to be free. Judge Wallace decided otherwise in the Bartram case, now before the Supreme Court. Congress, in 1883, declared that nothing in the Tariff act should "in any way change or impair the force or effect of any treaty between the United States and any other Government," and it was under this act that the present suits were brought.

Judge Coxe says that at the date of the act Congress found the Hawaiian treaty in full operation, a treaty which made the ports of the United States free to a group of Pacific Ocean islands, upon the express condition, however, that the island ports should be open to a much larger number of articles of United States growth and manufacture. No other similar treaty was in operation. San Domingo sugar had paid duty for sixteen years. The aim of the lawmakers was to have unchanged and unimpaired the existing state of things.

"It is hardly supposable," says the Judge, "that it was their purpose to make sweeping changes in the law, to open our ports without advantage or consideration to the industry of many countries, to destroy a vast source of revenue, and to encourage fraud by making the Dominican republic the dumping ground for the sugar and molasses of the West Indies."

District Attorney Walker and Assistant District Attorney Thomas Greenwood appeared for the Government.
