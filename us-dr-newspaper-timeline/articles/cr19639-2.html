Writing from Porto Plata on September 6, 1869, the correspondent recalls that Columbus founded Isabella, the first European town in the New World, near here, and that only ruins remain where it stood. He describes a country whose trade has been almost annihilated, with Porto Plata and St. Domingo City as its principal outlets and small trade also at Azua and Samana, and reports an unusually large tobacco crop, estimated at 100,000 quintals for export, shipped almost exclusively to Hamburg, with talk of a railroad from Santiago to carry it to the sea.

Escribiendo desde Puerto Plata el 6 de septiembre de 1869, el corresponsal recuerda que Colón fundó cerca de aquí La Isabela, la primera ciudad europea del Nuevo Mundo, y que solo quedan ruinas donde estuvo. Describe un país cuyo comercio ha sido casi aniquilado, con Puerto Plata y la ciudad de Santo Domingo como sus principales salidas y un pequeño comercio también en Azua y Samaná, e informa de una cosecha de tabaco inusualmente grande, estimada en 100.000 quintales para la exportación, embarcada casi exclusivamente a Hamburgo, mientras se habla de un ferrocarril desde Santiago para llevarla al mar.

This port and St. Domingo City are the only two principal outlets to the island. A very small trade is accomplished also at the open ports of Azua and Samana. Porto Plata is now the only port on the northern side of the island, and the proceeds of the Custom House here are the main source of revenue to the government. The town itself is small and very uninviting; it has a small harbor well protected by reefs; a vessel anchors some distance from the shore: all cargo is put on board the vessels or taken off by lighters, which cannot themselves approach the shore within fifty yards, owing to the shallowness of the water on the beach which sweeps in front of the town. The shippers have a very novel mode of conveying cargo from shore to lighters and vice versa. This is done by means of carts drawn into the water a sufficient depth by bullocks which are manoeuvred by ropes passing through the cartilage of the nose. All this trouble, inconvenience and expense could be saved by a pier run out to a sufficient depth of water to allow vessels to lie alongside. Although Porto Plata is one of the early towns of the Island the Spaniards, upon being driven out of the country, completely destroyed it.
