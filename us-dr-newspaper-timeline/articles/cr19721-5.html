This special report, datelined St. Domingo, July 12, 1869, presents Samana Bay as an American Gibraltar of the Antilles and describes its history, geography and importance to the United States. It recounts a fight in the bay between the Dominican war schooners Capotilla and Alta Gracia and the craft Telegrafo, and then surveys the bay's entrance, headlands, channels and islands, and the Yuna river, which enters the bay at its western end and is one of the largest rivers on the island.

Este reportaje especial, fechado en Santo Domingo el 12 de julio de 1869, presenta la bahía de Samaná como un Gibraltar americano de las Antillas y describe su historia, su geografía y su importancia para los Estados Unidos. Relata un combate en la bahía entre las goletas de guerra dominicanas Capotilla y Alta Gracia y la embarcación Telégrafo, y luego describe la entrada de la bahía, sus cabos, canales e islas, y el río Yuna, que desemboca en la bahía en su extremo occidental y es uno de los ríos más grandes de la isla.

The Yuna, with a large population on its banks, would prove a valuable tributary of supplies by native craft to a population on the shores of Samana. The stream, with its branches, courses [illegible] the most fertile valleys in the world, and also [illegible] the mineral districts of Maimon and the pine forests of the Cibao. In 1807 the trade of the greater portion of the valley, which stretches from the Bay of Samana to Cotuy on the Yuna and Conception on the [illegible], averaging eight miles in width, was carried on by native canoes of about two tons burden. A railroad is in contemplation from the head of the bay to the towns of Conception and Santiago, which will go far to open the country. Towards the southward from the Yuna are the anchorages of Barranca Chica and Barranca Grande, both anchorages not now used. The Naranjo river here enters the Bay of Samana, passing under a hill of calcareous rock, through which it has forced an outlet. The central chain of mountains traversing the peninsula of Samana from west to east in its geographical formation consists principally of mountain limestone, schistose rocks, with veins of calcareous spar and sandstone. Quartz occurs frequently, and at the eastern end of the peninsula there is found an abundance of mica slates. A large quantity of fossil shells in great variety are met with.
