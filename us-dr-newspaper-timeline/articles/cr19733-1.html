The Evening Star reported that Dominican exiles attempted to seize consulates in New York and San Juan following news of Rafael Trujillo's assassination, while neighboring Haiti placed its military on full alert and sealed the border. The report reviewed Trujillo's rise to power and noted that none of the estimated 5,200 American residents in the Dominican Republic were harmed.

El Evening Star informó que exiliados dominicanos intentaron tomar consulados en Nueva York y San Juan tras conocerse la noticia del asesinato de Rafael Trujillo, mientras el vecino Haití puso a sus fuerzas armadas en alerta máxima y clausuró la frontera. El despacho repasó el ascenso al poder de Trujillo y señaló que ninguno de los aproximadamente 5200 residentes estadounidenses en la República Dominicana resultó herido.

Cuba’s Fidel Castro once charged him with financing an invasion to overthrow his regime but the two dictators were reported lately to have been drawn together by the United States opposition to both of them. For years Gen. Trujillo enjoyed United States favor be cause of the stability of his regime and later because of his strong anti-Communist stand. But in recent years the United States Government bowed to the wave of anti dictator sentiment in the Americas. 5,200 Americans in Area An estimated 5,200 American citizens live in the Dominican Republic. Even though the United States broke off diplomatic relations with the Trujillo regime, a consulate is maintained there and reports reaching Washington said no Americans had been harmed as a result of the assassination. At the news of the assassination, Dominican exiles tried to invade their country's consulates in New York and San Juan and seize control. Police subdued them. Neighboring Haiti, which shares the island of Hispaniola, put its army on full alert and closed its interior border. Relations have been bad between the two neighbors since Dominican forces in 1937 killed several thousand Haitians in an invasion. Gen. Trujillo died as he prophesied he would—still a dictator.
