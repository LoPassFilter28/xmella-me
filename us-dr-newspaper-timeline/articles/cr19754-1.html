On June 28, a ship arrived at Cap Haitien and took on board Pablo Villanueva, the former Minister of War and Marine under Dominican President Rousales, who carried a Dominican passport bound for St. Domingo City. Upon arriving at the capital, officials recognized Villanueva, but he was subsequently warned that he would be arrested if he came ashore, prompting him to seek the assistance of American Consul Mr. Paul Jones to explain his non-political intentions of visiting his family and collecting money. The following day, the first notification of hostilities by the Dominicans occurred when a cannon was brought down to the wharf.

El 28 de junio, un buque arribó a Cap-Haïtien y recibió a bordo a Pablo Villanueva, antiguo ministro de Guerra y Marina del presidente dominicano González, quien portaba un pasaporte dominicano con destino a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. Al recalar en la capital, los funcionarios identificaron a Villanueva, a quien seguidamente se advirtió de que sería apresado si saltaba a tierra, lo que le llevó a impetrar el auxilio del cónsul estadounidense, Sr. Paul Jones, a fin de explicar sus propósitos estrictamente pacíficos de ver a su familia y cobrar un dinero. Al día siguiente se produjo la primera muestra de hostilidad por parte de los dominicanos al emplazar un cañón en el muelle.
