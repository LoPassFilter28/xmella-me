A dispatch from Monte Cristi dated February 6 reports that the Clyde Line steamer New York arrived from New York, followed by the Dominican gunboat Presidente, and that when lighters carrying ammunition neared the steamer the gunboat fired on them and the revolutionists on shore replied with cannon and muskets. The failure to land the ammunition is called a serious blow to General Jiminez, for whom it was intended, and an attack on Santiago de los Caballeros was fixed for Sunday.

Un despacho de Monte Cristi fechado el 6 de febrero informa que el vapor New York de la Clyde Line llegó desde Nueva York, seguido por la cañonera dominicana Presidente, y que cuando las lanchas cargadas de municiones se acercaron al vapor, la cañonera disparó contra ellas y los revolucionarios en tierra respondieron con cañones y mosquetes. El fracaso en desembarcar las municiones se califica de golpe severo para el general Jiménez, a quien iban destinadas, y un ataque a Santiago de los Caballeros estaba fijado para el domingo.

Sullivan, world's pugilistic champion for many years, and a willing performer, whose achievements are well known, will be at the St. Louis Exposition, in charge of the diamond belt won by him. Mr. Sullivan, who is appearing at the Park Theatre this week, will be in Cuba before making the trip west. James F. Murray, manager of John L., has arranged for the Cuban tour. They will start the latter part of next month. Upon their return the diamond belt, which was presented to John L. in Boston Theatre in 1887, will be shown at the World's Fair. Since the case on 42d Street, New York, formerly owned by John L., changed hands, the diamond belt, valued at [illegible], has been in the hands of Jere Donahue of Philadelphia. John L. doesn't like the account of the Paddy Ryan fight, recently recalled in a Boston paper.
