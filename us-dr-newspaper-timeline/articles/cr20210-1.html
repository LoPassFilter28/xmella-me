The Atlanta Daily World reported that the Dominican Republic launched an educational campaign aimed at eradicating illiteracy among 830,000 citizens aged 14 to 60. Minister of Education Dr. Joaquín Balaguer announced that 18,000 schools had been established, aiming for a ratio of one teacher for every 20 students to eliminate illiteracy within two years.

El Atlanta Daily World informó que la República Dominicana puso en marcha una campaña educativa destinada a erradicar el analfabetismo entre 830 000 ciudadanos de 14 a 60 años de edad. El secretario de Educación, Dr. Joaquín Balaguer, anunció el establecimiento de 18 000 escuelas, con la meta de alcanzar una proporción de un maestro por cada 20 alumnos para eliminar el analfabetismo en el plazo de dos años.

able improvement has been noted Dominican Republic Wages Drive Against illiteracy TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic —(ANP)—A drive to bring a measure of education to this country’s 830,000 illiterate citizens effects everybody between the ages of 14 and 60. Any person in that age bracket who is unable to prove ability to read and write is subject to punishment for not reporting to one of the 18,000 schools which have been set up. Dr. Joaquin Balaguer, minister of education, has said that in two more years “we will have no more illiteracy in the Dominican Republic.”’ A government census in 1950 disclosed that there were 830,537 illiterates. A major problem was the recruiting of teachers; a majority of the educators employed in the present campaign are those who are working toward full-time teachers certificates and who volunteered their services to gain experiences. The Education Department has announced a goal of providing one teacher every 20 students. In addition to the professional teacher candidate, a large number of voluntary non-professional teachers are helping in the classrooms. Classes are conducted five nights a week, and some students beginning their second year have advanced from a state of complete illiteracy to a point where they [can?] read and write on simple themes.
