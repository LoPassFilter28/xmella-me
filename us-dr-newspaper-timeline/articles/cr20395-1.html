The Daily Record carried a commentary discussing official Dominican promotional literature claiming that national gambling revenues were utilized for cultural advancement and international goodwill. The author mused whether other nations reliant on American assistance could emulate the Dominican model of financing public needs through casinos, sports, and tourist entertainment. The piece highlighted the Dominican Republic's advertised status as free of foreign debt alongside its programs for crop diversification and industrial development.

El Daily Record publicó un comentario que analizaba material promocional oficial dominicano en el que se sostenía que los ingresos nacionales del juego se empleaban para el progreso cultural y la buena voluntad internacional. El autor cavilaba sobre si otras naciones dependientes de la ayuda estadounidense podrían emular el modelo dominicano de financiar necesidades públicas mediante casinos, deportes y atracciones turísticas. El artículo resaltó la promocionada condición de la República Dominicana de estar libre de deuda externa, junto con sus programas de diversificación de cultivos y desarrollo industrial.

I forgot to save the envelope in which this circular came and therefore I cannot tell you whether it used a paid-for stamp or came diplomatic mail, free of charge. I get a lot of that kind of mail, all free, the object of which is always to educate me to love some foreign country. But if anyone wants to send some mail about loving America, a paid-for stamp is essential. It is a fine point, this business of who pays to have mail delivered advocating love. The Dominican circular tells what the gambling money is used for. It is for culture. The profits of gambling or part of them are used to teach Americans to love the Dominican Republic, which is apparently free of foreign debt. Maybe gambling makes up for the foreign debt and this type of fin ancing might be tried by some of our debtors and those who take our charity. The Ford Foundation which is busy spending money anywhere at all, so long as it is unprofitable, might take this one [on?] as a project. Perhaps they could encourage all the countries that have become ac customed to mooching on the Amer ican tax-payer to get free of foreign debt by slot machines, casinos, gay nightclubs, cock-fights, Sunday horse-racing, baseball and boxing. For it is those sports which the Dominican Republic advertises as available to all who want them, along with "planned crop diversification. industrial expansion and other progressive economic and human relations developments.” It must be admitted that this is a great program for the little Re public. which compares favorably
