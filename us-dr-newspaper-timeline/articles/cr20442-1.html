The Ventnor news published a first-hand travel narrative praising the stabilization of Santo Domingo under the U.S. Marine Corps, highlighting administrative order and revenue collection. The author described motoring two hundred miles from Monte Cristi through Santiago to Santo Domingo City over modern mountain highways recently constructed by American military engineers. The account commended the marines for modernizing infrastructure, civilizing the region, and establishing public safety.

El Ventnor news publicó una crónica de viaje testimonial que elogiaba la estabilización de Santo Domingo bajo el Cuerpo de Marines de Estados Unidos, destacando el orden administrativo y la recaudación fiscal. El autor describió su trayecto en automóvil de doscientas millas desde Monte Cristi pasando por Santiago hasta la ciudad de Santo Domingo a través de modernas carreteras de montaña recién construidas por ingenieros militares estadounidenses. El relato alabó a los marines por modernizar la infraestructura, pacificar la región y establecer la seguridad pública.

When Mrs. Palmer and I arrived at Monte Cristi we found Colonel Cutts, U. S. M. C., with his motor car and an orderly waiting for us and at 6.20 P. M. we started to motor the two hundred miles through the mountains over an excellent road to Santo Domingo City, across the island to [the?] South coast on Caribbean Sea. The first sixty miles lay through a desert country parallel to the North shore, then we got into the mountains where there was plenty of rain and luxuriant tropical vegetation. At eighty miles we arrived at Santiago, the second largest city at about 9 P. M. where we were entertained with a delicious dinner by Major and Mrs. Watson, of the Marine Corps at their home. After dinner we then started out to motor the remaining 120 miles through the steepest and wildest part of the mountains over this wonderful road that has been recently built by American engineers. All through the night we passed natives clad in white clothes on their little donkeys making their way to market to the Capitol Santo Domingo City. JOHN K. KAHLE, President At about 3 A. M. we arrived at Colonel Cutts' residence and found Mrs. Cutts up and waiting for us.
