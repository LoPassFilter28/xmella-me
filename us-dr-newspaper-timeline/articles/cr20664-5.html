This narrative tells how a Puerto Plata senator chosen as president, on trial for one year after the previous president was killed, announced after some ten months that he intended to hold onto the job, sparking a general uprising against him as a usurper and tyrant. As his forces prepared to shell Puerto Plata unless the city surrendered by two o'clock, and Washington had sent two contingents of Marines to Puerto Plata and Monte Cristi to stand by, the American commander warned him that the first gun fired would result in the landing of Marines and a general cleaning out of the belligerents.

Este relato cuenta cómo un senador de Puerto Plata escogido como presidente, a prueba por un año tras la muerte del presidente anterior, anunció después de unos diez meses que pretendía quedarse en el puesto, lo que desató un levantamiento general contra él como usurpador y tirano. Mientras sus fuerzas se preparaban para bombardear Puerto Plata si la ciudad no se rendía a las dos en punto, y Washington había enviado dos contingentes de marines a Puerto Plata y Monte Cristi para estar listos, el comandante americano le advirtió que el primer cañonazo disparado provocaría el desembarco de los marines y una limpieza general de los beligerantes.

he knew that he wouldn't stand as much chance as a wax cat in a certain warm climate, he nevertheless hesitated over agreeing to the terms offered by the representative of Uncle Sam — these being unconditional surrender. Just there fate took a hand. The guns which were mounted to cover the city of Puerto Plata had not been fired for some time. The soldiers were aching to start something and they could not understand why the President did not order the war to begin. One man after imbibing more than an ordinary man's share of rum began to cry. With tears coursing down his jetty cheeks he begged to know why this and that was so and why there had been no opportunity for himself and his mates to show their bravery, their skill with the big guns. This thought became an obsession with the lachrymose one. "By Cristyfer Colon!" he ejaculated, "must I, whose forebears landed in this paradise of a land with the great Discoverer stand by and listen to the orders of the Yankee dogs from another land? Let it not be so!" A few minutes later this man had "begun the war." Under cover of darkness he sidled up to one of the largest guns, loaded the [illegible] with an explosive [illegible], and bing! a house or two in the city had been unroofed and confusion came. Hardly had the echo of this shot died away when the war ship in the harbor hopped into action. A salvo of six pounders was sent back into the hills and then to quote one of the marines who took part in the [fracas?], "there was hell to pay."
