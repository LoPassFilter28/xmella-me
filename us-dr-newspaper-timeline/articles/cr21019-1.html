Evening Star. Washington, D.C., September 9, 1913. Page 12. View the originalVer el original

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt: it will be made known to rebel sympathizers that the Wilson administration is without sympathy with any government not founded upon law and orderly succession. Officials here to-day expressed confidence in the administrative ability and the integrity of President Bordas. They pointed out that one element of strength in his favor is the support of Desiderio Arias, who has been a leading spirit in many previous revolutions. Bordas is an ad interim provisional president, and is serving out that portion of the regular term of the presidency which was unexpired when Archbishop Nouel, provisional president succeeding President Victoria, gave up the office. Governor Cespedes of Puerto Plata province has a strong political following among the people of that province, and likewise commands large financial resources. He has built and equipped a private arsenal at Puerto Plata. It is, therefore, taken for granted that he is prepared to put up a strong fight against the attack being made by the gunboats in the harbor. Former Provisional President Vasquez, whose fortunes are cast with Cespedes in the present attempt to overthrow the regular government, commands equally great influence and resources in the rich farming countries in the interior. As it would be almost impossible to send an army overland to put down this uprising, the authorities in Santo Domingo City are depending altogether on the successful bombardment of Puerto Plata.The Star publica comentarios de Washington sobre la revuelta de Puerto Plata: se hará saber a los simpatizantes rebeldes que la administración Wilson no simpatiza con ningún gobierno que no se funde en la ley y la sucesión ordenada. Ha construido y equipado un arsenal privado en Puerto Plata. Por tanto, se da por sentado que está preparado para dar una fuerte pelea contra el ataque que hacen las cañoneras en el puerto. El expresidente provisional Vásquez, cuya suerte está echada con Céspedes en el intento actual de derrocar al gobierno regular, dispone de una influencia y unos recursos igualmente grandes en los ricos países agrícolas del interior. Como sería casi imposible enviar un ejército por tierra para sofocar este alzamiento, las autoridades de la ciudad de Santo Domingo dependen por completo del éxito del bombardeo de Puerto Plata.
