The Springfield Weekly Republican reported that Horace G. Knowles, former U.S. minister to the Dominican Republic and currently a Dominican consul, strongly condemned U.S. interventions in Nicaragua, Haiti, and Santo Domingo during a speech in Northampton. Knowles told the Hampshire County Progressive Club that these military actions constituted unauthorized acts of war undertaken by the executive government without congressional approval.

El Springfield Weekly Republican informó que Horace G. Knowles, exministro de Estados Unidos ante la República Dominicana y en ese momento cónsul dominicano, condenó enérgicamente las intervenciones estadounidenses en Nicaragua, Haití y Santo Domingo durante un discurso pronunciado en Northampton. Knowles manifestó ante el Club Progresista del Condado de Hampshire que estas acciones militares constituían actos de guerra no autorizados y emprendidos por el poder ejecutivo sin el consentimiento del Congreso.

Northampton, Nov. 20 — The policy of the government at Washington in practicing intervention on the countries of Nicaragua, Haiti, and Santo Domingo was severely criticised by Horace G. Knowles, former American minister to the Dominican republic and now consul for that country, speaking under the auspices of the Hampshire County Progressive club at Carnegie hall tonight. Mr. Knowles said that the policies and acts presented were not those of the American people, nor even of the American Congress, but of the American government, which notwithstanding the fact that only Congress can declare war, had committed acts of war in these republics by the landing of armed forces. Northampton, Nov. 21 — The two buildings occupied by J. E. Lambie Co. of Main street, and the stock of dry goods, suits and coats, were destroyed by fire this morning at 4, and there were heavy losses on the furniture stock of J. H. Quinn, and the block occupied by Quinn, owned by J. H. O'Dea, and smaller losses on the clothing stocks of M. J. Keating, on one side of the Lambie store, and Coffey Burke in the other side. The present owners of Lambie Co. are George F. Edwards and James P. Fairchild. The losses, as insurance men figure them, probably are not more than $250,000, but replacement values at present high costs of building construction might add another $50,000 to the estimate of total loss.
