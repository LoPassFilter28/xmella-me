The Semi-Weekly Messenger. Wilmington, N.C., August 8, 1899. Page 5. View the originalVer el original

The Messenger prints the closing part of the Havana interview with General Jiminez: the present government, he had said, could not possibly last beyond the 15th of this month. 'I did hear,' he continued, 'that Heureaux was sending men to Cuba to kill me, but I knew nothing whatever regarding the plot to kill him. I had no connection and have no connection with Ramon Caceres, who did the shooting. I do not believe that the United States will interfere with my government in San Domingo, which will be a government peaceful and helpful to all, for the United States did not interfere with the government of Heureaux, who robbed and murdered for fourteen years.' General Jiminez admits that he has often been to the United States incognito, but says he has not had interviews with official persons; he considers that there is no need of promoting expeditions to land in San Domingo, as there already are a thousand men waiting for him, a force which he deems quite sufficient for his purposes; referring to the future relations of San Domingo with the United States, he expressed a desire to be on friendly terms with Washington, and said he believed he could count upon American sympathy; Gomez he referred to as esteemed by Dominicans, but not sought for as president; and the people of San Domingo, he declares, are impetuous, and are urging him to come to them at once, but for a time he prefers delay, as 'each day is worth a battle won.'The Messenger publica la parte final de la entrevista de La Habana con el general Jiminez: el gobierno actual, había dicho, no podría durar en modo alguno más allá del 15 de este mes. 'Sí oí', continuó, 'que Heureaux enviaba hombres a Cuba para matarme, pero no supe absolutamente nada del complot para matarlo a él. No tuve ni tengo conexión alguna con Ramón Cáceres, quien hizo el disparo. No creo que los Estados Unidos interfieran con mi gobierno en San Domingo, que será un gobierno pacífico y útil para todos, porque los Estados Unidos no interfirieron con el gobierno de Heureaux, quien robó y asesinó durante catorce años'. El general Jiminez admite que ha estado a menudo en los Estados Unidos de incógnito, pero dice que no ha tenido entrevistas con personas oficiales; considera que no hay necesidad de promover expediciones para desembarcar en San Domingo, pues ya hay mil hombres esperándolo, una fuerza que considera bastante suficiente para sus propósitos; refiriéndose a las futuras relaciones de San Domingo con los Estados Unidos, expresó su deseo de mantener relaciones amistosas con Washington, y dijo que creía poder contar con la simpatía americana; de Gómez dijo que es estimado por los dominicanos, pero no buscado como presidente; y la gente de San Domingo, declara, es impetuosa y le insta a acudir a ellos de inmediato, pero por un tiempo prefiere esperar, pues 'cada día vale una batalla ganada'.
