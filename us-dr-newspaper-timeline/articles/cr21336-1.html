The paper says its latest intelligence from Haiti leaves scarcely room for doubt that Soulouque has been compelled to abdicate and is probably on his way to a foreign land for safety. It gives a biographical sketch: about sixty-eight years of age, born a slave on Mr. Vallet's property and freed by him, entered the army under General Dessalines at the French evacuation, rose to colonel by the fall of President Boyer, and was admitted into the secrets of the political conspiracies that followed from 1843 to 1847.

El periódico dice que su información más reciente de Haití apenas deja lugar a dudas de que Soulouque se ha visto obligado a abdicar y probablemente está en camino a una tierra extranjera en busca de seguridad. Ofrece un esbozo biográfico: unos sesenta y ocho años de edad, nacido esclavo en la propiedad del Sr. Vallet y liberado por él, entró en el ejército bajo el general Dessalines en la evacuación francesa, ascendió a coronel para la caída del presidente Boyer y fue admitido en los secretos de las conspiraciones políticas que siguieron de 1843 a 1847.

HAYTI AND ITS DEPOSED EMPEROR.

Our latest intelligence from Hayti leaves

scarcely room for a doubt that his Royal High

ness, Faustin Soulouque, the black Republican

Emperor of Hayti, who for the past seven

years has held imperial sway over what was

the Haytien Republic, has been compelled

to abdicate, and that he is probably, ere this,

on his way to some foreign clime, there to seek

the safety and repose which the civil dissen

sions in the land of 1 is birth forbid him longer,

to enjoy.

Soulouque, who is of pure African descent,

is at the present time about sixty-eight years of

age. He was born a slave upon the Island,

on the property of Mr. Vallet, who gave him

his liberty, and, at the period of the evacuation

of the Island by the French, he entered the

army under General Dessalines. From step to

step, he rapidly rose to the rank of Colonel,

and he held that rank in the army at the fall

of President Boyer. From his remarkable

taciturnity—a quality which, among whites, as

well as blacks, is considered a certain indica

tion of superior wisdom and discretion—he was

admitted into all the secrets of the various po

litical conspiracies which followed each other

in such rapid succession, from 1843 to 1847.

Having, during this time, been elevated to the

rank of General under Ricker, he owed his
