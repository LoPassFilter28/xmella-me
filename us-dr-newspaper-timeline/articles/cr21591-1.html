The Daily Kennebec Journal reported that former Customs Receiver Walter W. Vick testified that Minister James M. Sullivan improperly attempted to negotiate an unauthorized $500,000 bank loan for the Dominican government. Vick presented a letter from Sullivan to Dominican banks proposing the loan without the sanction of the State Department or the Dominican Congress. The proposed borrowing provoked protests from Dominican legislative leaders until an outbreak of revolution halted the negotiations.

The Daily Kennebec Journal informó que el exreceptor de aduanas Walter W. Vick testificó que el ministro James M. Sullivan intentó indebidamente concertar un préstamo bancario no autorizado de 500.000 dólares para el gobierno dominicano. Vick exhibió una carta dirigida por Sullivan a instituciones bancarias dominicanas en la que planteaba el empréstito sin la aprobación del Departamento de Estado ni del Congreso dominicano. El proyectado crédito suscitó reclamos de los dirigentes legislativos dominicanos hasta que el estallido de la revolución frenó las negociaciones.

That $500,000 Loan Mr. Vick submitted evidence designed to show that Minister Sullivan had endeavored without the knowledge of the government at Washington and without the approval of the Dominican Congress to pave the way for the negotiation of a loan of $500,000 to be made to the Dominican government through banks in the Republic. He asserted that W. W. Smith, secretary of the legation, first told him of the proposed loan, and that later a Dominican banker showed him a letter from Minister Sullivan, which was sent to the three banks in San Domingo City seeking to open negotiations for the loan. A copy of this letter was placed in the record. Protest Organized. President [Bares?] of the Dominican chamber of deputies, and a deputy named Moscosco organized a protest against the proposed loan, Mr. Vick said, and the recent revolution brought the entire matter to a close. The tramp steamship Riverton sailed from Portland late Saturday with 200,000 bushels of grain. The Zeeland of the White Star-Dominion Line sailed early Sunday morning and will take several hundred Canadian recruits for the British Army from Halifax, her first port of call. Her cargo consisted of 88,450 bushels of wheat, 4,000 barrels of apples and the balance flour, oatmeal and provisions. There seems to be a great demand in Great Britain for provisions, and the steamships sailing from Portland are taking but little general freight.
