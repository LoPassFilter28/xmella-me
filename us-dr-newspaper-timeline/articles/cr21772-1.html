The Birmingham Age-Herald reported testimony by Walter W. Vick alleging that Minister James M. Sullivan secretly solicited a $500,000 loan from Dominican banks, falsely claiming U.S. approval to raise the republic's debt limit. Vick produced correspondence indicating Sullivan offered Dominican bonds as security without authorization from Washington or the Dominican Congress. Vick also defended his own record against personal countercharges raised during the official inquiry into Sullivan's diplomatic fitness.

The Birmingham Age-Herald informó sobre el testimonio de Walter W. Vick alegando que el ministro James M. Sullivan gestionó en secreto un préstamo de 500.000 dólares ante bancos dominicanos, aduciendo falsamente la venia de los EE. UU. para elevar el tope de endeudamiento de la república. Vick presentó correspondencia según la cual Sullivan ofreció bonos dominicanos como garantía sin el consentimiento de Washington ni del Congreso dominicano. Vick defendió además su propia gestión frente a imputaciones personales formuladas en la investigación oficial sobre la idoneidad diplomática de Sullivan.

fitness of James M. Sullivan to be minister to the Dominican republic. His testimony today was offered to show that Sullivan, without the knowledge of the government at Washington and without the approval of the Dominican congress, had endeavored to pave the way for negotiations of a $500,000 loan for the Dominican government through banks in the republic. Vick asserted that W. W. Smith, secretary of legation, first told him of the proposed loan, and that later a Dominican banker showed him a letter from Sullivan which was sent to the three banks in San Domingo city seeking to open negotiations for the loan. A copy of this letter was placed in the record. It set forth that Sullivan had been instructed by his government to arrange the loan, that Dominican bonds would be issued to repay it and that the United States would agree to an extension of the legal limit of the Dominican debt to cover the amount. The recent revolution brought the entire matter to a close, Vick said. Vick denied charges as to his personal conduct made by H. S. Dickey and supported by an affidavit of two Dominicans. He discussed at length various charges made against him in the course of the hearings and denies that a mining scheme with which he was once connected had “swindled the public out of $6,000,000. Ohio River Falling. Cincinnati, February 8.—The Ohio river was falling here today and railroads prepared to resume their service in the Union station tomorrow.
