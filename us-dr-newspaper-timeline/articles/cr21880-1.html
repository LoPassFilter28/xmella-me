The Evening Star reported that Cuban magistrate Evelio Tabio ordered the release of nearly 1,200 participants in the aborted anti-Trujillo invasion expedition, holding only its purported leaders in custody. A captured diary published by the newspaper Hoy detailed training at Holguin and miserable conditions on Cayo Confites, where troops endured internal infighting, accidental drownings, and failed logistics aboard vessels including the Aurora, Berta, and Angelita. The collapse of the operation culminated in mass surrenders at Guinchos Cay and Antilla.

El Evening Star informó que el magistrado judicial cubano Evelio Tabío dispuso la libertad de casi 1.200 integrantes de la frustrada expedición armada antitrujillista, reteniendo en presidio únicamente a los sindicados como cabecillas principales. Un diario de bitácora ocupado y divulgado por el diario Hoy detalló los adiestramientos castrenses en Holguín y las precarias penalidades sufridas en Cayo Confites, donde los reclutas padecieron motines internos, ahogamientos accidentales y severos descalabros de abasto a bordo de los barcos Aurora, Berta y Angelita. El fracaso del complot culminó con rendiciones multitudinarias en Cayo Guinchos y la bahía de Antilla.

In the meantime, Magistrate Evelio Tabio of the supreme tribunal of the Criminal Court announced that only the purported leaders of the expeditionary force would be kept in custody after today. Nearly 1,200 others now being held will be released. About 725 members of the expedition who surrendered Tuesday at the port of Antilla arrived here last night by train to join 360 others who were picked up earlier from Guinchos Cay, a sandspit off the Cuban coast. Mr. Tabio did not disclose the names of those he considered to be the leaders, nor their number. Hoy withheld the name of the trooper who wrote the diary. The diary's account: The volunteer enlisted July 20 for the campaign against President Rafael Trujillo, was trained at Holquin and then transferred with other revolutionaries from a coastal point to Confites Key. The diary said the men were moved aboard the ships Aurora and Berta and a Dominican schooner registered as the Angelita, which the expeditionary force captured near Confites Keys. Two volunteers drowned while being disembarked. One was a Cuban, the other a Venezuelan. One man was killed and some others were wounded in fighting among themselves. The troops became discontented over delays in action against the Dominican Republic. On August 16, which was D-day, a ship expected to arrive with war material and food did not make port.
