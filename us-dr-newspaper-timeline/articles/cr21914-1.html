The Waterbury Democrat reported on a high-profile political libel trial in Port-au-Prince concerning financial corruption by Haiti's former finance minister in state telegraph transactions and scrip speculation. The paper also noted diplomatic friction between Haiti and France resulting from Haitian reluctance to pay compensation for damages suffered by a French citizen.

El Waterbury Democrat informó sobre un sonado juicio político por difamación en Puerto Príncipe relativo a la corrupción financiera del exministro de Hacienda de Haití en operaciones telegráficas estatales y especulación con certificados de deuda. El periódico señaló asimismo las fricciones diplomáticas entre Haití y Francia derivadas de la renuencia haitiana a pagar una compensación por los perjuicios sufridos por un ciudadano francés.

the crowd. Outside Port-au-Prince three regiments were held under arms and in readiness to march on the town. The plea set up by M. Frederick was practically that his allegations were true in substance and in fact. During the trial some extraordinary evidence was elicited in regard to Fouchard's financial transactions. One witness admitted that he was the go-between who had conducted the negotiations between Fouchard and M. d'Aubigny, a prominent merchant in Port-au-Prince, on the one hand, and M. Charmant on the other, whereby the inland telegraph system was purchased for $37,600 and sold to the government of Haiti for $100,000. Other evidence was called to prove that Fouchard and his son-in-law had privately bought up government scrip given to employees as salary, and which was discounted at the bank at 60 to 70 per cent, and had then, as finance minister, issued orders to the bank to pay the scrip at its face value. After two days' trial, during which every effort possible was made to bribe or intimidate the jury, a unanimous verdict was given in favor of Mr. Frederick, thus practically substantiating the charges made against Fouchard. Haiti and France. Manigat has not yet been received by President Faure, and it is rumored in Port-au-Prince that diplomatic relations between Haiti and France will be broken off owing to the refusal of the Haitien government to comply with the French demand for compensation for damages done to a French subject in Haiti, as already reported in these dispatches.
