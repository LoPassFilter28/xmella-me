An individual interviewed by the newspaper disputed a prior New York Times report regarding Mr. Lamar, asserting that Lamar received a fair trial and competent legal representation after killing a man in self-defense in San Domingo. Additionally, the source stated that the island's primary need is capital, noting that New York capitalists are currently considering propositions to establish a local bank and build a railroad to develop the interior.

Un interlocutor entrevistado por el rotativo rebatió una crónica anterior de The New York Times relativa al Sr. Lamar, sosteniendo que este disfrutó de un juicio con todas las garantías y de una adecuada defensa letrada tras ultimar a un individuo en legítima defensa en Santo Domingo. Aseguró por añadidura la fuente que la necesidad primordial de la isla radica en allegar capitales, manifestando que financieros neoyorquinos evalúan a la sazón propuestas para instituir una entidad bancaria local y construir un ferrocarril de fomento hacia las comarcas del interior.

I noticed an article in The Times a few days ago about a Mr. Lamar who claimed that he had been outrageously treated by the San Domingo authorities in consequence of his killing a man in self-defence. Now I was there at the time and am familiar with the case. Mr. Lamar was fairly treated and has little to complain of in the way in which he was treated. I myself secured to him the best counsel that was to be had and it was mainly through the efforts of his lawyer, I am told, that the jury acquitted him. I took pains to see that he should have a fair trial before I came away. This is a sample of the stories that get into print and do great injustice to the San Domingo people. "The great need of the island at present is capital. Just think of a Government being compelled to pay from [illegible] to 5 per cent to a local syndicate a month for money, as is done down there. This will not, however, be the case much longer. I carry a proposition to the San Domingo Government from a company of New-York capitalists in regard to the establishment of a bank there which, if accepted, will place the financial affairs of the country on an entirely new basis. Another company is considering the project of building a railroad right through the island to open up the interior portions. There is only an outer rim of San Domingo settled as yet.
