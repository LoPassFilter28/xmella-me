The Evening Star reported that the Costa Rican government appealed to Nicaragua and the Dominican Republic for military assistance against insurgent forces led by Jose Figueres. Rebel troops sabotaged electrical installations at Brazil and launched surprise assaults at Quepos, threatening the advance toward Cartago. The armed conflict arose following the disputed presidential election of Otilio Ulate, which the government-controlled Congress had annulled in favor of Rafael Calderon Guardia.

El Evening Star informó que el gobierno constitucional de Costa Rica recabó socorro de armas a Nicaragua y a la República Dominicana con el propósito de combatir a las columnas rebeldes acaudilladas por José Figueres. Los destacamentos insurrectos sabotearon redes de energía en la localidad de Brasil y acometieron asaltos sorpresivos en Quepos, poniendo en jaque el avance hacia Cartago. La contienda intestina estalló a raíz de los controvertidos sufragios presidenciales que ungieron a Otilio Ulate, los cuales fueron anulados por el Congreso oficialista para beneficiar al candidato Rafael Calderón Guardia.

Costa Rica Asks Aid From Two Republics By the Associated Press SAN JOSE, Costa Rica, Mar. 27.— The Costa Rican government has renewed appeals to Nicaragua and the Dominican Republic for help in fighting the Figueres revolutionary forces, reliable sources said tonight, What the response would be no one could say. Nicaraguan troops have been fighting on the government's side and some are still here, although an order for their return home has been issued. Rebel troops, led by Jose Figueres, slipped through government lines this morning and dynamited power plant installations at Brazil on the Pacific Coast. They also began a surprise attack against government troops at Quepos north of Dominical which the government has been trying to hold since last week. To the south Figueres soldiers moved down the side of Cerro de la Muerte Mountain toward Cartago and apparently were ready to storm that ancient capital of Costa Rica. The fighting which began 17 days ago is the outgrowth of last month's turbulent and disputed presidential election in which Otilio Ulate was elected. On March 1, the government-controlled Congress annulled the election, upholding fraud charges brought by Rafael Calderon Guardia, government-backed candidate. [illegible] [illegible]
