The Herald reported on the armed revolt against President Heureaux led by General Juan Isidro Jimenes, detailing initial reports that rebel columns were advancing on Puerto Plata and Santiago de los Caballeros. The article noted Jimenes's background as a popular merchant and son of a former president who had organized the uprising from abroad to challenge Heureaux's despotic rule. However, later dispatches from Puerto Plata confirmed that the revolutionary landing force had been repulsed at Monte Cristi.

The Herald informó sobre la revuelta armada contra el presidente Heureaux encabezada por el general Juan Isidro Jimenes, detallando los primeros informes que indicaban que columnas rebeldes avanzaban hacia Puerto Plata y Santiago de los Caballeros. El artículo destacaba la trayectoria de Jimenes como un popular comerciante e hijo de un expresidente que había organizado el levantamiento desde el extranjero para desafiar el régimen despótico de Heureaux. Sin embargo, despachos posteriores desde Puerto Plata confirmaron que la fuerza de desembarco revolucionaria había sido repelida en Monte Cristi.

Morales and Villanueva are marching on Puerto Plata, while another detachment is moving against Santiago de los Caballeros on the Yaques river, 100 miles east of this place. Gen. Jiminez is a merchant, who has been in business at Monte Cristo, at Puerto Plata and Hamburg. In his youth he served in the army, and for many years he has been very popular in Dominican political circles. President Heurreaux, according to a common saying among the Dominicans, has killed off all the prominent men on whom he could lay hands and who might be rivals. The president's hatred for Gen. Jiminez is especially marked, but the latter has kept out of his reach, residing chiefly in Paris. About two months ago Gen. Jiminez left Cape Haytien, ostensibly for Europe, via Port Au Prince, but, it is said, he did not go any further than New York, and it is believed that while in that city he perfected the arrangements for a revolution in Santo Domingo, over which, if successful, the great majority of the Dominicans, for the time being at least, will rejoice. Gen. Jiminez is 55 years of age and of a mild temperament. He has refused several times to be a candidate for the presidency. His father was formerly president of the republic. Revolutionary Repulse.
