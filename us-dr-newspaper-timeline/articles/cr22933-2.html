Receiver Pulliam, discussing political conditions in the republic, reports that General Ramon Caceres, who succeeded to the presidency in January 1906 after the withdrawal and deportation of President Morales, has been elected for the new constitutional term of six years and will be formally inaugurated on July 1. Pulliam describes Caceres as a man of the people from the most prosperous agricultural district of the republic, well liked and respected by all classes, and says that now that the government's financial affairs have been adjusted and placed on a stable basis, his election is a guarantee against the internal disorders that have frequently found lodgment in Dominican history.

El receptor Pulliam, al hablar de las condiciones políticas de la república, informa que el general Ramón Cáceres, que sucedió en la presidencia en enero de 1906 tras la retirada y deportación del presidente Morales, ha sido elegido para el nuevo período constitucional de seis años y será investido formalmente el 1 de julio. Pulliam describe a Cáceres como un hombre del pueblo procedente del distrito agrícola más próspero de la república, muy querido y respetado por todas las clases, y dice que ahora que los asuntos financieros del gobierno han sido ajustados y puestos sobre una base estable, su elección es una garantía contra los desórdenes internos que con frecuencia han encontrado cabida en la historia dominicana.

With President Caceres's inauguration an accomplished fact, it is believed by the best informed that the last vestige of formidable revolutionary tendencies in the republic effectually will be discouraged. "The Dominican republic starts upon a new governmental era with the inauguration of President Caceres. By provisions of the new Constitution, adopted in February, the term of the President was extended from four to six years. This fact alone will operate to remove even remote excuses for disturbances, formerly always contemporaneous with presidential elections. The legislative branch of the government has been remodeled to embrace two deliberative bodies — a Senate, consisting of twelve members, one for each district, and the House of Deputies, or Congressmen, each district electing two. Heretofore there was no Senate." Financial Exhibit. In his financial statement, Receiver Pulliam shows a condition far more satisfactory than had been expected, a statement which reflects distinct credit upon the American receivership. Mr. Pulliam writes: "The new budget for the coming fiscal year to commence July 1, 1908, estimates the aggregate Federal income at $3,948,300, which is the largest in the history of the republic.
