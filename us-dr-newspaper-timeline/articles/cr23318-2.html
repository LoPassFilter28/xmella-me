Writing from San Domingo on September 15, the Herald's correspondent reports that the assassination of President Heureaux removed the hand that had for eighteen years preserved a bloody peace, and that Vice President Figuereo's government weakened as he hesitated to dismiss the Heureaux ministry. Late in August a revolutionary proclamation was issued at San Francisco de Macoris in the interest of Jimenez, telegraph communication was cut, and the correspondent reports that Macoris, Moca and La Vega went over to a revolution headed by Horacio Vasquez, Ramon Caceres and Samuel Mayo.

Escribiendo desde Santo Domingo el 15 de septiembre, el corresponsal del Herald informa que el asesinato del presidente Heureaux quitó la mano que durante dieciocho años había mantenido una paz sangrienta, y que el gobierno del vicepresidente Figuereo se debilitó al vacilar en destituir al ministerio de Heureaux. A fines de agosto se emitió una proclama revolucionaria en San Francisco de Macorís en favor de Jiménez, se cortó la comunicación telegráfica, y el corresponsal informa que Macorís, Moca y La Vega se pasaron a una revolución encabezada por Horacio Vásquez, Ramón Cáceres y Samuel Mayo.

Vain attempts were made by the French Cable Company to reopen communication. Within two days, however, the Governor of Concepcion de la Vega, who had escaped with a small retinue, arrived on horseback and brought the news that set everybody by the ears. Macoris had been the cradle of the revolution, and even the government troops there had gone over without firing a shot. Moca, where Heureaux was assassinated, quickly fell in line, and Le Vega was preparing to follow, when the Governor concluded that the capital, under the circumstances, would be a safer residence. The government promptly tried to suppress this news. The newspapers were forbidden to print it, and any person discussing it in the street was promptly arrested. Nevertheless, within three hours after General Garcia, the Governor of Le Vega, arrived the fact that a revolution headed by Horacio Vasquez, Ramon Caceres and Samuel Mayo, was well under way in the interior, was known in every "casa" between San Carlos and the sea wall. In this knowledge lay the doom of the government. The country was ripe for revolution. It needed but to be known that the flag was raised for thousands to flock to it.
