With very few exceptions the white and better class of the people were in sympathy with the Moa or revolutionary party. The latter part of last month and the beginning of this signalized a series of successes for the Government troops. While matters were at the worst for the [illegible] a number of the young men in the city of Santo Domingo began securing arms and making arrangements for the seizing of the city. A boy who was employed as messenger and who was thoroughly in the confidence of the conspirators was arrested by the authorities, who had some information or suspicion as to what was in progress. He was [illegible] taken to the fort. A file of soldiers was told off and he was informed that he had the alternative of confessing all that he knew or being shot in half an hour. He confessed, and that evening the Government authorities arrested about sixty young men who were implicated. They had three places of meeting at different points in the city, and from the circumstances of their arrest and the condition in which those places were found, it is believed that their rising was intended to have taken place within a very short time. Those who were arrested are all of them sons of the best people in the country. It is supposed that some slight punishment will be inflicted, or that they may be imprisoned for a short time, but the latter is doubtful because the [illegible] prisons are already crowded [illegible] with political prisoners.
