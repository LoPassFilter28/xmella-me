The Tribune recounted a travelogue by visiting members of the Pan American Medical Association touring historic and medical sites across Ciudad Trujillo, including the Alcazar de Colon and the Cathedral of Santa Maria la Menor. The physicians met with Dominican medical colleagues at a modern hospital facility before being received at the presidential palace by President Hector Trujillo. In accepting a diploma of honor for national public health achievements, Trujillo declared that state social welfare and health initiatives were essential to safeguarding civilization against communism.

The Tribune relató una crónica de viaje de miembros visitantes de la Asociación Médica Panamericana que recorrieron sitios históricos y médicos en Ciudad Trujillo, incluidos el Alcázar de Colón y la Catedral de Santa María la Menor. Los médicos se reunieron con colegas dominicanos en unas modernas instalaciones hospitalarias antes de ser recibidos en el palacio presidencial por el presidente Héctor Trujillo. Al aceptar un diploma de honor por los logros nacionales en salud pública, Trujillo declaró que las iniciativas estatales de bienestar social y salud eran esenciales para proteger la civilización contra el comunismo.

From the ruins of the first hospital, we drove to the very latest modern hos pital where a meeting was held with physicians of the cruise and the physicians of the Dominican Republic. The place was in a long patio between two parts of the hospital. After the meet ing, we went on to the Pres ident’s palace. On the way, along the banks of the Ozama River, we passed the Tower of Homage, the oldest stone fortress in the Americas; it is now a pri son. Farther on, we saw Alcazar de Colon, a castle built in 1512 by Don Diego in honor of his father, Christopher Columbus. Next, we visited the Basil ica of Santa Maria la Menor, the oldest cathedral in the world where the remains of Columbus are supposed to be buried. Some of the jew els of Queen Isabella are on display there. By this time we reached the palace. In a Spanish address, President Trujillo welcomed the members of the Pan American Medical Association, we received copies of his speech in Spanish and English. After thanking the group for the Diploma of Honor presented to him by the group for his work toward the health of the population, he spoke of the policy of his government, and, I quote: The health and social welfare policy of the Dominican government has been inspired in the convic tion that the fate of Amer ican civilization depends largely upon the success of the measures taken in that field.
