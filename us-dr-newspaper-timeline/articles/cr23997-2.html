The Evening Star reported on statements by Dominican Minister Dr. Jesús María Troncoso, who expressed confidence that anti-submarine measures would soon reopen sea lanes for Dominican sugar shipments to Britain while expanding food exports to Puerto Rico. Troncoso asserted that Dominican economic warfare was its main Allied war contribution, defended strong Dominican leaders by comparing them to President Andrew Jackson, and denied that the country was run by a dictatorship. He also praised the agricultural settlement for European refugees established following the 1938 Évian Conference.

El Evening Star reseñó declaraciones del ministro dominicano Dr. Jesús María Troncoso, quien exteriorizó su convicción de que las operaciones antisubmarinas restablecerían pronto las rutas marítimas para despachar azúcar dominicano hacia Gran Bretaña, incrementando a la par el aprovisionamiento de comestibles para Puerto Rico. Troncoso afirmó que la cooperación económica de su país constituía el aporte bélico medular a la causa aliada, justificó la firmeza de los caudillos patrios equiparándolos con el presidente Andrew Jackson y negó taxativamente que rigiera una dictadura en su patria. Ponderó de igual modo la colonia agrícola de refugiados europeos radicada en el país a consecuencia de la Conferencia de Évian de 1938.

He is sure that by the end of next year the Nazi U-boats will have been sufficiently checked to allow for the shipment of the mounting sugar supply to its British markets. Many other exports are sent to the United States. Senor Troncoso's government is developing the exportation of food to nearby Puerto Rico, which is facing an acute food shortage. Though the Republic has an army and navy, the economic warfare they wage is their most effective contribution toward an Allied victory. Unlike her friendly sister republic, Haiti, which is basically French, the tongue and customs of the Dominican Republic are from Spain. The government is modeled after that of the United States and the people enjoy freedom of press and speech. Dr. Troncoso explains that the leaders of the republic often have been strong men who bore out the strength of their convictions with action, but who, nevertheless, were far from being dictators. He compares these leaders to the American President Andrew Jackson, whose opinions in his time brought down cries of dictator. Dr. Troncoso praises the success of the refugee settlement in his country, an idea originated by the republic at the Evian Conference in France in 1939. The agricultural settlement was tested on a scientific basis, with a group of refugees from Europe selected for youth and health and character.
