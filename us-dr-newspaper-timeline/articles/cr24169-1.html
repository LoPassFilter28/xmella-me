The Evening Star reported that Dominican Ambassador Anselmo Copello addressed the Political Study Club at the Washington Club in commemoration of Dominican independence. Copello highlighted Santo Domingo's historic significance as Columbus's first New World landing site and described plans by American nations to construct a monumental Columbus memorial lighthouse. He added that the remains of Columbus, currently resting in the Cathedral of Santo Domingo, would eventually be transferred to the lighthouse.

El Evening Star informó que el embajador dominicano Anselmo Copello disertó ante el Political Study Club en el Washington Club con motivo de las efemérides patrias dominicanas. Copello ensalzó el valor histórico primordial de Santo Domingo como el primer asiento colonial erigido por Colón en las Américas y delineó el proyecto suscrito por las naciones del continente para levantar el monumental Faro a Colón. Subrayó que las cenizas del Gran Almirante, depositadas en la Catedral de Santo Domingo, serían trasladadas solemnemente a dicho mausoleo una vez finalizado.

[illegible]pendence of the Dominican Republic, which will be celebrated next Sunday was commemorated in an address by Ambassador Anselmo Copello before members of the Political Study Club yesterday at the Washington Club. Reviewing highlights in the history of the little nation, which he represents in Washington, the Ambassador recalled that Santo Domingo was the first land in the Western Hemisphere on which Christopher Columbus landed. He told of a "great Columbus memorial lighthouse that the American nations will build as a tribute to the memory of the discoverer." which, he declared, "will be one of the greatest wonders of the world." Columbus' remains, which now rest in the Cathedral of Santo Domingo, will be moved to the lighthouse, he added. The program included a presentation of a group of songs by Mrs. Helen Turley, contralto, and piano selections by Mrs. Dorothy Guion. The artists were introduced by Mrs. Edgar Briant Meritt, chairman of music. War Bonds and stamps were sold at a booth directed by Mrs. John J. Darby, national defense chairman. Those at the tea table included Mrs. Herbert Griffith, Mrs. John Cation and Mrs. Leo Kasehagen. The names of Mrs. Harry A.
