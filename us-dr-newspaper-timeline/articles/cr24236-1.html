The Chronicle-Star Combined with the Moss Point Advertiser reported on political and economic tensions in Haiti as the government awaited an Export-Import Bank loan from the United States. Local factions criticized long-standing American financial controls dating to the 1922 occupation, while Argentina sought commercial influence by offering loans and exchanging ambassadors. The account highlighted geopolitical dynamics involving Haitian President Dumarsais Estime and Dominican President Rafael Trujillo on Hispaniola.

El Chronicle-Star Combined with the Moss Point Advertiser reseñó las tensiones políticas y financieras que sacudían a Haití mientras el ejecutivo esperaba el desembolso de un préstamo del Export-Import Bank de Estados Unidos. Diversos sectores locales impugnaban las ataduras fiscales estadounidenses que pervivían desde la ocupación de 1922, mientras la Argentina procuraba ganar ascendiente mercantil brindando créditos e intercambiando embajadores. La crónica puntualizó los equilibrios de poder que vinculaban en La Española al mandatario haitiano Dumarsais Estimé y al presidente dominicano Rafael Trujillo.

An aggressive Communist front which could not muster enough strength to take command after the revolution, is supporting President Dumarsais Estime because he is “a Democrat and a man of the people." United States delay in offering a loan is but one of the arguments upon which the Communists base denunciation of the Good Neighbor policy as “the most cruel policy" in the world. In 1922. during the American occupation, Haiti borrowed 22 million dollars from private United States sources at 6 per cent interest. Communists, as well as men in the government, assert that although Haiti has repaid 38 million dollars in principal and interest, she still owes seven million dollars on that account. Haiti also owes the U. S more than seven million on later loans totaling ten million. Argentina Moving In President Estime says Argentina and Haiti are to exchange ambassadors shortly. That indicates that President Juan Domingo Peron of Argentina is interested. Other well-informed individuals say that Argentina has offered a loan in furtherance of commercial policy of that nation. An Argentine cruiser visited Port au Prince, the Haitian capital, recently. Even the sailors who thronged shops, their hands filled with U. S. dollar bills, proved good ambassadors.
