A correspondent of the steamer Tybee, writing from Santo Domingo City on June 22, 1869, describes five lively days loading an assorted cargo of exports and visiting the capital, which he calls the oldest city in the western hemisphere, its site selected by Christopher Columbus. He describes the old walls and fortifications, the chapel built by Columbus and his sundial, the first cathedral with its gilded high altar and silver furnishings, and the many ancient Spanish buildings still in use, in a city whose population is now about half the 20,000 it once held.

Un corresponsal del vapor Tybee, que escribe desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo el 22 de junio de 1869, describe cinco días animados cargando un surtido cargamento de exportaciones y visitando la capital, a la que llama la ciudad más antigua del hemisferio occidental, cuyo sitio fue escogido por Cristóbal Colón. The Oldest City in America—Mementos of Columbus—Present Aspect—Climate and Diseases—An Interview with President Baez and His Cabinet—Views and Feelings Regarding the Future. St. Domingo, June 22, 1869. The past five days have been unusually lively, both on board the steamer and in the city, on the steamer in taking in an assorted cargo of articles of export from the island, on shore in visiting the scenes of early historic associations dating from the remote days of Christopher Columbus, receiving the attentions of newly found and attentive friends and otherwise agreeably and profitably passing the time. The hotel of the town, kept by a Frenchman, was opened for the accommodation of the passengers of the Tybee, and was a sort of headquarters from which business negotiations and pleasure excursions were directed. The hotel had been closed some time, but upon the arrival of the steamer among the first persons on board was the gentlemanly proprietor inquiring as to the number of guests he might expect in order that he make the necessary preparations. As a merited compliment it may be mentioned that the first dinner, although much abused by the persistant apologies of the proprietor himself, was well gotten up.
