The Carrizozo news traced Dominican history from its 1844 independence from Haiti under Pedro Santana through twelve years of alternating rule between Santana and Buenaventura Baez. Santana annexed the nation to Spain in 1861, but a popular revolt restored Dominican independence in 1865. The article noted that President Grant subsequently proposed annexation of the republic to the United States, which Dominican citizens approved but the U.S. Senate rejected on a tie vote.

El Carrizozo news trazó la trayectoria dominicana desde la gesta emancipadora de 1844 frente a Haití conducida por Pedro Santana, prosiguiendo con doce años de alternancia en el poder entre Santana y Buenaventura Báez. Santana reincorporó el país a España en 1861, mas una gesta popular restableció la independencia patria en 1865. El rotativo apuntaba que el presidente Grant propuso más tarde la anexión a los Estados Unidos, aprobada por los comicios dominicanos pero desestimada por el Senado de los EE. UU. debido a una votación empatada.

Up to [illegible] the history of what is now the Dominican Republic is the history of Haiti. In that year the Spanish population, under the leadership of Don Pedro Santana, proclaimed its independence and set up a republic. Santana became the first president. Five years later Baez became president and for twelve years he and Santana alternated in the presidency. In [illegible] Santana proclaimed the annexation of the Dominican Republic to Spain. The United States was entering upon the Civil war and had neither the time nor the strength for the prompt and effectual enforcement of the Monroe doctrine. One result was that Napoleon III, counting on the success of the Confederacy, constituted the Republic of Mexico an empire and put Archduke Maximilian of Austria on the throne. Two years after the Dominican Republic was annexed to Spain its people revolted and restored the republic. In [illegible] Spain acknowledged its independence. Incidentally President Grant desired to annex the republic to the United States. A treaty for this purpose was ratified by the Dominican people, but was rejected by the American senate by a tie vote. At the close of the Civil war in [illegible] the United States had both the time and the strength to enforce the Monroe doctrine in Mexico and the French troops withdrew, leaving Maximilian to his fate — execution in [illegible] at the hands of the revolutionists.
