Evening Star. Washington, D.C., January 15, 1915. Page 13. View the originalVer el original

The Evening Star prints an item: Farnham said he had met Samuel N. Jarvis and that Jarvis had proposed that his interests and those of the National City Bank be pooled in negotiating the loan. The witness added he had declined to consider the proposition. The minister and Michelena reported that the government favored their proposition in preference to propositions of other bankers. Farnham said he had met Walker W. Vick, instigator of the present investigation, shortly after Vick had been appointed receiver general. Vick had not in any way made representations to him either for or against the Banco Nacional or any other institution, he said. He had sought an interview with Vick, he continued, in connection with the question of the depositary of the customs receipts, out of which the bank's notes were being paid. 'We wanted to know where the funds were going,' said the witness. Testimony in which it was asserted that changes made by the Washington administration in the personnel of this government's official colony in the Dominican Republic were due to the influence of interests seeking to exploit the finances of the country was heard yesterday. Secretary Bryan was accused of lending ear in appointing Sullivan to the Samuel M. Jarvis coterie of New York financiers who controlled the Banco Nacional at Santo Domingo.The Evening Star publica una nota: Farnham dijo que había conocido a Samuel N. Vick, instigador de la presente investigación, poco después de que Vick había sido nombrado recaudador general. Vick no le había hecho en modo alguno representaciones ni a favor ni en contra del Banco Nacional ni de ninguna otra institución, dijo. Había buscado una entrevista con Vick, continuó, en relación con la cuestión del depositario de los ingresos de aduanas, de los cuales se estaban pagando los pagarés del banco. «Queríamos saber adónde iban los fondos», dijo el testigo. Se oyó ayer testimonio en el que se afirmó que los cambios hechos por la administración de Washington en el personal de la colonia oficial de este gobierno en la República Dominicana se debían a la influencia de intereses que buscaban explotar las finanzas del país. El secretario Bryan fue acusado de prestar oído a la camarilla de Samuel M. Jarvis de financistas de Nueva York que controlaban el Banco Nacional en Santo Domingo al nombrar a Sullivan.
