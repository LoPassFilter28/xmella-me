News from Santo Domingo reports the failure of the Samana Bay Company as complete, with its principal losers named and the loss put at perhaps a quarter of a million dollars. The Provisional Government has declared the Samana Bay Convention null and void, after a proposed treaty with Haiti, said to include cession of disputed frontier territory and annulment of the convention, and the company's commissioners have protested and demanded arbitration.

Noticias de Santo Domingo informan que el fracaso de la Compañía de la Bahía de Samaná es completo, nombran a sus principales perdedores y calculan la pérdida en quizá un cuarto de millón de dólares. El Gobierno Provisional ha declarado nula y sin efecto la Convención de la Bahía de Samaná, tras un tratado propuesto con Haití que, según se dice, incluía la cesión del territorio fronterizo en disputa y la anulación de la convención, y los comisionados de la compañía han protestado y exigido un arbitraje.

The Government of San Domingo Annuls the Lease—Failure of the Company Conceded—The Loss About $350,000. Special Dispatch to The Chicago Tribune. New York, April [illegible].—The failure of the Samana Bay Company is recognized as complete, though the amount sacrificed is far less than generally supposed. This result has been anticipated for months past, and few others than the sanguine men who went into the speculation at the start, when it was supposed, Grant and Babcock were largely interested, and meant to force the Senate to confirm the treaty, have lost anything by the bursting of the bubble. Many had supposed, from the representations of the Company, that millions of dollars were invested in it. Probably a quarter of a million will cover the whole loss, and possibly not so much was ever invested in the absurd project. THE PRINCIPAL LOSERS by the failure of the Company are Paul N. Stafford, Fabens, and [illegible] L. [illegible] Barlow, and a few minor projectors of the scheme.
