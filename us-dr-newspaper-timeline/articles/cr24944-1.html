The Springfield Weekly Republican argued that foreign capitalists and commercial planters in the Dominican Republic actively encouraged political unrest to terminate indigenous rule. It cited State Department admissions that American firms had supplied money and weapons to opposing Dominican factions while simultaneously petitioning Washington for naval protection. The editorial noted that President Morales, having secured power through United States naval support, signed the customs receivership agreement to align with foreign property interests.

El Springfield Weekly Republican sostuvo que los capitalistas y hacendados comerciales extranjeros en la República Dominicana fomentaban activamente la inestabilidad política para poner fin al gobierno autóctono. Citó admisiones del Departamento de Estado respecto a que firmas estadounidenses habían suministrado dinero y armas a facciones dominicanas rivales mientras pedían simultáneamente protección naval a Washington. El editorial señaló que el presidente Morales, habiendo asegurado el poder mediante el apoyo naval estadounidense, firmó el acuerdo de administración aduanera para alinearse con los intereses de los propietarios extranjeros.

The Dominican planters and all the foreign capitalists concerned in the republic have had a strong economic object in bringing the native rule to a close. There is, indeed, reason for thinking that the frequent revolutions of late years have been secretly encouraged, and even fomented in part, by the foreign interests.. For proof of this, we may quote what appeared on March 16 last in the Washington Star, the paragraph having all the appearance of being inspired by the state department. “It is admitted at the ‘state department," said the Star, “that ‘some representations have been made in a ‘forcible way to American firms and indi ‘viduals having an interest in San Domingo ‘that they have small ground for complaint ‘of revolutionary disturbances affecting •their interests unfavorably so long as they themselves are in the. habit of furnishing •arms, ammunition and money to both sides •engaged in this fratricidal strife.” It is necessary to point out, also, that President Morales, now the head of the Dominican government, who has voluntarily, it appears, signed the agreement for the American receivership, represents the property interests of the republic more than his late opponents did. This conclusion is not far-fetched. Morales has been seeking, it might almost be said, American protection ever since his government was firmly established through the armed intervention of the United States navy in the last revolution.
