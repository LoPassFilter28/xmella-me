ST. DOMINGO.—Capt. Ogler, of the schooner Cabot, from the city of St: Domingo, the capital of the Dominican republic, April 14th, reports affairs there tranquil. Apprehension was entertained of further invasion from the blacks of the West End, which keeps the Dominicans in a constant attitude of defence and uncertainty, preventing productive labor, and destroying commerce.

The United States steamer Vixen, Lieutenant Commanding Ward, arrived at the city of St. Domingo on the 28th of March—all well. The Vixen was to sail for Washington in a few days with the United States chargé, Mr. Green. The Vixen takes home a magnificent altar, highly wrought with carved work, in solid mahogany—a present, through Mr. Green, from the Archbishop of St. Domingo to the Archbishop of Baltimore. It is in size 44 by 26 feet, weighs ten tons, and belonged to the ruined chapel of the Jesuit college. The Vixen has also a superb painting, a Magdalen, by “Murillo”—a present, from an ancient but decayed Castilian family, to the surgeon of the vessel, “Dr. Bell,” in token of gratitude for the numerous delicate and skilful professional operations performed among all classes of inhabitants who have come to him from various parts of the island.—New York Journal of Commerce.
