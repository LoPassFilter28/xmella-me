The Washington daily news published an editorial criticizing American assertions of non-imperialism, pointing to U.S. control across the Caribbean and Pacific, including customs administration in the Dominican Republic and marine occupation in Haiti. The piece derided official claims of purely benevolent motives while exercising regional dominance. It argued that Washington's treatment of its dependencies, particularly the curtailment of self-rule in the Philippines under General Leonard Wood, contradicted its anti-imperialist rhetoric.

El Washington daily news publicó un editorial en el que criticaba las afirmaciones estadounidenses de no ejercer imperialismo, señalando el control de Estados Unidos en el Caribe y el Pacífico, incluida la administración de aduanas en la República Dominicana y la ocupación de la infantería de marina en Haití. El artículo ridiculizó los argumentos oficiales de motivaciones puramente benévolas mientras se ejercía el dominio regional. Sostuvo que el trato que Washington daba a sus dependencias, en particular el menoscabo de la autonomía en Filipinas bajo el general Leonard Wood, contradecía su retórica antiimperialista.

"You say you are not imperialistic," it jeers. And it sticks its tongue in its cheek and looks at the Philippines and Porto Rico and Hawaii and Guam and the Virgin Islands; at our virtual protectorate over Cuba and the Republic of Panama; at our control of the customs of the Dominican Republic and Haiti, and our occupation of the latter country by marines; at our hegemony, as they call it, over Central and much of South America. And reviewing it all, it asks insinuatingly: "What are you up to in all those places?" "Just protecting the interests of the unfortunate peoples," we say. "Oh!" says the world, winking prodigiously! WELL, a showdown is approaching. The Philippines may well be the test case. And the case of the Philippines is this: Congress officially promised the Filipinos their freedom as soon as they proved they could govern themselves. Gov. Gen. Harrison from 1916 to 1921 gave them a chance, and they did a pretty good job. And President Wilson in 1920 told Congress the time had about come for America to make good her promise. Then bang! Something happened. It was Gen. Leonard Wood. He arrived in the islands and as governor general, instead of giving the Filipinos more freedom, took what they had away. Now the islanders have put the whole thing squarely up to President Harding, meantime refusing to co-operate with Gen. Wood. They have gone out on strike.
