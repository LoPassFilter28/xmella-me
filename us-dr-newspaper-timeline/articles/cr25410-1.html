The Douglas Daily Dispatch reported that Secretary of State Henry L. Stimson contemplated an early withdrawal of U.S. Marines from Haiti, dependent on the efficiency of the local national guard. Stimson stated that Washington aimed to establish a system similar to the Dominican Republic, where security was entrusted to native forces while an American customs receiver managed public revenue. He noted that financial adviser Sidney de la Rue and the guard would safeguard American bondholders.

El Douglas Daily Dispatch informó que el secretario de Estado Henry L. Stimson contemplaba una pronta retirada de los infantes de marina estadounidenses de Haití, supeditada a la eficacia de la guardia nacional local. Stimson declaró que el objetivo de Washington era implantar un sistema similar al de la República Dominicana, donde la seguridad quedaba encomendada a fuerzas nacionales mientras un receptor de aduanas estadounidense administraba los ingresos fiscales. Indicó que el asesor financiero Sidney de la Rue y la guardia protegerían los intereses de los tenedores de bonos estadounidenses.

In discussing marine forces in Haiti, the secretary said those contingents may be withdrawn sooner than was anticipated providing the Haitian national guard proves sufficiently stable. The secretary said the United States was aiming in general to create a status for Haiti similar to that in the Dominican republic, where the protection was turned over to the national guard with finances still in the hands of an American receiver of customs. The United States is obligated under a treaty, the secretary pointed out, to protect American holders of Haitian bonds, a large majority of whom already have been paid off. It was possible, Stimson said, the state department would determine that the presence of Sidney de la Rue, American financial adviser in Haiti, and protection by national guard was a sufficient guarantee and that this would permit a quicker withdrawal of marine forces. Withdrawal of the 178 marines from Nicaragua makes the total left there about 800. Under an agreement between the secretary and President Moncada of Nicaragua for the withdrawal by increasing the efficiency of the Nicaraguan national guard, about 500 were to be left.
