HONORS TO PRESIDENT BAEZ. Baez started out upon the journey, from which he has just returned, accompanied only by Gautier, Delmonto, Curiel, and a colonel of cavalry. He went unarmed — a thing said to be before unknown in his history. A few days ago, the Commandante Macoris became intoxicated, abused his office and his people, was removed, and undertook to rebel against his successor. He was captured and put in prison. This was said to be a new revolution, and was reported to Gen. Camenero as such. The General, with 60 men, went to Macoris, but the man had been captured before he reached there, and was lodged in the old castle in this city. Gen. Camenero has just told me that the only foundation for the story was, that a mob was raised to punish the man for abusing his wife. The German war steamer Gazelle, Capt. Avent, was here some 10 days ago, and remained several days. Thirty-five of the officers came up into the city, and had a jolly time. The captain gave a dinner on board of his ship to foreign representatives, and prominent merchants of the place. He gave a toast to the Dominican Government — the Governor being present — and remarked that he hoped they would be able to sustain themselves, and the principles they had avowed before the world. He had hoped to set forth his views more fully to the Government, but as the President was absent he would reserve further remarks till the return. He was going to Hayti, and would return in a few days, when he hoped to see the President, and more fully make known the object of his visit. T
