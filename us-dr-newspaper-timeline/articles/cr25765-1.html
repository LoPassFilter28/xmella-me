SUMNER AND GRANT.

Maj. Poore's Story of their Disagreement.

Washington, Oct. 26.—Maj. Ben. Perley Poore, who was Senator Sumner’s guest at the time of his estrangement from President Grant, has explained the cause of the trouble. He says the removal of Jas. M. Ashley, ex-Congressman from Ohio, from the governorship of Montana, and the appointment of Col. Potts, which had just happened, was the subject of a table conversation, and a letter from Ashley protesting against the change had been read, when the President was announced. He was received and shown Ashley’s letter, and became immediately excited, denouncing Ashley as a worthless mischief-maker. The next topic discussed was the San Domingo treaty. Sumner dwelt especially upon the expenditure by Gen. Babcock on San Domingo of a large sum taken from a secret service fund, for promoting intercourse with the West India Islands, which Seward had prevailed on Thad. Stevens to have inserted in an appropriation bill during the war, and the President impressed Sumner with the idea that he feared an attack in Congress for that expenditure. Poore adds his opinion that Sumner thought that the President had come to enlist his services in defending this expenditure by Gen. Babcock, but the President meant the treaty for the acquisition of the Dominican Republic. The President and the Senator misunderstood each other. After a while the President promised to send Gen. Babcock to call on the Senator the next day with copies of the papers, and left. As Sumner escorted him to the door, he told him that he was a Republican, and a supporter of the Republican administration, and that he should sustain the administration in this case if he possibly could, after he had examined the papers. He meant to sustain the expenditure of $100,000 in cash and $50,000 in a light battery and muskets purchased at New York, but the President meant the treaty.

Maj. Poore's statement thus removes a grave historical doubt, and seems to set at rest the stories recently published of the cause of the difficulty between Grant and Sumner.
