The Barre Daily Times reported that the Dominican government held cabinet meetings and mobilized troops to the border following reports of a frontier clash with Haiti over road construction near Pedernales. The gunboat Independencia and the Clyde Line steamer Cherokee were prepared for troop transport, while American customs officials M. E. Beall and James J. McLean were reported out of contact. The Dominican government expressed a desire for a peaceful settlement while insisting on a permanent boundary determination.

The Barre Daily Times informó que el gobierno dominicano celebró reuniones de gabinete y movilizó tropas hacia la frontera tras los informes de un choque con Haití por la construcción de una carretera cerca de Pedernales. El cañonero Independencia y el vapor de la Clyde Line Cherokee quedaron alistados para el transporte de tropas, mientras se notificaba la pérdida de comunicación con los funcionarios aduaneros estadounidenses M. E. Beall y James J. McLean. El gobierno dominicano manifestó su deseo de un arreglo pacífico, insistiendo a la vez en la fijación definitiva de los límites.

Monday night. In the absence of official confirmation, the Dominican officials attached enough importance to the report to indulge in a hasty cabinet meeting and to begin negotiations with the resident American minister to Santo Domingo. To guard against the failure of these negotiations, Dominican troops were or dered from various parts of the country to the border, the Independencia, the only Dominican gunboat of any consequence, was made ready to convey the troops and supplies immediately to Pedernales. The Clyde line steamer Cherokee, which was at San Domingo City when the news came, was detained, the government contemplating a request to the captain of the ship to transport troops to some point near the border on the south coast. According to advices received here the trouble is due to an old quarrel over the boundary lines between the two republics. The dispute recently took on a critical phase owing to the [assertion?] by the Dominicans of the right of occupation of the disputed territory and the constructions of roads by them north of Pedernales. The Dominican government is reported to be anxious to effect an amicable settlement, but failing in that will demand a definite adjustment of the boundary issue. Three American customs collectors are in the disturbed section, and two of them, M. E. Beall at El Sondo and James J. McLean at Commendador, have not been heard from, while word has been received from John P. Hollesen at Dajabon. All of our Dark and Light Prints. They cost us
