Correspondence of the Tribune.

WASHINGTON, Nov. 20, 1845.

The comments which I thought it my duty to make on the proposed Annexation of this delightful island to the United States, seem to have attracted some general attention. As the subject is one of considerable importance I have thought worth while to run through some dozen works, in the library of Congress, upon the past history and present state of Hayti. It is the fact, as said, that the only object of the mission of Mr. Hogan was the promotion of commercial relations between Hayti and the United States, and that the government has not yet entered upon the question of the recognition of Dominican Independence. It may be so; but if it be, the conduct of the Executive is the best possible illustration of 'how not to do it.' The truth itself is that it is in the identical garden of the Hesperides, and that the government is in the position of the children of Eden sent out of cultivation. Hayti is the Cerbi for the world, and is enough to feed and clothe all the world of mountains. It is just large enough to make a noble State, and it is now divided into two parts, the Haytien and the Dominican. The population of the whole island is about eight hundred thousand.
