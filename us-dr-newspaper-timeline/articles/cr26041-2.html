The report describes the Dominican government's costly borrowing, including a recent loan of $150,000 made in Puerto Plata at a stated two per cent a month, of which the government actually received far less, and the Puerto Plata syndicate's plan to issue $500,000 in paper currency. It says the country's only hopes of relief are a commercial treaty with the United States or American capital at a reasonable rate, and praises United States Consul H. C. C. Astwood for settling American claims, including a $63,000 indemnity for the widow of Piatt, an American carpenter shot by government troops.

El informe describe los costosos préstamos del gobierno dominicano, incluido un préstamo reciente de $150,000 hecho en Puerto Plata a un declarado dos por ciento mensual, del que el gobierno en realidad recibió mucho menos, y el plan del sindicato de Puerto Plata de emitir $500,000 en papel moneda. Dice que las únicas esperanzas de alivio del país son un tratado comercial con los Estados Unidos o capital estadounidense a una tasa razonable, y elogia al cónsul de los Estados Unidos, H. C. C. Astwood, por resolver reclamaciones estadounidenses, incluida una indemnización de $63,000 para la viuda de Piatt, un carpintero estadounidense muerto a tiros por tropas del gobierno.

syndicate. During recent years the rate of interest has been reduced from time to time until it reached three per cent. per month. To deceive the public a recent loan was effected in Puerto Plata of $150,000 at, it was stated, two per cent. per month. The Government received from the syndicate $130,000, less seven per cent., and then agreed to pay two per cent. per month on the $150,000. so that it might appear to the public that only interest at that reduced rate was being paid. The Puerto Plata syndicate has become emboldened by its success, and is now planning to issue $500,000 in paper currency under its own guarantee, and proposes for the present making this the circulating medium of the country. This scheme has gone so far that it is understood that a well-known New York house has by this time got the order for the printing of the bills. Notwithstanding this fact, Congress and the merchants of the city of San Domingo will oppose the matter. The latter, with the assistance of Mr. Alexander [Bird?], the English capitalist who is building the railroad here, have established a bank with a cash capital of $75,000. Their rates of interest are proportionate with the risk, and can be estimated when it is seen that the Government, on as good security as the country affords, has to pay all the way from 24 to 30 per cent. per annum.
