The Jersey City News reported that Dominican soldiers fired upon a landing boat from the American schooner Henry Crosby near Azua, critically wounding a sailor and injuring the first officer. The schooner's captain sailed to Azua and filed a formal report with the United States Consul General in Santo Domingo, who forwarded the complaint to Washington. The unprovoked attack was believed to have resulted from soldiers mistaking the crew for insurgents attempting to land and overthrow President Heureaux.

The Jersey City News informó que soldados dominicanos abrieron fuego cerca de Azua contra un bote de desembarco de la goleta estadounidense Henry Crosby, hiriendo de gravedad a un marinero y lesionando al primer oficial. El capitán de la goleta navegó hacia Azua y presentó un informe formal ante el cónsul general de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo, quien remitió la queja a Washington. Se creía que el ataque no provocado se debió a que los soldados confundieron a la tripulación con insurgentes que intentaban desembarcar para derrocar al presidente Heureaux.

drew up in line, and, levelling their carbines at the men in the Crosby's small boat, fired. One of the sailors threw up his hands and fell at the feet of his companions. The others paused in surprise and affright, when suddenly another volley was fired from the shore. By this the first officer was shot. Then the boat was put about and turned towards the schooner. The sailors rowed for their lives. The wounded man was taken aboard the Crosby. Then it was discovered that the first officer had been shot in the shoulder and that the seaman had been struck by several bullets. A wound in the back is believed to be fatal. The Crosby's captain did not know what to make of such barbarous and unwarranted treatment. He hoisted sail and after a time reached the port of Azua. From there he reported the outrage to the United States Consul General at San Domingo City. At the latter's request the full details of the shooting were written out by the captain and the report has been forwarded to the United States authorities at Washington. The letter came by the George W. Clyde and will probably reach the State Department tonight. No information or satisfaction was given to the American skipper by the Dominican authorities. It is supposed, however, that the soldiers believed that the American sailors were a band of conspirators who intended to overthrow the Government of President Heureaux.
