A Chance for a Diplomatic Muss.

The steamship Tybee, from Santo Domingo on the 30th ult., brings the following news: The British Consul General of Port au Prince had been on a tour of observation to Samana Bay, and had afterwards visited Santo Domingo with propositions from the Hytien Government for a treaty between the two republics. Among the conditions of the proposed treaty it is said were the cession to Hayti of the disputed territory on the frontier, and the annulment of the Samana Bay convention made with citizens of the United States.

These conditions being admitted by the Dominican Government, Hayti or England was to loan a considerable sum to Santo Domingo, payable at convenience and without interest.

The discussion of this measure was causing great excitement throughout the Island.

The President elect, Gousales, has not been installed in office, as the convention called for the purpose of framing a new Constitution was still unable to agree upon the article thereof. The special commission of Samana Bay Company have not succeeded in obtaining an amicable settlement of the question pending between the Government and the Company, and the provisional government has declared the Samana Bay convention null and void. The commissioners have protested against this summary act as arbitrary and ellegal, and instigated by British and Haytien influence, and demand an arbitration upon the matters in dispute according to the terms of the convention. The Company will, in the meantime, call upon the United States Government for protection against any violent proceedings on the part the Dominican authorities.

Dr. Howe remains at Samana as acting Governor, while Samuels and Fabens, and other members of the commission, returned to New York.
