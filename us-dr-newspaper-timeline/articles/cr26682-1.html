The Ohio Daily-Express reported that captured correspondence revealed former Haitian diplomat Col. Astrel Roland conspired with Dominican President Rafael Trujillo and Interior Minister Alvarez Paulino to orchestrate an invasion of Haiti. A Belgian woman named Lady Verbracken, operating as an ethnologist, gathered Haitian military intelligence and coordinated strategy meetings in New York with Dominican officials. Following his dismissal from the diplomatic service in January, Roland launched subversive radio broadcasts targeting the Haitian government.

El Ohio Daily-Express informó que correspondencia confidencial incautada puso al descubierto que el exdiplomático y coronel haitiano Astrel Roland confabulaba con el mandatario dominicano Rafael Trujillo y el ministro de Interior Álvarez Paulino para organizar una invasión armada contra Haití. Una dama belga apodada Lady Verbracken, amparada en una presunta labor de etnóloga, recopiló secretos de inteligencia militar haitiana y articuló reuniones operativas en Nueva York con dignatarios dominicanos. Tras su exoneración del escalafón exterior en enero, Roland comenzó a emitir arengas de agitación subversiva por las ondas radiales hostigando al gobierno de su patria.

Communications written by Roland and captured by the Haitian government reveal that he worked with Lady Verbracken, the president of the Dominican Republic, referred to as Desire in his letters the Dominican minister of interior, Alvarez Paulino, referred to as P. in his letters, and other Dominican officials. The most important person to the success of Roland's plans evidently was the Belgian lady, with whom Roland was reported to be in love. Originally, she went to Haiti as an ethnologist with aim of destroying those named as Roland's enemies. In July and August of 1948, she failed in this mission and left Haiti for Brazil. She returned to Haiti in December, but this time was trailed by the police. She sought information on the nation's military strength and setup, and promised that Col. Roland would return to Haiti soon. She left Haiti December 29 and met Roland two days later in Quito, R. D. Two weeks later she met with [illegible] Roland, Paulino, and another person in New York at the Hotel Martinique. There they discussed an invasion of Haiti. During this time Roland was a Haitian official, councillors of legation in Quito, D. R. His job was dropped by Haiti January 19. Not long after the loss of his job Roland began his propaganda broadcasts against Haiti. The Haitian ambassador to the U.
