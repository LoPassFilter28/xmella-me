An editorial attacks the report of the Commissioners appointed by Grant to investigate whether it was politic to annex San Domingo to the United States, calling it a document prepared to please Baez and full of falsehoods, and noting that Grant, after laying it before Congress, backed down and refused to press its consideration. The paper also publishes two letters from General Cabral inviting the Commissioners to visit his headquarters and the country held by his forces and ask the people whether they favored annexation, an invitation the editorial says the Commissioners were very careful not to accept.

Un editorial ataca el informe de los comisionados nombrados por Grant para investigar si era conveniente anexar Santo Domingo a los Estados Unidos, calificándolo de documento preparado para complacer a Báez y lleno de falsedades, y señalando que Grant, después de presentarlo al Congreso, dio marcha atrás y se negó a impulsar su consideración. El periódico publica también dos cartas del general Cabral invitando a los comisionados a visitar su cuartel general y el territorio ocupado por sus fuerzas y a preguntar al pueblo si favorecía la anexión, una invitación que el editorial dice que los comisionados tuvieron mucho cuidado de no aceptar.

The American Union Forever. Address all letters to [illegible], [illegible] County, Ohio. The San Domingo Report. The first page of this issue of the [illegible] contains the report of the Commissioners appointed by Grant to investigate whether it was politic to annex that Negroes' nest, San Domingo, to the United States. [illegible] Of course the report was prepared with the view of pleasing the partner of [Baez?] who squandered the people's money to obtain it. It is full of falsehood, if we are to believe the correspondent who accompanied the expedition, or the speeches of Senators [illegible], [illegible] and [illegible]. And, what brands it with falsehood more than anything else, is the fact that Grant, after laying it before Congress, backed down and refused to press its consideration at this session of Congress. He knew that Congress understood the game to be one in which he was to be whitewashed, therefore they would not be entrapped by its misrepresentations. General Cabral's Letters to the San Domingo Commissioners. [illegible] publish two letters from Gen. Cabral to the Commissioners inviting them to visit his headquarters, and the country held by his forces, and inquire of the people whether they favored annexation. This invitation the Commissioners were very careful not to accept; had they accepted, the probabilities were very strong that their report would have contained information that would not have been, to the interest of the Grant-Baez party, to have made public.
