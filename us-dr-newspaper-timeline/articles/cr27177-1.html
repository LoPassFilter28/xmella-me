The Evening Star published a profile of Dominican President Rafael Trujillo, noting his rise from private to captain in the Marine-trained constabulary and his disciplined administrative methods. The article detailed his measures eliminating government automobiles for legislators and dropping 500 redundant employees from the state payroll. Despite the depression cutting government revenues by more than half, the piece credited Trujillo and his financial adviser with balancing the national budget and maintaining civic order.

El Evening Star publicó una semblanza del presidente dominicano Rafael Trujillo, destacando su ascenso de soldado raso a capitán en la guardia entrenada por los marines y sus métodos de disciplina administrativa. El artículo detalló sus medidas para suprimir los automóviles oficiales para legisladores y dar de baja a 500 empleados innecesarios de la nómina estatal. A pesar de que la depresión redujo los ingresos fiscales a más de la mitad, la nota atribuyó a Trujillo y a su consejero financiero el haber equilibrado el presupuesto nacional y preservado el orden cívico.

sidelights respecting Gen. Rafael Leonides Trujillo, the President of the republic. Trujillo began life as a private in the constabulary forces in the island, long operated by the United States Marines; rose to be captain of it, and is now running the country in the thoroughly efficient, if somewhat hard-boiled, fashion of a marine. Trujillo's first act when he took office was to take away from all the members of the Dominican House and Senate their free government automobiles; his next act to drop 500 supernumeraries off the official pay roll. The depression in Santo Domingo cut the state's revenue in half—from $15,000,000 to $7,000,000, and Trujillo found an empty treasury. But now, thanks to Trujillo and to the advice of his financial adviser, the republic has a balanced budget and is sweet, sanitary and safe. [illegible] The historic United States mint at San Francisco is now antiquated and to all intents and purposes obsolete. It might have gone on and on in genteel decay but for the fact that with the rise in the paper money price of gold and the resultant boom in gold mining and boom in the gold melting pots for gold teeth and wedding rings the Government mints have had a new lease on life. And the San Francisco mint physically has been quite unequal to the strain. This mint's gold hoard is being shifted to the Denver mint and funds have been allotted and plans are afoot for the construction of a brand-new, up-to-date mint in the city of the Golden Gate.
