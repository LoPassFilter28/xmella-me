A report on President Heureaux's assassination at Moca says his death caused a panic, that Juan Isidoro Jiminez, who took part in the attempted insurrection of the previous year, is a candidate for the presidency, and that General Maximo Gomez is also reported to aspire to it. It notes that the father of Caceres, the assassin, was put to death by order of Heureaux in 1884, that Vice President Wenceslao Figuereo at once assumed the direction of affairs, and that President McKinley sent Figuereo a cable of condolences in the name of the American people.

Un informe sobre el asesinato del presidente Heureaux en Moca dice que su muerte causó pánico, que Juan Isidoro Jiminez, que tomó parte en el intento de insurrección del año anterior, es candidato a la presidencia, y que también se informa que el general Máximo Gómez aspira a ella. Señala que el padre de Cáceres, el asesino, fue ejecutado por orden de Heureaux en 1884, que el vicepresidente Wenceslao Figuereo asumió de inmediato la dirección de los asuntos, y que el presidente McKinley envió a Figuereo un cable de condolencias en nombre del pueblo americano.

Isidoro Jiminez, who took part in the attempted insurrection of last year, is a candidate for the presidency, Jiminez landed from the Fanita, but was driven off with the loss of most of his band of ten men. It is reported that General Maximo Gomez, formerly president of the Cuban insurrectionists, who is a native of Santo Domingo, also aspires to the presidency. The father of Caceros, the assassin, was put to death by order of President Heureaux in 1884. Vice President Wenceslao Figuereo, immediately upon the announcement of the president's death, assumed the direction of affairs. It has long been expected that Heureaux would be assassinated. He used to boast that he was a more absolute ruler than any czar or emperor in the world. His ability and shrewdness made a great impression on Europeans and Americans who met him. His daring was remarkable and his shrewdness was said to be equal to that of the most experienced European diplomats. The president was frequently reported assassinated. He had numerous enemies. As a political mystifier he had great talent and boldness. He himself caused reports to be circulated that he had been seized by revolutionists, imprisoned and subsequently shot. This was done to draw out his enemies. When they appeared, Heureaux would ride up, followed by his army, and capture them. They were usually put to death at once.
