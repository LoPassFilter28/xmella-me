The Evening Star published a profile of Dominican President Rafael Trujillo on the republic's sixty-ninth national anniversary, recounting his rise from enlistment during the U.S. Marine Corps occupation to top commander of the Dominican Army within a decade. The article highlighted his friendly relations with the United States and his employment of American technical advisers to overhaul national finances and develop natural resources. It also commended Trujillo's programs to diversify Dominican agriculture beyond its four traditional export crops.

But the efficiency of his administration and his successful conduct of the political and economic problems of the Dominican Republic are not the only things which make President Trujillo outstanding among the statesmen of the day. Probably the youngest elected Chief Executive of an independent nation, yet the story of his life is full of human interest, and should be particularly appealing to American readers, for President Trujillo started his military career in the ranks of the United States Marine Corps. Since he joined the Marines — while they were on duty in the Dominican Republic — Gen. Trujillo was promoted in the short period of 10 years from the lowest to the highest rank in the Dominican Army. It is probably his contacts with Americans that have made him such an admirer and good friend of the United States as he has proven himself to be. The first act of his administration was the announcement that he would contract American technical experts to aid in the solution of the nation's financial problems and in the development of its natural resources.
