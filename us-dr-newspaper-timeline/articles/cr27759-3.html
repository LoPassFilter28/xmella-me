A Washington item explains that the Seward-Stevens West India fund is now exhausted: Seward spent most of $100,000 of it on his West Indies trip, and General Babcock took the remaining $150,000 to lease the Bay of Samana. It reports that Mr. Dawes, chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations, refused two senators' request for a bill to pay the rent now due to Envoy Fabens, saying he could only make appropriations authorized by law, and that Senator Sumner calls the whole proceeding without the shadow of law.

Una nota de Washington explica que el fondo Seward-Stevens para las Indias Occidentales ya se agotó: Seward gastó la mayor parte de $100,000 de ese fondo en su viaje a las Indias Occidentales, y el general Babcock tomó los $150,000 restantes para arrendar la bahía de Samana. Informa que el señor Dawes, presidente del Comité de Asignaciones de la Cámara, rechazó la petición de dos senadores de un proyecto de ley para pagar la renta ya vencida al enviado Fabens, diciendo que solo podía hacer asignaciones autorizadas por la ley, y que el senador Sumner califica todo el procedimiento como carente de toda sombra de legalidad.

Porter, started for the West Indies. They took with them $100,000 out of Old Thad's West India fund and spent the most of it. as is well known, this trip did not result in anything. When General Babcock went to St. Domingo he took the balance of this Seward Stevens fund, [illegible], with which he leased the Bay of Samana. This fund is now exhausted, so that Envoy Fabens will not be able to collect his rent from that source. It is stated that a couple of Senators, friends of the administration, visited Mr. Dawes, chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations, and requested him to bring in a bill appropriating 160,000 for the payment now due. Dawes, who is a very careful fellow, and who does not seem to be unaware of the existence of the "extra article" of the treaty, positively refused to comply with the request, for the reason, as he alleged, that there is no law for it. He told the Senators that he could only make appropriations which are authorized by law. Should Mr. Dawes adhere to his determination it is difficult to see where Mr. Fabens will get his rent. It might be taken out of the State Department Secret Service fund, but of late years this fund has been limited. Last year it was cut down from $150,008 to [illegible]. This year Judge Kelsey, who has charge of the Diplomatic and Consular Appropriation bill, says he intends to keep it down to $100,000.
