From the Dominican paper El Oasis: the victories of San Tomé and Cambronal (Dec. 22), where General Cabral killed the Haitian commander Lafleur in single combat, the pursuit through Las Matas, and the siege and night flight of the invaders at Neyba, with 695 enemy dead counted at San Tomé.

Del periódico dominicano El Oasis: las victorias de San Tomé y Cambronal (22 de diciembre), donde el general Cabral mató en combate singular al comandante haitiano Lafleur, la persecución por Las Matas y el sitio y la fuga nocturna de los invasores en Neyba, con 695 enemigos contados entre los muertos en San Tomé.

The two armies disputed the victory for a while, but at last victory declared for our heroic soldiery, whose impetuous charge nothing could resist. The sublime heroism, of both officers and soldiers, is beyond all praise. The four divisions of the enemy, incomplete as they were, could not have amounted to less than 6,000 men. "In the middle of the battle, Brigadier-General José Maria Cabral engaged Lafleur, the commander-in-chief of the hostile army, in single combat, and killed him on the spot. He sent the spoils of the Haytian commander to Santana, who has forwarded them to this capital, where they are now to be seen. Six hundred and ninety-five of the enemy have been counted as slain on the field of battle, and [illegible] military chests, among other things, were taken. "Col. Martinez pursued the enemy with 500 cavalry, overtook them at Las Matas, and from that point drove them from our soil. In their disorderly flight the Haytians lost a great number of killed; it cannot be ascertained exactly how many. At Cambronal the enemy left upon the field of battle 350 killed — among them their Commander-in-Chief, Caras; and, as the prisoners say, two regiments had full employment in carrying off the wounded.
