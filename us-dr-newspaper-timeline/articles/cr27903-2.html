The Wheeling Daily Intelligencer reported that a German envoy threatened to embargo Dominican tobacco and offered President Ulises Heureaux an annual bribe of $50,000 to terminate the reciprocity treaty with the United States. In response, Heureaux sent a confidential emissary to New York merchants to determine whether the United States government would pay him $75,000 to preserve the treaty.

The Wheeling Daily Intelligencer informó que un enviado alemán amenazó con embargar el tabaco dominicano y ofreció al presidente Ulises Heureaux un soborno anual de 50.000 dólares para rescindir el tratado de reciprocidad con los Estados Unidos. En respuesta, Heureaux envió a un emisario confidencial ante comerciantes de Nueva York para averiguar si el gobierno de los Estados Unidos le pagaría 75.000 dólares para mantener el tratado.

the two, in which Germany was named as the "most favored of nations." Her goods were permitted to enter the Dominican ports at extremely low imposts, and her citizens were to receive special favors and exemptions. On the other hand, many Dominican products were given precedence in Germany, and under this treaty a large and valuable trade grew up between the two nations. Dominican tobacco, especially, found favor in Germany and soon crowded out the Sumatra product, and at present the entire product of Puerto Plata is used in Germany. The loss of this market for its staple product would be a serious blow to the San Domingan people, and it was on this line that the conspirators went to work. [illegible] A special envoy was sent from Germany to San Domingo, and about two weeks ago he had an interview with President Ulises Heureaux, in which the harm which could be done his country should Germany close her ports to Dominican products was set forth. The German envoy then offered a personal subsidy of $50,000 a year to the president "to recompense him for any unpopularity he might incur" if the reciprocity treaty with the United States was abrogated. President Heureaux at once dispatched an emissary to this country to see if he could not obtain $75,000 from the United States.
