The Evening Star reported that the Organization of American States unanimously referred the latest border dispute between Haiti and the Dominican Republic to an investigatory fact-finding commission. U.S. officials acknowledged having little independent information regarding the rebel invasion in northern Haiti, while a representative for insurgent leader General Léon Cantave reported only minor skirmishes.

El Evening Star informó que la Organización de los Estados Americanos remitió por unanimidad la más reciente disputa fronteriza entre Haití y la República Dominicana a una comisión investigadora de determinación de los hechos. Funcionarios estadounidenses reconocieron disponer de escasa información independiente sobre la invasión rebelde en el norte de Haití, mientras un representante del líder insurgente, general Léon Cantave, reportó únicamente escaramuzas menores.

Like A Charade The performance on all sides had the earmarks of an oft repeated charade. It took the council exactly, four minutes of open debate—after an hour of private talk in an upstairs conference room—to approve unanimously the resolution turning the dispute over to the fact-finding commission. To a great extent, the attitude in the OAS reflects a similar outlook in the United States State Department. Officials admit that they have almost no first-hand information about how the northern invasion is proceeding—if it is proceeding. The United States Embassy in Haiti, which has been whittled down to a skeleton staff to show displeasure with the Duvalier regime, has no on-the-spot representatives in the Cap Haitian area. Since telephone communications in Haiti broke down years ago, contact is reduced to radio talks with the estimated 125 American residents of the area. But no one in Washington seems to be able to discover whether any fighting actually has taken place and whether government or rebel forces have suffered any casualties.
