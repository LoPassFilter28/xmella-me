The Essex County Herald emphasized the strategic importance of Samana Bay as the naval key to the Caribbean, recalling President Grant's earlier annexation efforts. It reported that an unexpected revolution had seized the capital of Santo Domingo during the president's absence, complicating recent American diplomatic agreements compensating a U.S. steamship company. With the Dominican president on the run and the foreign minister sheltering in the American consulate, the paper suggested U.S. marine landings or intervention might follow.

El Essex County Herald subrayó la importancia estratégica de la bahía de Samaná como la llave naval del Caribe, recordando los anteriores intentos de anexión del presidente Grant. Informó que una revolución imprevista se había apoderado de la capital de Santo Domingo durante la ausencia del presidente, complicando recientes acuerdos diplomáticos estadounidenses que indemnizaban a una compañía naviera de los Estados Unidos. Con el presidente dominicano en fuga y el ministro de Relaciones Exteriores refugiado en el consulado estadounidense, el periódico sugirió que podrían producirse desembarcos de la infantería de marina de EE. UU. o una intervención militar.

well enough that we should keep in view. That is Samana bay. the real Strategic key of the West Indies and the Caribbean. Commercially this bay, which Is the finest and most easily defended natural harbor In the West Indies, has already been annexed by an American corporation; politically and In the military sense It lies fallow. All our proceedings relative to Santo Domingo should continue to have reference to the future control of this bay. President Grant saw Its consequences, and If he had had his way the United States 'would have peaceably taken over both It and the entire Dominican Republic thirty-four years ago. The present revolution, which quickly resulted in the capture of the capital city, Santo Domingo, by a rebel army during the temporary absence of the president, may be of more serious consequence to this country than usually attends a Dominican revolt. Our minister had Just succeeded In extorting from the government an agreement to compensate an American steamship company for a violation of the govern ment's contract with It, and now that the president who made the agreement Is practically a fugitive and the minister of foreign affairs with whom he dealt Is a refugee In the United States consulate the situation becomes some what complicated and may require and Justify the landing of American marines and possibly necessitate the ultimate seizure of Samana bay.
