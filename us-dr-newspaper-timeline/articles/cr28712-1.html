The Chariton Courier reported that French Admiral Richard concurred with the Dominican government's position on the Boismare-Caccavelli claims dispute and accepted the terms previously proposed by Dominican authorities. The Dominican government secured a loan to satisfy the financial claim, releasing citizens from the funds collected by popular subscription. Meanwhile, the French warship Suchet departed for Haiti to collect 200,000 francs connected to a secret border agreement negotiated with the late President Heureaux.

El Chariton Courier informó que el almirante francés Richard coincidió con la postura del gobierno dominicano respecto a la controversia por las reclamaciones Boismare-Caccavelli y aceptó los términos propuestos con anterioridad por las autoridades dominicanas. El gobierno dominicano obtuvo un préstamo para saldar la reclamación financiera, liberando a los ciudadanos de los fondos reunidos por suscripción popular. Entretanto, el buque de guerra francés Suchet zarpó hacia Haití para cobrar 200.000 francos vinculados a un acuerdo fronterizo secreto negociado con el difunto presidente Heureaux.

Disputed Claim. Santo Domingo, Jan. 16. — The French admiral finds on investigation of all facts that the Dominican government was correct in its attitude on the Boismare-Caccavelli claim. Admiral Richard of the French navy is not demanding money, but he has accepted the terms which the Dominican government proposed to the French consul before his protest. The Dominican government, having contracted a loan, prefers to pay the amount of the claim, and will thank the people of the republic for the popular subscription, which will not be used. It is probable that the French consul will be recalled soon. The French warship Suchet has left for Haiti in order to enforce the payment of 200,000 francs paid to the late President Heureaux for lands never ceded, as provided in the secret settlement of the boundary questions. Kruger Still Confident. Pretoria, Thursday, Jan. 11, via Lourenco Marques. — President Kruger, in the course of a stirring address just issued to the burghers, affirms that providence is on their side, that their cause is just and that they must succeed. To Be Tried as Rebels. Cape Town, Jan. 16. — Col. Pilcher's prisoners will be tried as rebels, it is understood, before the Supreme court, as the military authorities have no jurisdiction, martial law not having been proclaimed in Cape Colony.
