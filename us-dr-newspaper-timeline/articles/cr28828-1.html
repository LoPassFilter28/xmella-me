Independence, Recognition, and Samaná SchemesIndependencia, Reconocimiento y Proyectos de Samaná · 1844–1861

On February 27, 1844, Los Trinitarios proclaimed Dominican independence. American press coverage surged with reports on the new republic, repeated Haitian invasion threats, diplomatic recognition appeals, and southern schemes to secure Samaná Bay as a naval base.El 27 de febrero de 1844, Los Trinitarios proclamaron la independencia. La prensa estadounidense cubrió la nueva república, las invasiones haitianas, llamamientos de reconocimiento diplomático y proyectos para adquirir la Bahía de Samaná.

Intelligence from St. Domingo to the 25th ult., has been received. A revolution had broken out.—The troops and the navy had declared in favor of Gen. Santa Anna for President. The President Hernandes, met the troops on the outskirts of the city, and declared that he was still their commanding officer. Gen. Santa Anna instantly withdrew from the army and went to his plantation. The troops then disbanded; but not being satisfied, they again requested Santa Anna to head them, which he did, and immediately marched to the city.—Previous to his arrival, by the order of the President Hernandes, the city of San Carlos was burnt, to prevent the army from marching in. It was supposed that the city would surrender; but this the burnt of the town; and that Santa Anna would be declared President of the Dominican Government.
