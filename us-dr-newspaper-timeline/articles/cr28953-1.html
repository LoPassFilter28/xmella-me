The Sentinel=Record prints an item: At the investigation by Senator-elect James D. Phelan into the fitness of James M. Sullivan, American minister to the Dominican republic, to hold his post, Collett asserted that in his opinion Sullivan was eminently fit to represent the United States in Santo Domingo in the double capacity in which the American minister there is obliged to act, that of business man as well as diplomat. Collett is a son-in-law of former Mayor James C. Dahlman of Omaha, Neb., and said his wife's family and Secretary Bryan's were personal friends. He had not been recommended for his post, he said, until after he had passed a civil service examination as engineer for the Interstate Commerce Commission. Sullivan, he conceded, was not popular with Dominicans, but he held that Sullivan was not to blame. 'I don't think any American minister to Santo Domingo ever will be popular,' he asserted. 'Santo Dominicans are always against their government, and if this government is recognized by the United States the American minister is bound to be unpopular.' Never, said Collett, had he been asked by Sullivan to favor one contractor above another, nor had he seen anything in the minister's private or official conduct that was improper. Commissioner Phelan announced that documents received from Minister Sullivan in his defense would be put into evidence tomorrow and that the investigation would then recess to be resumed in Washington on Monday or Tuesday of next week.The Sentinel=Record publica una nota: Collett afirmó que Sullivan, ministro americano en la República Dominicana, era eminentemente apto para representar a los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo en la doble capacidad en que el ministro americano allí está obligado a actuar, la de hombre de negocios además de diplomático, en la investigación del senador electo James D. Phelan sobre la aptitud de James M. Sullivan para ocupar su puesto. Collett es yerno del exalcalde James C. Dahlman de Omaha, Nebraska, y dijo que la familia de su esposa y la del secretario Bryan eran amigas personales. No había sido recomendado para su puesto, dijo, hasta después de haber aprobado un examen de servicio civil como ingeniero para la Comisión de Comercio Interestatal. Sullivan, concedió, no era popular entre los dominicanos, pero sostenía que Sullivan no tenía la culpa. «No creo que ningún ministro americano en Santo Domingo llegue a ser popular», afirmó. «Los santodominicanos siempre están contra su gobierno, y si este gobierno es reconocido por los Estados Unidos el ministro americano está condenado a ser impopular». Nunca, dijo Collett, Sullivan le había pedido que favoreciera a un contratista sobre otro, ni había visto nada en la conducta privada u oficial del ministro que fuera impropio. El comisionado Phelan anunció que los documentos recibidos del ministro Sullivan en su defensa se presentarían como evidencia mañana y que la investigación entonces entraría en receso para reanudarse en Washington el lunes o martes de la próxima semana.

The Sentinel=Record. Hot Springs, Ark., January 28, 1915.
