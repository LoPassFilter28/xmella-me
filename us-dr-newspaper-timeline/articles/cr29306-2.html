The Evening Times published a statement from Dominican revolutionists detailing their aims to reform land taxes, modify tariffs, eliminate export duties, and establish habeas corpus protections. The statement asserted that Generals Morales and Jimenez had reached an understanding, while former President Horacio Vasquez had deserted President Caceres. It also alleged that Dominican Minister of Finance Federico Velasquez was seeking to secure half of the $1,300,000 deposited for foreign creditors to fund government war expenses.

The Evening Times publicó una declaración de revolucionarios dominicanos en la que detallaban sus objetivos de reformar los impuestos sobre la tierra, modificar los aranceles, suprimir los derechos de exportación y establecer garantías de habeas corpus. El manifiesto aseguraba que los generales Morales y Jimenes habían alcanzado un entendimiento, mientras que el expresidente Horacio Vásquez había abandonado al presidente Cáceres. Alegaba además que el ministro de Hacienda dominicano Federico Velásquez procuraba asegurarse la mitad de los 1.300.000 dólares depositados para los acreedores extranjeros con el propósito de costear los gastos bélicos del gobierno.

It is our purpose, if we are victorious, as I am certain we shall be, to establish land taxes, modify the tariff and remove the pernicious export duties, which retard the progress of our industries. We also want the right of habeas corpus, which is unknown in our country. As matters are, the government puts in Jail and exiles citizens without any process of law. The revolution was started without the consent or advice of General Jimenez. General Morales and General Jimenez have come to an understanding. The government has suspended payment of the army and civil employes and seeks to pay preferred creditors, which cannot be done according to the modus vivendi. Of course we recognize the Santo Domingo treaty, and we are anxious for an even better one. It is probably not generally known in this country, but I know that Federico Velasquez, minister of finance, who is now in this city, has come here to get, if possible, half of the $1,300,000 on deposit for the payment of Santo Domingo's foreign creditors, that the money might be used for the expenses of the war. If the United States could guarantee us free elections the revolution would end, but as we are without help we were compelled to protest with arms. Even General Horacio Vasquez, formerly president of the republic, who was the delegate to the Cibao for President Caceres, has deserted him. CAPTAINS FOR CREWS.
