The Wheeling Daily Intelligencer

The Revolution in Hayti—President Geffrard preparing to escape—Suffering among the Negroes on the Island of Jamaica—An Outbreak Feared—San Domingo Affairs.

The Herald's Kingston, Jamaica, correspondent says the progress the Haytian revolutionists are making in Hayti, has been deemed by President Geffrard as sufficiently alarming to induce him to make preparations for flight from the island. A small steamer has therefore been chartered by Geffrard to convey him to Port au Prince and there remain till the final action, when he is to take steam in flight or till the danger of such outbreak has passed.

Owing to the severe drought of the present season on the Island of Jamaica, the heavy taxes and other causes, the negro inhabitants have been reduced to the most abject poverty and great suffering, and thousands of them are on the verge of starvation. They blame the government for all this, as the cause of their afflictions, and so strong and bitter is their feeling against the local government that an outbreak on their part is feared in the western part of the Island, in consequence of which two gunboats have been dispatched to that locality.

The Herald's Santiago correspondent says, notwithstanding the havoc and destruction of the Spaniards during their invasion of San Domingo, the crops are now coming forward which promise ample subsistence for the inhabitants, and the war which has compelled the people to utilize their resources has united and determined them to maintain their independence. The Spanish forces have hardly borne out by reports which we have received, as to the resumption of hostilities between two native chiefs, but it has been ascertained beyond doubt that the Spaniards stole and destroyed property on the island, especially the property of American merchants. Geffrard has sent commissioners to different places, and now Gen. Gandara, the Spanish commander, having withdrawn his troops from the interior, has blockaded the principal ports of San Domingo, because the Dominican Government will not accede to his haughty and unreasonable demands in the peace treaty now pending. He has seized two large stocks of tobacco belonging to Americans as well as Dominicans. To offset this blockade the Dominicans have determined to commission privateers to prey on Spanish commerce and so far as reported one American merchant vessel has already left for the United States and Europe.
