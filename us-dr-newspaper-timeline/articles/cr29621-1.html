THE INTERNATIONAL MAGAZINE FOR JUNE.

SourceFuente: New-York daily tribune, New-York [N.Y.], 1851-06-06

Price 25 cents.

This number is one of the best and most attractive yet issued. It contains a great variety of interesting and valuable matter, among which we notice a very interesting and well-written account of the mission of Mr. Robert M. Walsh to Hayti, as Special Agent of the United States. Mr. Walsh's instructions were to act in concert with the agents of France and Great Britain in an endeavor to induce the Emperor Soulouque to recognize the independence of the Dominican Republic. The writer gives a very graphic description of the character and court of the sable monarch, and of the progress and results of the mission. He represents Mr. Walsh as having acted with great energy and decision, and at various places in Hayti, as threatening to blockade and bombard Port-au-Prince in case Soulouque should still refuse to recognize the eastern part of the island as separate and independent. Armed coercion was apparently as familiar to him as his own title of "Special Agent," and his dreams by night and his talk by day seem to have been mainly of seventy-fours, frigates, paixhans, bomb-shells, rockets and thirty-two pounders. An inexorable and most unmitigated "interventionist" was Mr. Walsh, and if his advice had been followed, the "Napoleon of the West" would have been taught a lesson which he would not soon have forgotten. The Magazine contains also a great variety of other interesting matter, including several original stories and poems, and a large number of well-executed wood-cuts.
