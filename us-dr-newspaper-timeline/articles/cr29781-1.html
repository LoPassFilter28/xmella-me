SANTO DOMINGO.

Signs of Progress—Wonderful Resources of the Island.

The following is from the London Times: Holders of the Santo Domingo loan, who remember that Colonel Mendes, the diplomatic representative of Santo Domingo, expressed before the Foreign Loans Committee the willingness of his government to refund to those entitled the sum of £38,000, which the government had received out of the total proceeds of the loan, will read with interest Major Stuart’s hopeful report upon the resources of the Dominican Government. The report appears among the commercial papers recently issued by the Foreign Office. Major Stuart is Minister Resident at Hayti and Charge d’Affaires to the Dominican Republic. The imports in 1875 were £171,159 in value, the exports £97,936 at the port of Santo Domingo alone. The direct imports from Great Britain amounted to only £3,581, but British manufactures (cotton, hardware, crockery) to the value of £72,000 were imported through St. Thomas and Curacoa. This country is thus the leading state in the trade, the United States coming next with £48,000, while France, the next European state to ourselves, in this branch of commerce only sends goods to the value of £13,731. The principal exports are timber and sugar, and although the timber trade has declined, the result has been to direct labor to more profitable employment. Lignum vitæ, logwood and fustic do, indeed, still occupy a large number of hands, but the sugar-cane and coffee are increasing in favor. France took, in 1875 100,000 pounds of extract of logwood and 323,450 pounds of the some material was shipped to the West Indies, no doubt for re-exportation. From Porto Plata 11,613,320 pounds of leaf tobacco was exported last year, principally to Hamburg.
