From all that the Commissioners could ascertain, President Baez has the respect of the great majority of the Dominican people for his administration and abilities, and the strong attachment of many leading men, who regard him as the only statesman among them who can hold the nation against domestic factions and foreign foes. In the year [illegible], after five years of anarchy, consequently upon the expulsion of Haytien power, General Baez was regularly elected President of the Republic. It appears that he was chosen as the man who, by his education, fortune and public services, would be most likely to secure the general confidence and heal wounds inflicted by civil and foreign wars. In one of the delegations of leading citizens who tendered to him the chief magistracy, was General Cabral. There is credible testimony to the fact that under him, the Republic has enjoyed as much liberty as any of his predecessors dared allow, and more than they knew how to give. Nor do the Commissioners find that there is any opponent to the present administration of the republic who has now, or who has ever had, any claim to the chief magistracy by a title superior to that of the present incumbent.
