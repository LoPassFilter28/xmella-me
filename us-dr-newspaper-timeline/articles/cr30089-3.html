A travel narrative through the interior describes riding into La Vega, in the Vega Real, the royal meadow of Columbus, and looking over a fertile valley stretching between blue mountains. It praises the valley's inexhaustibly fertile soil, its mighty forests of valuable trees, and its cacao, which it calls the best in the world and says has made the island fourth in the world's production.

Un relato de viaje por el interior describe la llegada a caballo a La Vega, en la Vega Real, la real pradera de Colón, y la vista de un valle fértil que se extiende entre montañas azules. Elogia el suelo inagotablemente fértil del valle, sus poderosos bosques de árboles valiosos y su cacao, al que llama el mejor del mundo y dice que ha hecho de la isla la cuarta en la producción mundial.

High in the island on the northern limit of this grand valley is a little hill reverently named by him [Santo Cerro?], the holy hill. Standing on its crest he looked thirty miles eastward over a scene that makes one's eyes misty with admiration and inspiration to this day. From the grandly beautiful blue mountains on the left a score of miles it stretches to the blue and beautiful mountains on the right, while eastward, far as the eye can see, it lies, picturesque with palm-trees, dense with tropical growth and filling with spicy odors the air that quivers.
