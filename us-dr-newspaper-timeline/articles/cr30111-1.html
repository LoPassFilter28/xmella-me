The Trujillo Era & DictatorshipLa Era de Trujillo y la Dictadura · 1924–1960

Rafael Trujillo ruled for 31 years. US papers reported the 1930 hurricane that leveled Ciudad Trujillo, the 1937 Haitian massacre, sugar commerce, WWII U-boat sinkings, Tito Mella's Compañía Naviera Dominicana shipping fleet, and Trujillo's Washington lobbying.Rafael Trujillo gobernó durante 31 años. Funeral Arranged

For Tomorrow

Members of the diplomatic corps and high Government officials will attend funeral services for Don Anselmo Copello, Ambassador from the Dominican Republic, at 11 a.m tomorrow at the Sacred Heart Church, Sixteenth street and Park road N.W. After temporary burial in Arlington Cemetery, the body will be taken to the Dominican Republic.

The Ambassador, who was 65, died yesterday in Doctors Hospital of a heart attack which followed pneumonia. He had been ill two days.

His work was praised in the following statement made yesterday by Secretary of State Stettinius:

“I have been greatly grieved to learn of the death of the distinguished Ambassador of the Dominican Republic. His excellency Anselmo Copella. In my association with him in dealing with the problems of mutual interest, I have come to know his outstanding qualities as a diplomat and as a friend.

“He has represented his country with distinction and has always evinced a most helpful spirit of cooperation in dealing with matters of common concern. The Dominican people will rightly mourn the passing of a gifted statesman and his many friends in this country will sincerely share their sorrow.”

Senor Copello returned to Washington Monday from the Dominican Republic, where he had been on official business for three months. He was accompanied by his only son, Jeronimo.

Before his appointment as Ambassador to this country in November, 1943, Senor Copello had never served as a government official except for several honorary posts. His credentials were received by President Roosevelt on December 20, 1943. He succeeded Dr. J. M. Troncoso, now Secretary of the Treasury of the Dominican Republic.

President of the principal tobacco company of the Dominican Republic, Senor Copello’s home was in Santiago.

Besides his widow, Senora de Copello, and his son, he is survived by four daughters, Adelina, Argentina, Ileana and Margarita. The family lives at the Embassy, 4530 Sixteenth street N.W.

Dr. J. R. Rodriguez, Minister Counselor, will direct the affairs of the Embassy until further arrangements are made by his government.
