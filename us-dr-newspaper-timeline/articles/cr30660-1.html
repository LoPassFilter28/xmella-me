THE New York Sun has discovered something out of which to manufacture a sensation. That paper says, editorially, in its issue of the 11th inst., that the extraordinary preparations now going on in the navy yards are not in expectation of war with Spain about Cuba, but simply to keep guard over the annexation of the Dominican Republic to the United States. The conditions of annexation have been fully settled between Gen. Babcock, confidential officer of Grant's staff, and the authorities of San Domingo. The treaty is similar to that for the annexation of Texas, and will be laid before the Senate when Congress opens. All members of the Dominican government and leading men among the people are anxious for annexation, and the approval of the Senate is confidently expected. The ships of war now going to the West Indies are sent at the request of President Baez and his advisers, so that there may be one or more in each port when the United States flag is hoisted. No trouble is expected from the people generally, and the only opposition would be from a revolutionary faction aided by Cabral. This may be something more than a mere sensation.
