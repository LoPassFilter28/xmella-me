SAN DOMINGO.

A PRONUNCIAMENTO BY GENERAL DON CESAREO GUILLERMO.

HAVANA, May 19.--The Herald's correspondent writes that the general of division in the national army and ex-president of the Dominican Republic, Don Cesareo Guillermo, has issued a revolutionary manifesto addressed to his fellow citizens. The document itself does not show where it was promulgated, and its date can only be presumed from the fact that it bears the impress of April, 1882. General Guillermo claims to have been urgently called upon by many men best known in the national political parties of the country to place himself at the head of the revolution, which he at once initiated in the Eastern province. In consequence of the evils imposed upon the nation through the dictatorship of Merino, which commenced in June, 1881, General Guillermo considers himself justified in the course he has taken, and also in deciding upon forming an expedition to open the campaign. The general refers back to the triumphs which placed the present government in power, and says that if it were possible for him to blot from the pages of history all the abominable acts which have happened since those triumphs, he would efface them at the cost of his life. The manifesto goes on in a strain of fierce denunciation against the obnoxious government which is the object of General Guillermo's wrath. He is not, however, explicit in naming the particular act of misgovernment that might justify revolution. He is no doubt a brave soldier, for his paper says for him: "All Dominicans! Here you have my sword and my life! Here I am, disposed to shed, even to the last drop, my blood, to be the first in danger and the last after victory! Therefore study my words! Long live all the parties regulated by law! Hurrah for national reconciliation!"
