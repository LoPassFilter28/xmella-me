The Evening Star prints an item: W. S. McGunness of Washington was to be the first witness at this afternoon's hearing of the inquiry by Phelan of California into the conduct of James M. Sullivan, minister to the Dominican Republic. McGunness, who has been active in politics, was expected to tell what he knew of the efforts made in Washington by financiers interested in Santo Domingo to have Sullivan appointed, and the appointment confirmed by the Senate. At yesterday's session Walker W. Vick, former receiver general of Dominican customs and instigator of the present investigation, testified: 'One man told me that he had been promised a postmastership if he would 'get me.'' He intimated that Joseph P. Tumulty, secretary to the President, had been responsible for Secretary Bryan's attitude toward him. This had grown out of rivalry between them for Tumulty's present post, he thought. Earl Harding, a newspaperman, who has been investigating Sullivan in behalf of his paper, testified that he had been told that Tumulty had said that 'the White House regarded Vick as a sore-head and that he would have been removed from his position had he not resigned.' Joseph P. Tumulty, secretary to President Wilson, issued a statement today referring to testimony given yesterday by Walker W. Vick, saying he was ready to testify before Mr. Phelan at any time, either here or in New York.The Evening Star publica una nota: W. S. McGunness, de Washington, sería el primer testigo en la audiencia de esta tarde de la investigación de Phelan de California sobre la conducta de James M. Sullivan, ministro en la República Dominicana. Se esperaba que McGunness, que ha sido activo en la política, dijera lo que sabía de los esfuerzos hechos en Washington por financieros interesados en Santo Domingo para que Sullivan fuera nombrado, y el nombramiento confirmado por el Senado. En la sesión de ayer, Walker W. Vick, anteriormente recaudador general de las aduanas dominicanas e instigador de la presente investigación, declaró: «Un hombre me dijo que se le había prometido un puesto de administrador de correos si acababa conmigo». Dio a entender que Joseph P. Tumulty, secretario del Presidente, había sido responsable de la actitud del secretario Bryan hacia él. Esto había surgido de la rivalidad entre ellos por el puesto actual de Tumulty, pensaba. Earl Harding, un periodista, que ha estado investigando a Sullivan en nombre de su periódico, declaró que se le había dicho que Tumulty había dicho que «la Casa Blanca consideraba a Vick un resentido y que habría sido removido de su puesto si no hubiera renunciado». Joseph P. Tumulty, secretario del presidente Wilson, emitió hoy una declaración en referencia al testimonio dado ayer por Walker W. Vick, diciendo que estaba listo para declarar ante el Sr. Phelan en cualquier momento, ya fuera aquí o en Nueva York.

Evening Star. Washington, D.C., January 19, 1915.
