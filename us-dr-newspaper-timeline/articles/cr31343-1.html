The Age-Herald reported that General Horacio Vásquez established a provisional government in Santo Domingo after the capital capitulated following two days of intense combat. President Jimenes took refuge in the French consulate, and victorious insurgent troops occupied the capital without public celebration. On the northern coast, the crew of a Dominican war vessel mutinied in support of the revolution, forcing Governor Deschamps at Puerto Plata to negotiate surrender terms.

El Age-Herald informó que el general Horacio Vásquez estableció un gobierno provisional en Santo Domingo después de que la capital capitulara tras dos días de intensos combates. El presidente Jimenes se refugió en el consulado francés y las victoriosas tropas insurgentes ocuparon la capital sin celebraciones públicas. En la costa norte, la tripulación de un buque de guerra dominicano se amotinó en apoyo a la revolución, obligando al gobernador Deschamps en Puerto Plata a negociar las condiciones de capitulación.

The provisional government established there will retain power until new elections have been held. Peace is completely re-established in the southern part of Santo Domingo. Advices from Puerto Plata, on the north coast, announce that the crew of the Dominican war vessel at that port has sided with the insurgents, and yesterday conveyed to Governor-General Deschamps an order to surrender. An agreement providing for a suspension of hostilities at Puerto Plata was signed later in the day, so as to permit the drawing up of the conditions on which the port will capitulate to the revolutionists. When San Domingo City surrendered to Vice President Vasquez, President Jiminez sought refuge in the French consulate there. San Domingo, Santo Domingo, Friday, May 2.—The Dominican capital capitulated today to the revolutionary forces commanded by General Horacio Vasquez, the vice president of the republic. President Jiminez sought refuge in the French consulate and many government officers obtained protection in the French and United States legations. No Enthusiasm Shown. The revolutionary troops entered the capital quietly. There was no enthusiasm shown by the population here at the entry of the victorious army.
