The Washington grand jurors find probable cause for fearing that Mr Bill King of Minnesota, congressman-elect, has committed an indiscretion known to the law as perjury; and they go for him with a true bill accordingly. King promises by his counsel to face the music. We dare say he will—unless he hears of another fine bull in Canada.

Judges Peck and Loring are not a-going to leave the court of claims at present,—to the great disappointment of half a dozen republican politicians, who were eager for their places.

Vice President Wilson continues to receive distinguished honors in his tour of the Southwest. At Nashville, Tenn., there was a brilliant social party in his honor, attended by distinguished ex-confederates and local officials of every grade. He went on to Arkansas and Texas,—to the latter state to visit the grave of his only son,—and is now in St Louis on his way to Colorado.

Mr Fish is going again. This time, the exact date of his departure for private life is fixed—June 30. A highly eulogistic article in the Washington National Republican “carries the inference unmistakably,” it is thought; and several of Mr Fish's associates in the cabinet are quoted as saying they fear it is too true.

“There is so much lying and stealing,” said Spotted Tail, “I can't tell where it begins; but I know where it ends.” The honest tax-payers of the country will find the remark perfectly intelligible.

Mr Edward Herzberg Hartmont testifies before Mr Lowe's foreign loans committee that, in 1869, he negotiated in London for the Dominican republic a loan of $3,788,500. He received in cash $1,860,445. Of this sum, $250,000 ultimately reached San Domingo. The balance, Mr Hartmont says, was disbursed in London and Washington. How, what for, and to whom?

Ben Hill is elected to Congress from the 9th Georgia district in place of McMillan, deceased.

The Boston News is unkind enough to recall a remark of Arthur Tappan's about one of our distinguished diplomatists: “What do I think of Mr Jay? What can I think of a man who spends his time imitating his grandfather's signature?”

Miss Ann Pamelia Cunningham, the first lady regent of Mount Vernon, died at Rosement, Laurens county, S. C., on Monday last.

President Grant and family, including Mr and Mrs Sartoris, will spend the summer in the Grant cottage at Long Branch. His nearest neighbors will be George W. Childs, ex-Collector Thomas Murphy, and Mr Seligman, the New York banker. Secretary Fish and family will pass the season at their country seat on the Hudson. Secretary Belknap and wife have engaged apartments at the Pequot house, New London.
