A profile of President Heureaux tells how his brother-in-law, the husband of the half sister for whom he had a real affection, was advanced rapidly in the government service, warned once after joining a plot to assassinate the president, and found within six months plotting again. Heureaux gave a state breakfast in the young man's honor, seated him at his side, and as the meal ended told him he was going to be shot in five minutes; the official was then shot in the courtyard while the president's sister screamed from the windows above, and the uncoffined body was quickly buried in a flower garden.

Un perfil del presidente Heureaux cuenta cómo su cuñado, el esposo de la media hermana por quien sentía un verdadero afecto, fue ascendido rápidamente en el servicio del gobierno, advertido una vez tras unirse a un complot para asesinar al presidente, y descubierto en menos de seis meses conspirando de nuevo. Heureaux ofreció un desayuno de Estado en honor del joven, lo sentó a su lado y, al terminar la comida, le dijo que iba a ser fusilado en cinco minutos; el funcionario fue fusilado entonces en el patio mientras la hermana del presidente gritaba desde las ventanas de arriba, y el cuerpo sin ataúd fue enterrado rápidamente en un jardín de flores.

"Heureaux's heartlessness in the discharge of what he believed to be his duty was never better shown than in the execution, for conspiracy, of his brother-in-law. With the president lived a half sister, much younger than he, and the one person in the world for whom he seemed to have a real affection. The girl met and married a young Dominican merchant Heureaux soon took the young man into the government service and advanced him rapidly. But the young official was soon deep in the councils of a group of conspirators who had for their object the assassination of the president and the overthrow of his government. The all-wise secret service soon knew all about it, and [illegible] himself with warning his brother-in-[illegible] Heureaux, for his sister's sake, content[illegible] law that a repetition of his offense would cost him his life. The young man's treachery was kept from the public and was allowed to retain his government position on promise of future good behavior. In less than six months, however, he was again at work plotting against his chief's life, and again his guilt was reported to the president Heureaux carefully concealed his knowledge of the treachery, and issued orders for a state breakfast at his palace. With the noon hour officials of army and state gathered, and with them came the young conspirator. He arrived without his uniform, and Heureaux ordered him to go and don it.
