Diario de las Americas reported that the Canadian and Dominican governments formally agreed to elevate their bilateral diplomatic missions to full embassy status. The issue also noted diplomatic changes regarding Argentine and Panamanian envoys in Mexico. Additionally, the paper profiled Dr. Helene Wallace Toolan of the Sloan-Kettering Institute for pioneering cancer research involving animal tissue growth.

Diario de las Américas informó que los gobiernos de Canadá y la República Dominicana acordaron formalmente elevar sus misiones diplomáticas bilaterales a la categoría de embajadas de pleno rango. La edición señaló asimismo cambios diplomáticos respecto a enviados argentinos y panameños en México. Además, el periódico perfiló a la Dra. Helene Wallace Toolan, del Instituto Sloan-Kettering, por sus pioneras investigaciones oncológicas sobre el cultivo de tejidos animales.

Woman Researcher Marks A First in Cancer Battle NEW YORK —(UP)— She was a brilliant student, graduated from the University of Chicago with top honors when she was 17. Today, a woman in her 30’s she is credited with being the first person to grow human cancer in animals. The result of her work may speed up science's efforts to find a cure for the dread malady. Dr. Helene Wallace Toolan, a small, pretty woman with curly brown hair and grey eyes, is an associate at the Sloan-Kettering Institute research unit of the Memorial Center for Cancer and Allied Diseases. Dr. Toolan, an example of the women of our time who have become VIP’s in fields once considered for men only, works with her six assistants in one of the liveliest laboratories in the Institute. Some 400 animals —hamsters and rats— are treated there each day.
