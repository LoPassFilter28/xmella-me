The Daily Morning Journal and Courier reported that Dominican President Heureaux mobilized three warships after insurgent leader General Jimenes escaped aboard the steamer Fanita following the defeat at Monte Cristi. The report highlighted Jimenes's past role in financing President Hyppolite in Haiti and noted the significant use of a Clyde Line steamer in the conspiracy. It also reported that Heureaux had resumed arbitrary executions and forced disappearances of prominent political opponents.

The correspondent, in concluding his dispatch, says the revolutionary movement is regarded at Puerto Plata as of no consequence. The Dominican consul here at Cape Haytien reasserts positively that General Jimenes escaped on the Fanita. He says that had Jimenes been killed President Heureaux would have informed him of the fact. General Jimenes was regarded as the wheelhorse of the revolution which put Hippolyte in power in Hayti, managing the financial end of the movement. When he was here the last time it was understood that President Heureaux warned him to leave Dominica for his own safety. Last April his principal partner came here from Hamburg to replace him in the direction of the business. The fact that the Clyde company furnished the conspirators with a steamer is regarded as very significant of the completeness of the preparations and the prospect of their success, since the Clyde steamship concession is the most valuable held under the Dominican government. President Heureaux is said to have recently resumed the arbitrary execution of his opponents, sometimes openly, sometimes stealthily.
