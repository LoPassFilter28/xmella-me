The San Antonio Daily Light reported allegations by Dominican exiles that President Heureaux hired assassins who stabbed General Mosco in Ponce, Puerto Rico, to thwart an impending uprising. Mosco survived the attack and garnered backing from determined Dominican exiles in New York who opposed Heureaux's prolonged three-term rule. The exiles expressed confidence in the revolutionary movement following reports from Puerto Plata.

El San Antonio Daily Light informó sobre denuncias de exiliados dominicanos según las cuales el presidente Heureaux contrató sicarios que apuñalaron al general Moscoso en Ponce, Puerto Rico, para frustrar una insurrección inminente. Moscoso sobrevivió al atentado y obtuvo el respaldo de decididos exiliados dominicanos en Nueva York contrarios al prolongado régimen de tres mandatos de Heureaux. Los exiliados manifestaron confianza en el movimiento revolucionario a raíz de los informes procedentes de Puerto Plata.

openly declare their belief that the would-be assassins were in the pay of President Hereaux, the desperate method having been resorted to, they claim, because Mosco was rapidly perfecting his plans to return secretly to San Domingo and start up an uprising which Hereaux wanted to prevent by any means. Mosco's assailants in Ponce came near killing him. He was taken unawares and stabbed once before he realized his danger, and then managed to escape only because one of the men, thinking a fatal blow had been struck, ran away and the other was too scared and bewildered after the flight of his companion to follow up his attack. Mosco is barely thirty-five years of age. He was educated in this country and speaks several languages with fluency. His personal bravery is extolled as highly as his mental accomplishments. He refuses to discuss his plans, but talks readily when President Hereaux's administration is mentioned. He makes no secrecy of his opposition to the president, whom he freely denounces. Dominican exiles are not numerous in New York, but they make up in determination what they lack in numbers. They declare they intend to stake their all in the alleged Mosco movement and base their hopes on recent advises from Porto Plata, San Domingo's chief port, and are confident that it will be successful. Hereaux has been president of San Domingo three terms, a much longer term than any of his predecessors. His enemies declare that he has been able to retain the reigns of power so long because he has been unscrupulous
