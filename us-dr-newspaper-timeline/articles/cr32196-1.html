WITH UNITED STATES FORCES IN SOUTHERN TUNISIA. March 30 (Delayed).—Ivan "Cy" Peterman, correspondent of the Philadelphia Inquirer, became the second American newspaper casualty in the Tunisian campaign today when he broke a wrist diving into a ditch east of El Guetar to escape strafing by a German plane. Mr. Peterman, a former sports columnist, also suffered head cuts when he struck a stone, but his helmet protected him from serious injury. With him in a jeep at the time of the accident were Phil Ault of United Press and Michael Chinigo of International News Service. The other newspaper casualty was William Stoneman of the Chicago Daily News, who suffered a machine gun wound in the hip during the Ousseltia Valley battle late in January. Red Guerrillas Derail Nine Trains in Ukraine By the Associated Press. LONDON, March 31.—The Moscow radio in a broadcast recorded by the Soviet monitor here reported today that Russian guerrillas had derailed 9 enemy trains recently in the Ukraine, destroying 6 locomotives and 87 cars bearing troops and supplies. The broadcast also reported the guerrillas had killed 147 Germans
