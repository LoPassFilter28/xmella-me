The president recognized Carlos Jumion as consul of the Dominican Republic at New York. This was reported alongside various other national and international news items in the February 4, 1887 issue of The Evening Telegram.

El dignatario reconoció a Carlos Jumion en calidad de cónsul de la República Dominicana en Nueva York. La reseña se publicó entre diversas noticias del ámbito nacional y foráneo en la edición del 4 de febrero de 1887 de The Evening Telegram.

The fourth trial of Beckwith, the Austerlitz murderer, has begun. The Pennsylvania senate has passed a bill to submit prohibition to the people. Charles Doyle, of New York, and Frank Hayes of Chicago fought, with bare knuckles. Doyle won. The president has recognized Carlos Jumion, consul of the Dominican Republic at New York. The ghost of Stonewall Jackson is said to haunt the grounds of the military institute at Lexington, Va. Henry Shipman, of New York, in love with his landlady, Mrs. Josephine Mason, shot and killed her. The Suez canal is blocked by the grounding in the channel of a steamer belonging to the Australian Lloyds. Lord Randolph Churchill has gone to Algiers with a view to restoring his health. He expects to be absent a month. Three thousand carpet workers for E. S. Higgins Co., of New York, have obeyed District Assembly 49 and struck. The ice in the South Shrewsbury river has carried away the cable and cut off communication with New York. The letter carriers association has presented a memorial to congress asking that eight hours be made a day's work. At the diplomatic dinner given by the president, eleven varieties of wine were served, but Mrs. Cleveland drank ice water and Apollinaris. Over 200 petitions from different parts of the country were presented by Senator Blair protesting against the running of Sunday mail trains. The president has accepted the resignation of Gen. F. M. B. Young, of Georgia, consul general at St. Petersburg, but has not yet selected his successor,
