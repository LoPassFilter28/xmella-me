A feature on the American occupation reports that 2,600 additional United States Marines, armed with hand grenades and the latest machine guns, are to be sent to San Domingo to cooperate with the 3,000 Marines already there, where Marines have been stationed for three years under a strict censorship that keeps news of the fighting from the United States. It says Dominicans hate the Americans and take shots at the uniform when they can do so safely, that officers would tell of leathernecks driven insane searching the brush for bandits, and that few of the Marines know why they are there beyond a vague idea of a debt of some $20,000,000 and the United States being in charge of the customs.

Un reportaje sobre la ocupación americana informa que 2,600 infantes de marina adicionales de los Estados Unidos, armados con granadas de mano y las ametralladoras más recientes, serán enviados a Santo Domingo para cooperar con los 3,000 infantes de marina ya allí presentes, donde los marines llevan tres años estacionados bajo una censura estricta que impide que las noticias de la lucha lleguen a los Estados Unidos. Dice que los dominicanos odian a los americanos y disparan contra el uniforme cuando pueden hacerlo sin riesgo, que los oficiales contarían de marines enloquecidos buscando bandidos entre la maleza, y que pocos de los marines saben por qué están allí más allá de una vaga idea de una deuda de unos $20,000,000 y de que los Estados Unidos están a cargo de las aduanas.

he knew that he wouldn't stand as much chance as a wax cat in a certain warm climate, he nevertheless hesitated over agreeing to the terms offered by the representative of Uncle Sam — these being unconditional surrender. Just there fate took a hand. The guns which were mounted to cover the city of Puerto Plata had not been fired for some time. The soldiers were aching to start something and they could not understand why the President did not order the war to begin. One man after imbibing more than an ordinary man's share of rum began to cry. With tears coursing down his jetty cheeks he begged to know why this and that was so and why there had been no opportunity for himself and his mates to show their bravery, their skill with the big guns. This thought became an obsession with the lachrymose one. "By [illegible] Colon!" he ejaculated, "must I, whose forebears landed in this paradise of a land with the great discoverer stand by and listen to the orders of the Yankee dogs from another land? Let it not be so!" A few minutes later this man had "begun the war." Under cover of darkness he sidled up to one of the largest guns, loaded the same with an explosive [illegible], and bing! a house or two in the city had been unroofed and confusion came. Hardly had the echo of this shot died away when the war ship in the harbor hopped into action. A salvo of six pounders was sent back into the hills and then to quote one of the marines who [illegible] part in the [illegible], "there was hell to pay."
