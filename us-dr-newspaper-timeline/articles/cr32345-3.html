Others have been murdered in the streets at night, and one during carnival time was stabbed to the heart with a dagger, the mask of his assailant allowing him to get away unnoticed and undetected. In Port-au-Prince a political exile was shot through the heart by an unknown assailant, and in Porto Rico a young journalist, whose writings had become especially obnoxious to Lilis, was stabbed in the street, although not killed. Hundreds of similar cases can be cited as instances of the cruelty and despotism of Lilis, his enemies declare. The advisers of Lilis were few, and his [illegible] while he would attentively listen to any suggestion his friends would make, he generally used his own judgment and carried out his own plans and ideas without confiding the details of them to any one. He was president of the country and managed its interests in every department. He ran congress to suit himself. The supreme court of justice gave its verdicts in accordance with his previously expressed wishes, and so did the local tribunals. He created a divorce law in defiance of the clergy.
