The Seattle Post-Intelligencer reported that an armed insurgent force led by General Pablo Reyes and Horacio Vásquez crossed from Haiti into Dajabón on March 10 to invade the Dominican Republic. Dominican government forces commanded by Governor Anderson repulsed the invasion, resulting in twelve deaths during the combat. Haitian President Florvil Hyppolite cooperated by disarming the retreating insurgents and deporting their leaders aboard the steamer Ozama to Turks Island.

El presidente haitiano Florvil Hyppolite cooperó desarmando a los insurgentes en retirada y deportando a sus líderes a bordo del vapor Ozama hacia las islas Turcas.

AN EPHEMERAL REVOLUTION. How the Late Invasion of San Domingo Was Squelched. NEW YORK, April 2. — Advices from Cape Haytien, under the date of March 26, say: An invasion of Santo Domingo occurred at Dajabon, on the borders between Santo Domingo and Haiti, on March 10. The attacking force consisted of [illegible] men, under the insurgent leaders, Gen. Pablo Reyes, R. Vasquez and others. Two men were killed. The insurgents were repulsed by a force of 200 Dominican soldiers, led by Gov. Anderson, of Samana, and aides, and ten of the Dominicans lost their lives. By order of President Hyppolite, of Haiti, the insurgents were disarmed and brought here. The rebels, including Gens. Pablo Reyes and H. Vasquez and others, were forced to leave the country by being put aboard the steamer Ozama to be left at Turk's island. World's Fair Matters.
