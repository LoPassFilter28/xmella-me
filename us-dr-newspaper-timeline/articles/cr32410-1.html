The Evening Star reported that the United States prepared naval assets to safeguard Dominican customs houses against rebel capture, maintaining the gunboat Petrel in Santo Domingo harbor with other vessels positioned nearby. A new rebellion broke out in the interior between Puerto Plata and Sanchez, where government forces claimed victory in a recent engagement despite rebel forces remaining intact. Officials anticipated further fighting and prepared to deploy additional warships if necessary.

The Evening Star informó que los Estados Unidos alistaron medios navales para impedir que los rebeldes tomaran las aduanas dominicanas, manteniendo el cañonero Petrel en el puerto de Santo Domingo y otros buques emplazados en las proximidades. Una nueva rebelión estalló en el interior entre Puerto Plata y Sánchez, donde las tropas del gobierno se atribuyeron la victoria en un combate reciente a pesar de que las fuerzas rebeldes permanecían intactas. Las autoridades preveían nuevos enfrentamientos y se preparaban para desplegar buques de guerra adicionales si fuere necesario.

Aside from the ordinary charge of the protection of American life and property in case of revolution in the West Indian Islands, there is a special duty incumbent upon the United States resulting from the American control of the customs houses of the republic, which must be prevented from seizure by revolutionists. Therefore arises the policy of stationing American warships at the principal ports when trouble is threatened. But One U. S. Vessel There. At present the sole representative of the American navy in Dominican waters is the gunboat Petrel, in the harbor of Santo Domingo. But the gunboat Wheeling is at Guantanamo, about a day's run distant; the gunboat Tacoma is at Bluefields, Nicaragua, distant three days' steaming, and the protected cruiser Des Moines is at Key West. The last named vessel is on her way from Boston to Vera Cruz and stopped at Key West for coal, but may be easily diverted to Dominican waters. The present revolt centers in the very middle of the republic. Although the rebellious sentiment is supposed to be widely disseminated, the focus is about midway on the road between Puerto Plata, on the north coast, and Sanchez, on the Bay of Samana. News has just reached here that a battle was fought within the past forty-eight hours at a small town in that section. The Dominican government claims victory for its army, but the rebel movement is said to have sustained no serious Setback. Further hostilities are expected. Decision of Officials.
