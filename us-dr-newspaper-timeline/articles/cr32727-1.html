DETAINING THE MADRID.

A CRAFTY RUSE TO SLIP OUT TO SEA.

DOES SHE WANT TO OVERTAKE THE CARONDELET?

Santo Domingo and Hayti, or that portion of the island represented by the Legitime party, were on the verge of war in the Custom House yesterday, and not only did Deputy Collector Charles P. McClelland, who is acting Collector in the absence of Mr. Magone at Washington, act as peacemaker, but it required the full command of his keen observation and his legal ability to avoid making a mistake upon nice technicalities. His suspicions were awakened upon the presentation of a certificate, carefully and shrewdly written, to the effect that "Citizen Leoncio Julia, Dominican Consul at this port, representing his Government," had purchased the steamship Madrid. At the same time an application was made for a clearance of the vessel under her new name, the Conserva, "in command of Captain J. Henry Aukers and twenty-three of a crew, with 400 tons of coal for the use of the ship, and ballast and stores, for Samana, Santo Domingo.

The certificate was signed by Manuel de Jesus Galvan, jr., as "acting secretary" for the Dominican Consul, who also engaged Campbell & Gardiner as the brokers. To the brokers Senor Galvan gave another certificate of sale which was to the effect that the Madrid had been "transferred and sold to Citizen Leoncio Julia, Dominican Consul at this port, as the representative of his Government." Soon after the application for a clearance, Minister Preston with Charles A. de Chambrun, the counsel for the Haytian Legation at Washington for the last fifteen years, appeared to protest against giving the Madrid a clearance. This protest was supported by an affidavit of Counsel de Chambrun.

They maintained that it was evident that the vessel was intended for war purposes against a Government with which the United States is at peace; that the evidence in the Carondelet case proved that the vessel was bought with the money furnished by General Hyppolite, the leader of the Haytian rebels; and that the Dominican officials were aiding and abetting them as proven by the transfer of the Madrid to Mr. Julia, the changing of her name to the Conserva, and the application to clear her from this port under the Dominican flag for Samana, where Consul Julia's brother is the Haytian Consul. The new point made by Mr. de Chambrun was that an alliance had been made between Santo Domingo and the Haytian insurgents.

While the matter was being discussed before Acting Collector McClelland, the crowd of Haytians and Dominicans indulged in some loud and acrimonious talk, but the Acting Collector quieted this and then decided to postpone further consideration of the case until the defective certificates could be corrected. Then Consul Julia was sent for, but even his urgent appeal and flood of apologies, combined with those of his secretary, Captain Aukers and others could not induce Mr. McClelland to construe his interpretation of the language of the two certificates in the interest of the professed new owners of the vessel. The Madrid lay at Pier A, Erie Basin, last night, and was ready to sail at any moment. It was stated that there was a desire to get her away at once to go down the coast and meet the Carondelet in one of the inlets on the New-Jersey shore, and after receiving the guns and ammunition on board the latter to proceed to Samana and there be turned over to the Haytians. The Carondelet is then to put into Newport News, go to Samana and get a cargo of logwood and coffee and return to this port.
