Tho report that tho Haytian Republia sailed yesterday for Now Ifork

Among the passengers on the Saginaw was Dr. Nemour Auguste of Cape Haytien. The Doctor is said to be on his way to Washington, where he will try to persuade President Cleveland to recognize him as the representative of Hippolyte, the anti-Légitimist leader. M. Auguste denies this statement as a matter of course, and says, as all Haytian insurgents do who arrive, that he is on his way to Paris. Other passengers were Joseph and J. E. Hernandez, H. T. Jones, and Thomas Good, but they only confirmed the reports already given. The Saginaw also brought Capt. M. H. Greeno of the English brig M. E. Brady, from Windsor, N. S. With the Captain came his wife, First Mate J. P. Vaughn, and six sailors. The brig left Annapolis on Oct. 13 and was struck by a gale on Oct. 28, and stranded on a Dominican reef shortly after. Capt. Greeno sold his cargo of lumber to a Dominican merchant and made his way to Porto Plata with his wife and crew.

The Captain, his wife, and the crew reached the shore in the brig's boats, losing all but their personal effects. On the morning of the 30th they went on board the wreck and brought some sails ashore, with which they erected temporary tents. On the same day fifty armed Haytians came to the beach and demanded all the shipwrecked crew possessed, threatening to kill them unless everything was given up. A sailor from the brig, who talked Spanish, conciliated the natives somewhat, and they finally gave the crew till the next morning to surrender. Meanwhile, under cover of the night, Capt. Greeno sent a boat's crew to a brig anchored up the coast a few miles, to ask her Captain to assist them. The latter responded promptly by arming his crew, and sending them with sufficient arms to equip the castaway crew, so when the Haytians came back in the morning the tables were turned, and they were told that their opponents were Englishmen and Americans and all well armed. "If you make any attempt to molest us," said the brig's Captain, "we will kill every one of you." The Haytians scattered in a hurry. "I never saw people so scared," said the Captain. Capt. Greeno and his wife made their way to a fishing village, she remaining there until their effects could be transferred to the village. From there they were finally taken in a brig to Santo Domingo city.

The report that the Haytian Republic sailed yesterday for New York in charge of the crew which was sent out for the purpose in the Atlas steamer Alene that sailed for Jamaica on Dec. 8, could not be verified yesterday. Mr. Forwood of Pim & Forwood, the agents of the Atlas line, said yesterday:

"The report came from Charles W. Brooke of 111 Broadway, and no one was more astonished at reading it than myself. Mr. Brooke is not our counsel and I never saw him in my life. Mr. E. P. Wheeler handles most of our law business. It is true that men did go down on the Alene other than the crew, but I do not know that they were intended for the Haytian Republic. They may have been intended to help man the Belize for all I know. They did not pay any passage money, and I cannot say who paid it for them. Several of the men will work as boiler makers for us at Kingston."

A Haytian merchant said yesterday: "The men sent down on the Alene signed their articles at the British Consulate, and agreed to serve on any vessel of the Atlas line. This would bring them under the English flag, and if they are put aboard the Haytian Republic that vessel might fly the flag of Great Britain, and even if she had not left the harbor of Port-au-Prince when the Galena and Yantic arrived, those vessels would not be likely to interfere with her under the circumstances. It is, in fact, a clever coup on the part of the Haytian Government."

Minister Preston, Gen. Contreras, and Lord & Austin knew nothing of any such crew being sent to man the Haytian Republic. Just before sailing in the Galena Mr. Morse, the agent of the Boston owners of the steamer, said that no crew had been sent down, nor would any be sent until after his arrival. If the crew is intended for the Haytian Republic it will be interesting to know what Capt. Compton has to say on the subject.
