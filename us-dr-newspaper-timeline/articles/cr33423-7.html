The report sets out Admiral Porter's opinion of Samana Bay, calling its position beside the Mona Passage of vast importance to the United States, since the power holding it could command the commerce of any nation it was at war with. Porter is quoted praising the bay as the best harbor in the West Indies, with timber, iron, coal and copper for shipbuilding, and the article adds similar English and French opinions, including Sir R. Schomburgk's description of Samana as one of the principal keys to the Isthmus of Central America and the Gulf of Mexico.

The harbor is considered to be one of the finest in the world, and its position, both in a naval and military point of view, is of the highest importance. ADMIRAL PORTER'S OPINION OF SAMANA. As the geographical position of Samana Bay is of vast importance to the United States, in case it should be placed at the disposition of such a Power as France, I deem it worthy of a passing notice. Situated close to the highway of all vessels coming from Europe (the Mona passage) and of most of the vessels from the United States bound to the West Indies, the Power that owned it could command the commerce of any nation with whom they were at war. It contains within itself all the requisites for shipbuilding—the finest timber, iron, coal and copper—and, no doubt, a good geological survey would develop resources in minerals yet unthought of. The bay and harbor are the best in the whole West Indies, and is capable, I am told, of containing the combined fleets of Europe and rendering them perfectly secure against the most destructive tempests. Very great numbers of whales find refuge here in the large bay, where they increase and multiply because not pursued, the government having prohibited all vessels from taking whales in those waters. One American alone has been allowed to fish there, and the government, I think, could be induced to extend the permission to other citizens.
