The grave stipulations that Dominica shall not be subject to this or that hazard or loss by reason of war or minor hostilities, might force a grin on the countenance of an iron statue. Though three times as long as it need be, all the meaning there is in this document accrues to the benefit of the more powerful and smarter of the contracting parties, except so much of Art. III as guarantees to citizens of Dominica (nearly all of them imperfectly bleached niggers) the legal (not political) rights of citizens: but this clause the Dominican Legislature deems too vague and equivocal, to secure the end it meditates, and proposes an amendment according to mulattoes expressly the rights thus assured in general terms. This our Plenipotentiaries will not agree to, and so there is a 'hitch' at present, which Santana is urged to remove by a coup d'état, like Louis Napoleon's; but he has not yet screwed his courage up to that point. We doubt that he ever will. The beauty of this Treaty is strikingly exhibited in Art. XIX, respecting Consuls, which in effect forbids Dominica to accredit her coffee-colored citizens as Consuls in any of the Slave States. With this sweeping reservation, we should say the wiser course would be to say plumply that the United States may accredit as many and such Consuls as they choose to Dominica, but that Dominica shall not send her niggers here as Consuls, especially to the Slave States. Decidedly, this is a great Treaty, and we urge all who are interested in seeing how white folks deal
