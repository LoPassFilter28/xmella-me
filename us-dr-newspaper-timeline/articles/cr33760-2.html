The Evening Star reported on political controversy in Haiti surrounding President Borno's eligibility, his decree extending the Council of State to secure reelection, and extended American financial control under the 1915 treaty. The article also noted heavy outward migration among Haitian farmers, with tens of thousands departing for Cuba and the Dominican Republic. An estimated 150,000 natives had emigrated since the American occupation began, resulting in a local labor scarcity.

El Evening Star informó sobre la controversia política en Haití en torno a la elegibilidad del presidente Borno, su decreto de prórroga del Consejo de Estado para asegurarse la reelección y la extensión del control financiero estadounidense bajo el tratado de 1915. El artículo también señaló una intensa emigración de agricultores haitianos, con decenas de miles partiendo hacia Cuba y la República Dominicana. Se estimaba que unos 150 000 naturales habían emigrado desde el inicio de la ocupación estadounidense, lo que provocó escasez de mano de obra local.

[A?] new constitution was prepared by Assistant Secretary of the Navy Franklin D. Roosevelt and adopted by Haiti, by decree of its President, acting as dictator. The council of State elected President Borno in 1922. It is claimed that he is ineligible to serve as President, since he was the son of alien French, although he was born in Haiti. The decree establishing the council of state was for a term of 10 years, which term has now expired, but President Borno, a candidate to reelect [himself?] next April 12, has decreed its extension for another 10 years, and it is alleged he has filled several vacancies on it with his own relatives and cabinet officials and expects it to reelect him. In the meanwhile the people have not been permitted to vote for a new Assembly, whose constitutional function it is to elect the President. The treaty of 1915 with the United States has been extended by presidential decree until 1936, without coming before our Senate. This extends our control over Haitian finances. The National City Bank is negotiating an additional loan of $16,000,000 to Haiti, the bids for the loan having been advertised by our State Department. In 1922 the Haitian foreign debt was $6,971,874. Haiti has a population of 3,000,000, mostly small farmers. In 1924 there was an emigration of 21,013 from Haiti to Cuba; in 1925 there were upward of 30,000 emigrated to Cuba, and many more to the Dominican Republic.
