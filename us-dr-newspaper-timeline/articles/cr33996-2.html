The Evening Star reviewed the political background of Haiti and the establishment of the Dominican Republic on the shared island. The report detailed decades of political turmoil and violent depositions of Haitian executives that led to the 1915 marine intervention following the murder of President Guillaume Sam. It noted that the treaty signed with Haiti went beyond the Dominican agreement by establishing an American-led constabulary and directing urban public sanitation.

El Evening Star repasó los antecedentes políticos de Haití y el establecimiento de la República Dominicana en la isla compartida. El informe detalló décadas de turbulencia política y violentas deposiciones de mandatarios haitianos que desembocaron en la intervención de los marines en 1915 tras el asesinato del presidente Guillaume Sam. Hizo notar que el tratado firmado con Haití fue más allá del acuerdo dominicano al instaurar un cuerpo policial comandado por oficiales estadounidenses y dirigir el saneamiento público urbano.

"The history of the Republic of Haiti, as well as the 'empire' and 'kingdom,' has been a series of revolutions, assassinations of rulers and general strife between factions in control of the government and those desiring forcibly to gain control. Of [illegible] rulers of Haiti since its first 'Emperor' Dessalines, only four completed their terms of office. Five were assassinated and most of the others were driven from office and even from the country. After a particularly bloody incident in 1915, when President Guillaume Sam was taken from the French embassy and drawn and quartered in the streets of the capital by an infuriated mob, the United States has occupied the island. A treaty was entered into similar to one with the Dominican Republic, but going further in that it provided for an American-officered constabulary, regulation of matters pertaining to arms and ammunition and the appointment of an American sanitary engineer to recommend methods for cleaning up the cities of the republic. The latter were said by globe trotters to offer a greater affront to human nostrils than any other communities in existence. "The entire island of Haiti, with its two republics, has an area of approximately 28,000 square miles — slightly less than that of Ireland or the State of South Carolina. The Republic of Haiti covers an area a trifle greater than that of Massachusetts and has a population of about 2,000,000." Scouts Enough in Senate.
