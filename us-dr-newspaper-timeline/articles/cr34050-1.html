Involving Treaty Relations With Two Countries.

WASHINGTON, Dec. 14.—An important case, involving the treaty relations of the United States with Hawaii and San Domingo, was argued to-day in the United States supreme court. The question presented is the alleged exemption from duty, under the existing tariff, of products of the Dominican republic which are identical in kind with products of the Sandwich islands admitted free of duty under the special act of congress, approved Aug. 15, 1876. About twenty years ago the United States entered into a treaty with the Dominican republic by the terms of which each country agreed not to charge higher import duties on the products of the other than were imposed upon like products from any other country. The importers, who are the plaintiffs in error in this and another similar case, claim exemption from duty for certain sugars and other products imported by them from San Domingo, upon the ground that the articles were of such a character that had they been imported from Hawaii they would have been admitted free of duty under the treaty between the United States and Hawaii and the act of Aug. 15, 1876, enforcing that treaty.
