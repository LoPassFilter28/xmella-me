The Evening Star reported on an embassy social gathering where guests were entertained with demonstrations of the Dominican Republic's national dance. The dance was demonstrated by Beatrice Rodriguez, daughter of the Dominican OAS Ambassador, and Senora Eva Finkel, accompanied by music provided by musicians visiting from New York. The article noted that the dance, originally practiced by rural peasants, was introduced to elite Dominican society twenty-four years earlier by President Rafael Trujillo.

El Evening Star informó sobre una velada social en la embajada donde los invitados fueron agasajados con demostraciones del baile nacional de la República Dominicana. El baile fue demostrado por Beatrice Rodríguez, hija del embajador dominicano ante la OEA, y la señora Eva Finkel, acompañadas por música interpretada por músicos llegados desde Nueva York. El artículo señaló que el baile, practicado originariamente por campesinos, fue introducido en la alta sociedad dominicana veinticuatro años antes por el presidente Rafael Trujillo.

Dr. and Mrs. William A. Morgan and some members of the Embassy staff. Dance Demonstrated. After this sustenance, the dancing began. Demonstrating the spritely steps were Senorita Beatrice Rodriguez, daughter of the Dominican Ambassador to the Organization of American States; and Senora Eva [Finkel?] and their escorts. The dance is between a rumba and a samba and once the rhythm is caught, the rest is easy. Four Dominican boys came down from New York to [illegible] for the evening. The dance is native of the Dominican Republic and is referred to as their national dance. It was first seen only in the country and danced by the peasants, but 24 years ago it was introduced to Dominican society by the present President, General Trujillo. Looking in at the party for a little while were the Moyas’ two sons who are home for the holidays from boarding school. They are Tony, 14, and Raphael, 9. Diplomatic Dining Another diplomatic dinner last evening took on a more formal note. It was given by the Am bassador of New Zealand and Mrs. Munro and was in honor of Sir Clifford Norton, K. C. M. G., and C. V. O., and Lady Norton. Sir Clifford is an alternate representative of the United Kingdom Delegation to the United Nations and he and his wife are in town from New York just for a few days. The black tie party took place at the Em bassy at 8 o’clock. Among those at the dinner were Greek Ambassador Politis, British Ambassador and Lady Makins.
