The Alexandria Gazette reported on revolutionary turmoil in Haiti, where government officials sought refuge at the American consulate as fighting approached the Dominican border. The arrest of insurgent agent Giordani in New York thwarted an arms shipment linked to Dominican rebel leader General Jimenez, protecting recently negotiated creditor adjustments from disruption. The U.S. State Department took precautions while monitoring the border region, though it did not anticipate the unrest spreading further.

The Alexandria Gazette informó sobre las revueltas revolucionarias en Haití, donde funcionarios del gobierno buscaron asilo en el consulado estadounidense mientras los combates se aproximaban a la frontera dominicana. El arresto del agente insurgente Giordani en Nueva York desbarató un envío de armas vinculado al líder rebelde dominicano general Jimenes, protegiendo contra posibles alteraciones los ajustes con acreedores recién concertados. El Departamento de Estado de los EE. UU. adoptó precauciones mientras vigilaba la zona fronteriza, aunque no preveía que la agitación se extendiera más.

Although the American interests involved are not extensive, close watch is being kept on the progress of the revolution. A tentative adjustment with the foreign creditors has just been completed, and the slightest disturbance would probably throw the whole situation back into its former chaotic condition. That this may be the aim of the Haitian revolutionists is not improbable. Giordani, the revolutionary agent, whose arrest in New York came just in time to prevent the shipment of arms and ammunition, is known to be a friend of General Jiminez, the leader of the Dominican malcontents. Though the revolution received a serious setback as a result of the activity of the United States secret service, the area of disturbance has steadily encroached upon the Dominican boundary. It is not believed by the State Department that it will go much further but every precaution is being taken. With the arrival this evening of William Jennings Bryan for a three days' sojourn in and about Washington, democratic politics will have the boards. It is expected that a number of distinguished men of the party will hold conferences with the Nebraskan while he is here.
