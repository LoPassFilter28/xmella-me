The Evening Star reported that a mob breached the Dominican legation in Port-au-Prince and executed General Oscar in retribution for his mass killings of political prisoners during a rebel assault on the presidential palace. His corpse remained outside the Dominican mission while demonstrators threatened to storm the French legation to capture fleeing President Guillaume. U.S. Rear Admiral Caperton sailed aboard the cruiser Washington with an expeditionary force of marines and sailors to restore security in the Haitian capital.

The Evening Star informó que una turba asaltó la legación dominicana en Puerto Príncipe y ejecutó al general Oscar en represalia por la matanza indiscriminada de prisioneros políticos durante un asalto rebelde al palacio presidencial. Su cadáver quedó tendido frente a la misión dominicana mientras los manifestantes amenazaban con irrumpir en la delegación de Francia para apresar al presidente en fuga Guillaume. El contralmirante de los EE. UU. Caperton zarpó a bordo del crucero Washington con un contingente expedicionario de infantes de marina y marineros para restablecer la seguridad en la capital haitiana.

general outbreak of indignation. A mob invaded the Dominican legation, where Gen. Oscar took refuge after his unsuccessful defense of the presidential palace against the rebels, seized Oscar and dragged him before the door of the legation, where he was promptly executed In his turn by a fusillade of rifle shots. The mob, excited to a great degree, then turned to the French legation and threatened to Invade this building to compass the execution of President Guillaume. Guillaume took refuge under the flag of France yesterday morning after he had been compelled to flee before the rebel attack. Why Executions Were Ordered. Gen. Oscar ordered the execution of the political prisoners in his power as soon as he perceived that the revolutionary outbreak was a serious menace. It was in March, 1908, that a similar execution of political prisoners, though not by any means of so great an extent, took place. Nord Alexis then was president of Haiti. It was declared by his officials that those so executed were engaged in a revolution, but a few months later Nord Alexis was deposed and deported from the country. The body of Gen. Oscar today remained unburied before the consulate of the Dominican Republic. U. S. Cruiser Washington Sails for Cape Haitien With Expeditionary Force.
