The Indianapolis Times reported that a major rebellion had broken out against Dominican President Horacio Vásquez, prompting his wife to take shelter at the American Legation. Insurgents captured the fort and police station at Santiago de los Caballeros and took control of Dajabón near the Haitian border after disarming local authorities. The uprising was reported to be fueled largely by severe economic distress resulting from the sugar industry depression.

El Indianapolis Times informó del estallido de una importante rebelión contra el presidente dominicano Horacio Vásquez, obligando a su esposa a refugiarse en la legación estadounidense. Los insurgentes tomaron la fortaleza y la estación de policía de Santiago de los Caballeros y asumieron el control de Dajabón, cerca de la frontera haitiana, tras desarmar a las autoridades locales. Trascendió que el alzamiento estuvo alimentado en gran medida por la grave penuria económica provocada por la depresión en la industria azucarera.

It is a coincidence that the commission appointed recently by President Hoover to investigate conditions in Haiti and recommend a permanent policy sails just as revolt flares up in the sister republic. Reports received by the state department from Minister Charles B. Curtis indicated rebellion against the Vasquez government and said Senora Vasquez had sought safety in the American legation. Rebels Sweep Country By United Press PORT AU PRINCE, Haiti, Feb. 25. — An insurrection of serious proportions has upset the administration of President Horacio Vasquez of the Dominican republic and is sweeping through the country, according to meager information reaching here through strict censorship today. Telegrams indicated rebels have seized the fort and police station at Santiago de Los Caballeros, the principal town in the tobacco district of the republic, and that the Dominican town, Dajabon, near the Haitian border, is in the hands of rebels. At Dajabon the municipal police have been disarmed and administration sympathizers rendered helpless. According to information received here, the revolution is due principally to depression in the sugar business.
