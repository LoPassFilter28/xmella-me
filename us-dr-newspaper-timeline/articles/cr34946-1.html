The Springfield Weekly Republican reported that former President Juan Isidro Jimenes traveled to Monte Cristi to seek the presidency with the backing of fugitive President Morales and broad northern support. The paper profiled insurgent leader General Demetrio Rodríguez, an American-educated commander who had previously controlled customs revenues in Monte Cristi before being defeated by General Céspedes. It noted that Céspedes, originally a key supporter of Morales, had turned against the president in fierce opposition to the fiscal convention with the United States.

El Springfield Weekly Republican informó que el expresidente Juan Isidro Jimenes viajó a Monte Cristi para aspirar a la presidencia con el respaldo del presidente prófugo Morales y un amplio apoyo en el norte. El periódico reseñó al líder insurgente, general Demetrio Rodríguez, un militar educado en los Estados Unidos que anteriormente había controlado los ingresos de aduanas en Monte Cristi antes de ser derrotado por el general Céspedes. Señaló además que Céspedes, quien en un principio fue un firme partidario de Morales, se volvió contra el mandatario en feroz oposición a la convención fiscal con los Estados Unidos.

Gen Jiminez, the former president of San Domingo, has left Puerto Plata for Monte Cristi, and, it is understood, will, with the assistance of Morales, compete for the presidency of the republic against the other candidates for that office. According to the reports brought to Cape Haytien by messengers, a large part of the population of northern San Domingo is in Favor of Gen Jiminez. Gen Demetrio Rodriguez had long been a prominent factor in the affairs of San Domingo. He was a graduate of Fordham college. New York, spoke English fluently, and was described as an affable and scholarly man and a born leader of men. He resided about 25 miles from Monte Christi; and was considered to be one of the most formidable of Morales's opponents. At the close of the last revolution Rodriguez only consented to lay down his arms under the promise of being allowed to collect the customs and other revenues in the province of Monte Christi. Gen Cespedes, who defeated Gen Rodriguez, was one of the leading men in the movement which placed Morales in the presidential chair of San Domingo, but Cespedes quarreled with Morales on account of the latter's transactions with the American government, and when the enemies of Morales became active Cespedes hastened to join issue with them in their opposition to the Morales government and the fiscal agreement between the United States and San Domingo.
