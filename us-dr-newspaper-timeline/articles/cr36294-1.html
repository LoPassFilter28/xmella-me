According to The New York herald on April 19, 1861, three members of the Dominican cabinet orchestrated a transfer of power to Spain through a coup, supported by secret agents landed from the war steamer Don John de Austria and Spanish vessels arriving from Cuba and Porto Rico with paid emigrants, arms, and soldiers. Cuando las autoridades locales inquirieron sobre estos desembarcos, se les contestó oficialmente que la tropa llegaba para ayudar a rechazar invasiones haitianas y los colonos para labrar la tierra, explicaciones que la población terminó por creer al cabo de varios meses sin que se registraran nuevos movimientos.

This settled the question as far as the two governments went; but, then, what to do with the Dominican people?tomar el país por soberanía ocupante si España no lo hacía. Esto resolvió la cuestión en cuanto a los dos gobiernos iban; pero, entonces, ¿qué hacer con el pueblo dominicano?

Three members of the Cabinet took charge of that. The transfer was to be effected by a coup de main, and after that, the resistance of the people could be crushed out by Spanish artillery in legal form, and, as General Scott would say, "According to the science of war." The war steamer Don John de Austria was despatched from Cadiz early last summer to begin the game by landing at St. Domingo no less than ninety secret agents to hunt up proselytes, help set them at work, and report progress to the 'mother government." Spanish vessels of war were continually coming and going between Cuba, St. Domingo and Porto Rico, and with every visit the Spanish members of the Cabinet manifested an increased taste and means for the costly luxuries of rank. Vessel after vessel came in loaded with Spanish subjects, their passage and three months' provisions being paid by government.
