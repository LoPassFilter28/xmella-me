Washington, Jan. 12.—The Government probably will take cognizance of the troubles in San Domingo and will take such action as is necessary for the protection of American interests there. Thus far, however, the State Department has not heard from Consul-General Maxwell on the reports that the Dominican Government has resumed the direction of its own fiscal affairs, thus terminating the system by which an American syndicate collected the duties and paid the debts of the Government. This plan of private management was adopted some years ago, and it is understood that some Belgian bond owners raised an objection to it, this objection terminating in the present course of the authorities. The State Department will, however, await Information from Consul-General Maxwell before taking action on the matter. Messrs. Holls, Abbott and Ward, of New-York, representing the San Domingo Improvement Company, have held long conferences with Acting Secretary Hill and Solicitor Penfield, of the State Department, concerning the reported repudiation of the company's grant by San Domingo.
