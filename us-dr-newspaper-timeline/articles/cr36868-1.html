The Billings Gazette described the tense diplomatic negotiations that preceded the surrender of Santo Domingo to General Horacio Vásquez. United States Minister Powell and representatives from France, Spain, Germany, Italy, and Great Britain met with Vásquez to enforce the rules of war and protect non-combatants. Although Vásquez initially pledged a temporary ceasefire, his forces swiftly encircled the capital, prompting the diplomatic delegation to mediate formal surrender terms.

La Billings Gazette describió las tensas negociaciones diplomáticas que precedieron a la rendición de Santo Domingo ante el general Horacio Vásquez. El ministro de los Estados Unidos Powell y representantes de Francia, España, Alemania, Italia y Gran Bretaña se reunieron con Vásquez para hacer cumplir las leyes de la guerra y proteger a los no combatientes. Aunque Vásquez prometió en un principio un alto el fuego temporal, sus fuerzas cercaron rápidamente la capital, lo que llevó a la delegación diplomática a mediar en los términos formales de la rendición.

When the revolutionists were sighted Minister Powell called a meeting of the diplomatic and consular bodies at the United States legation for the purpose of deciding on the best means for protecting life and property, and to prevent injury to non-combatants in case of an assault being made on San Domingo, with the result that a delegation composed of the United States minister, the French minister, M. De Jouax; the Spanish consul, Dr. Perera Blesa; the German consul, Herr Von Krosigk; the Italian consul, Signor Cammbiaso, and the British consul, Mr. Gosling, was appointed to wait on General Vasquez and inform him that no attack could be made on the capital under the rules of war until due notice had been given to the diplomatic and consular representatives here. A messenger was then dispatched to General Vasquez, asking him to select the time and place for an Interview with the delegation. The messenger returned with the reply that the revolutionary general had appointed the following morning for the interview, adding that in the meantime he would suspend all hostilities, if the government would agree to adopt the same course.
