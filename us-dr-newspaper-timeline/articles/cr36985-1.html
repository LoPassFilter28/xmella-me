1851-04-17 · New York [N.Y.] · 1844-1861 Independence, recognition, Samana schemes

From Washington. REPORTS ON AWARDS ON THE MEXICAN CLAIMS—MAJOR DONELSON'S SALVATORY—THE WORLD'S FAIR.

SourceFuente: The New York herald, New York [N.Y.], 1851-04-17

The Commissioners on Mexican Claims have this morning completed their awards. They amount to forty thousand dollars less than the sum appropriated to meet them, of one million and a quarter of dollars.

Major Donelson publishes a letter in the Union this morning, three columns and a half long, to the editor of the Union, upon assuming the editorial control of that journal. The Major endeavors to make the compromise a tower of patriotism above the ordinary spirit of party; but says that 'if the ultra Democratic party of this nation can be brought together in no other way than by an abandonment of the Union, or a compromise with the enemies of the compromise, we can never consent to buy peace on such terms.' He thinks the Democratic party, nevertheless, will unite on a constitutional basis, and elect the next President.

The intelligence brought by the last arrival from England, relative to the movement of British naval force, is considered to have a direct bearing upon the movements of Southern adventurers. It is supposed that the British vessels which have been dispatched may first meet the West India fleet somewhere near the shores of St. Domingo, for the purpose of obtaining redress, instant and immediate, from the Emperor of the black republic; while the adventurers from the Southern States may effect a landing on Dominica, and unite with those forces to assault the black republic in the flank. It is very true that our government at Washington can't declare war, or commence hostilities, without an act of Congress, but it can connive at the movement of a body of armed men, who may choose to emigrate to Dominica.

The intelligence brought by a letter from Mr. Riddle, United States Commissioner to the World's Fair, relative to the space allotted to the United States, is believed to be very onerous and expensive. The custom house commission on each package amounted to nearly $2, besides which, all the duties, tares, draperies, &c., for the display of goods, have to be provided by each nation, and those of other countries are very brilliant. He has had to employ a number of clerks to make out a descriptive catalog. Among these, he says, he is behind hand, owing to the late hour at which goods arrived.

The charge against Collector Lewis, of Philadelphia, after a full investigation by the President and Secretary of the Treasury, has been dismissed.

The Hon. John M. Clayton arrived here yesterday.
