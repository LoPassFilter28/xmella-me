The Phillipsburg Herald reviewed the military career of General Máximo Gómez, noting his early service with Spanish forces during the Dominican annexation before becoming a leader in Cuba's ten years' war for independence. In 1887, Gómez returned to Santo Domingo to cultivate tobacco in Guayubín near Monte Cristi, where his widespread popularity quickly aroused political suspicion. Fearing that Gómez harbored presidential ambitions and planned an insurrection, President Heureaux secretly arranged for his arrest and execution.

El Phillipsburg Herald repasó la carrera militar del general Máximo Gómez, destacando su servicio inicial en las fuerzas españolas durante la anexión dominicana antes de convertirse en un caudillo de la guerra de los Diez Años por la independencia de Cuba. En 1887, Gómez regresó a Santo Domingo para cultivar tabaco en Guayubín, cerca de Monte Cristi, donde su inmensa popularidad despertó pronto suspicacias políticas. Por temor a que Gómez albergara ambiciones presidenciales y tramara una insurrección, el presidente Heureaux dispuso secretamente su arresto y ejecución.

an annexationist. In the early '60's,' when Santo Domingo was annexed to Spain, through the intrigue of Gen. 'When the war of the restoration began he took sides with the Spaniards instead of the patriots. The patriots won, the Spaniards were forced to capitulate, and Gomez, with other Dominicans, retired to Havana, with the title of Mariscal de Campo. "Gomez soon grew tired of Spanish rule, and when the ten years' war for Cuban independence began, under the leadership of Maceo and others, Gomez was one of the leading spirits who battled for Cuban independence. After the capitulation his political life was one of adventure and uncertainty. "In [illegible] Gomez seemingly tired of exile. Perhaps it was to delude the Spanish that he came to Santo Domingo and settled in the district "of Monte Christi and began the cultivation of tobacco in Guayabin. near the Haytian frontier. He became at once a popular idol of a large number of Dominicans, which caused some political jealousies. "Gen. Heureaux, who was then the president of the republic, became suspicious of Gomez political aspirations, and his arrest was ordered. Private advices from Guayabin announced that Gomez was aspiring to the presidency, and an uprising was imminent to usurp the reins of the government. The oppositionists began to be so active that Gen. Heureaux was seriously concerned, and the imprisonment and execution of Gomez were planned.
