A search started, and airports along both Florida coasts were checked without success, as were other Gulf areas. Meanwhile no Washington agency could be found that would admit ordering the alert.

Then, yesterday afternoon, the State Department acknowledged it had heard the rumor and said it had relayed it to the Customs Bureau and other agencies for investigation.

The statement disclosed that the State Department has had in effect for months a general alert, based on “general rumors of revolutionary plotting,” to make sure that “United States territory would not be used for conspiracy.”

Rumor Involves 7 Planes.

The statement, issued by Press Officer Reginald Mitchell, said the State Department has been receiving “rumors” and “fragmentary reports” of impending trouble in the Caribbean area but has declined to comment because of their unsubstantial nature. The statement continued:

“One such rumor was to the effect that there were seven combat planes at an airfield in Florida ready to depart for a Caribbean destination. The department Friday immediately communicated this rumor to the Customs Bureau and other United States Government agencies concerned and requested that an investigation be made and that these alleged planes be prevented from leaving the United States.

“The department has been informed that the Customs Bureau promptly notified its Florida headquarters office at Tampa which in turn alerted its subordinate customs offices at Miami, Jacksonville, West Palm Beach and elsewhere in Florida. The department has received no further information in this particular matter.

“The department heard general rumors of revolutionary plotting in the Caribbean area as long ago as January of this year and in an effort to make sure that United States territory would not be used for conspiracy alerted the appropriate law enforcement agencies, which since that date have kept a scrupulous watch.”

Easy Flight for Fighters.

At least some of the rumors were based on reports in Haitian and Dominican newspapers.

The P38 Lightning and the P51 Mustang, Army fighter planes extensively used in the war, would easily be able to fly from Florida to the Dominican Republic. The shortest airline distance is a little over 650 miles. There are the Bahama Islands and the tip of Cuba in between.

A War Department official said some Lightnings and Mustangs have been sold as surplus and may be in the hands of civilians.

“There are plenty of young fellows in this country who know how to fly these ships,” he said.

The Dominican Ambassador, Julio Ortega Frier, told a reporter some days ago that he knew the revolutionaries had at least seven planes. He said yesterday he didn’t know where these planes were bought.

Reported Sailing.

The Ambassador said last Saturday that an army of 3,000 “Communist revolutionaries” from Cuba, Guatemala, Venezuela and Puerto Rico was poised in Cuba for an invasion. The governments concerned denied it.

Thursday night he said two landing craft and one corvette had sailed from Cuba with invasion forces, but he had no further word on this.

The Ambassador said a call to Ciudad Trujillo produced this information:

“The government has air patrols around the coast. Everything is calm in Ciudad Trujillo and not a single aircraft has been heard in the sky. Nevertheless, the government is taking all precautions.”

Haitians returning from the Dominican Republic were quoted in Port-au-Prince newspapers as saying they left because of apprehension over heavy troop movements throughout the republic, which shares the island of Haiti.
