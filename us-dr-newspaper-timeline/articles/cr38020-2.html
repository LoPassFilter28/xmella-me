The Evening star reported on trade and economic conditions in Haiti, citing American Chamber of Commerce statistics showing that annual exports reached over $21,000,000 before fluctuating with post-war global market cycles. The article noted that annual import duties averaged over $2,100,000 and pointed out the country's extensive undeveloped natural resources. It also observed that the 1916 constitution modified previous bans on foreign land ownership, allowing foreigners to acquire property for commerce, industry, or agriculture.

Haitian business — exports and imports — like that of other countries, is affected by world market conditions, and no one need be reminded that the world has had both fat and lean years since the world war. Prior to American intervention exports had been paralyzed by non-production, due to the anarchistic conditions in Haiti. Haiti has an area of 10,204 square miles and a population of 2,500,000, mostly negroes. The dominant language is French. It has great natural resources, not well developed. Until the new constitution was adopted, in 1916, no foreign-born person could hold property in that country. The predominating colored people excluded whites as rigidly as we are about to exclude the orientals. The new constitution modifies that exclusion somewhat and permits foreigners to own property needed for the carrying on of commercial or manufacturing enterprises or for farming.
