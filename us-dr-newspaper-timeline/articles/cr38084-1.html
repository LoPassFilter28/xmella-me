The Salt Lake Herald analyzed congressional resolutions seeking commercial data on reciprocity agreements with the Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, and other Latin American nations. The editorial questioned the value of these trade pacts, arguing that they benefited coastal manufacturers while offering little advantage to Western farmers and merchants.

The Salt Lake Herald analizó las resoluciones del Congreso que solicitaban datos comerciales sobre acuerdos de reciprocidad con la República Dominicana, Puerto Rico y otras naciones de América Latina. El editorial cuestionó el valor de estos pactos comerciales, argumentando que beneficiaban a los fabricantes costeros al tiempo que ofrecían escasas ventajas a los agricultores y comerciantes del oeste.

revolution is coming on and we may have hopes of better things hereafter. THE TRADE TREATIES. Representative Stone of Kentucky has introduced in Congress a resolution which was adopted calling upon the secretary of the treasury for a tabular statement of all goods imported into Porto Rico and the Dominican republic under the operation of the reciprocity treaty, and upon the secretary of state for copies of all communications between the United States and Spain, San Salvador and Brazil on the subject of reciprocal trade relations with the United States. The aim of this is to ascertain whether there has been any practical benefit to our people from reciprocity as applied to these nations. The scope of the proposed inquiry is a limited one. It might very well be applied to all the Central and South American countries and no doubt will eventually be extended to embrace the whole of them. There is little doubt that this country is paying or is proposing to pay dear for the much-vaunted reciprocity whistle, and that the reciprocal trade relations mean a market for manufactures and products of our coast states without material benefits to western agriculturists, merchants and people generally. The latest treaty is with San Salvador, and gives it free of duty all kinds of machinery, agricultural implements, lumber, wagons, iron and steel products, furnaces, building material, coal, fruits, cereals and preparations of flour, all of which when imported into the United States are taxed from 35 to 100 per cent.
