The Hawaiian Star

The Star prints a dispatch from Cape Haytien on the Santiago capture:

October 27, 190327 de octubre de 1903

The Star prints a dispatch from Cape Haytien on the Santiago capture: the Santo Domingo rebels by a vigorous move have captured the town of Santiago; the rebels are moving with surprising rapidity in their attacks upon the chief towns of the country. Santiago, the Dominican city which has fallen into the hands of the rebels, is the most populous inland city of Santo Domingo; it is 85 miles from the capital, Santo Domingo, and is situated in the most thriving and richest portion of the republic. The dispatch, an Associated Press dispatch dated October 26, appears under the head 'Revolution in Hayti.'

El Star publica un despacho de Cabo Haitiano sobre la captura de Santiago: los rebeldes de Santo Domingo, con un movimiento vigoroso, han capturado la población de Santiago; los rebeldes se mueven con sorprendente rapidez en sus ataques contra las principales poblaciones del país. Santiago, la ciudad dominicana que ha caído en manos de los rebeldes, es la ciudad del interior más populosa de Santo Domingo; está a 85 millas de la capital, Santo Domingo, y está situada en la parte más próspera y rica de la república. El despacho, un despacho de Associated Press fechado el 26 de octubre, aparece bajo el titular «Revolution in Hayti».

The Wichita Daily Eagle

The Eagle prints the closing part of a Paris dispatch and a Cape Haytien dispatch of October 25:

October 27, 190327 de octubre de 1903

The Eagle prints the closing part of a Paris dispatch and a Cape Haytien dispatch of October 25: the revolutionary movement in the republic of Santo Domingo is in favor of former President Jiminez; the revolution is spreading and, according to the dispatches received here, all the inhabitants of the region of Monte Cibao have joined the uprising against the government; and the election of General Morales as president of the provisional government is said to be a step taken pending the arrival of General Jiminez, who will be elected president of the republic if the revolution is successful. The Cape Haytien dispatch says the town of Santiago in the Dominican republic was surrounded this morning by insurgent troops under the command of General Epifanio Rodriguez; after severe fighting, which lasted for several hours, and during which a number of men were wounded, the revolutionists triumphed and Santiago fell into their hands. The signal for the outbreak of the revolt was given at 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots; that same evening all the partisans of President Woss y Gil at Puerto Plata were arrested; the inhabitants of Monte Cristi, La Ligne and Moca united and attacked Santiago; and telegraphic communication between Santiago and La Vega has been severed.

El Eagle publica la parte final de un despacho de París y un despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 25 de octubre: el movimiento revolucionario en la república de Santo Domingo es a favor del expresidente Jiménez; la revolución se extiende y, según los despachos recibidos aquí, todos los habitantes de la región de Monte Cibao se han unido al levantamiento contra el gobierno; y se dice que la elección del general Morales como presidente del gobierno provisional es un paso dado a la espera de la llegada del general Jiménez, quien será elegido presidente de la república si la revolución triunfa. El despacho de Cabo Haitiano dice que la población de Santiago, en la república dominicana, fue rodeada esta mañana por tropas insurgentes bajo el mando del general Epifanio Rodríguez; tras una lucha severa, que duró varias horas, y durante la cual varios hombres resultaron heridos, los revolucionarios triunfaron y Santiago cayó en sus manos. La señal para el estallido de la revuelta se dio a las 2 de la tarde del sábado con tres cañonazos; esa misma noche todos los partidarios del presidente Woss y Gil en Puerto Plata fueron arrestados; los habitantes de Monte Cristi, La Ligne y Moca se unieron y atacaron Santiago; y la comunicación telegráfica entre Santiago y La Vega ha sido cortada.

October 27, 190327 de octubre de 1903

The Times prints the Santiago dispatch from its account of the causes of the revolution: the revolution which has broken out in the northern part of the republic of Santo Domingo and which has already resulted in the establishment of a provisional government under the presidency of General Morales at Puerto Plata was, according to advices received here, caused by the numerous custom house frauds and the prevarications of the ministers of war and of finance. The signal for the outbreak of the revolt was given at 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots. That town is now surrounded by revolutionists, who have severed telegraphic communication between Santiago and La Vega. The revolutionary movement is extending. Vice President Deschamps has sought safety in flight and is said to be hidden in Monte Cristi, where he is seeking an opportunity to embark on board some vessel bound for a foreign port. The general opinion is that the government of President Woss y Gil is lost. General Woss y Gil headed the revolution which broke out in Santo Domingo in March of the present year and resulted in the overthrow of Horacio Vasquez; Gil was elected president in June, and in July the Dominican congress ratified his election. The present revolution is said to be in favor of former President Jiminez, who was ousted in May, 1902, by the revolutionary forces commanded by Vasquez, at that time the vice president.

Esa población está ahora rodeada por los revolucionarios, quienes han cortado la comunicación telegráfica entre Santiago y La Vega. El movimiento revolucionario se extiende. El vicepresidente Deschamps ha buscado la seguridad en la fuga y se dice que está escondido en Monte Cristi, donde busca una oportunidad de embarcarse en algún buque con destino a un puerto extranjero. La opinión general es que el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil está perdido. El general Woss y Gil encabezó la revolución que estalló en Santo Domingo en marzo del presente año y que resultó en el derrocamiento de Horacio Vásquez; Gil fue elegido presidente en junio, y en julio el congreso dominicano ratificó su elección. Se dice que la presente revolución es a favor del expresidente Jiménez, quien fue expulsado en mayo de 1902 por las fuerzas revolucionarias comandadas por Vásquez, en ese entonces el vicepresidente.

The Republic prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution

October 28, 190328 de octubre de 1903

The Republic prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution and a Washington item of October 27 headed 'Explanation Due': the leaders of the revolution are stated not to be in need of supplies, and it is the general opinion that the government of Woss-y-Gil is lost. The Washington item says a full explanation of the stopping of the American mail steamer Cherokee at Porto Plata by a Dominican warship will to-morrow be demanded of the Dominican Republic by the State Department; an American cruiser or gunboat will meantime be ordered to Porto Plata and prevent further arbitrary interference with commerce, either by so-called blockades or otherwise. Up to the time a Republic dispatch stating the Cherokee had been turned back at Porto Plata was shown to a State Department official to-night the department had received no advices regarding this occurrence; the only advices received to-day came from Minister Powell at Santo Domingo City and stated that Porto Plata was in a state of revolution. From this it is inferred the Dominican government has instituted a blockade with the idea of cutting off the revolutionists' source of supplies. According to international law, however, a blockade, to be recognized by other powers, must be preceded by a formal notification of its institution. The tendency of the Central American and West Indian governments, particularly Haiti and Santo Domingo, has been so arbitrary in this regard that no blockade not preceded by formal notification long enough to exempt ships sailing before notice is given will be tolerated by the United States government.

El Republic publica las palabras finales de un despacho sobre la revolución y una nota de Washington del 27 de octubre titulada «Explanation Due»: se afirma que los líderes de la revolución no necesitan suministros, y es la opinión general que el gobierno de Woss-y-Gil está perdido. La nota de Washington dice que mañana se exigirá a la República Dominicana, por conducto del Departamento de Estado, una explicación completa de la detención del vapor correo estadounidense Cherokee en Porto Plata por un buque de guerra dominicano; entretanto, un crucero o cañonera estadounidense será enviado a Porto Plata para impedir nuevas interferencias arbitrarias con el comercio, ya sea mediante supuestos bloqueos o de otro modo. De ello se infiere que el gobierno dominicano ha instituido un bloqueo con la idea de cortar la fuente de suministros de los revolucionarios. Según el derecho internacional, sin embargo, un bloqueo, para ser reconocido por otras potencias, debe ir precedido de una notificación formal de su institución. La tendencia de los gobiernos centroamericanos y de las Indias Occidentales, particularmente Haití y Santo Domingo, ha sido tan arbitraria a este respecto que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos no tolerará ningún bloqueo que no haya sido precedido por una notificación formal con antelación suficiente para eximir a los buques que zarparon antes de que se diera el aviso.

Evening Star

The Star prints part of an account of the exiles' expedition

November 16, 190316 de noviembre de 1903

The Star prints part of an account of the exiles' expedition from Cuba: the exiles decided to co-operate with a general uprising along the frontier of Haiti, taking in the towns of Monte Cristy, Dajabon and Guayubin, and extending to the towns of Santiago de los Caballeros and La Vega, having received notification from the governor of Puerto Plata, Gen. and ex-Priest Don Carlos Morales, of the result of their plans. Then they prepared to take the Cuban steamer Maria Herrera with a view of landing in Puerto Plata, which was held by Gen. Carlos Morales, and so join the revolutionary movement. Unfortunately the Dominican consul in Cuba became aware of their movements and applied to the Cuban authorities to have them prevented from embarking, but he was informed by them that as there was no evidence that these men were taking arms and war stores, and as they were leaving the country as peaceable citizens, they could not be interfered with. The Dominican consul then telegraphed to the president of the Dominican republic at the city of Santo Domingo informing him of their having embarked for some port of the republic, presumably Puerto Plata. Under the head 'Took Passage for San Juan,' the account continues: the Dominican exiles, being perfectly aware of the uncertainty of such uprisings, took passage for San Juan, Porto Rico, intending to disembark in Puerto Plata if all were well, but if not to continue to Porto Rico and join ex-President Jiminez. Upon arriving off the port of Puerto Plata a Dominican warship was sighted, but no attention was paid to her, and they continued their course.

El Star publica parte de un relato de la expedición de los exiliados desde Cuba: los exiliados decidieron cooperar con un levantamiento general a lo largo de la frontera de Haití, que abarcaba las poblaciones de Monte Cristi, Dajabón y Guayubín, y se extendía a las poblaciones de Santiago de los Caballeros y La Vega, habiendo recibido notificación del gobernador de Puerto Plata, el general y ex sacerdote don Carlos Morales, del resultado de sus planes. Desafortunadamente el cónsul dominicano en Cuba se enteró de sus movimientos y solicitó a las autoridades cubanas que les impidieran embarcar, pero fue informado por ellas de que, como no había evidencia de que estos hombres llevaran armas y pertrechos de guerra, y como salían del país como ciudadanos pacíficos, no se podía interferir con ellos. El cónsul dominicano telegrafió entonces al presidente de la república dominicana en la ciudad de Santo Domingo informándole que habían embarcado hacia algún puerto de la república, presumiblemente Puerto Plata.
