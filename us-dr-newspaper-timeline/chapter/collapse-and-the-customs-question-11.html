Waterbury Evening Democrat

The Democrat prints part of a New York item on the sailing of the Cherokee

November 17, 190317 de noviembre de 1903

The Democrat prints part of a New York item on the sailing of the Cherokee, opening with the Clyde line's answer: 'Well, you may say that the Clyde line is attending to its own business and that a part of that business is to touch at Dominican ports, and that is what we are going to do.' Senor Galvan, consul general for Santo Domingo, who is a godson of General Jiminez, the head of the rebels, said: 'Well, the gunboats will not parley with her this time. If the Independencia does not sink her El Presidente certainly will.' The offices of the consul general were besieged with anxious shippers who wanted to know what the government was going to do about it; one of them informed Senor Galvan that the Clyde line had informed him that it would carry his goods to Puerto Plata and that two United States gunboats were to convoy the Cherokee; the consul general snapped his fingers. 'My instructions are that the cargo of any vessel entering rebel ports will be confiscated and the crew treated as pirates. Now, you can take your chances if you wish, but I am telling you what will happen.' The Cherokee is the ship that ran the Dominican blockade some days ago.

El Democrat publica parte de una nota de Nueva York sobre la partida del Cherokee, que abre con la respuesta de la línea Clyde: «Bueno, puede decir que la línea Clyde atiende sus propios negocios y que una parte de esos negocios es tocar en puertos dominicanos, y eso es lo que vamos a hacer». El señor Galván, cónsul general de Santo Domingo, que es ahijado del general Jiménez, jefe de los rebeldes, dijo: «Bueno, las cañoneras no parlamentar con él esta vez. Si la Independencia no lo hunde, El Presidente ciertamente lo hará». Las oficinas del cónsul general fueron asediadas por cargadores ansiosos que querían saber qué iba a hacer el gobierno al respecto; uno de ellos informó al señor Galván que la línea Clyde le había informado que llevaría sus mercancías a Puerto Plata y que dos cañoneras estadounidenses escoltarían al Cherokee; el cónsul general chasqueó los dedos. «Mis instrucciones son que el cargamento de cualquier buque que entre en puertos rebeldes será confiscado y la tripulación tratada como piratas. Ahora, pueden correr el riesgo si lo desean, pero les estoy diciendo lo que sucederá». El Cherokee es el barco que forzó el bloqueo dominicano hace algunos días.

Willmar Tribune

The Tribune prints a Port-au-Prince dispatch: the French steamer

November 18, 190318 de noviembre de 1903

The Tribune prints a Port-au-Prince dispatch: the French steamer St. Simon, with General Jiminez aboard, has left Port-au-Prince, Hayti, with the intention, according to report, of forcing the blockade of Puerto Plata, on the northern coast of Santo Domingo; the Haytien authorities formally opposed the landing in Hayti of Gen. Jiminez, in spite of his strong insistence upon being permitted to do so; it is said that if the St. Simon is interfered with she will ask for the assistance of the German cruiser Gazelle, or any other foreign warship which may be in Dominican waters; the French cruiser Jurien de la Graviere, which was in these waters recently, is understood to have returned to the island of Martinique. La nota de Washington dice que los revolucionarios de San Domingo solicitaron hoy al Departamento de Estado el reconocimiento por los Estados Unidos, siendo presentada la solicitud por J. M. Giordan, quien se presentó como agente provisional del gobierno revolucionario del cual el general Jiménez es el jefe, y que el Departamento de Estado declinó el reconocimiento.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints a New York item of November 17: the United States government

November 18, 190318 de noviembre de 1903

The Call prints a New York item of November 17: the United States government has refused to recognize the blockade of San Domingo ports and has protested against it, as confirmed by N. N. The Press prints a San Juan item of November 29: Woss y Gil arrived there last night on the German cruiser Falke, which started at daylight this morning on her return to San Domingo; in an interview with a representative of the Associated Press the general said that no significance attaches to the fact of his traveling on the cruiser Falke, that United States Minister Powell had offered him passage on the gunboat Newport to New Orleans or Kingston, Jamaica, but he preferred to come to San Juan, and the German officials kindly offered him transportation; regarding the overthrow of his government, he said it was due to the impatience of the people, and that with his respect for the constitution this impatience caused the revolution; if the new government starts constitutionally his people will agree with it, and if not they will try evolution rather than revolution; he had the chair through peace and does not want it through war, which is demoralizing to the country; and the future state of the Dominican republic hinges upon the recognition of the protocol signed by the Vasquez government with Minister Powell; the item appears under the head 'Wos y Gil at San Juan'.
