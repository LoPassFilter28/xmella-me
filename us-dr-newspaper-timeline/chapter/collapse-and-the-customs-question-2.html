The Denison Review

The Review completes its assassination dispatch and prints a Washington item of July 27

July 28, 189928 de julio de 1899

The Review completes its assassination dispatch and prints a Washington item of July 27 on the official attitude: at present calmness prevails in the republic, and the remains of President Heureaux will probably be taken to Santo Domingo for the funeral services. The Washington item says no information was received last night from the consular representatives of the United States in San Domingo regarding the assassination of President Heureaux, and that pending official advices of the assassination no formal action will be taken by this government: William F. Powell, the minister to Hayti, is also chargé d'affaires to San Domingo, while this government is directly represented in the republic in the person of Campbell Maxwell, who is consul general, and John A. Read, who is vice consul; officials here recall that attempts have heretofore been made on the life of President Heureaux, and Secretary Hay paid a brief tribute to the work of the deceased president, saying he understood that he had given the country a good administration; and Secretary Long said he could not recall that there were any ships of the United States now in San Domingo waters, but that should the developments of the next few days show a feeling of unrest and uncertainty a United States man-of-war will be dispatched to protect American interests.

El Review completa su despacho del asesinato y publica una nota de Washington del 27 de julio sobre la actitud oficial: en el presente prevalece la calma en la república, y los restos del presidente Heureaux probablemente serán llevados a Santo Domingo para los servicios fúnebres. La nota de Washington dice que anoche no se recibió información de los representantes consulares de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo respecto al asesinato del presidente Heureaux, y que, pendientes los avisos oficiales del asesinato, este gobierno no tomará ninguna acción formal: William F. Powell, el ministro en Haití, es también chargé d'affaires en Santo Domingo, mientras este gobierno está directamente representado en la república en la persona de Campbell Maxwell, que es cónsul general, y John A. The Silver State

The Silver State prints an editorial sketch of Heureaux's career

July 28, 189928 de julio de 1899

The Silver State prints an editorial sketch of Heureaux's career and character: the sketch, in the paper's editorial voice, traces the development of his character to his ancestry, and says that in a more civilized community he would have become a more or less successful highwayman, while in the West Indies he developed into a patriot, a revolutionary leader, and a President. He commenced his public and military career at the same time, as a common soldier; he soon distinguished himself, for none dispute his bravery, and eventually became a commander; he served with distinction in Santo Domingo's war with Spain, pushing himself to the fore until the dignity of the title of General was his; after the war he kept on pushing himself forward, and in 1882 he was elected President of the Republic; he had, at the time of his death, held that office for seventeen years, having passed through four elections, more dangerous than war, and numerous plots to assassinate, more dangerous than either. The Dominican Republic, the paper says, is scarcely a republic as Americans understand it, nor Heureaux a President made such by popular suffrage: he was, in fact, a little tyrant of a small field, as proud as the queen of all the English, who took upon himself the airs of the greatest of rulers, yet could, when occasion required, assume the ways of the polished man of the world, and none were more courteous and thoughtful than he; and his morals, it adds, were quite as bad as those of the Prince of Wales.

El Silver State publica una semblanza editorial de la carrera y el carácter de Heureaux: la semblanza, en la voz editorial del periódico, atribuye el desarrollo de su carácter a su ascendencia, y dice que en una comunidad más civilizada él se habría convertido en un salteador de caminos más o menos exitoso, mientras en las Indias Occidentales se desarrolló en un patriota, un líder revolucionario, y un Presidente. Él comenzó su carrera pública y militar al mismo tiempo, como soldado raso; pronto se distinguió, pues nadie disputa su valentía, y eventualmente llegó a ser un comandante; sirvió con distinción en la guerra de Santo Domingo con España, abriéndose paso hasta que la dignidad del título de General fue suya; después de la guerra siguió abriéndose paso hacia adelante, y en 1882 fue elegido Presidente de la República; él había, al tiempo de su muerte, ocupado ese cargo por diecisiete años, habiendo pasado por cuatro elecciones, más peligrosas que la guerra, y numerosos complots para asesinarlo, más peligrosos que cualquiera de las dos. La República Dominicana, dice el periódico, es escasamente una república como los americanos la entienden, ni Heureaux un Presidente hecho tal por el sufragio popular: él era, de hecho, un pequeño tirano de un campo pequeño, tan orgulloso como la reina de todos los ingleses, que tomó sobre sí los aires del más grande de los gobernantes, pero podía, cuando la ocasión lo requería, asumir las maneras del hombre de mundo refinado, y ninguno era más cortés y considerado que él; y su moral, añade, era tan mala como la del Príncipe de Gales.

Richmond Dispatch

The Dispatch prints the close of the Cape Haytien exiles item

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Dispatch prints the close of the Cape Haytien exiles item and a Washington item of July 28 on the warships: the death of President Heureaux was hardly credited by the people generally until last evening, when undoubted confirmation was received, and then there was great rejoicing among the Dominican exiles, many of whom live in and around Cape Haitien; these exiles, who were driven out of their country by Heureaux, are now preparing to arm themselves and enter Santo Domingo, where they expect their chief, Don Juan Isidoro Jiminez, to join them. Under the head 'To Protect American Interests,' the Washington item says Secretary Long to-day issued orders detaching the cruiser New Orleans from the North Atlantic squadron at Newport, and directing her to proceed at once to Santo Domingo city; orders were issued also to the Machias, now at St. Thomas, to proceed to the same point, via San Juan; these orders were made at the instance of the State Department, and as a precautionary measure, owing to the disturbed condition following the assassination of President Heureaux; neither the State nor the Navy Department has received any advices of an alarming character, but owing to the large American interests on the island it was deemed advisable to take every precaution; and it is understood that American capitalists have an arrangement with the Dominican authorities by which practically the entire machinery of the island administration is under their direction.

El Dispatch publica el cierre de la nota de los exiliados de Cabo Haitiano y una nota de Washington del 28 de julio sobre los buques de guerra: la muerte del presidente Heureaux apenas fue creída por la gente en general hasta anoche, cuando se recibió confirmación indudable, y entonces hubo gran regocijo entre los exiliados dominicanos, muchos de los cuales viven en Cabo Haitiano y sus alrededores; estos exiliados, que fueron expulsados de su país por Heureaux, se están preparando ahora para armarse y entrar en Santo Domingo, donde esperan que su jefe, don Juan Isidoro Jiminez, se les una. The San Francisco Call

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo and the foreign interests at stake: the vessels are being sent solely as a precautionary measure; the press dispatches indicate political intrigues which may result in revolution; and, as American interests in the little republic are paramount to those of any other country, it is the part of prudence to have ample force at hand to see that full protection is given to those interests. As to the possibility of annexation as an immediate outcome of the assassination, Secretary Hay and Secretary Long think that it is going too fast to expect such a result and rather discourage this kind of talk; they do not deny, however, that the United States may be forced to serious responsibilities in connection with the future government of San Domingo. While no detailed information has been received regarding the plot which resulted in the assassination of President Heureaux, it is believed to be probable that it was planned and executed by partisans of Jiminez, the revolutionary leader; it is known that Heureaux had lived in fear of assassination for many months, and had frankly announced that he proposed to continue the Presidency as long as he lived. German interests in this republic are considerable, and President Heureaux was strongly backed by them; the future of the republic may depend largely upon the course taken by the foreign element in this crisis, and the course of the German Government in the present crisis will be watched with considerable interest by the American Government.

El Call publica una nota de Washington sobre la crisis en Santo Domingo y los intereses extranjeros en juego: los buques están siendo enviados únicamente como medida precautoria; los despachos de prensa indican intrigas políticas que pueden resultar en revolución; y, como los intereses americanos en la pequeña república son primordiales frente a los de cualquier otro país, es propio de la prudencia tener amplia fuerza a mano para ver que se dé plena protección a esos intereses. En cuanto a la posibilidad de la anexión como resultado inmediato del asesinato, el secretario Hay y el secretario Long piensan que es ir demasiado rápido esperar tal resultado y más bien desalientan esta clase de conversación; ellos no niegan, sin embargo, que los Estados Unidos puedan verse forzados a serias responsabilidades en conexión con el gobierno futuro de Santo Domingo. Aunque no se ha recibido información detallada respecto al complot que resultó en el asesinato del presidente Heureaux, se cree probable que fue planeado y ejecutado por partidarios de Jiminez, el líder revolucionario; se sabe que Heureaux había vivido en temor de un asesinato por muchos meses, y había anunciado francamente que se proponía continuar la Presidencia mientras viviera. Los intereses alemanes en esta república son considerables, y el presidente Heureaux estaba fuertemente respaldado por ellos; el futuro de la república puede depender en gran medida del curso que tome el elemento extranjero en esta crisis, y el curso del Gobierno alemán en la crisis presente será vigilado con considerable interés por el Gobierno americano.

Wheeling Register

The Register prints the New York interview with General Moscoso under the head 'An Exile's Views': Gen. Abelardo A. Moscoso, an exiled leader of the Liberal party of Santo Domingo, now living in New York, said last night: 'The death of President Heureaux will, I think, surely be followed by a long state of disorder and revolution. I shall be glad to return to my country and support such a policy if it is inaugurated by Gen. Figuereo, the vice president, who succeeds to the head of the government. But if something is not quickly done to establish order and peace, more revolutionary expeditions will follow, like that of Gen. Jimenez and Gen. Morales, who led a band from Cuba against Heureaux. There are now in this country Gen. Toribio Garcia and Edward Grullon, who share my views. We think that the United States ought to take an interest in our West Indian republic. It will be a great commercial ally. It lies between Cuba and Porto Rico. It must of necessity, I believe, ultimately become attached to this country.' Gen. Moscoso has been an exile in New York for three years; he opposed the late president politically and had to flee to save his life.

Ulysses Heureaux, who for 17 years had been president of the republic of Santo Domingo, was a sort of Napoleon, who, after years of autocratic power, had assumed the manners of an oriental despot; Heureaux was 53 years old; he was born in Puerto Plata, the nearest city to the little town of Moca, where he was assassinated Tuesday; his parents were poor; he became a common soldier and by bravery, brains and dash soon won high command; all the West Indies at one time or another have fought Spain — Santo Domingo did, and Heureaux became a general in that war; he was a fighter, a fearless, reckless one, whom bullets could not stop; in 1882 Heureaux was elected president of the republic and with four re-elections continued in power to the day of his death.

El Columbian completa el despacho del asesinato de Fort de France y publica un bosquejo del presidente Heureaux: el asesino logró escapar, pero se inició de inmediato una persecución enérgica, y es probable que pronto sea capturado; el vicepresidente general Wenceslao Figuereo, inmediatamente después del anuncio de la muerte del presidente, asumió la dirección de los asuntos; en el presente prevalece la calma en todas partes de la república; los restos del presidente Heureaux probablemente serán llevados a Santo Domingo para los servicios fúnebres. Ulysses Heureaux, quien por 17 años había sido presidente de la república de Santo Domingo, era una suerte de Napoleón, quien, después de años de poder autocrático, había asumido los modales de un déspota oriental; Heureaux tenía 53 años; nació en Puerto Plata, la ciudad más cercana al pequeño pueblo de Moca, donde fue asesinado el martes; sus padres eran pobres; se hizo soldado raso y por su bravura, inteligencia y arrojo pronto ganó alto mando; todas las Indias Occidentales en una u otra ocasión han peleado contra España: Santo Domingo lo hizo, y Heureaux llegó a ser general en esa guerra; era un luchador, uno temerario e intrépido, a quien las balas no podían detener; en 1882 Heureaux fue elegido presidente de la república y con cuatro reelecciones continuó en el poder hasta el día de su muerte.

The Herald prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of July 28 on the murder

August 3, 18993 de agosto de 1899

The Herald prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of July 28 on the murder headed 'Heureaux's Assassination': details of the murder of President Heureaux, of Santo Domingo, received here are to the effect that he was assassinated as he was about to leave on horseback for Santiago de las Caballeros; the assassin, Ramon Caceros, surrounded by several accomplices, approached the president as he was talking with some friends and fired twice with a revolver; the first bullet struck the president in the left side and penetrated the heart, causing instant death; the second bullet killed an old man who was standing near the president; the assassin and his accomplices then fled to avoid the shots fired at them by the president's friends; the president's death has caused a panic.

El Herald publica un despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 28 de julio titulado «Heureaux's Assassination»: los detalles del asesinato del presidente Heureaux, de Santo Domingo, recibidos aquí son en el sentido de que fue asesinado cuando estaba a punto de salir a caballo hacia Santiago de los Caballeros; el asesino, Ramón Caceros, rodeado por varios cómplices, se acercó al presidente mientras este hablaba con algunos amigos y disparó dos veces con un revólver; la primera bala alcanzó al presidente en el lado izquierdo y le penetró el corazón, causándole la muerte instantánea; la segunda bala mató a un anciano que estaba de pie cerca del presidente; el asesino y sus cómplices huyeron entonces para evitar los disparos que les hicieron los amigos del presidente; la muerte del presidente ha causado pánico.

The Herald prints a Fort de France dispatch of July 29 on the unrest

August 3, 18993 de agosto de 1899

The Herald prints a Fort de France dispatch of July 29 on the unrest headed 'Santo Domingo in a State of Unrest': the funeral services over the remains of President Heureaux, which took place at Santiago de las Caballeros last evening, were attended by a large number of people; the city of Santo Domingo is in a state of great unrest and business is entirely suspended; the people fear an outbreak; last night a band of armed men attacked Moca; the government has taken measures to put down the threatened insurrection.

El Herald publica un despacho de Fort de France del 29 de julio titulado «Santo Domingo in a State of Unrest»: los servicios fúnebres sobre los restos del presidente Heureaux, que tuvieron lugar en Santiago de los Caballeros anoche, fueron concurridos por un gran número de personas; la ciudad de Santo Domingo está en un estado de gran inquietud y los negocios están enteramente suspendidos; el pueblo teme un estallido; anoche una banda de hombres armados atacó Moca; el gobierno ha tomado medidas para sofocar la insurrección amenazada.
