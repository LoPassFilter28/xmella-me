The San Francisco Call

The Call prints two Cape Haytien dispatches of October 29 from Cape Haytien: Señor Caceres

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The Call prints two Cape Haytien dispatches of October 29 from Cape Haytien: Señor Caceres has gone now to join General Jiminez, the leader of the revolution, at some unknown point, and together they will make another attempt to land in Santo Domingo; telegraphic communication with La Vega, Santo Domingo, has been restored; it is rumored that the French cruiser Jean de la Gravier, now at Port au Prince, Hayti, has been ordered to Santo Domingo. General Navarro, the revolutionary governor of Monte Cristi, Santo Domingo, at the head of a strong force of troops, has joined General Morales, commander in chief of the forces of the Dominican revolution, with the reported intention of moving the combined forces on the capital, San Domingo; the blockade of the port of Puerto Plata, on the north coast of Santo Domingo, which is held by the revolutionists, has caused provisions there to become very costly; a force of Dominican revolutionists has occupied Cotuy; and it is presumed that the Dominican government forces will be concentrated at the capital, where heavy fighting is expected to take place. The second dispatch, sent via Paris, says the Dominican government cruiser Independencia to-day attacked Puerto Plata, the port on the northern side of the island which is in the hands of the insurgents.

El Call publica dos despachos de Cabo Haitiano del 29 de octubre desde Cabo Haitiano: el señor Cáceres ha partido ahora para unirse al general Jiménez, el líder de la revolución, en un punto desconocido, y juntos harán otro intento de desembarcar en Santo Domingo; la comunicación telegráfica con La Vega, Santo Domingo, ha sido restablecida; se rumora que el crucero francés Jean de la Gravier, ahora en Puerto Príncipe, Haití, ha recibido órdenes de dirigirse a Santo Domingo. El general Navarro, gobernador revolucionario de Monte Cristi, Santo Domingo, al frente de una fuerte fuerza de tropas, se ha unido al general Morales, comandante en jefe de las fuerzas de la revolución dominicana, con la intención reportada de mover las fuerzas combinadas sobre la capital, Santo Domingo; el bloqueo del puerto de Puerto Plata, en la costa norte de Santo Domingo, que está en manos de los revolucionarios, ha hecho que las provisiones allí se vuelvan muy costosas; una fuerza de revolucionarios dominicanos ha ocupado Cotuy; y se presume que las fuerzas del gobierno dominicano se concentrarán en la capital, donde se espera una lucha fuerte. El segundo despacho, enviado vía París, dice que el crucero gubernamental dominicano Independencia atacó hoy Puerto Plata, el puerto en el lado norte de la isla que está en manos de los insurgentes.

Evening Star

The Star prints an item headed 'Minister Powell's Report' and a Norfolk dispatch of October 30

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The Star prints an item headed 'Minister Powell's Report' and a Norfolk dispatch of October 30: reports say the whole of the northern part of the island has joined in the revolutionary movement. A cablegram received at the State Department to-day from United States Minister Powell at San Domingo reports that conditions in the republic are in a state of great disturbance; the State Department is making vigorous efforts to communicate with Mr. Powell by cable, but with unsatisfactory results, owing to the fact that the insurgents appear to be in possession of the land telegraph lines connecting the capital, San Domingo, with the cable station on the east coast; incidentally this has also interrupted communication with Venezuela. To guard against any further cable lapses the State Department is sending, through the Navy Department, written instructions to Minister Powell for his guidance during the revolution, and these will go forward on the Baltimore, which sails to-day from Hampton Roads directly for San Domingo. The Norfolk dispatch says the cruiser Baltimore sailed at high tide this afternoon for Puerto Plata, San Domingo, where she goes to protect American interests at that port.

El Star publica una nota titulada «Minister Powell's Report» y un despacho de Norfolk del 30 de octubre: los informes dicen que toda la parte norte de la isla se ha unido al movimiento revolucionario. Un cablegrama recibido hoy en el Departamento de Estado del ministro estadounidense Powell en Santo Domingo informa que las condiciones en la república están en un estado de gran agitación; el Departamento de Estado hace esfuerzos vigorosos para comunicarse con el señor Powell por cable, pero con resultados insatisfactorios, debido a que los insurgentes parecen estar en posesión de las líneas telegráficas terrestres que conectan la capital, Santo Domingo, con la estación de cable en la costa este; incidentalmente esto también ha interrumpido la comunicación con Venezuela. Para precaverse contra nuevas interrupciones del cable, el Departamento de Estado envía, por medio del Departamento de la Marina, instrucciones escritas al ministro Powell para su orientación durante la revolución, y estas irán en el Baltimore, que zarpa hoy de Hampton Roads directamente hacia Santo Domingo. El despacho de Norfolk dice que el crucero Baltimore zarpó con la marea alta esta tarde hacia Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, a donde va a proteger los intereses estadounidenses en ese puerto.

A short editorial note reports that revolution had broken out in the Santo Domingo republic, in the manner of…

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

A short editorial note reports that revolution had broken out in the Santo Domingo republic, in the manner of revolutions in Latin-American countries. It recalls that in 1869, at the instance of President Grant, a treaty of annexation was agreed upon and adopted by the Dominican people but was strenuously opposed by leading American statesmen and never ratified.

Una breve nota editorial informa que la revolución había estallado en la república de Santo Domingo, al modo de las revoluciones en los países latinoamericanos. Recuerda que en 1869, a instancias del presidente Grant, se acordó un tratado de anexión que fue adoptado por el pueblo dominicano pero se opusieron enérgicamente destacados estadistas estadounidenses y nunca fue ratificado.

Iowa State Bystander

The Bystander prints the Santiago dispatch: Cape Haytien, Hayti

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The Bystander prints the Santiago dispatch: Cape Haytien, Hayti, October 27 — the town of Santiago in the Dominican republic was surrounded yesterday morning by insurgent troops under the command of General Epifanio Rodriguez; after severe fighting which lasted for several hours, and during which a number of men were wounded, the revolutionists triumphed and Santiago fell into their hands. The revolution which has broken out in the northern part of the republic of Santo Domingo, and which has already resulted in the establishment of a provisional government at Puerto Plata, under the presidency of General Morales, was caused, according to advices received here, by the numerous customs house frauds and the prevarications of the ministers of war. The revolutionary outbreak is extending, and the general opinion is that the government of President Woss y Gil is lost. Vice-President Deschamps has arrived here in a small sail boat from Monte Cristi; he has abandoned the government. The dispatch appears under the head 'Dominican Revolt'.

El Bystander publica el despacho de Santiago: Cabo Haitiano, Haití, 27 de octubre — la población de Santiago en la república dominicana fue rodeada ayer por la mañana por tropas insurgentes bajo el mando del general Epifanio Rodríguez; tras una lucha severa que duró varias horas, y durante la cual varios hombres resultaron heridos, los revolucionarios triunfaron y Santiago cayó en sus manos. La revolución que ha estallado en la parte norte de la república de Santo Domingo, y que ya ha resultado en el establecimiento de un gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata, bajo la presidencia del general Morales, fue causada, según informes recibidos aquí, por los numerosos fraudes de aduanas y las prevaricaciones de los ministros de Guerra. El estallido revolucionario se extiende, y la opinión general es que el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil está perdido. El vicepresidente Deschamps ha llegado aquí en un pequeño bote de vela desde Monte Cristi; ha abandonado el gobierno. El despacho aparece bajo el titular «Dominican Revolt».

Oakes Republican

The Republican prints the closing part of the Santiago dispatch

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The Republican prints the closing part of the Santiago dispatch and a Paris item of October 28 headed 'Jiminez Back of It': after severe fighting the revolutionists triumphed and Santiago fell into their hands. The revolution which has broken out in the northern part of the republic of Santo Domingo and which has already resulted in the establishment of a provisional government at Puerto Plata under the presidency of General Morales, was caused, according to advices received here, by the numerous custom house frauds and the prevarications of the ministers of war and finance. The signal for the outbreak of the revolt was given at 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots. That same evening all the partisans of President Woss y Gil at Puerto Plata were arrested. The inhabitants of Monte Cristi, La Ligne and Moca united and attacked Santiago. Telegraphic communication between Santiago and La Vega has been severed. The revolutionary outbreak is extending and the general opinion is that the government of President Woss y Gil is lost. Vice President Deschamps has abandoned the government and has arrived here in a small sailboat from Monte Cristi. The Paris item says advices received from Cape Haytien, Hayti, confirm the reports that the revolutionary movement in the republic of Santo Domingo is in favor of former President Jiminez; the revolution is spreading, and, according to the dispatches received here, all the inhabitants of the region of Monte Cibao have joined the uprising against the government; and the election of General Morales as president of the provisional government is said to be a step taken pending the arrival of General Jiminez.

El Republican publica la parte final del despacho de Santiago y una nota de París del 28 de octubre titulada «Jiminez Back of It»: tras una lucha severa los revolucionarios triunfaron y Santiago cayó en sus manos. La revolución que ha estallado en la parte norte de la república de Santo Domingo y que ya ha resultado en el establecimiento de un gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata bajo la presidencia del general Morales, fue causada, según informes recibidos aquí, por los numerosos fraudes de aduanas y las prevaricaciones de los ministros de Guerra y de Hacienda. La señal para el estallido de la revuelta se dio a las 2 de la tarde del sábado con tres cañonazos. Esa misma noche todos los partidarios del presidente Woss y Gil en Puerto Plata fueron arrestados. Los habitantes de Monte Cristi, La Ligne y Moca se unieron y atacaron Santiago. La comunicación telegráfica entre Santiago y La Vega ha sido cortada. El estallido revolucionario se extiende y la opinión general es que el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil está perdido. El vicepresidente Deschamps ha abandonado el gobierno y ha llegado aquí en un pequeño bote de vela desde Monte Cristi. La nota de París dice que los informes recibidos desde Cabo Haitiano, Haití, confirman los reportes de que el movimiento revolucionario en la república de Santo Domingo es a favor del expresidente Jiménez; la revolución se extiende, y, según los despachos recibidos aquí, todos los habitantes de la región de Monte Cibao se han unido al levantamiento contra el gobierno; y se dice que la elección del general Morales como presidente del gobierno provisional es un paso dado a la espera de la llegada del general Jiménez.

The Tribune prints the closing part of a New York item on the revolution

The Tribune prints the closing part of a New York item on the revolution: Señor Galvan said 500,000 cartridges are held at the order of the government and will be shipped by special steamer, probably to reach San Domingo to-morrow; the government has now a supply on hand of 1,000,000 cartridges, but the additional supply is sent, Señor Galvan said, 'so they don't run short.' The rebels are reported to be poorly armed and munitioned. It is asserted by exporters in this city, doing business with San Domingo, that the insurrection will be suppressed before it makes much headway. General Carlos Morales, who heads the movement, has been governor of Puerto Plata under the present government, having fought against President Vasquez to place General Woss y Gil in power; in fact, General Morales has rebelled against every government under which he has at various times served; he was formerly a priest, but renounced the church for politics, and it has long been his ambition to be president. General Juan Isidro Jiminez, ex-president of San Domingo, who left this city a short time ago and is now at Mayaguez, Porto Rico, is also anxious to get back to power; President Woss y Gil appointed him fiscal agent to the United States and Europe, with a salary of $12,000 a year, to keep him quiet, it is said; he resigned this office, however, that he might take part in the new revolutionary movement; and it is not considered likely that he will be selected as chief executive, even in the event of rebel success, which is regarded as improbable.
