The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints a special cable from Puerto Plata reporting

December 18, 190318 de diciembre de 1903

The Republic prints a special cable from Puerto Plata reporting that Puerto Plata has surrendered to the revolutionists without fighting; the government forces, after a severe battle, have been forced to abandon Santiago de los Caballeros — they were short of ammunition; the general who announced that he would support the provisional President Morales has fled, and has covered up his tracks. The dispatch shows that the adherents of General Jiminez, who aspires to the Dominican presidency, have gained supremacy in the northern part of Santo Domingo; the new revolution started last week around Santiago de los Caballeros, where there has been fighting several days; the Morales provisional government came into existence about two weeks ago after the capture of the Dominican capital and the flight of President Woss y Gil.

El Republic publica un cable especial de Puerto Plata que informa que Puerto Plata se ha rendido a los revolucionarios sin luchar; las fuerzas del gobierno, tras una severa batalla, se han visto obligadas a abandonar Santiago de los Caballeros: les faltaban municiones; el general que anunció que apoyaría al presidente provisional Morales ha huido y ha borrado su rastro. El despacho muestra que los partidarios del general Jiménez, que aspira a la presidencia dominicana, han ganado la supremacía en la parte norte de Santo Domingo; la nueva revolución comenzó la semana pasada en los alrededores de Santiago de los Caballeros, donde ha habido combates durante varios días; el gobierno provisional de Morales surgió hace unas dos semanas tras la captura de la capital dominicana y la huida del presidente Woss y Gil.

The Saint Paul Globe

The Globe prints a San Juan, Porto Rico, item of December 17

December 18, 190318 de diciembre de 1903

The Globe prints a San Juan, Porto Rico, item of December 17 under the head 'Wos y Gil Says Santo Domingo Doesn't Want to Join United States': Gen. Woss y Gil, former president of Santo Domingo, discussing the resolution introduced in the United States Senate by Mr. Heyburn, of Idaho, requesting the President to negotiate for the annexation of Santo Domingo, said: 'Although Santo Domingo is under the commercial and political influences of the United States, annexation would not be agreeable, because the republic is still feeling the effects of the annexation to Spain, forty years ago, which left a deep impression. The civil wars of the republic date from that time.'

El Globo publica una nota de San Juan, Puerto Rico, del 17 de diciembre bajo el titular «Wos y Gil Says Santo Domingo Doesn't Want to Join United States»: el general Woss y Gil, expresidente de Santo Domingo, al comentar la resolución presentada en el Senado de los Estados Unidos por el Sr. Heyburn, de Idaho, solicitando al presidente negociar la anexión de Santo Domingo, dijo: «Aunque Santo Domingo está bajo las influencias comerciales y políticas de los Estados Unidos, la anexión no sería agradable, porque la república aún siente los efectos de la anexión a España, hace cuarenta años, que dejó una profunda impresión. Las guerras civiles de la república datan de esa época».

The Birmingham Age-Herald

The Age-Herald prints a San Domingo dispatch of December 25

December 26, 190326 de diciembre de 1903

The Age-Herald prints a San Domingo dispatch of December 25 reporting that it is rumored that the greater part of the republic now under arms is in favor of General Jiminez, the former president, who is said to be marching on this city at the head of a large body of insurgents; the provisional government is making active preparations for defense; San Pedro de Macoris has declared in favor of the insurgents; the situation here is becoming extremely critical, though the city is quiet in expectation of events; there is a great depression in business circles as the result of the constant disturbance of the financial situation; the financial condition of the government is very bad, owing to the fact that the duties were mortgaged in advance by the government of President Woss y Gil. The United States gunboat Newport arrived in port today; the German cruisers Vineta and Gazelle also arrived today; the Dutch warship De Ruyter has been sighted.

El Age-Herald publica un despacho de Santo Domingo del 25 de diciembre que informa que se rumora que la mayor parte de la república hoy en armas está a favor del general Jiménez, el expresidente, de quien se dice que marcha sobre esta ciudad al frente de un numeroso cuerpo de insurgentes; el gobierno provisional hace activos preparativos de defensa; San Pedro de Macorís se ha declarado a favor de los insurgentes; la situación aquí se está volviendo extremadamente crítica, aunque la ciudad está tranquila a la expectativa de los acontecimientos; hay una gran depresión en los círculos comerciales a consecuencia de la constante perturbación de la situación financiera; la condición financiera del gobierno es muy mala, debido a que los derechos de aduana fueron hipotecados por anticipado por el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil. La cañonera estadounidense Newport llegó hoy al puerto; los cruceros alemanes Vineta y Gazelle también llegaron hoy; el buque de guerra holandés De Ruyter ha sido avistado.

The Washington Times

The Times prints a Washington item: Minister Powell advised

December 27, 190327 de diciembre de 1903

The Times prints a Washington item: Minister Powell advised the State Department yesterday that Jiminez and his followers are now moving upon Santo Domingo from Macoris, a city which is friendly to the Jiminez movement, and that the provisional president is still in charge of the government and is preparing to make a stubborn resistance to the movement against the Dominican capital. Jiminez is a disgruntled follower of General Morales; he assisted in the movement against Woss y Gil, who was overthrown several weeks ago; the provisional government was not organized to suit Jiminez and he left the capital and organized the forces which are now threatening Santo Domingo. Tourists from Santo Domingo say the republic is a hopeless mass of revolutions, which cannot be checked until a foreign power takes a hand; even in Puerto Plata, the home of General Morales, a strong Jiminez party has sprung up which is making trouble; Morales is threatening to send warships to check the movement against him, but it is so general that he will doubtless find much trouble in controlling it.

El Washington Times publica una nota de Washington: el ministro Powell avisó ayer al Departamento de Estado que Jiménez y sus seguidores avanzan ahora sobre Santo Domingo desde Macorís, ciudad favorable al movimiento de Jiménez, y que el presidente provisional sigue a cargo del gobierno y se prepara para oponer una tenaz resistencia al movimiento contra la capital dominicana. Jiménez es un seguidor descontento del general Morales; ayudó en el movimiento contra Woss y Gil, que fue derrocado hace varias semanas; el gobierno provisional no se organizó al gusto de Jiménez, que abandonó la capital y organizó las fuerzas que ahora amenazan a Santo Domingo. Turistas procedentes de Santo Domingo dicen que la república es una masa desesperada de revoluciones, que no podrá contenerse hasta que una potencia extranjera intervenga; incluso en Puerto Plata, la tierra del general Morales, ha surgido un fuerte partido de Jiménez que está causando problemas; Morales amenaza con enviar buques de guerra para frenar el movimiento en su contra, pero este es tan general que sin duda le costará mucho controlarlo.

Evening Star

The Star prints part of Minister Powell's report on the revolution

December 28, 190328 de diciembre de 1903

The Star prints part of Minister Powell's report on the revolution: 'What may surprise the department is that this may be called a bloodless revolution. Though over 80,000 rounds of ammunition have been used on both sides, and there has been constant firing by both sides night and day (in the attack on Santo Domingo city), the total loss on both sides numbers twenty-five, and these principally from stray bullets and accidents. The insurgent loss numbered two men. Nor was this loss greater in other sections. At Monte Cristi, none; at Puerto Plata, none; at Santiago, about seventy (this includes the loss on both sides); at Moca, none; at La Vega, none; at Macoris, none. The only places I have not heard from are Azua and Barahona.' Mr. Powell also describes the house in which he formerly resided, about one mile from the city: it suffered more than any of the surrounding buildings and is almost a wreck, as if both sides had used it as a target; several shells have burst inside and rifle balls have perforated it; many of the things he left there are ruined, and had they remained, the office would have been in need of another representative.

El Star publica parte del informe del ministro Powell sobre la revolución: «Lo que puede sorprender al departamento es que esto puede llamarse una revolución sin sangre. Aunque se han usado más de 80,000 cartuchos de munición entre ambos bandos, y ha habido fuego constante de ambos lados noche y día (en el ataque a la ciudad de Santo Domingo), la pérdida total de ambos lados suma veinticinco, y estos principalmente por balas perdidas y accidentes. La pérdida insurgente sumó dos hombres. Tampoco fue mayor la pérdida en otras secciones. En Monte Cristi, ninguna; en Puerto Plata, ninguna; en Santiago, unas setenta (esto incluye la pérdida de ambos lados); en Moca, ninguna; en La Vega, ninguna; en Macorís, ninguna. Los únicos lugares de los que no he tenido noticias son Azua y Barahona». El Sr. Powell describe también la casa en la que residía antes, a una milla aproximadamente de la ciudad: sufrió más que cualquiera de los edificios circundantes y está casi en ruinas, como si ambos bandos la hubieran usado como blanco; varias granadas han estallado dentro y las balas de fusil la han perforado; muchas de las cosas que dejó allí están arruinadas, y de haber permanecido allí, la oficina habría necesitado otro representante.

New-York Tribune

Should the insurgents succeed in effecting an entrance to the city

December 29, 190329 de diciembre de 1903

The New-York Tribune reported that the besieged Dominican provisional government was unable to raise a $20,000 loan for city defense, leaving the capital vulnerable to insurgent assault and street fighting. With the gunboat Newport remaining as the only foreign warship in port, concerns grew over the safety of foreign residents. Meanwhile, insurgent leader Juan Isidro Jimenes scored a decisive victory at Moca against 500 government troops under General Ramón Cáceres, who was wounded, clearing the path for an advance on Santo Domingo.

El New-York Tribune informó que el asediado gobierno provisional dominicano fue incapaz de obtener un préstamo de 20.000 dólares para la defensa de la ciudad, dejando a la capital expuesta a asaltos insurgentes y combates callejeros. Siendo el cañonero Newport el único buque de guerra extranjero en el puerto, creció la inquietud por la seguridad de los residentes extranjeros. Mientras tanto, el caudillo rebelde Juan Isidro Jimenes logró una victoria decisiva en Moca contra 500 soldados gubernamentales al mando del general Ramón Cáceres, quien resultó herido, despejando el camino para avanzar sobre Santo Domingo.

Will march on capital.'

New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints the closing part of a Santo Domingo dispatch

December 30, 190330 de diciembre de 1903

The Tribune prints the closing part of a Santo Domingo dispatch: the minister, who has been living in the suburbs, has been compelled to move the legation inside the city boundaries; President Morales returned here last night — he found it impossible to land on the northern part of the island, but bombarded Puerto Plata, and the damage done is not known; Morales is taking all possible steps to defend San Domingo; many arrests have been made; the South and East have joined in the Northern revolution; the situation is complicated and serious; the government troops are preparing to leave here and attack the rebels at San Cristobal, two hours from here; much excitement prevails at this place, and the arrival of another American warship, to reinforce the gunboat Newport, is anxiously desired. The Age-Herald prints a San Juan, P. R., dispatch of January 3

R., dispatch of January 3 under the head 'Wos y Gil Is Active': thirty of the followers of General Woss y Gil, former president of Santo Domingo, including all the Dominican revolutionary generals now here, will sail tomorrow on the French steamer St. Simon, going direct to Puerto Plata; from there they will hasten to join General Jiminez before San Domingo; they declare themselves confident that they will be able to overthrow the government of President Morales.
