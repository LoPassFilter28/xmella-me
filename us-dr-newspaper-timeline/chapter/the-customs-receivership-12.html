The Montgomery Advertiser

The Montgomery Advertiser prints a dispatch: The Dominican republic

October 8, 19148 de octubre de 1914

The Montgomery Advertiser prints a dispatch: The Dominican republic is feeling the effects of the European war heavily, reports received here show, because virtually all of that country's tobacco crop and most of its cocoa went to Germany and that market now is shut off. Some recompense, however, is gained by San Domingo from the better prices for sugar, which is sent almost entirely to the United States. Washington, Oct. 7.

The Montgomery Advertiser publica un despacho: La guerra europea está haciendo sentir con fuerza sus efectos en la república dominicana, porque prácticamente todo el cultivo de tabaco de ese país y la mayor parte de su cacao iban a Alemania y ese mercado ahora está cerrado, según muestran los informes recibidos aquí. Alguna compensación, sin embargo, obtiene San Domingo de los mejores precios del azúcar, que se envía casi en su totalidad a los Estados Unidos. Washington, 7 de octubre.

The Guthrie Daily Leader

The Guthrie Daily Leader prints a dispatch: An appeal for aid

November 6, 19146 de noviembre de 1914

The Guthrie Daily Leader prints a dispatch: An appeal for aid for the tobacco interests of the Dominican Republic was made to the State Department today by Doctor Soler, the Dominican minister. He said there was fear that the entire crop, the principal product of the northern part of the island, might be lost, as it had been sold in advance and was to have gone to Hamburg, Germany. No ships can be found to carry it, as it had always been sent in German vessels. The financial stringency already is being felt, the minister explained, and the tobacco cannot be kept long without spoiling. An effort will be made to secure American or other neutral vessels to handle the crop.

The Guthrie Daily Leader publica un despacho: Una apelación de ayuda para los intereses tabacaleros de la República Dominicana se hizo hoy al Departamento de Estado por el doctor Soler, el ministro dominicano. Dijo que había temor de que se perdiera la cosecha entera, el principal producto de la parte norte de la isla, pues se había vendido por adelantado y debía haber ido a Hamburgo, Alemania. No se encuentran buques que la lleven, pues siempre se había enviado en buques alemanes. La estrechez financiera ya se está sintiendo, explicó el ministro, y el tabaco no puede guardarse mucho tiempo sin echarse a perder. Se hará un esfuerzo por conseguir buques americanos u otros neutrales para manejar la cosecha.

The Montgomery Advertiser prints a dispatch: Juan Isidro Jimenez, who was proclaimed president of the Dominican Republic yesterday by the National Congress, took the oath of office today. Quiet prevails throughout the country. Jimenez, former president of San Domingo, was driven by a revolution into exile in Porto Rico. A recent election was held under the supervision of an American commission selected by the State Department. On November 25 Secretary of the Navy Daniels, fearing another revolution, sent back to San Domingo 800 marines who shortly before had been relieved of the duty of preserving order there. Opponents of Jimenez, in control of the Dominican Congress, for a time declined to hold a session, and the new president, who was to have assumed office in November, was therefore unable to appear before that body to take the oath.

The Washington Herald prints an item: President Wilson has ordered an investigation into charges that James M. Sullivan, of New York, now minister to the Dominican republic, had been guilty of misconduct in office, mainly through alleged use of influence to obtain contracts from the Dominican government for a relative and friends, and by having Dominican revenue collections transferred to a bank in Santo Domingo City, which New York financial interests, friendly to Mr. Sullivan, were said to control. Al dar a conocer que había ordenado la investigación, el presidente Wilson dijo que su acción había sido tomada a petición cablegráfica del ministro Sullivan.

The Montgomery Advertiser

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Bryne's affidavit

January 17, 191517 de enero de 1915

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Bryne's affidavit continued: 'I told all these things in substance as set forth in this affidavit to Secretary Bryan. I know all the officials of the State Department have been advised of the true conditions in Santo Domingo by many other Americans whose standing and integrity cannot be questioned. The American people, the President of the United States, and the Democratic party have been discredited and dishonored in Santo Domingo by the conduct of Minister Sullivan. The net result in Santo Domingo is that sincere and honest American business men who have invested their money in legitimate enterprises for the development of the Dominican Republic have suffered great loss.' Supplementing his affidavit, Bryne testified that he had asked Secretary Bryan 'most urgently to clean out the situation before it got public and created a scandal.' 'Secretary Bryan said to me,' testified the witness, 'I wish you'd write this out and sign your name to it and leave it here.' Then, keeping his eyes on me, he said, 'Perhaps you wouldn't care to do this.' I said, 'Mr. Bryan, I wouldn't have come here if I wasn't willing to stand by what I've told you.' I said I would be glad to write out a statement, and then he said it would not be necessary. I did everything I could to get Mr. Bryan to take the matter up.' Bryne testified that as far as he knew the alleged conspiracy had not matured, chiefly because of Timothy J. Sullivan's frankness in discussing it.

The Montgomery Advertiser publica una nota: La declaración jurada de Bryne continuaba: «Dije todas estas cosas en sustancia, como se expone en esta declaración jurada, al secretario Bryan. Sé que todos los funcionarios del Departamento de Estado han sido informados de las verdaderas condiciones en Santo Domingo por muchos otros americanos cuya reputación e integridad no pueden ser cuestionadas. El pueblo americano, el presidente de los Estados Unidos y el Partido Demócrata han sido deshonrados y desacreditados en Santo Domingo por la conducta del ministro Sullivan. El resultado neto en Santo Domingo es que hombres de negocios americanos sinceros y honestos que han invertido su dinero en empresas legítimas para el desarrollo de la República Dominicana han sufrido grandes pérdidas». Complementando su declaración jurada, Bryne declaró que había pedido al secretario Bryan «con la mayor urgencia que limpiara la situación antes de que se hiciera pública y creara un escándalo». «El secretario Bryan me dijo», declaró el testigo, «desearía que escribieras esto, lo firmaras con tu nombre y lo dejaras aquí». Luego, manteniendo su mirada en mí, dijo: «Quizás no querrías hacer esto». Dije: «Sr. Bryan, no habría venido aquí si no estuviera dispuesto a sostener lo que le he dicho». Dije que con gusto escribiría una declaración, y entonces él dijo que no sería necesario. Hice todo lo que pude para lograr que el Sr. Bryan se ocupara del asunto. Bryne declaró que, hasta donde él sabía, la supuesta conspiración no había madurado, principalmente por la franqueza de Timothy J. Sullivan al discutirla.

The Washington Herald prints an item: William S. McGinnis testified

January 20, 191520 de enero de 1915

The Washington Herald prints an item: William S. McGinnis testified today before Commissioner Phelan, who is investigating charges against James Mark Sullivan, United States Minister to the Dominican republic, that Beer had captured the finances of Santo Domingo, and was planning to put its officers into Nicaragua. Sullivan was appointed by the efforts of Beer, McGinnis said, and he called him the 'big chief.' When the two met in a Washington hotel, after Sullivan's appointment as Minister, the latter told him: 'Things are going fine, Bill. The big chief will soon be not only financial agent of Santo Domingo, but of Nicaragua as well.' When McGinnis repeated the conversation to Beer, the 'big chief' said, 'for God's sake, keep quiet about that.' On another occasion Beer told him in Washington, 'This Santo Domingo deal has been the dream of my life. There's millions in it. I've been waiting for it for years.' Representative James A. Hamill, of New Jersey, according to the witness, said: 'Beer gets me so worked up over Nicaragua that every morning I wake up thinking I'm going to get $999,000.' McGinnis, formerly assistant superintendent of the railway mail service, told in detail the final steps to put Sullivan into office, even after Secretary Tumulty had told Representative Hamill that the White House heard Beer was interested in the Banco Nacionale of Santo Domingo.

The Washington Herald publica una nota: William S. McGinnis declaró hoy ante el comisionado Phelan, que investiga las acusaciones contra James Mark Sullivan, ministro de los Estados Unidos en la República Dominicana, que Beer había capturado las finanzas de Santo Domingo, y planeaba poner a sus oficiales en Nicaragua. Sullivan fue nombrado por los esfuerzos de Beer, dijo McGinnis, y lo llamó el «gran jefe». Hamill, de Nueva Jersey, según el testigo, dijo: «Beer me altera tanto con lo de Nicaragua que cada mañana me despierto pensando que voy a recibir $999,000». McGinnis, anteriormente superintendente asistente del servicio de correo ferroviario, contó en detalle los pasos finales para poner a Sullivan en el cargo, incluso después de que el secretario Tumulty había dicho al representante Hamill que la Casa Blanca había oído que Beer estaba interesado en el Banco Nacionale de Santo Domingo.

The Evening Star prints an editorial on the report of a deficit in the funds of the Dominican Republic, over which the United States exercises a custodial supervision: The money loss, which would presumably have to be made good by this government, is inconsequential, but the damage to American prestige in the countries of the south and the West Indies would be serious if such a deficit were in fact to have resulted from the carelessness of any official named by the United States. The Wheeling Intelligencer

The Wheeling Intelligencer prints a dispatch on Santo Domingo

February 6, 19156 de febrero de 1915

The Wheeling Intelligencer prints a dispatch on Santo Domingo, whose governmental and financial troubles were generally discussed by government officials today before Phelan, special commissioner named by President Wilson to investigate the conduct and fitness for office of James M. Sullivan, minister to the Dominican republic. Washington, Feb. 6. The witnesses included First Assistant Secretary of State Osborne, Third Assistant Secretary Phillips, Assistant Secretary of War Breckinridge, William Walter Smith, former secretary of the legation in San Domingo, W. W. Russell, former minister to Santo Domingo, Captain E. W. Eberle, of the navy, and Frank P. Mitchell, of the Banco Nacional of Santo Domingo.

The Wheeling Intelligencer publica un despacho sobre Santo Domingo, cuyos problemas gubernamentales y financieros fueron discutidos en general hoy por funcionarios del gobierno ante Phelan, comisionado especial nombrado por el Presidente Wilson para investigar la conducta y aptitud para el cargo de James M. Sullivan, ministro en la República Dominicana. Washington, 6 de febrero. Los testigos incluyeron al primer subsecretario de Estado Osborne, el tercer subsecretario de Estado Phillips, el subsecretario de Guerra Breckinridge, William Walter Smith, exsecretario de la legación en San Domingo, W. W. Russell, exministro en Santo Domingo, el capitán E. W. Eberle de la marina, y Frank P. Mitchell, del Banco Nacional de Santo Domingo.

The Montgomery Advertiser

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Washington, Feb. 6

February 7, 19157 de febrero de 1915

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Washington, Feb. 6. Testifying today at the investigation into charges against James M. Sullivan, Minister to the Dominican republic, C. H. Albrecht, former vice and deputy consul general to the republic, testified Sullivan had become unpopular with certain factions there because of his conduct in relation to a revolution. He said Sullivan had promised that the United States would guarantee a 'free and fair election' after the revolution and that the people were dissatisfied with the election when it was held. He said he never heard that Sullivan was responsible for 'bloodshed or the destruction of property,' as had been said. Mr. Albrecht said that Sullivan was untiring in his work as minister, and had accomplished many reforms in the conduct of the Department of Public Works.

The Montgomery Advertiser publica: Washington, 6 de febrero. Al declarar hoy en la investigación sobre las acusaciones contra James M. Sullivan, ministro en la República Dominicana, C. H. Albrecht, ex vicecónsul y cónsul general adjunto en la república, declaró que Sullivan se había vuelto impopular entre ciertas facciones allí por su conducta en relación con una revolución. Dijo que Sullivan había prometido que los Estados Unidos garantizarían una «elección libre y justa» después de la revolución y que el pueblo quedó insatisfecho con la elección cuando se celebró. Dijo que nunca oyó que Sullivan fuera responsable de «derramamiento de sangre o la destrucción de propiedad», como se había dicho. El Sr. Albrecht dijo que Sullivan era incansable en su trabajo como ministro, y había logrado muchas reformas en la conducción del Departamento de Obras Públicas.

The Logan Republican

The Logan Republican prints the National Civil Service Reform League resolution

February 9, 19159 de febrero de 1915

The Logan Republican prints the National Civil Service Reform League resolution on the customs service: The league profoundly regrets that the Secretary of State appears to have suggested and advocated and later publicly justified the use of the customs service of Santo Domingo, constituting a national trust for the benefit of another American republic and its creditors, to reward partisan services in our domestic politics. The National Civil Service Reform League, by its governing body, declares this course plainly inconsistent, not only with fidelity to the principles of the merit system and with the purpose avowed in the Democratic national platform of 1912 to make merit and ability the standard of appointment and promotion rather than service to a political party, but with ordinary good faith towards the Dominican republic and its creditors. It hereby publicly expresses its earnest hope that the President will promptly take such effective action as may prevent the practical application of a policy so generally and justly condemned.

The Logan Republican publica la resolución sobre el servicio de aduanas de la Liga Nacional de Reforma del Servicio Civil: La liga lamenta profundamente que el secretario de Estado parezca haber sugerido y propugnado y luego justificado públicamente el uso del servicio de aduanas de Santo Domingo, que constituye un fideicomiso nacional para beneficio de otra república americana y de sus acreedores, para recompensar servicios partidistas en nuestra política doméstica. La Liga Nacional de Reforma del Servicio Civil, por su junta gobernante, declara este curso claramente inconsistente, no solo con la fidelidad a los principios del sistema de méritos y con el propósito proclamado en la plataforma nacional demócrata de 1912 de hacer del mérito y la capacidad la norma de nombramiento y ascenso, y no el servicio a un partido político, sino también con la ordinaria buena fe hacia la República Dominicana y sus acreedores. Por la presente expresa públicamente su sincera esperanza de que el Presidente tome prontamente una acción efectiva tal que impida la aplicación práctica de una política tan general y justamente condenada.

Daily Kennebec Journal

Vick submitted evidence design ed to show that Minister Sullivan had

February 9, 19159 de febrero de 1915

The Daily Kennebec Journal reported that former Customs Receiver Walter W. Vick testified that Minister James M. Sullivan improperly attempted to negotiate an unauthorized $500,000 bank loan for the Dominican government. Vick presented a letter from Sullivan to Dominican banks proposing the loan without the sanction of the State Department or the Dominican Congress. The proposed borrowing provoked protests from Dominican legislative leaders until an outbreak of revolution halted the negotiations.

The Daily Kennebec Journal informó que el exreceptor de aduanas Walter W. Vick testificó que el ministro James M. Sullivan intentó indebidamente concertar un préstamo bancario no autorizado de 500.000 dólares para el gobierno dominicano. Vick exhibió una carta dirigida por Sullivan a instituciones bancarias dominicanas en la que planteaba el empréstito sin la aprobación del Departamento de Estado ni del Congreso dominicano. El proyectado crédito suscitó reclamos de los dirigentes legislativos dominicanos hasta que el estallido de la revolución frenó las negociaciones.
