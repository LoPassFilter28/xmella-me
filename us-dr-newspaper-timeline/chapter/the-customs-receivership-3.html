General Berroa, an exiled opponent of President Ramon Caceres

June 27, 190627 de junio de 1906

General Berroa, an exiled opponent of President Ramon Caceres, has arrived in New York, an Associated Press dispatch in the San Antonio Daily Light reports. Berroa, formerly governor of Macoris, had just arrived from Turks Island following imprisonment, and stated that the Dominican insurrection is growing daily and will overthrow the Caceres government. Announcing a meeting of opposition leaders in New York next month, he described conditions in Santo Domingo as terrible, citing daily political executions, packed jails, denial of constitutional rights, starvation, and suppressed suffrage. Calling the revolution a Christian uprising against barbarism, Berroa stated that while American guarantees of free elections would end the rebellion, insurgents are compelled to protest with arms.

El general Berroa, opositor exiliado del presidente Ramón Cáceres, ha llegado a Nueva York, según un despacho de la Associated Press en el San Antonio Daily Light. Berroa, quien fuera gobernador de Macorís, acababa de llegar de las islas Turcas tras sufrir prisión, y declaró que la insurrección dominicana cobra fuerza a diario y derrocará al gobierno de Cáceres. Al anunciar una reunión de dirigentes de la oposición en Nueva York para el próximo mes, describió las condiciones en Santo Domingo como terribles, citando ejecuciones políticas cotidianas, prisiones repletas, privación de derechos constitucionales, hambruna y supresión del sufragio. Al calificar la revolución como un levantamiento cristiano contra la barbarie, Berroa afirmó que, aunque las garantías estadounidenses de elecciones libres pondrían fin a la rebelión, los insurgentes se ven obligados a protestar con las armas.

A Washington report in the Evening Star announces that several United States warships

July 6, 19066 de julio de 1906

A Washington report in the Evening Star announces that several United States warships have been ordered to Dominican waters to reinforce the naval patrol maintained since American authorities assumed control of Dominican finances. Newly dispatched vessels include the cruiser Columbia carrying a battalion of 400 marines, the presidential yacht Mayflower, the auxiliary cruiser Tacoma, and the supply ship Culgoa. They join naval vessels already stationed across Dominican ports of entry, including the Yankee, Scorpion, and Dixie at Monte Cristi, the Paducah at Macoris, the Dubuque at Santo Domingo, the Don Juan de Austria at Samana, the Marietta at Puerto Plata, and the Newport at Sanchez. While naval observers consider the existing fleet sufficient to control the island, the deployment is widely understood as an effort by the United States to preserve the status quo and protect Dominican funds.
