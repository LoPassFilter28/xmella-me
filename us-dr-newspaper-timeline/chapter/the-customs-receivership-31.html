The Sun

The Sun prints a Washington dispatch: a vessel will be lent

September 9, 19139 de septiembre de 1913

The Sun prints a Washington dispatch: a vessel will be lent by the Treasury Department and will proceed at once from San Juan to the Mona Passage, there to act as a relay wireless station between the Dominican capital, the United States gunboat Des Moines and the city of San Juan. The Des Moines is expected to arrive at Puerto Plata to-morrow. According to meager advices received to-day, the Government gunboat is firing shells at the fort in that city now occupied by the provincial rebels. It is said that the lives and property of the Americans there are greatly endangered by the firing. It is likely that when the Des Moines arrives, steps will be taken by her commander to bring about a cessation of any firing dangerous to American interests. James Sullivan, the New York lawyer who has been appointed Minister to the Dominican Republic, was at the State Department to-day and was instructed to sail for Santo Domingo City in a few days, somewhat earlier than he had expected to go. He probably will leave New York Thursday or Friday.

The Sun publica un despacho de Washington: prestará un buque el Departamento del Tesoro, que saldrá de inmediato de San Juan hacia el Pasaje de la Mona, para actuar allí como estación inalámbrica de relevo entre la capital dominicana, la cañonera estadounidense Des Moines y la ciudad de San Juan. Se espera que la Des Moines llegue mañana a Puerto Plata. Según los escasos avisos recibidos hoy, la cañonera del gobierno está disparando obuses contra el fuerte de esa ciudad, ocupado ahora por los rebeldes provinciales. Se dice que las vidas y las propiedades de los estadounidenses allí corren gran peligro por el fuego. Es probable que, cuando llegue la Des Moines, su comandante tome medidas para lograr el cese de todo fuego peligroso para los intereses estadounidenses. James Sullivan, el abogado neoyorquino nombrado ministro en la República Dominicana, estuvo hoy en el Departamento de Estado y recibió instrucciones de embarcar para la ciudad de Santo Domingo dentro de unos días, algo antes de lo que esperaba partir. Probablemente saldrá de Nueva York el jueves o el viernes.

The Journal prints a Washington dispatch: hurry orders went

September 9, 19139 de septiembre de 1913

The Journal prints a Washington dispatch: hurry orders went from here to-day to the cruiser Des Moines, at Guantanamo, Cuba. The cruiser had been preparing to go to Santo Domingo since reports of the latest revolution there were received several days ago. The cruiser is coaling to-day, and is expected to leave for Santo Domingo to-night. Communication between Puerto Plata and the outside world is cut off. Nothing has been received at the State Department since the brief message to-day announcing the bombardment. Under the head 'In Hand of Rebels', the paper adds: Puerto Plata has been in the hands of the Dominican rebels ever since the latest revolution started with the announcement of the governor that the state had seceded from all allegiance to the central government. The present danger to Americans and their property comes from the attempt of the Dominican government to force the rebels' stronghold to capitulate. Until the receipt of to-day's dispatch, State Department officials had not thought the flare-up would be serious. The supposed leader of the uprising is Hector Valesque, one time provisional president of the republic. The Dominican navy consists of two or three converted yachts, armed with six-pounders and machine guns.

The Journal publica un despacho de Washington: hoy salieron de aquí órdenes urgentes para el crucero Des Moines, en Guantánamo, Cuba. El crucero se preparaba para ir a Santo Domingo desde que hace varios días se recibieron los informes de la última revolución allí. El crucero está carboneando hoy, y se espera que salga esta noche para Santo Domingo. La comunicación entre Puerto Plata y el mundo exterior está cortada. Nada se ha recibido en el Departamento de Estado desde el breve mensaje de hoy que anunció el bombardeo. Bajo el título 'In Hand of Rebels', el periódico añade: Puerto Plata ha estado en manos de los rebeldes dominicanos desde que comenzó la última revolución con el anuncio del gobernador de que el estado se había separado de toda obediencia al gobierno central. El peligro actual para los estadounidenses y sus propiedades proviene del intento del gobierno dominicano de forzar la capitulación de la plaza fuerte de los rebeldes. Hasta la recepción del despacho de hoy, los funcionarios del Departamento de Estado no habían creído grave el brote. El supuesto jefe del alzamiento es Héctor Valesque, en otro tiempo presidente provisional de la república. La marina dominicana consiste en dos o tres yates convertidos, armados con cañones de seis libras y ametralladoras.

Evening Star

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt

September 9, 19139 de septiembre de 1913

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt: it will be made known to rebel sympathizers that the Wilson administration is without sympathy with any government not founded upon law and orderly succession. Officials here to-day expressed confidence in the administrative ability and the integrity of President Bordas. They pointed out that one element of strength in his favor is the support of Desiderio Arias, who has been a leading spirit in many previous revolutions. Bordas is an ad interim provisional president, and is serving out that portion of the regular term of the presidency which was unexpired when Archbishop Nouel, provisional president succeeding President Victoria, gave up the office. Governor Cespedes of Puerto Plata province has a strong political following among the people of that province, and likewise commands large financial resources. He has built and equipped a private arsenal at Puerto Plata. It is, therefore, taken for granted that he is prepared to put up a strong fight against the attack being made by the gunboats in the harbor. Former Provisional President Vasquez, whose fortunes are cast with Cespedes in the present attempt to overthrow the regular government, commands equally great influence and resources in the rich farming countries in the interior. As it would be almost impossible to send an army overland to put down this uprising, the authorities in Santo Domingo City are depending altogether on the successful bombardment of Puerto Plata.

The Star publica comentarios de Washington sobre la revuelta de Puerto Plata: se hará saber a los simpatizantes rebeldes que la administración Wilson no simpatiza con ningún gobierno que no se funde en la ley y la sucesión ordenada. Los funcionarios aquí expresaron hoy confianza en la capacidad administrativa y la integridad del presidente Bordas. Señalaron que un elemento de fuerza a su favor es el apoyo de Desiderio Arias, que ha sido una figura dirigente en muchas revoluciones anteriores. Bordas es presidente provisional interino, y está sirviendo la parte del período regular de la presidencia que quedaba sin expirar cuando el arzobispo Nouel, presidente provisional que sucedió al presidente Victoria, renunció al cargo. El gobernador Céspedes, de la provincia de Puerto Plata, tiene un fuerte seguimiento político entre la gente de esa provincia, e igualmente dispone de grandes recursos financieros. Ha construido y equipado un arsenal privado en Puerto Plata. Por tanto, se da por sentado que está preparado para dar una fuerte pelea contra el ataque que hacen las cañoneras en el puerto. El expresidente provisional Vásquez, cuya suerte está echada con Céspedes en el intento actual de derrocar al gobierno regular, dispone de una influencia y unos recursos igualmente grandes en los ricos países agrícolas del interior. Como sería casi imposible enviar un ejército por tierra para sofocar este alzamiento, las autoridades de la ciudad de Santo Domingo dependen por completo del éxito del bombardeo de Puerto Plata.

Norwich Bulletin

The Bulletin prints an item: Washington, September 9. James M. Sullivan

September 10, 191310 de septiembre de 1913

The Bulletin prints an item: Washington, September 9. James M. Sullivan, the new American minister to Santo Domingo, and Charles M. Hathaway, consul at Puerto Plata, left Washington late today for Puerto Plata to tell the Dominican revolutionists there in emphatic terms that the United States has no sympathy with their efforts to overthrow President Bordas's government. They received final instructions at the State Department just before their departure. Mr. Sullivan will take steps to protect the customs houses in the revolutionary zone, which, like all those in the island republic, are operated under American supervision. The cruiser Des Moines was due at Puerto Plata late today, and should it be necessary there will be no hesitancy in the landing of marines, which besides guarding the customs houses would afford protection to the American consulate and all American interests. If hostilities still are in progress, an effort will probably be made to persuade the belligerents to do their fighting outside the city limits.

The Star prints an item: President Bordas of the Dominican Republic cabled the legation here today that the revolution was confined to the cities of Puerto Plata, Samana and Moca; that it was not general in the northern part of the country; and that he had taken personal command of his troops. The Advertiser prints an item: the news received by Dominicans

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Advertiser prints an item: the news received by Dominicans in this city today, which they consider of the greatest importance, is that Ricardo Limardo, Minister of Public Works and Communications in the Bordas government, has resigned and joined the forces of the secession movement in the north. The secessionists in Puerto Plata have formed a provisional government with Horacio Vasques at the head. According to a cable dispatch received by F. Deetjen, the Consul General of Santo Domingo in New York, the Dominican war vessel Jacaqua has left Santo Domingo for Puerto Plata with between 500 and 600 of the best trained soldiers in the country on board. The Consul General said he would not be surprised to learn that President Bordas himself accompanies the expedition. 'My advices,' said Mr. Deetjen, 'are that the representatives of the United States in Santo Domingo are supporting the central government. The United States has agreed to the closing and blockading of the ports which have seceded. The news that the east and south of the country are quiet is confirmed. The secession movement is confined to the north.'
