Representations that Haiti's credit was good and that the government had met international obligations have been made to President Wilson by Minister Menos. Springfield Weekly Republican

Springfield Weekly Republican prints: Bordas's hold upon the capital

July 23, 191423 de julio de 1914

Springfield Weekly Republican prints: Bordas's hold upon the capital, San Domingo, in the south is weakening, while President Bordas of the Dominican republic, with his scanty army, is besieging the northern rebels at Puerto Plata, and, according to Saturday's advices to the State Department at Washington, the southern insurgents are already in a position to dictate terms which may mean abdication or flight of Bordas. American Charge d'Affaires White, in the absence of Minister Sullivan, has been trying hard to prevent a clash near the capital, as it would be disastrous to foreigners and the many noncombatants who have flocked to the city. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan regarding the Dominican situation. It will be determined at this conference whether the Hancock marines shall be landed and a virtual, though temporary, protectorate established.

Springfield Weekly Republican publica: El presidente Bordas sitia a los rebeldes del norte en Puerto Plata, República Dominicana, con su escaso ejército. Su dominio sobre la capital, San Domingo, en el sur se debilita, y, según los avisos del sábado al Departamento de Estado en Washington, los insurgentes del sur ya están en posición de dictar términos, lo que puede significar la abdicación o la huida de Bordas. El encargado de negocios americano White, en ausencia del ministro Sullivan, ha estado tratando arduamente de impedir un choque cerca de la capital, pues sería desastroso para los extranjeros y los muchos no combatientes que han acudido en multitud a la ciudad. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan sobre la situación dominicana. En esta conferencia se determinará si los infantes de marina del Hancock serán desembarcados y se establecerá un protectorado virtual, aunque temporal.

A dispatch from Captain Eberle reports that the opposing factions in Santo Domingo seem to distrust each other and…

July 23, 191423 de julio de 1914

A dispatch from Captain Eberle reports that the opposing factions in Santo Domingo seem to distrust each other and that there are no conditions in the capital on which to treat. The Catholic Bulletin

The Catholic Bulletin prints an item: There is a movement on foot

July 25, 191425 de julio de 1914

The Catholic Bulletin prints an item: There is a movement on foot to raise, by subscriptions limited to fifty cents each from the people of the United States, of the twenty other republics of North, Central and South America and of Canada, the sum of $500,000 for a splendid Pan-American tomb and memorial for Christopher Columbus in Santo Domingo, where his remains are declared to rest. A superb site is available on the coral coast of the Caribbean Sea, where the Dominican Republic about twenty years ago dedicated the Plaza Columbina for the location of a monument to the great discoverer. It is proposed to construct a massive tomb, somewhat similar to the Grant Tomb on the Hudson, for the base, and then to sweep into the air with a superstructure, on the top of which will be placed one of the most powerful lights in the world, to be known as Columbus Light. The plan further provides for an endowment fund for the perpetual maintenance of the light.

The Catholic Bulletin publica una nota: Se ha comenzado un movimiento para reunir, mediante suscripciones limitadas a cincuenta centavos cada una, del pueblo de los Estados Unidos, de las otras veinte repúblicas de América del Norte, Central y del Sur y de Canadá, la suma de $500,000 para una espléndida tumba panamericana y monumento a Cristóbal Colón en Santo Domingo, donde se declara que reposan sus restos. Hay disponible un sitio soberbio en la costa coralina del mar Caribe, donde la República Dominicana dedicó hace unos veinte años la Plaza Columbina para la ubicación de un monumento al gran descubridor. Se propone construir una tumba maciza, algo similar a la Tumba de Grant en el Hudson, como base, y luego elevarse en el aire con una superestructura, en cuya cima se colocará una de las luces más potentes del mundo, que se conocerá como la Luz de Colón. El plan prevé además un fondo de dotación para el mantenimiento perpetuo de la luz.

New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union

Doubtless take steps to protect their interests is they deem it

July 29, 191429 de julio de 1914

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrasted unrest in the Caribbean, noting that while Dominican disturbances were likely to prompt U.S. armed intervention, the country presented fewer military and diplomatic difficulties than neighboring Haiti. European nations maintained smaller financial interests in Santo Domingo, whereas Haitian instability threatened foreign defaults. The Wilson administration sought guarantees that Haitian customs revenues would be managed to satisfy foreign debts in a manner analogous to the Dominican receivership.

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrastó las revueltas en el Caribe, observando que, si bien era probable que las perturbaciones dominicanas provocaran una intervención militar estadounidense, dicho país planteaba menores dificultades bélicas y diplomáticas que el vecino Haití. Las naciones europeas conservaban intereses financieros más reducidos en Santo Domingo, al tiempo que la inestabilidad haitiana ponía en riesgo moratorias con el exterior. La administración Wilson demandaba garantías de que los ingresos de las aduanas de Haití fueran administrados para cancelar deudas foráneas de modo análogo a la receptoría dominicana.
