On May 1, Mr. Roosevelt asked Attorney General Brownell to exert and exhaust every effort to solve the disappearance of Mr. Galindez, a Dominican opponent of Generalissimo Rafael L. Trujillo who vanished in New York on March 12 where he lectured at Columbia University. Assistant Attorney General Warren Olney III, head of the Justice Department's criminal division, replied asking the former congressman whether he had addressed any such appeal to the Dominican government, for which Roosevelt is a registered agent, and what response was received. Speaking from his farm near Hopewell Junction, New York, Mr. Roosevelt stated he had not received the letter and would not comment until seeing it. The Justice Department indicated there was no evidence of a federal violation warranting a full-scale investigation, though President Eisenhower stated the FBI was prepared to enter the case if warranted.

El 1 de mayo, el señor Roosevelt solicitó al fiscal general Brownell agotar todos los esfuerzos para esclarecer la desaparición del señor Galíndez, un dominicano opuesto al régimen del generalísimo Rafael L. Trujillo que desapareció en Nueva York el 12 de marzo, donde se desempeñaba como profesor en la Universidad de Columbia. El fiscal general adjunto Warren Olney III, jefe de la división penal del Departamento de Justicia, respondió preguntando al excongresista si había dirigido alguna petición similar al gobierno de la República Dominicana, del cual Roosevelt es agente registrado, y cuál había sido la respuesta obtenida. Desde su finca cercana a Hopewell Junction, Nueva York, el señor Roosevelt manifestó que no había recibido la carta y que no formularía comentarios hasta revisarla. El Departamento de Justicia señaló que no existían pruebas de una infracción federal que justificara una investigación exhaustiva, aunque el presidente Eisenhower declaró que el FBI estaba preparado para intervenir si correspondía.

June 19, 195719 de junio de 1957

A State Department statement disclosed allegations that the death of Dominican citizen Octavio de la Maza was not a suicide, but rather a murder resulting from a knife wound in the abdomen. The United States Embassy in Ciudad Trujillo was first notified of the death at 11:45 a.m. on January 7, 1957, after being told he had committed suicide at 4 a.m. Independent information gathered by embassy officials indicated De la Maza's body was transported for burial to a town sixty miles away, where two American officers examined the corpse and observed a wound on the left side three inches above the waistline, alongside neck bruising consistent with strangulation. The department concluded that all it knows with certainty is that De la Maza died within the territory of the Dominican Republic.

Información independiente obtenida por funcionarios de la embajada indicó que el cuerpo de De la Maza fue trasladado para su entierro a una localidad situada a sesenta millas de distancia, donde dos oficiales estadounidenses examinaron el cadáver y observaron una herida en el costado izquierdo a tres pulgadas por encima de la cintura, además de hematomas en el cuello compatibles con estrangulamiento. A feature article describes the Dominican Republic as an agricultural

June 22, 195722 de junio de 1957

A feature article describes the Dominican Republic as an agricultural country and the "Cradle of the Americas," with sugar serving as the primary driver of its economy by supplying over half of export earnings and more than one-third of government revenues. Major agricultural exports include sugar, coffee, cacao, beef, tobacco, molasses, corn, and yucca starch, with milling centered on the southern coastal plain and smaller plantations near Puerto Plata. The capital, Ciudad Trujillo, with a population of 250,000, was founded on the Ozama River in August 1496 under the name Santo Domingo before being renamed in honor of Generalissimo Rafael Leonidas Trujillo. The city blends modern steel-and-concrete buildings with historic colonial sites, including the University of Santo Domingo, the hospital of San Nicolas de Bari, and the Convent of San Francisco.

Las principales exportaciones agrícolas abarcan azúcar, café, cacao, carne vacuna, tabaco, melaza, maíz y almidón de yuca, con la molienda concentrada en la llanura costera del sur y plantaciones más pequeñas cerca de Puerto Plata.
