Cape Haytien-Haiti.

The *New York Herald* reported that Spanish General Salcedo sent a flag of truce and a letter to Haytien General Pierot, stating his mission was to occupy the Dominican Republic's frontiers rather than engage in hostilities. General Pierot responded by seizing the officers and men, stripping them of their belongings, and confining them together in a narrow dungeon without communication. Meanwhile, the Spaniards retired before General Pierot's advancing forces—who marched from Dajabon to take Santiago and join the President—while Salcedo, unaware of his first envoy's fate, dispatched a second flag of truce to Pierot.

El *New York Herald* informó que el general español Salcedo remitió una bandera de parlamento y una carta al general haitiano Pierot, exponiendo que su misión consistía en ocupar las fronteras de la República Dominicana y no en trabar hostilidades. El general Pierot reaccionó capturando a los oficiales y a la tropa, despojándolos de sus pertenencias y encerrándolos juntos e incomunicados en una estrecha mazmorra. Entretanto, los españoles retrocedieron ante el avance de las fuerzas del general Pierot —que marchaban desde Dajabón para tomar Santiago y sumarse al presidente—, al tiempo que Salcedo, desconociendo el destino de sus primeros emisarios, despachó una segunda bandera de parlamento ante Pierot.

Cape Haytien—Haiti.

According to *The New York Herald* on April 29, 1844, Spanish forces forced a retreat and occupied the town of Azua, with private loss estimates reaching eleven hundred men while the President awaited reinforcements near Port-au-Prince. Meanwhile, on the northern side of the island, General Pierot mustered eighteen thousand men at Dajabon while Spanish General Salcedo advanced nearby. When Salcedo sent a flag of truce and a letter stating his mission was to occupy the frontiers rather than wage war, General Pierot seized the officers and men, stripped them, and confined them together in a narrow dungeon without communication.

Según *The New York Herald* del 29 de abril de 1844, tropas españolas forzaron una retirada y tomaron la villa de Azua, calculándose las pérdidas extraoficiales en mil cien hombres mientras el presidente esperaba refuerzos en las inmediaciones de Puerto Príncipe. Por su parte, en el flanco norte de la isla, el general Pierot congregó a dieciocho mil hombres en Dajabón mientras el general español Salcedo avanzaba en las proximidades. Cuando Salcedo envió una bandera de parlamento con una misiva indicando que su propósito era ocupar las fronteras y no emprender la guerra, el general Pierot apresó a los oficiales y soldados, los despojó y los confinó juntos e incomunicados en una angosta mazmorra.

FROM PORT AU PLATT—By the Hero, Boyd, arrived last night in eight days from Port au Platt, we learn that everything was tranquil in that place when she left. Business was entirely at a stand still. The other portions of the island were in a fine condition, but from want of attendance would fall short, on account of the planters being engaged in the revolution. The following is the capitulation between the French and Spaniards of Hayti:—

The May 9, 1844 issue of *The New York herald* reported on the terms of capitulation involving the fort and garrison at Port-au-Plat. The agreement outlined protections for Haytian citizens and their legally acquired property, established conditions and a one-month timeframe for residents to swear allegiance to Dominican independence or be considered strangers, and guaranteed safe departure and transport options for those wishing to leave. Additionally, it detailed provisions allowing foreign property to be respected, the National guard and regular troops to retain their arms as members of the Dominican Republic, and General C. Antonio and others to embark on vessels of their choice.

El ejemplar del 9 de mayo de 1844 de *The New York herald* reseñó las capitulaciones convenidas respecto al fuerte y la guarnición de Puerto Plata. Las estipulaciones garantizaban la protección de los ciudadanos haitianos y sus bienes legítimamente adquiridos, establecían condiciones y un término de un mes para que los residentes jurasen lealtad a la independencia dominicana o fuesen considerados forasteros, y aseguraban salvoconductos y facilidades de transporte para quienes decidiesen marcharse. De igual modo, preveía el respeto a la propiedad extranjera, la conservación de las armas por parte de la Guardia Nacional y las tropas regulares en calidad de miembros de la República Dominicana, y la autorización al general C. Antonio y a otros jefes para embarcarse en los navíos de su preferencia.

RECENT RIOTS IN HAYTI.

According to the newspaper report, the Haytien armies from Port au Prince and the Cape advanced to the frontiers, where they were checked and subsequently driven back over the borders by Dominican forces after a series of combats and an attack at Azua on the 9th of the previous month. Additionally, a naval engagement in the bay of Azua resulted in the Haytien fleet of three vessels being driven onto reefs and sunk by the Dominican fleet of equal force, with heavy casualties reported among the Haytien crews and none on the Dominican side. The report also notes that the Cape army had lost 500 men to desertion by the time it reached the frontiers.

Según la reseña periodística, los ejércitos haitianos provenientes de Puerto Príncipe y de El Cabo marcharon hacia las fronteras, donde las fuerzas dominicanas lograron contenerlos y luego rechazarlos tras sucesivos combates y un asalto en Azua el día 9 del mes previo. Además, un combate naval en la bahía de Azua culminó con la flotilla haitiana de tres navíos encallada en arrecifes y echada a pique por la escuadrilla dominicana de similar porte, reportándose considerables pérdidas entre las tripulaciones haitianas frente a ninguna baja dominicana. El parte añade que el ejército de El Cabo había sufrido quinientas bajas por deserción antes de alcanzar la frontera.

LATE FROM HAYTI

The *New-York daily tribune* reported that Port au Prince and the South were submissive to Guerrier's Government, and that ex-President Hérard and his kinsman were ordered to leave the island after his troops were beaten by the Dominicans. LATE FROM HAYTI.

According to the June 15, 1844 issue of the *Daily National Intelligencer*, Gurrier was proclaimed President of the North on the 18th, with plans to erect the island's divisions into States similar to the United States and potentially propose that the Dominicans join as a State. The newspaper reported that the ex-President and Herard Dumesle were ordered to be banished to Jamaica, that Aux Cayes and the South remained submissive, and that no new expedition was currently designed against the Dominicans, who had severely beaten Herard’s troops. Additionally, it noted unconfirmed talk of a treaty between the Dominicans and France, the arrival of the British frigate *Spartan* from Port au Prince, and the presence of the U.S. sloop *Preble* at Aux Cayes.

Conforme al ejemplar del 15 de junio de 1844 del *Daily National Intelligencer*, Gurrier fue proclamado presidente del Norte el día 18, proyectando estructurar las demarcaciones insulares en estados a semejanza de Estados Unidos y plantear en su caso que los dominicanos se integrasen en calidad de estado. El periódico refirió que se dispuso el confinamiento en Jamaica del expresidente y de Herard Dumesle, que Aux Cayes y el Sur acataban la autoridad y que no se preparaba de momento ninguna expedición contra los dominicanos, quienes habían desbaratado a los destacamentos de Hérard. Además, aludió a negociaciones no confirmadas de un tratado dominicano con Francia, al arribo de la fragata británica *Spartan* desde Puerto Príncipe y a la estancia de la corbeta estadounidense *Preble* en Aux Cayes.

Hayti

According to *The Liberator*, the new administration sought to restore peace and national unity following a period of civil war, sending influential deputations across the island that successfully garnered support for union beyond the ancient Spanish limits. Furthermore, correspondence between the Haytien Secretary of War and General Pedro Santana, commander-in-chief of the Dominican army, indicated a favorable disposition on both sides toward the ultimate incorporation of the entire island into one body politic.

De acuerdo con *The Liberator*, el nuevo régimen procuró afianzar la concordia y la unidad nacional tras la guerra civil, comisionando representaciones notables a lo ancho de la isla que consiguieron adhesiones a la unión más allá de las antiguas lindes españolas. Por otra parte, la correspondencia intercambiada entre el ministro de Guerra haitiano y el general Pedro Santana, general en jefe de las fuerzas dominicanas, reveló una inclinación propicia en ambas partes orientada a la fusión definitiva de toda la isla en una sola entidad política.

FROM HAYTI.

The newspaper reports that a French squadron was waiting at Port-au-Prince for an indemnity installment, though funds were scarce. FROM ST. DOMINGO.

The *Daily National Intelligencer* reported that the project to divide the island into states was abandoned, leaving the government restored to its pre-revolution status minus municipalities, with Port au Prince serving as the capital and Port au Platt maintaining the Dominican flag. The daily union

According to *The Daily Union* on October 15, 1845, the Haytian navy suffers from unskillful and undisciplined seamen, raising fears that their commanders might interfere with foreign commerce. Correspondence of the Journal of Commerce.

The *Republican herald* reported on November 12, 1845, that while petitions had been sent to Congress for the recognition of Hayti's independence, any potential U.S. government interference would likely be through recognizing the independence of the Dominican Republic, whose course regarding England and France remained uncertain. The article noted that the Dominicans received sympathy as representatives of Columbus's first American colony, though it claimed their incompetency for self-government had been proven and an impending revolution might restore the island. Additionally, the newspaper reported a Cabinet rumor that Mr. Buchanan would remain in his position, and noted an occurrence from the *Turk's Island Gazette* of October 23 regarding a man named Benjamin Lightbourn who was killed by a wild bull at Salt Rock, Caicos.

El *Republican herald* dio noticia el 12 de noviembre de 1845 de que, pese a elevarse solicitudes al Congreso propugnando la formalización de la independencia haitiana, una eventual injerencia estadounidense se encauzaría más bien por la convalidación soberana de la República Dominicana, cuya política respecto a Inglaterra y Francia continuaba indefinida. La nota observaba simpatías hacia los dominicanos por encarnar el primer asentamiento colombino en América, si bien argüía su supuesta impericia para el régimen autónomo y vaticinaba un vuelco revolucionario capaz de reconfigurar la isla. THE DOMINICAN REPUBLIC.

The *Mobile Register and Journal* reported on the geography, population demographics, and government structure of the island, noting that the Spanish (Dominican) portion features a republican government, good racial relations, and a Catholic majority alongside Protestant churches. The excerpt further stated that the Dominican military possesses approximately 35,000 to 40,000 regular and National Guard troops, and predicted that the Dominicans would inevitably conquer the entire island. Additionally, it mentioned that the negro part of the island owes about 12 million dollars to France, that English and French mutual jealousies exist regarding influence, and that the true interest of Spain, France, the United States, and England is to aid and countenance the region.

El *Mobile Register and Journal* examinó la demarcación geográfica, el censo y el ordenamiento institucional insular, acentuando que el sector hispano (dominicano) goza de régimen republicano, armonía interétnica y confesión mayoritariamente católica junto a templos reformados. El pasaje puntualizaba que el ejército dominicano reunía de 35.000 a 40.000 combatientes entre tropa de línea y Guardias Nacionales, augurando el sometimiento inexorable de la totalidad de la isla. Indicaba además que el sector negro registraba un débito aproximado de doce millones de duros con Francia, constataba los recelos hegemónicos anglo-franceses y juzgaba conforme a la conveniencia de España, Francia, Estados Unidos y Gran Bretaña respaldar e impulsar aquel territorio.

St. Domingo.

THE DOMINICAN REPUBLIC

In 1845, the newspaper reported that Mr. Calhoun, while in the State Department, dispatched Mr. Hogan to investigate the resources of the Dominican Republic following a petition to have the United States acknowledge its independence. Important News from St. Domingo—Progress of General Annexation.

According to the *New York Herald*, documents including a memorial presented to the U.S. government by a San Domingo agent and letters to U.S. agent Mr. Hogan indicate that the United States is pursuing a policy toward San Domingo similar to the one that led to the annexation of Texas. Mr. Hogan was sent to San Domingo by Secretary of State Mr. Calhoun to investigate the island's resources and capabilities, while the ongoing war between San Domingo and Hayti reportedly nears an end.

Conforme al *New York Herald*, pliegos que comprenden una memoria cursada al gobierno norteamericano por un comisionado de Santo Domingo y comunicaciones libradas al delegado estadounidense Sr. Hogan evidencian que Estados Unidos aplica hacia Santo Domingo una directriz análoga a la que precedió a la incorporación de Texas.
