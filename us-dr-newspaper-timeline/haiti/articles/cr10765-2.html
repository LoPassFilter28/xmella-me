CUBA.

We learn by private letters received

here on the 191 h of the arrival of H.M.S,

“Royalist” at the Northside, bringing

despatches for the Commodore. The

reported rising in Cuba sometime ago

appears to be confirmed. It broke out

at Bayanno, headed by a Dominican and

two Cuban Generals, who at the head

of 1200 men attacked some places in the

interior aund carried off the public T'rea

sury. We learn that the cause of this

movement was the heavy taxation lately

imposed on the public. The negroes

were quiet. Large detachments of troops

had been sent acainst the insurgents.

No place of inportance had joined in the

movement, and it was generally believed

that the insurgents would soon be put

down. Insome parts of the Island the

telegraphic wire had been cut, so that no

reliable information could be received.
