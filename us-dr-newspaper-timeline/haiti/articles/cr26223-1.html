The Unquiet Englishman

The news that Graham Greene Is In

Haiti Is Itself worth keeping an eye on.

The British novelist has a long post-war

record of turning up in places like Vien

na, the African Gold Coast and Viet

Nam In time to have a novel published

just as some violent change takes place

In the region’s politics. His Haitian in

terlude is a revisit to the Caribbean.

Last time was Cuba, where he wrote

“Our Man In Havana” shortly before

the Kremlin managed to get their man

In that city.

But In a recent Haitian dispatch,

Mr. Greene was quoted as complaining

of a ten-year experience with someone

using his name and always keeping just

out of reach In England, India and

Saudi Arabia. The mysterious double

has hired secretaries for overseas work,

stood up French ladies in Paris and was

once arrested for gun-running In Assam.

If this had happened to anyone else,

we’d suspect a master-stroke by a pub

licity genius In the employ of a pub

lisher. But not Mr. Greene. The con

nections are too clear between the

double and old Greene Inventions like

Sparrow, of “This Gun For Hire,” and

Harry Lime, "The Third Man.”

The mysterious stranger Is obviously

a character escaped from a Greene novel

and now gathering material for a novel

of his own about a brilliant suspense

novelist slowly driven out of his mind

by sheer suspense on the global scale.
