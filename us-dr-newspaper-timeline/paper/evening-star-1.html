Advices from San Domingo announce the re-establishment of the Dominican Republic

May 13, 186513 de mayo de 1865

Advices from San Domingo announce the re-establishment of the Dominican Republic and the departure of the Spanish troops from the island. The National Convention ended its sessions in late March and published a decree of amnesty that excepts, among others, the high functionaries implicated in the intrigues of the annexation, the soldiers who fought against their country under the Spanish flag, and the writers who opposed the restoration in the press. A Spanish steamer brought the order from Madrid for a total evacuation, and the troops embarked on March 30.

Noticias de Santo Domingo anuncian el restablecimiento de la República Dominicana y la salida de las tropas españolas de la isla. La Convención Nacional terminó sus sesiones a fines de marzo y publicó un decreto de amnistía que exceptúa, entre otros, a los altos funcionarios implicados en las intrigas de la anexión, a los soldados que lucharon contra su país bajo la bandera española y a los escritores que se opusieron a la restauración en la prensa. Un vapor español trajo la orden de Madrid para la evacuación total, y las tropas embarcaron el 30 de marzo.

The acquisition of the bay of Samana is the subject of an editorial

September 25, 186725 de septiembre de 1867

The acquisition of the bay of Samana is the subject of an editorial recounting that the obvious intent was to acquire the bay with its valuable coal mines, which, with the development of steam navigation in the Pacific after the discovery of the treasures of California, were regarded as one of the most valuable properties that could be opened up; the population of the country is estimated at only about 200,000. At that period, it says, France was represented as having designs upon the republic, and a futile attempt was made to raise a Dominican loan of £750,000 in London; afterwards the temporary reoccupation of San Domingo by Spain and the exigencies of the American civil war diverted attention from the subject. Its resumption at the present moment, the writer holds, may in its commercial bearings be regarded with satisfaction: the bay is among the finest harbors in the world, its coal supply alleged to be virtually inexhaustible and so situated that vessels may even load alongside the mines, and the station would prove of incalculable importance to the United States in a naval sense, whatever mixture of motives produced the acquisition.

La adquisición de la bahía de Samaná es el tema de un editorial que refiere que la intención evidente era adquirir la bahía con sus valiosas minas de carbón, las cuales, con el desarrollo de la navegación a vapor en el Pacífico tras el descubrimiento de los tesoros de California, eran consideradas una de las propiedades más valiosas que pudieran explotarse; la población del país se estima en solo unos 200,000 habitantes. En aquella época, dice, se representaba que Francia tenía designios sobre la república, y se hizo un intento inútil de levantar en Londres un empréstito dominicano de £750,000; después, la reocupación temporal de Santo Domingo por España y las exigencias de la guerra civil americana desviaron la atención del asunto. Su reanudación en el momento presente, sostiene el autor, puede considerarse con satisfacción en sus aspectos comerciales: la bahía está entre los mejores puertos del mundo, su suministro de carbón se alega virtualmente inagotable y situado de tal modo que los buques pueden cargar incluso junto a las minas, y la estación resultaría de incalculable importancia para los Estados Unidos en el plano naval, cualquiera que haya sido la mezcla de motivos que produjo la adquisición.

In a Senate speech published in the Evening Star, Senator Charles Sumner reviews

March 27, 187127 de marzo de 1871

In a Senate speech published in the Evening Star, Senator Charles Sumner reviews Dominican political history, stating that while he always wished to extend assistance to Dominica, he never encouraged armed uprisings. Sumner recounts that when President Cabral sought American aid and offered a lease of Samaná Bay to resolve financial embarrassments, Báez denounced the proposal, secured military assistance from Haitian ruler Salnave, and forced Cabral to capitulate on February 8. Following his takeover, an unelected convention nominated Báez as dictator, an appointment he modified to 'Grand Citizen' with unlimited powers while imprisoning, exiling, and executing opponents. Sumner charges that Báez subsequently pursued the annexation scheme out of personal greed.

En un discurso en el Senado publicado en el Evening Star, el senador Charles Sumner repasa la historia política dominicana, afirmando que si bien siempre deseó brindar asistencia a Dominica, nunca alentó insurrecciones armadas. Sumner relata que cuando el presidente Cabral buscó ayuda estadounidense y ofreció el arrendamiento de la bahía de Samaná para resolver sus apuros financieros, Báez denunció la propuesta, obtuvo apoyo militar del gobernante haitiano Salnave y obligó a Cabral a capitular el 8 de febrero. Tras su toma del poder, una convención no electa designó a Báez dictador, nombramiento que este modificó al de «Gran Ciudadano» con poderes ilimitados, mientras encarcelaba, desterraba y ejecutaba a sus adversarios. Sumner acusa a Báez de haber impulsado posteriormente el plan de anexión movido por la codicia personal.

The Star reviews the quarrel between President Heureaux and France

January 6, 18946 de enero de 1894

The Star reviews the quarrel between President Heureaux and France over the Banco Nacional: it had been declared that the president had broken open the bank and abstracted the cash therein, about 200,000 francs, but the Santo Domingo authorities assert that no money was touched and that the documents abstracted consisted merely of securities deposited against a contract never executed; the president said that he had demanded the return of these documents from the French authorities of the bank and that his application had been refused, and later he himself refused the proposition of the French admiral, De Libran, to deposit the money or the securities in the hands of a responsible third party until arbitration should have decided upon the merits of the case. France, the Star says, was satisfied with sending occasionally one or two men-of-war to maneuver off the Dominican coast and with suspending diplomatic relations; meanwhile Heureaux keeps in prison a French merchant named Bolmare, whose only crime, it is said, is that he started a soap factory in competition with one in which the president had an interest. Heureaux is the more interested in picking no quarrel with the United States on account of the decrease in his financial resources: in 1888 the customs revenues were given in guarantee at Amsterdam to Dutch bankers, who took up a loan whose amount the window does not print legibly; the revenues were stated then as amounting to $1,500,000, they fell to $1,305,101 in 1892, and they are collected by an American syndicate, which has acquired the rights and the concession granted in 1888 to the Amsterdam bankers.

El Star reseña la disputa entre el presidente Heureaux y Francia a causa del Banco Nacional: se había declarado que el presidente había forzado el banco y sustraído el efectivo que contenía, unos 200,000 francos, pero las autoridades de Santo Domingo afirman que no se tocó dinero alguno y que los documentos sustraídos consistían meramente en valores depositados en garantía de un contrato nunca ejecutado; el presidente dijo que había exigido la devolución de estos documentos a las autoridades francesas del banco y que su solicitud había sido denegada, y más tarde él mismo rechazó la proposición del almirante francés, De Libran, de depositar el dinero o los valores en manos de un tercero responsable hasta que un arbitraje decidiera sobre los méritos del caso. Francia, dice el Star, se contentó con enviar de vez en cuando uno o dos buques de guerra a maniobrar frente a la costa dominicana y con suspender las relaciones diplomáticas; mientras tanto, Heureaux mantiene en prisión a un comerciante francés llamado Bolmare, cuyo único crimen, se dice, es haber establecido una fábrica de jabón en competencia con una en la que el presidente tenía interés. Tanto más interesado está Heureaux en no buscar disputa con los Estados Unidos a causa de la disminución de sus recursos financieros: en 1888 los ingresos aduaneros fueron dados en garantía en Ámsterdam a banqueros holandeses, que tomaron un empréstito cuyo monto la ventana no imprime legiblemente; los ingresos se declararon entonces como ascendentes a $1,500,000, cayeron a $1,305,101 en 1892, y son recaudados por un sindicato estadounidense, que ha adquirido los derechos y la concesión otorgados en 1888 a los banqueros de Ámsterdam.

The Star's pen picture of President Heureaux goes on to his manner:

June 25, 189825 de junio de 1898

The Star's pen picture of President Heureaux goes on to his manner: even in his most dangerous moments, it is said, can be bland, caressing and patronizing, and they even have a saying in Santo Domingo that many of the poor devils he sends to the tragic blank wall or freshly opened wayside ditch feel almost flattered when he tells them personally he cannot afford to pardon them; it is no wonder his astuter friends, as well as enemies, have learned to study his smile more closely than his frown. The president is about five feet eight inches in height, of athletic mold, though his right arm is still half disabled from the effects of two old wounds near the elbow; his movements are usually deliberate, as if all that he did in public was nicely calculated, yet he is capable of great animation, volubility and vivacity, spices his talk with satire and irony, and occasionally laughs broadly at his own joke or epigram, and it is impossible not to be impressed by the strong grasp of his mind and by the subtlety of his cunning. All the time the conversation went on, something over an hour, first with the correspondent and at intervals with visitor after visitor who came to pay his respects, there were three or four poor fellows down in the bowels of the ship, revolutionists taken with arms in their hands, who were waiting the word of this imperturbable dictator to determine whether they should live or die.

El retrato a pluma del Star sobre el presidente Heureaux continúa con sus modales: aun en sus momentos más peligrosos, se dice, puede ser blando, acariciante y protector, y hasta tienen un dicho en Santo Domingo de que muchos de los pobres diablos que él envía al trágico muro desnudo o a la zanja recién abierta a la orilla del camino se sienten casi halagados cuando él les dice personalmente que no puede permitirse perdonarlos; no es de extrañar que sus amigos más astutos, tanto como sus enemigos, hayan aprendido a estudiar su sonrisa más de cerca que su ceño. El presidente mide unas cinco pies ocho pulgadas de estatura, de complexión atlética, aunque su brazo derecho está todavía medio incapacitado por los efectos de dos viejas heridas cerca del codo; sus movimientos son por lo general deliberados, como si todo lo que hiciera en público fuera cuidadosamente calculado, pero es capaz de gran animación, volubilidad y vivacidad, sazona su conversación con sátira e ironía, y ocasionalmente ríe ampliamente de su propio chiste o epigrama, y es imposible no quedar impresionado por la fuerte comprensión de su mente y por la sutileza de su astucia. Todo el tiempo que duró la conversación, algo más de una hora, primero con el corresponsal y a intervalos con visitante tras visitante que venía a presentarle sus respetos, había tres o cuatro pobres hombres en las entrañas del buque, revolucionarios tomados con las armas en las manos, que esperaban la palabra de este imperturbable dictador para determinar si debían vivir o morir.

June 25, 189825 de junio de 1898

The Star's pen picture of President Heureaux closes with the story of his brother-in-law: it would matter very little to Heureaux, the correspondent writes, who of his own family interceded with him in favor of one of his enemies, and his conduct toward his own brother-in-law is still vividly remembered; he took the man to live with him in the presidential palace at Santo Domingo, clothed him, fed him and gave him official dignities, and twice the beneficiary conspired against the benefactor — the first time he was forgiven, but the second time Heureaux called him to him and said: 'I have tried to make a gentleman of you. Now I want you to put on your full-dress costume. In one hour you will be shot. There is no use for you to say anything. It must be. Only I particularly request that you behave like a gentleman' — and the brother-in-law was shot dead at the hour and minute. The prominent Haytiens who visited the president all hold offices related more or less to the administration of the port of Cape Haytien, and, with the exception of General Jules Pariden, whose wife is a cousin of Heureaux, they all presented veiled excuses or statements in exculpation of themselves in connection with the shipping of insurrectionists there when the Fanita departed for Monte Cristi, an implied acknowledgment of the comparative impotence of their own government and of the force and much-dreaded will power of the president of Santo Domingo.

El retrato a pluma del Star sobre el presidente Heureaux cierra con la historia de su cuñado: importaría muy poco a Heureaux, escribe el corresponsal, quién de su propia familia intercediera ante él a favor de uno de sus enemigos, y su conducta hacia su propio cuñado se recuerda todavía vívidamente; llevó al hombre a vivir con él en el palacio presidencial de Santo Domingo, lo vistió, lo alimentó y le dio dignidades oficiales, y dos veces el beneficiado conspiró contra el benefactor: la primera vez fue perdonado, pero la segunda Heureaux lo llamó ante sí y le dijo: «He tratado de hacer de usted un caballero. Ahora quiero que se ponga su traje de gala completo. Dentro de una hora será fusilado. No hay nada que usted pueda decir. Tiene que ser. Solo le pido particularmente que se comporte como un caballero», y el cuñado fue fusilado muerto a la hora y el minuto. Los prominentes haitianos que visitaron al presidente ocupan todos cargos relacionados más o menos con la administración del puerto de Cabo Haitiano, y, con la excepción del general Jules Pariden, cuya esposa es prima de Heureaux, todos presentaron excusas veladas o declaraciones en exculpación de sí mismos en conexión con el embarque de insurrectos allí cuando la Fanita partió para Monte Cristi, un reconocimiento implícito de la impotencia comparativa de su propio gobierno y de la fuerza y muy temida voluntad del presidente de Santo Domingo.

July 9, 18989 de julio de 1898

The Star prints the feature on the Columbus remains at Santo Domingo: the news of the discovery sent those of San Domingo into spasms of delight and those of Spain into corresponding rage, and when the news reached Madrid the Royal Academy instituted a searching inquiry, which resulted in sustaining the Havana theory, but the learned men of San Domingo are still firm in their belief that they have the only and original bones — the true and authenticated remains of the immortal Columbus. The writer was sent to San Domingo for the express purpose of sifting the existing evidence and making a personal examination, and bore letters of recommendation from the then Secretary of State, Mr. Blaine, to the president of the Dominican republic, and every opportunity was afforded him for a rigid investigation: the minister of public affairs himself accompanied him to the cathedral, and by his command the revered relics were brought from their place of deposit, in an ancient cedar chest within a dark cell near the presbytery, and exposed to the light of day.
