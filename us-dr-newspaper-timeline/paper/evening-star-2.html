Evening Star

No serious disorders have occurred in the Dominican capital

November 21, 191121 de noviembre de 1911

No serious disorders have occurred in the Dominican capital, amid intense excitement over the assassination of President Ramon Caceres, a Puerto Plata dispatch published in the Evening Star reports. Caceres was riding along a boulevard when his party was ambushed by gunfire from a side street by a group led by General Luis Tejera, former governor of Santo Domingo City; Caceres fell at the first volley, and Tejera was immediately shot and killed by the president's chief of staff. The cabinet has temporarily assumed executive authority and plans to convene the electoral college at once to choose a successor, with business communities expecting the government party to unite behind former leader General Horacio Vasquez. Dominicans of all political factions share the conviction that the United States will not permit a civil war in the country.

No se han registrado desórdenes de consideración en la capital dominicana, en medio de la viva agitación por el asesinato del presidente Ramón Cáceres, según un despacho de Puerto Plata publicado en el Evening Star. Cáceres se desplazaba por un bulevar cuando su comitiva fue emboscada desde una calle lateral a tiros por un grupo encabezado por el general Luis Tejera, antiguo gobernador de la ciudad de Santo Domingo; Cáceres cayó en la primera descarga y Tejera fue muerto a tiros en el acto por el jefe de Estado Mayor del mandatario. El gabinete asumió provisionalmente el poder ejecutivo y prevé convocar de inmediato al colegio electoral para elegir un sucesor, previendo los círculos comerciales que el partido gubernamental se cohesionará en torno al general Horacio Vásquez, antiguo jefe de dicha agrupación. Dominicanos de todas las corrientes políticas coinciden en la convicción de que los Estados Unidos no tolerarán una guerra civil en el país.

The Star prints an account of the question of the remains of Columbus: Mr. Barrett said

July 18, 191318 de julio de 1913

The Star prints an account of the question of the remains of Columbus: Mr. Barrett said that it is a subject of great significance, and that officials of the Dominican republic are very anxious to establish the authenticity of their claim. 'The Pan-American Union has always taken a deep interest in the subject, and we have a number of publications dealing with the claims of different places that the remains of Columbus rest there,' said Mr. Barrett. 'It is a question which has never been finally settled to the satisfaction of every one.' The Dominican minister to the United States is now at Santo Domingo, but before going there he selected a site for the Dominican building at the San Francisco exposition, and the little republic expects to make a good showing at the exposition; officials of the republic are now considering the suggestion that the remains be taken through the Panama canal, and then placed in their building at the exposition. An attempt was made a number of years ago by representatives of the Dominican republic to have their claims investigated by a committee from different American countries, but the claim has never been passed upon by any competent body. Spaniards scout the idea that the remains of Columbus are not in Seville, where a beautiful tomb has been erected; the remains were supposed to have been taken to Seville from Havana, following the Spanish-American war, and the Dominicans claim that the remains taken to Seville were really those of Diego, the son of Columbus.

El Star publica un relato sobre Colón y sus restos: el Sr. Barrett dijo que es un asunto de gran significación, y que los funcionarios de la República Dominicana están muy ansiosos por establecer la autenticidad de su reclamación. «La Unión Panamericana siempre ha tenido un profundo interés en el asunto, y tenemos una serie de publicaciones que tratan de las reclamaciones de los diferentes lugares de que los restos de Colón descansan allí», dijo el Sr. Barrett. «Es una cuestión que nunca ha sido resuelta definitivamente a satisfacción de todos». El ministro dominicano en Estados Unidos está ahora en Santo Domingo, pero antes de ir allí seleccionó un sitio para el edificio dominicano en la exposición de San Francisco, y la pequeña república espera hacer un buen papel en la exposición; los funcionarios de la república están considerando ahora la sugerencia de que los restos sean llevados a través del canal de Panamá, y luego colocados en su edificio en la exposición. Hace varios años, representantes de la República Dominicana intentaron que sus reclamaciones fueran investigadas por un comité de diferentes países americanos, pero la reclamación nunca ha sido fallada por ningún cuerpo competente. Los españoles desdeñan la idea de que los restos de Colón no estén en Sevilla, donde se ha erigido una hermosa tumba; se suponía que los restos habían sido llevados a Sevilla desde La Habana, tras la guerra hispano-estadounidense, y los dominicanos afirman que los restos llevados a Sevilla eran en realidad los de Diego, el hijo de Colón.

The Star prints a further account of the Columbus remains question

July 20, 191320 de julio de 1913

The Star prints a further account of the Columbus remains question: the late Thomas C. Dawson, formerly United States minister successively to the Dominican Republic, Chile, Colombia and Brazil, wrote an article some time ago in which he said that there could be no doubt but that the remains at Santo Domingo were really those of Columbus; he made this assertion after thoroughly examining all records, and wrote that the remains still rest in the little ancient Dominican city where they were sealed and deposited between the years 1541 and 1544. Señor Don Enrique Deschamps, formerly secretary of the Dominican legation at Washington, and still in the diplomatic service of his country, contributed an article on the question to the September, 1912, issue of the bulletin of the Pan-American Union, in which he reviewed all the authorities on the question, gave pictures of the casket in which the remains were found, and came to the conclusion that there can be no real doubt that Columbus' remains are in the Santo Domingo cathedral. Walker W. Vick, newly appointed collector of customs at Santo Domingo, has let it be known that when he goes to Santo Domingo to take up his new post he is going to make a thorough investigation of the subject.

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt

September 9, 19139 de septiembre de 1913

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt: it will be made known to rebel sympathizers that the Wilson administration is without sympathy with any government not founded upon law and orderly succession. Officials here to-day expressed confidence in the administrative ability and the integrity of President Bordas. They pointed out that one element of strength in his favor is the support of Desiderio Arias, who has been a leading spirit in many previous revolutions. Bordas is an ad interim provisional president, and is serving out that portion of the regular term of the presidency which was unexpired when Archbishop Nouel, provisional president succeeding President Victoria, gave up the office. Governor Cespedes of Puerto Plata province has a strong political following among the people of that province, and likewise commands large financial resources. He has built and equipped a private arsenal at Puerto Plata. It is, therefore, taken for granted that he is prepared to put up a strong fight against the attack being made by the gunboats in the harbor. Former Provisional President Vasquez, whose fortunes are cast with Cespedes in the present attempt to overthrow the regular government, commands equally great influence and resources in the rich farming countries in the interior. As it would be almost impossible to send an army overland to put down this uprising, the authorities in Santo Domingo City are depending altogether on the successful bombardment of Puerto Plata.

The Star publica comentarios de Washington sobre la revuelta de Puerto Plata: se hará saber a los simpatizantes rebeldes que la administración Wilson no simpatiza con ningún gobierno que no se funde en la ley y la sucesión ordenada. Los funcionarios aquí expresaron hoy confianza en la capacidad administrativa y la integridad del presidente Bordas. Señalaron que un elemento de fuerza a su favor es el apoyo de Desiderio Arias, que ha sido una figura dirigente en muchas revoluciones anteriores. Bordas es presidente provisional interino, y está sirviendo la parte del período regular de la presidencia que quedaba sin expirar cuando el arzobispo Nouel, presidente provisional que sucedió al presidente Victoria, renunció al cargo. El gobernador Céspedes, de la provincia de Puerto Plata, tiene un fuerte seguimiento político entre la gente de esa provincia, e igualmente dispone de grandes recursos financieros. Ha construido y equipado un arsenal privado en Puerto Plata. Por tanto, se da por sentado que está preparado para dar una fuerte pelea contra el ataque que hacen las cañoneras en el puerto. El expresidente provisional Vásquez, cuya suerte está echada con Céspedes en el intento actual de derrocar al gobierno regular, dispone de una influencia y unos recursos igualmente grandes en los ricos países agrícolas del interior. Como sería casi imposible enviar un ejército por tierra para sofocar este alzamiento, las autoridades de la ciudad de Santo Domingo dependen por completo del éxito del bombardeo de Puerto Plata.
