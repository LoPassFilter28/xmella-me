The New York Herald

In a Senate speech against annexing San Domingo, Mr.

April 8, 18718 de abril de 1871

In a Senate speech against annexing San Domingo, Mr. Morrill argues that the Bay of Samana would be a source of weakness rather than a defensive point, and that the Dominican people would prove a serious political, moral and financial encumbrance. He says Dominican commerce has steadily declined, the island would yield no revenue, and the United States would have to pay for a civil service, a permanent naval squadron, regiments of soldiers, harbors and railroads, while placing little reliance on claims that Dominicans favor annexation.

En un discurso en el Senado contra la anexión de Santo Domingo, el señor Morrill sostiene que la bahía de Samana sería una fuente de debilidad y no un punto defensivo, y que el pueblo dominicano resultaría una carga política, moral y financiera seria. Dice que el comercio dominicano ha disminuido constantemente, que la isla no produciría ingresos y que los Estados Unidos tendrían que pagar una administración civil, una escuadra naval permanente, regimientos de soldados, puertos y ferrocarriles, mientras da poco crédito a las afirmaciones de que los dominicanos favorecen la anexión.

April 8, 18718 de abril de 1871

Senator Morrill, speaking on President Grant's Santo Domingo policy, treats the commissioners' report with open skepticism, saying they praised the soil for producing bananas and pine-apples and were despondent about finding coal at Samana, where there is only a poor show of lignite. Their report, he argues, will be found neither more exhaustive nor more trustworthy than the works of impartial travelers who spent far more time on the island, and will be chiefly valuable for vindicating the personal integrity of the President and of the officer charged with negotiating the defeated treaty.

El senador Morrill, hablando sobre la política del presidente Grant hacia Santo Domingo, trata el informe de los comisionados con abierto escepticismo, diciendo que alabaron el suelo por producir bananas y piñas y que se mostraron desalentados por encontrar carbón en Samaná, donde solo hay una pobre muestra de lignito. Su informe, sostiene, no resultará ni más exhaustivo ni más digno de confianza que las obras de viajeros imparciales que pasaron mucho más tiempo en la isla, y será valioso sobre todo para vindicar la integridad personal del Presidente y la del oficial encargado de negociar el tratado derrotado.

Letters of General Luperon from Capotillo in early 1871 report him organizing a small force for a campaign in the Cibao, with anti-annexation propaganda very active around Guayabin and Monte Cristi and word that the American flag has been raised at Monte Cristi. He writes that Cabral could send him no men from the south, that he will make his sortie with only forty or fifty officers, and he urgently asks for at least fifty thousand chassepot cartridges; a Santo Domingo letter adds that Luperon was later defeated and his secretary, General Manuel Rodriguez Objio, captured and shot.

Escribe que Cabral no pudo enviarle hombres desde el sur, que hará su salida con solo cuarenta o cincuenta oficiales, y pide con urgencia al menos cincuenta mil cartuchos para fusiles chassepot; una carta de Santo Domingo añade que Luperon fue derrotado después y que su secretario, el general Manuel Rodriguez Objio, fue capturado y fusilado.
