The Wichita daily eagle

SummaryResumen

This profile of Heureaux recounts how he walked among men who had been swearing to take his life, told them they were not nearly so anxious for his life as they believed, and let them go free without taking any action. It describes his polished manner and entertaining conversation, his three-story palace in Santo Domingo covering a block, and the keen slight he felt because the aristocratic Dominicans refused to admit him to their social circles on account of his blood.

Este perfil de Heureaux relata cómo caminó entre hombres que habían estado jurando quitarle la vida, les dijo que no estaban ni de lejos tan ansiosos por su vida como ellos creían, y los dejó ir libres sin tomar ninguna medida. Describe su trato cortés y su conversación entretenida, su palacio de tres pisos en Santo Domingo que ocupa una manzana, y el desaire que sentía agudamente porque los dominicanos aristocráticos se negaban a admitirlo en sus círculos sociales a causa de su sangre.

The Salt Lake herald

SummaryResumen

The *Salt Lake Herald* reported that American authorities under Military Governor Leonard Wood were actively patrolling Cuban waters and seizing vessels to prevent Dominican filibustering expeditions from departing Cuba's northern coast. In Santo Domingo, President Figuereo expressed confidence in preserving national stability and dismissed recent street demonstrations as inconsequential while voicing goodwill toward the United States. Concurrently, a special session of the Dominican Congress adjourned without acting on a proposal to reduce national debt interest payments by twenty percent.

El *Salt Lake Herald* informó que las autoridades estadounidenses bajo el gobernador militar Leonard Wood patrullaban activamente las aguas cubanas e incautaban embarcaciones para impedir que zarparan expediciones filibusteras dominicanas desde la costa norte de Cuba. En Santo Domingo, el presidente Figuereo manifestó confianza en mantener la estabilidad nacional y desestimó las recientes manifestaciones callejeras calificándolas de irrelevantes, a la vez que expresaba buena voluntad hacia los Estados Unidos. Simultáneamente, una sesión extraordinaria del Congreso dominicano se clausuró sin tomar medidas sobre una propuesta para reducir en un veinte por ciento los pagos de intereses de la deuda nacional.

SummaryResumen

A feature on Santo Domingo describes its minerals, including rock salt, petroleum found near Azua, abundant copper and great masses of iron ore in the interior, and explains that the president is chosen by an electoral college for four years with a cabinet of six. It says sugar, tobacco, cacao and coffee are the leading exports, that the United States led all other countries in trade with the republic, that most sugar estates are owned by Americans with the crop estimated at 65,000 tons, and that steamers of the Samana Bay Fruit Company carry fifteen to eighteen thousand bunches of bananas on each trip to the United States.

This account describes the Republic of Santo Domingo, which covers 18,045 of the island's 28,240 square miles, its rivers, its gold washings, the rock-salt hills of the Neyba valley, petroleum near Azua and other minerals, and a government headed by a president chosen by an electoral college for four years. The Independent

SummaryResumen

The *Independent* reported that the Dominican government organized a commission to negotiate directly with European bondholders, hoping a financial settlement would dispel talk of United States annexation, which was widely opposed by the Dominican populace. Representantes de la San Domingo Improvement Company en Nueva York admitieron que, aunque la anexión podría reportar ventajas económicas, no había llegado el momento para dar tal paso. The sun

SummaryResumen

Writing from Santiago de los Caballeros on June 17, the correspondent says Santo Domingo has had some two hundred revolutions within sixty years of independence and that their battles were long fought with little bloodshed, but that this is no longer the rule. He asserts that more people have been killed in the present revolution than in the war with Spain forty years ago, and gives eyewitness accounts of the battle at Puerto Plata last January, where soldiers on both sides fired their guns off as fast as they could load until their ammunition was gone, and then ran.

Escribiendo desde Santiago de los Caballeros el 17 de junio, el corresponsal dice que Santo Domingo ha tenido unas doscientas revoluciones en sesenta años de independencia y que sus batallas se libraban durante mucho tiempo con poco derramamiento de sangre, pero que eso ya no es la regla. Afirma que en la revolución actual ha muerto más gente que en la guerra con España de hace cuarenta años, y ofrece testimonios de la batalla de Puerto Plata del enero pasado, donde los soldados de ambos bandos dispararon sus armas tan rápido como podían cargarlas hasta agotar las municiones, y luego huyeron.

The Minneapolis journal

SummaryResumen

A travel narrative follows the old Camino Real, the royal road running 150 miles north from Santo Domingo City to Cotui, La Vega and Moca, describing the ancient Spanish roads as broad paths cut a hundred feet wide through the forest, with no ditching, drainage, macadam or bridges, and the Isabella River crossed in native dugout canoes while the horses swim.
