The Birmingham age-herald

SummaryResumen

The Birmingham Age-Herald reported President Taft's address to Congress affirming the successful operation of the U.S. customs receivership in the Dominican Republic. Taft reported that despite a decline in customs revenues due to economic conditions and tariff anticipation, bond debt payments were fulfilled and progress was made in eliminating commercial monopolies. The message also addressed U.S. diplomatic interventions in contractual claims against Nicaragua and Chile.

The Birmingham Age-Herald informó sobre el mensaje del presidente Taft al Congreso confirmando el exitoso funcionamiento de la receptoría aduanera estadounidense en la República Dominicana. Taft comunicó que, pese a una disminución en las rentas aduaneras por circunstancias económicas y a la expectativa arancelaria, se cumplieron los pagos de la deuda en bonos y se avanzó en la erradicación de monopolios comerciales. El mensaje abordó asimismo las gestiones diplomáticas de los EE. UU. en reclamaciones contractuales contra Nicaragua y Chile.

The Topeka State Journal continued President Taft's address, noting that five years of treaty operations in Santo Domingo had eliminated insurrections by placing customs houses under American protection. The *Evening star* published a statement answering criticisms by Julio M. Cestero, pointing out that Cestero had originally supported the 1907 convention establishing American customs administration. The statement denied claims of financial mismanagement, asserting that Dominican treasury funds were in excellent health with a $5,000,000 surplus. It commended Military Governor Rear Admiral Thomas Snowden for conducting an honest administration and pursuing reforms intended to prepare the country for the restoration of native self-government.
