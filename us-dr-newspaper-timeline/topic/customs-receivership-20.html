The Salt Lake Tribune

The Tribune comments editorially on the Monroe doctrine: the United States

September 7, 19137 de septiembre de 1913

The Tribune comments editorially on the Monroe doctrine: the United States has in pursuance of the Monroe doctrine saved the Dominican Republic from pillage on the part of creditor peoples in Europe. The financial agent which the United States maintains in Santo Domingo has been able to scale down the debt of that Republic to less than half of the preposterous claims made and prepared for enforcement, and our agent there, the collector of the customs revenues, is collecting these revenues honestly and applying them effectively to the payment of interest and to the extinguishment of the principal of its debts. So that the Monroe doctrine is always a help, not a menace.

The Tribune comenta editorialmente la doctrina Monroe: los Estados Unidos salvaron en cumplimiento de la doctrina Monroe a la República Dominicana del pillaje por parte de los pueblos acreedores de Europa. El agente financiero que los Estados Unidos mantienen en Santo Domingo ha podido reducir la deuda de esa república a menos de la mitad de las reclamaciones desmedidas presentadas y preparadas para su cobro, y nuestro agente allí, el recaudador de las rentas aduaneras, está cobrando esas rentas con honradez y aplicándolas eficazmente al pago de intereses y a la extinción del principal de sus deudas. De modo que la doctrina Monroe siempre es una ayuda, no una amenaza.

Evening Star

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt

September 9, 19139 de septiembre de 1913

The Star prints Washington comment on the Puerto Plata revolt: it will be made known to rebel sympathizers that the Wilson administration is without sympathy with any government not founded upon law and orderly succession. Officials here to-day expressed confidence in the administrative ability and the integrity of President Bordas. They pointed out that one element of strength in his favor is the support of Desiderio Arias, who has been a leading spirit in many previous revolutions. Bordas is an ad interim provisional president, and is serving out that portion of the regular term of the presidency which was unexpired when Archbishop Nouel, provisional president succeeding President Victoria, gave up the office. Governor Cespedes of Puerto Plata province has a strong political following among the people of that province, and likewise commands large financial resources. He has built and equipped a private arsenal at Puerto Plata. It is, therefore, taken for granted that he is prepared to put up a strong fight against the attack being made by the gunboats in the harbor. Former Provisional President Vasquez, whose fortunes are cast with Cespedes in the present attempt to overthrow the regular government, commands equally great influence and resources in the rich farming countries in the interior. As it would be almost impossible to send an army overland to put down this uprising, the authorities in Santo Domingo City are depending altogether on the successful bombardment of Puerto Plata.

The Star publica comentarios de Washington sobre la revuelta de Puerto Plata: se hará saber a los simpatizantes rebeldes que la administración Wilson no simpatiza con ningún gobierno que no se funde en la ley y la sucesión ordenada. Los funcionarios aquí expresaron hoy confianza en la capacidad administrativa y la integridad del presidente Bordas. Señalaron que un elemento de fuerza a su favor es el apoyo de Desiderio Arias, que ha sido una figura dirigente en muchas revoluciones anteriores. Bordas es presidente provisional interino, y está sirviendo la parte del período regular de la presidencia que quedaba sin expirar cuando el arzobispo Nouel, presidente provisional que sucedió al presidente Victoria, renunció al cargo. El gobernador Céspedes, de la provincia de Puerto Plata, tiene un fuerte seguimiento político entre la gente de esa provincia, e igualmente dispone de grandes recursos financieros. Ha construido y equipado un arsenal privado en Puerto Plata. Por tanto, se da por sentado que está preparado para dar una fuerte pelea contra el ataque que hacen las cañoneras en el puerto. El expresidente provisional Vásquez, cuya suerte está echada con Céspedes en el intento actual de derrocar al gobierno regular, dispone de una influencia y unos recursos igualmente grandes en los ricos países agrícolas del interior. Como sería casi imposible enviar un ejército por tierra para sofocar este alzamiento, las autoridades de la ciudad de Santo Domingo dependen por completo del éxito del bombardeo de Puerto Plata.

The Colorado Statesman

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance, inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican Republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for wiping out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 in 1905 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.

The Statesman publica un reportaje bajo el título 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, de Nueva Jersey, ha sido nombrado por el presidente Wilson receptor general de las aduanas de la república de Santo Domingo, continuándose así un experimento novedoso en las finanzas nacionales, inaugurado en 1905. Es el primer caso en que una nación en bancarrota ha aceptado que otra nación actúe como receptora de sus rentas y se interponga entre ella y sus acreedores durante la liquidación de sus obligaciones pendientes. En la actualidad, la República Dominicana tiene más dinero para gastar en fines apropiados que nunca antes, al tiempo que se está proveyendo ampliamente para extinguir su deuda en fecha temprana. La deuda de $30,000,000 en 1905 se ha reducido a $17,000,000, mientras las cuotas aduaneras se han reducido, el comercio ha aumentado, el desgobierno ha llegado a su fin y las bendiciones de la paz se han establecido en el país antes turbulento.

The Washington Herald

The Herald comments editorially: information laid before President Wilson

September 19, 191319 de septiembre de 1913

The Herald comments editorially: information laid before President Wilson by State Department officials convinced him that the revolution in Santo Domingo was purely one of greed upon the part of a small group of malcontents, and that the rebellion against the Bordas government was not established on any principle other than that of a desire for office and the proceeds thereof. Consequently, it was determined almost at the outset that everything should be done to discourage the revolutionists. They were at once informed of the disfavor with which the Washington government viewed their attempt to bring on another political upheaval. This declaration of disapproval was soon followed by the double threat of nonrecognition and withholding funds in case the rebels should succeed by force in gaining control of the government authority. Minister Sullivan, recently appointed minister to Santo Domingo, has arrived at Puerto Plata, the stronghold of the revolutionists, and is reiterating to the rebel leaders the determined position of President Wilson. He is backed up by the gunboat Des Moines.

The Herald comenta editorialmente: la información presentada al presidente Wilson por funcionarios del Departamento de Estado lo convenció de que la revolución en Santo Domingo era puramente una de codicia por parte de un pequeño grupo de descontentos, y de que la rebelión contra el gobierno de Bordas no se fundaba en principio alguno que no fuera el deseo de cargos y de sus provechos. En consecuencia, se determinó casi desde el principio que debía hacerse todo lo posible por desalentar a los revolucionarios. Se les informó de inmediato del desagrado con que el Gobierno de Washington veía su intento de provocar otro trastorno político. Esta declaración de desaprobación fue seguida pronto por la doble amenaza del no reconocimiento y de la retención de fondos en caso de que los rebeldes lograran, por la fuerza, hacerse con el control de la autoridad gubernamental. El ministro Sullivan, recién nombrado ministro en Santo Domingo, ha llegado a Puerto Plata, baluarte de los revolucionarios, y está reiterando a los jefes rebeldes la determinada posición del presidente Wilson. Lo respalda el cañonero Des Moines.

Pinedale Roundup

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey

October 23, 191323 de octubre de 1913

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver-general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for paying out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.

Es el primer caso en que una nación en bancarrota ha aceptado que otra nación actúe como receptor de sus rentas y se interponga entre ella y sus acreedores durante la liquidación de sus obligaciones pendientes. En la actualidad, la república dominicana tiene más dinero para gastar en fines adecuados que nunca antes, mientras se provee ampliamente para pagar su deuda en una fecha temprana.
