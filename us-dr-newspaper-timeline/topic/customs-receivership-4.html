An article in the Arizona Republican quotes a female traveler

August 9, 19109 de agosto de 1910

An article in the Arizona Republican quotes a female traveler recounting her visit to the island republics alongside her husband. She characterizes Santo Domingo, described as the capital of the Spanish republic founded by Christopher Columbus, as one of the most fascinating places she ever visited, rejecting characterizations of the Dominican Republic as 'black' by noting that some of the best blood of Spain flows in the veins of its people. Comparing the capital to a miniature Madrid, she leaves commercial and political affairs to her husband while expressing admiration for the country's romantic history, folk songs, and the customs of its peasantry.

Caracteriza a Santo Domingo, descrita como la capital de la república española fundada por Cristóbal Colón, como uno de los lugares más fascinantes que jamás haya visitado, rechazando las caracterizaciones de la República Dominicana como «negra» al señalar que parte de la mejor sangre de España corre por las venas de su pueblo. The Fargo Forum and Daily Republican

The rice trade of the Dominican Republic is virtually monopolized

September 5, 19105 de septiembre de 1910

The rice trade of the Dominican Republic is virtually monopolized by Germany, The Fargo Forum and Daily Republican observes, quoting an item from the Pittsburg Dispatch, citing reports from Commercial Agent Turner in Santo Domingo City. To provide freight for its West Indies route between St. Thomas and Jamaica, the Hamburg-American Line arranged through rates and called at a half dozen Dominican ports. In 1909, the line transported 18,000,000 pounds of rice valued at $801,546 to those Dominican ports.

El comercio de arroz en la República Dominicana es monopolizado prácticamente por Alemania, observa The Fargo Forum and Daily Republican, al citar una nota del Pittsburg Dispatch y según informes del agente comercial Turner en la ciudad de Santo Domingo. Para asegurar flete en su ruta de las Antillas entre Saint Thomas y Jamaica, la Hamburg-American Line fijó tarifas directas e hizo escala en media docena de puertos dominicanos. En 1909, la línea transportó a dichos puertos dominicanos 18,000,000 de libras de arroz valoradas en $801,546.
