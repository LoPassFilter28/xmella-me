Under the subheading "Did It in Santo Domingo," an analytical feature

November 12, 191112 de noviembre de 1911

Under the subheading "Did It in Santo Domingo," an analytical feature in The Salt Lake Tribune examines how United States supervision stabilized the country following the 1865 withdrawal of Spanish control. The article recounts that the government established after Spanish evacuation was overthrown within a month, and that during the thirty years following, up to and including 1905, the Dominican Republic experienced twenty general revolutions and twenty-five successive administrations. Amid this chronic unrest, internal progress remained paralyzed, leaving not ten miles of roadway across the entire republic while industry lagged and national revenues failed to reach the treasury. During this prolonged turmoil, public debts expanded unchecked, interrupted only by a period of comparative peace from 1888 to 1899.

Bajo el subtítulo "Lo hizo en Santo Domingo", un artículo de fondo en The Salt Lake Tribune analiza cómo la supervisión de los Estados Unidos estabilizó al país tras el retiro del control español en 1865. El artículo relata que el gobierno establecido tras la evacuación española fue derrocado en menos de un mes, y que durante los treinta años subsiguientes, abarcando hasta 1905, la República Dominicana experimentó veinte revoluciones generales y veinticinco administraciones sucesivas. En medio de esta crónica inestabilidad, el desarrollo interno se mantuvo paralizado, no quedando ni diez millas de carreteras en toda la república mientras la industria decaía y las rentas nacionales no ingresaban a la tesorería. A lo largo de esta prolongada agitación, las deudas públicas crecieron sin control, con la única excepción de un período de relativa paz entre 1888 y 1899.

The Salt Lake Tribune reviewed the origin of the 1905 Dominican modus vivendi, crediting former American Minister Thomas C. Dawson with devising the administrative revenue plan. El artículo observó que las guerras civiles cesaron casi de inmediato una vez que los revolucionarios se vieron impedidos de apoderarse de las aduanas y de sus caudales.

El Sr. Sullivan ha dado a conocer que la última guerra civil de esta república ya se ha librado. La política estadounidense, tal como la anunció el Sr. Sullivan, ha sido aceptada por todos los partidos sin importar las afiliaciones políticas.

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey

October 23, 191323 de octubre de 1913

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver-general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for paying out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.
