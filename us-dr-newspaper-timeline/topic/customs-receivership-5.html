The Savannah Morning News

The Morning News prints the closing part of a Port-au-Prince dispatch

November 15, 190315 de noviembre de 1903

The Morning News prints the closing part of a Port-au-Prince dispatch: the French steamer St. Simon, with General Jiminez, head of the Dominican revolution, on board, has left Port Au Prince, Hayti, with the intention, according to report, of forcing the blockade of Puerto Plata on the northern coast of San Domingo; the Haytian authorities formally opposed the landing in Hayti of Gen. Jiminez, in spite of his strong insistence on being permitted to do so; it is said that if the St. Simon is interfered with she will ask the assistance of the German cruiser Gazelle or any other foreign warship which may be in Dominican waters. Telegraphic communication with the interior of Santo Domingo was re-established yesterday morning, but now is again interrupted, and it is reported that severe fighting must have occurred before San Domingo.

El Morning News publica la parte final de un despacho de Puerto Príncipe: el vapor francés St. Simon, con el general Jiménez, jefe de la revolución dominicana, a bordo, ha salido de Puerto Príncipe, Haití, con la intención, según se informa, de forzar el bloqueo de Puerto Plata, en la costa norte de San Domingo; las autoridades haitianas se opusieron formalmente al desembarco en Haití del general Jiménez, a pesar de su fuerte insistencia en que se le permitiera hacerlo; se dice que si el St. Simon es interferido pedirá la ayuda del crucero alemán Gazelle o de cualquier otro buque de guerra extranjero que pueda estar en aguas dominicanas. La comunicación telegráfica con el interior de Santo Domingo fue restablecida ayer por la mañana, pero ahora está de nuevo interrumpida, y se informa que debe haber ocurrido una lucha severa frente a San Domingo.

Daily Kennebec Journal

The Dominican government has appointed Minister of Foreign Rotations Galvan

November 18, 190318 de noviembre de 1903

The Daily Kennebec Journal reported that the United States government formally refused to recognize the Dominican naval blockade as effective and instructed port collectors to issue clearances for all Dominican harbors. In Dominican waters, insurgents fired unsuccessfully on the American steamer New York at Samana, while Minister Powell rejected rebel appeals for diplomatic recognition. In response to American diplomatic pressure, the Dominican government named Foreign Minister Galván and Judge Gray as arbitrators to resolve the San Domingo Improvement Company claim.

El Daily Kennebec Journal informó que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos se negó formalmente a reconocer el bloqueo naval dominicano como efectivo e instruyó a los administradores de aduanas a conceder despachos hacia todos los puertos dominicanos. En aguas dominicanas, insurgentes dispararon infructuosamente contra el vapor estadounidense New York en Samaná, al tiempo que el ministro Powell desestimaba las peticiones rebeldes de reconocimiento diplomático. En respuesta a la presión diplomática estadounidense, el gobierno dominicano designó al ministro de Relaciones Exteriores Galván y al juez Gray como árbitros para resolver la reclamación de la San Domingo Improvement Company.

The Montgomery Advertiser

The Advertiser prints part of a New York item on the Cherokee's sailing

November 19, 190319 de noviembre de 1903

The Advertiser prints part of a New York item on the Cherokee's sailing: C. I. Maxwell, consul general to San Domingo, sailed in the Cherokee; he said he would not be surprised to find the revolution ended when he reached there, and that in a way he has orders to advise the captain of the ship what to do in certain contingencies, though he cannot say what his advice will be until they reach the spot and he sees what confronts them. He thinks the Baltimore will be at Monte Cristi, their first port of call, before they get there, and in that event the officers will decide what they are to do; if the Dominican officials establish a blockade and can maintain it, of the five ports named, or any one of them, they cannot enter, but to make the blockade effectual it must be perpetual, the gunboats which meet them at Monte Cristi and prevent their entering that harbor being unable to follow them to another port and keep them out there. The Dominican government has two gunboats, capable of maintaining a blockade of two ports at the same time, but he does not think they could effectively blockade more than that; he has heard a rumor that the government has armed two sailing vessels as supplementary warships for this blockade service, but does not know that it is true. Consul Galvan said he understood several cases of arms were offered for shipment on the Cherokee to Puerto Plata, but the Clyde people would not take them unless he certified his consent; the shippers did not come to him for approval, and he does not know who they were.

El Advertiser publica parte de una nota de Nueva York sobre la partida del Cherokee: C. I. Maxwell, cónsul general en San Domingo, zarpó en el Cherokee; dijo que no le sorprendería encontrar la revolución terminada cuando llegara allí, y que en cierto modo tiene órdenes de asesorar al capitán del barco sobre qué hacer en ciertas contingencias, aunque no puede decir cuál será su consejo hasta que lleguen al lugar y vea lo que les espera. Cree que el Baltimore estará en Monte Cristi, su primer puerto de escala, antes de que ellos lleguen, y en ese caso los oficiales decidirán lo que deben hacer; si los oficiales dominicanos establecen un bloqueo y pueden mantenerlo, de los cinco puertos nombrados, o de cualquiera de ellos, no podrán entrar, pero para que el bloqueo sea efectivo debe ser perpetuo, pues las cañoneras que los reciban en Monte Cristi y les impidan entrar en ese puerto no podrán seguirlos a otro puerto y mantenerlos fuera de allí. El gobierno dominicano tiene dos cañoneras, capaces de mantener un bloqueo de dos puertos al mismo tiempo, pero no cree que pudieran bloquear efectivamente más que eso; ha oído un rumor de que el gobierno ha armado dos veleros como buques de guerra suplementarios para este servicio de bloqueo, pero no sabe que sea cierto. El cónsul Galván dijo que entendía que varias cajas de armas fueron ofrecidas para embarque en el Cherokee hacia Puerto Plata, pero que la gente de la Clyde no las aceptaría a menos que él certificara su consentimiento; los cargadores no acudieron a él por su aprobación, y no sabe quiénes eran.

The Indianapolis Journal

The Journal prints a Santo Domingo item: Commander Briggs's reports

November 19, 190319 de noviembre de 1903

The Journal prints a Santo Domingo item: Commander Briggs's reports indicate that the bombardments of San Domingo by the insurrectionists have been hot; having stated that his coal supply was running low, Commander Briggs was authorized to take the Baltimore to San Juan for coal and then return to San Domingo city, and, in order that the interests of the United States in that city should not be left unprotected in the absence of the cruiser Baltimore, orders were telegraphed to Commander Diehl, commanding the gunboat Newport, at Puerto Plata, on the north coast of San Domingo, to proceed at once to San Domingo city, on the southern coast; it is stated it will not take the Baltimore more than a day or two to make the run to San Juan and back. A Cape Haytien dispatch of November 18 adds that information has been received at Monte Cristi from Santo Domingo that United States Minister Powell, accompanied by Minister of Foreign Relations Galvan, has gone aboard the United States cruiser Baltimore, the object of their visit to the warship being unknown, and that the arrival of the United States gunboat Newport is anxiously awaited, she being believed to be still at Puerto Plata. A New York item adds that Consul General Galvan, of San Domingo, refused to sign clearance papers for the Clyde Line steamer Cherokee to the five Dominican ports in the hands of insurgents, for which the Cherokee sailed today, but granted clearance papers for the ports of San Domingo and Azua.

El Journal publica una nota de Santo Domingo: los informes del comandante Briggs indican que los bombardeos de San Domingo por los insurreccionistas han sido intensos; habiendo declarado que su suministro de carbón se estaba agotando, el comandante Briggs fue autorizado a llevar el Baltimore a San Juan por carbón y luego regresar a la ciudad de San Domingo, y, para que los intereses de los Estados Unidos en esa ciudad no quedaran desprotegidos en ausencia del crucero Baltimore, se telegrafiaron órdenes al comandante Diehl, al mando de la cañonera Newport, en Puerto Plata, en la costa norte de San Domingo, para que procediera de inmediato a la ciudad de San Domingo, en la costa sur; se afirma que el Baltimore no tardará más de un día o dos en hacer el viaje a San Juan y de regreso. Un despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 18 de noviembre añade que se ha recibido información en Monte Cristi desde Santo Domingo de que el ministro estadounidense Powell, acompañado por el ministro de Relaciones Exteriores Galván, ha subido a bordo del crucero estadounidense Baltimore, siendo desconocido el objeto de su visita al buque de guerra, y que la llegada de la cañonera estadounidense Newport es esperada con ansiedad, creyéndose que aún está en Puerto Plata. Una nota de Nueva York añade que el cónsul general Galván, de San Domingo, se negó a firmar papeles de despacho para el vapor Cherokee, de la línea Clyde, hacia los cinco puertos dominicanos en manos de los insurgentes, por los que el Cherokee zarpó hoy, pero concedió papeles de despacho para los puertos de San Domingo y Azua.

The Saint Paul Globe

The Globe prints a San Juan dispatch of November 18: the French steamer

November 19, 190319 de noviembre de 1903

The Globe prints a San Juan dispatch of November 18: the French steamer St. Simon arrived there today from Haiti and Santo Domingo; she touched at Puerto Plata, on the north coast of Santo Domingo, and reports that all of the Dominican republic, except the capital, San Domingo, was in the hands of the revolutionists; the St. Simon also reported that General Jiminez, leader of the Dominican revolutionists, left Haiti on November 16 and was due to arrive Friday next before San Domingo and assume direction of the siege of that city.

Evening Star

The Star prints a sketch of General Ramon Caceres, the slayer of President Heureaux

November 25, 190325 de noviembre de 1903

The Star prints a sketch of General Ramon Caceres, the slayer of President Heureaux on June 26, 1899 and ex-governor of Santiago de los Caballeros: he is one of the chief leaders of the present revolution, ranks next to Jiminez, and will most likely be appointed to the vice presidency should the revolution triumph; General Caceres is a man of great energy and courage and has set his heart upon the highest position in the republic, but he is unfortunately a man of the sword and not of the pen, and although he might make an excellent president for the quelling of revolutions, he would certainly not know how to set about extracting the republic from its present financial difficulties, which are many and pressing; if the present revolution triumphs it will have many claims upon its hands: there is a Cuban claim for preventing the Cuban steamer Maria Herrera to enter the port of Puerto Plata to land her passengers, mail and cargo without there being any notification whatever of the existence of a regular blockade; there is just such another claim to be made by the Clyde Line of steamers, there is a German claim, and there is said to be a Spanish claim for the death of the chancellor of the Spanish consulate who was killed by a bullet while writing in his office during the last revolution, eight months ago, and others; meanwhile the resources of the country have been completely paralyzed, and sugar, which is the chief product, is lacking a market, the American market being the only one it ever had.

El Star publica un bosquejo del general Ramón Cáceres, el asesino del presidente Heureaux el 26 de junio de 1899 y exgobernador de Santiago de los Caballeros: es uno de los principales líderes de la revolución actual, ocupa el segundo lugar después de Jiménez y muy probablemente será nombrado vicepresidente si la revolución triunfa; el general Cáceres es un hombre de gran energía y valor y ha puesto su corazón en la más alta posición de la república, pero es desafortunadamente un hombre de espada y no de pluma, y aunque podría ser un excelente presidente para sofocar revoluciones, ciertamente no sabría cómo sacar a la república de sus actuales dificultades financieras, que son muchas y apremiantes; si la revolución actual triunfa tendrá muchas reclamaciones entre manos: hay una reclamación cubana por impedir que el vapor cubano María Herrera entrara al puerto de Puerto Plata para desembarcar a sus pasajeros, el correo y la carga sin que hubiera notificación alguna de la existencia de un bloqueo regular; hay otra reclamación igual que hará la línea de vapores Clyde, hay una reclamación alemana, y se dice que hay una reclamación española por la muerte del canciller del consulado español, quien fue muerto por una bala mientras escribía en su oficina durante la última revolución, hace ocho meses, y otras; mientras tanto los recursos del país han quedado completamente paralizados, y el azúcar, que es el producto principal, carece de mercado, siendo el mercado estadounidense el único que jamás tuvo.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints a New York item of November 21 and three dispatches

The Democrat prints a San Domingo dispatch and a Washington item of November 23

The Democrat prints a San Domingo dispatch and a Washington item of November 23: President Woss y Gil requested the ministers of the United States, Belgium, Haiti and Spain to intervene in behalf of the government; the insurgents refused to accept the terms offered and demanded the immediate capitulation of the city; United States Minister Powell informed the revolutionary chiefs that periodical revolutions ought to cease, because they interrupted commercial relations with other countries and augmented the debt of Santo Domingo, which was without means to pay foreign creditors; the minister said further that these periodical revolutions endanger the peace of his government and of the governments of other nations and that the present revolution, if continued, would imperil the sovereignty of Santo Domingo; it is said that the tide of revolution will once again cast Don Isidro Jiminez into the presidential chair of the Dominican republic, and he will thus find himself for the third time ruler of that turbulent little country. The Washington item says revolutionary success in Santo Domingo may unpleasantly involve the United States, and there is a possibility that this government will have to adopt a strong policy to obtain respect for American property interests in the Dominican republic, which may mean intervention by the Washington government for the purpose of bringing order and stable government out of revolutionary chaos and constant danger to foreign interests.

The Portland Daily Press

The Press prints part of a feature on Samana bay in the West Indies

November 28, 190328 de noviembre de 1903

The Press prints part of a feature on Samana bay in the West Indies: the bay, one of the fine and valuable bays in the West Indies, thirty-one miles long, lies at the eastern extremity of the island, just across from Porto Rico; for almost one hundred years the United States has sought possession of this bay as a strategic point in the Caribbean sea; General Jackson first recommended the purchase in a message to Congress, President Buchanan made two or three recommendations, and Presidents Lincoln and Johnson followed with like suggestions; able engineers were sent to report on the waters and various conditions, and the project finally culminated in President Grant's open effort to secure the bay for the United States, which was defeated by Sumner in the famous and bitter fight along about 1871; the report of the commission sent to Santo Domingo at that time is an elaborate and interesting document, and while no President has openly taken any stand since Grant's day the statesmen have all recognized the value of the possession, and it is an open secret in Washington that negotiations have been opened several times through the State Department for the purchase of the bay; the failure of the project was something General Grant never forgot, it was the most bitter defeat he ever received, and every message, including his last, referred to the mistake which had been made in not acquiring the bay of Samana; and under the head 'Efforts of the Powers' the feature adds that rumor reaches this country occasionally of the attempted purchase of ports in Hayti and Santo Domingo by European powers.
