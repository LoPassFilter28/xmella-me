The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints a special cable from Puerto Plata reporting

December 18, 190318 de diciembre de 1903

The Republic prints a special cable from Puerto Plata reporting that Puerto Plata has surrendered to the revolutionists without fighting; the government forces, after a severe battle, have been forced to abandon Santiago de los Caballeros — they were short of ammunition; the general who announced that he would support the provisional President Morales has fled, and has covered up his tracks. The dispatch shows that the adherents of General Jiminez, who aspires to the Dominican presidency, have gained supremacy in the northern part of Santo Domingo; the new revolution started last week around Santiago de los Caballeros, where there has been fighting several days; the Morales provisional government came into existence about two weeks ago after the capture of the Dominican capital and the flight of President Woss y Gil.

El Republic publica un cable especial de Puerto Plata que informa que Puerto Plata se ha rendido a los revolucionarios sin luchar; las fuerzas del gobierno, tras una severa batalla, se han visto obligadas a abandonar Santiago de los Caballeros: les faltaban municiones; el general que anunció que apoyaría al presidente provisional Morales ha huido y ha borrado su rastro. El despacho muestra que los partidarios del general Jiménez, que aspira a la presidencia dominicana, han ganado la supremacía en la parte norte de Santo Domingo; la nueva revolución comenzó la semana pasada en los alrededores de Santiago de los Caballeros, donde ha habido combates durante varios días; el gobierno provisional de Morales surgió hace unas dos semanas tras la captura de la capital dominicana y la huida del presidente Woss y Gil.

New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints a dispatch: the supporters of Woss y Gil held

December 21, 190321 de diciembre de 1903

The Tribune prints a dispatch: the supporters of Woss y Gil held a long conference to-day; the members of the party on the Julia express the belief that the intervention of the United States in the affairs of San Domingo will be found necessary soon; they declare that a crisis is rapidly approaching and that the provisional government finds it impossible to negotiate loans. The Birmingham Age-Herald

The Age-Herald prints a San Domingo dispatch of December 25

December 26, 190326 de diciembre de 1903

The Age-Herald prints a San Domingo dispatch of December 25 reporting that it is rumored that the greater part of the republic now under arms is in favor of General Jiminez, the former president, who is said to be marching on this city at the head of a large body of insurgents; the provisional government is making active preparations for defense; San Pedro de Macoris has declared in favor of the insurgents; the situation here is becoming extremely critical, though the city is quiet in expectation of events; there is a great depression in business circles as the result of the constant disturbance of the financial situation; the financial condition of the government is very bad, owing to the fact that the duties were mortgaged in advance by the government of President Woss y Gil. The United States gunboat Newport arrived in port today; the German cruisers Vineta and Gazelle also arrived today; the Dutch warship De Ruyter has been sighted.

El Age-Herald publica un despacho de Santo Domingo del 25 de diciembre que informa que se rumora que la mayor parte de la república hoy en armas está a favor del general Jiménez, el expresidente, de quien se dice que marcha sobre esta ciudad al frente de un numeroso cuerpo de insurgentes; el gobierno provisional hace activos preparativos de defensa; San Pedro de Macorís se ha declarado a favor de los insurgentes; la situación aquí se está volviendo extremadamente crítica, aunque la ciudad está tranquila a la expectativa de los acontecimientos; hay una gran depresión en los círculos comerciales a consecuencia de la constante perturbación de la situación financiera; la condición financiera del gobierno es muy mala, debido a que los derechos de aduana fueron hipotecados por anticipado por el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil. La cañonera estadounidense Newport llegó hoy al puerto; los cruceros alemanes Vineta y Gazelle también llegaron hoy; el buque de guerra holandés De Ruyter ha sido avistado.

New-York Tribune

Should the insurgents succeed in effecting an entrance to the city

December 29, 190329 de diciembre de 1903

The New-York Tribune reported that the besieged Dominican provisional government was unable to raise a $20,000 loan for city defense, leaving the capital vulnerable to insurgent assault and street fighting. With the gunboat Newport remaining as the only foreign warship in port, concerns grew over the safety of foreign residents. Meanwhile, insurgent leader Juan Isidro Jimenes scored a decisive victory at Moca against 500 government troops under General Ramón Cáceres, who was wounded, clearing the path for an advance on Santo Domingo.

El New-York Tribune informó que el asediado gobierno provisional dominicano fue incapaz de obtener un préstamo de 20.000 dólares para la defensa de la ciudad, dejando a la capital expuesta a asaltos insurgentes y combates callejeros. Siendo el cañonero Newport el único buque de guerra extranjero en el puerto, creció la inquietud por la seguridad de los residentes extranjeros. Mientras tanto, el caudillo rebelde Juan Isidro Jimenes logró una victoria decisiva en Moca contra 500 soldados gubernamentales al mando del general Ramón Cáceres, quien resultó herido, despejando el camino para avanzar sobre Santo Domingo.

The Tribune prints the closing part of a Santo Domingo dispatch

December 30, 190330 de diciembre de 1903

The Tribune prints the closing part of a Santo Domingo dispatch: the minister, who has been living in the suburbs, has been compelled to move the legation inside the city boundaries; President Morales returned here last night — he found it impossible to land on the northern part of the island, but bombarded Puerto Plata, and the damage done is not known; Morales is taking all possible steps to defend San Domingo; many arrests have been made; the South and East have joined in the Northern revolution; the situation is complicated and serious; the government troops are preparing to leave here and attack the rebels at San Cristobal, two hours from here; much excitement prevails at this place, and the arrival of another American warship, to reinforce the gunboat Newport, is anxiously desired. The Tribune also prints a Washington item of December 29 under the head 'Hurry Call for a Warship': with three revolutions raging on the island, the forces of Jiménez within four hours of San Domingo City, and excitement prevailing, Minister Powell thinks the situation in San Domingo demands the presence of an additional warship, and in a cable message dated yesterday appeals to the State Department for aid; in anticipation of the crisis the State Department had already taken steps to send another warship to San Domingo, and at its request Secretary Moody yesterday ordered Rear Admiral Lamberton, commanding the South Atlantic Squadron, now at Trinidad, to dispatch one of his vessels to San Domingo at full speed to assist the gunboat Newport.

The Journal prints a San Juan dispatch of December 29 and a San Domingo dispatch of Sunday

December 30, 190330 de diciembre de 1903

The Journal prints a San Juan dispatch of December 29 and a San Domingo dispatch of Sunday, December 27: the Cuban steamer which touched here, via Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, has ceased to call at San Juan, thus severing the line of communication of Gen. Woss y Gil for Santo Domingo on a French steamer. In the San Domingo dispatch the insurgents are attacking this city, which is being defended by the troops of the provisional government, who are now fighting in force; the Clyde Line steamer Cherokee arrived in port today; the government authorities demanded that that portion of the Cherokee's cargo destined for Azua de Compostela be discharged here — this demand was resisted by the steamship agents, who notified the government that the Cherokee would proceed to Azua de Compostela; in case of aggression on the part of the government authorities the steamship company will ask Minister Powell for protection; a commission from San Pedro de Macoris, which has declared in favor of the insurgents, arrived here today and is demanding the surrender of San Domingo to the insurgent forces.

El Diario publica un despacho de San Juan del 29 de diciembre y un despacho de Santo Domingo del domingo 27 de diciembre: el vapor cubano que hacía escala aquí, vía Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, ha dejado de hacer escala en San Juan, cortando así la línea de comunicación del general Woss y Gil, expresidente de Santo Domingo, con el teatro de la guerra en esa república; el partido de Gil anunció abiertamente que zarparán para Santo Domingo en enero para cooperar con el general Jiménez; la recolección de municiones de guerra continúa en Puerto Rico; el gobernador Hunt sigue negándose a reconocer el consulado de Morales aquí, que ha apelado al cónsul francés para impedir la salida del general Woss y Gil hacia Santo Domingo en un vapor francés. En el despacho de Santo Domingo, los insurgentes están atacando esta ciudad, que está siendo defendida por las tropas del gobierno provisional, que ahora combaten con fuerza; el vapor Cherokee, de la línea Clyde, llegó hoy al puerto; las autoridades del gobierno exigieron que la parte de la carga del Cherokee destinada a Azua de Compostela se descargara aquí: esta exigencia fue resistida por los agentes del vapor, que notificaron al gobierno que el Cherokee seguiría hacia Azua de Compostela; en caso de agresión por parte de las autoridades del gobierno, la compañía del vapor pedirá protección al ministro Powell; una comisión de San Pedro de Macorís, que se ha declarado a favor de los insurgentes, llegó hoy aquí y está exigiendo la rendición de Santo Domingo a las fuerzas insurgentes.

The Montgomery Advertiser

San Domingo, Sunday, Janl 3.—The gun boat Independence arrived here last

January 7, 19047 de enero de 1904

The Montgomery Advertiser reported that the Dominican gunboat Independencia captured two armed insurgent schooners and shelled suburban districts of the capital to dislodge revolutionary fighters. Government forces successfully retook Baní and San Cristóbal and repelled a major assault on Santo Domingo following an engagement at Haina. To isolate the insurrection, the government issued a decree closing six major coastal ports to foreign shipping, supported by naval patrols.

El Montgomery Advertiser informó que el cañonero dominicano Independencia capturó dos goletas insurgentes armadas y cañoneó distritos suburbanos de la capital para desalojar a los combatientes revolucionarios. Las fuerzas del gobierno recuperaron con éxito Baní y San Cristóbal y repelieron un asalto de envergadura contra Santo Domingo tras un combate en Haina. Para aislar la insurrección, el gobierno emitió un decreto clausurando seis puertos costeros principales al comercio marítimo exterior, apoyado por patrullajes navales.

The Indianapolis Journal

Is In the possession of Jimines, and that If the State

January 15, 190415 de enero de 1904

The Indianapolis Journal reported that the State Department withheld diplomatic recognition from Dominican factions despite claims by General Jimenes's followers that they controlled nearly the entire country outside the capital. United States officials observed that three rival governing authorities were contending for power simultaneously, leaving policy implementation to Minister Powell's judgment. To protect American interests, the Navy dispatched the cruiser Columbia from Guantanamo to reinforce the warships Detroit and Newport at Santo Domingo.

El Indianapolis Journal informó que el Departamento de Estado se abstuvo de conceder reconocimiento diplomático a las facciones dominicanas, a pesar de que los partidarios del general Jimenes afirmaban controlar prácticamente todo el país fuera de la capital. Funcionarios de los Estados Unidos observaron que tres autoridades de gobierno rivales se disputaban el poder de forma simultánea, dejando la ejecución de la política al criterio del ministro Powell. Para resguardar los intereses estadounidenses, la Armada envió el crucero Columbia desde Guantánamo para reforzar a los buques de guerra Detroit y Newport en Santo Domingo.

The Savannah Morning News

17. —The government troops, which disembarked recently at Sosua

January 18, 190418 de enero de 1904

The Examiner prints the end of an item: the Morales government

The Herald-Advance

The Herald-Advance prints the official confirmation of the Pajarito bombardment

February 26, 190426 de febrero de 1904

The Herald-Advance prints the official confirmation of the Pajarito bombardment: the item confirms the report of the bombardment of the insurgents by the war vessels at a position near the capital city, the landing of marines and bluejackets to punish the revolutionists and their subsequent re-embarkation. According to Capt. Miller's dispatch the affair occurred on a river twenty miles from San Domingo, presumably at Pajarito, the place mentioned in the press dispatches as the locality where the bombardment occurred. The insurgents, who were stationed on the mainland, fired on a United States merchant vessel, believed to be the New York, which was lying in the river; the shots fell so dangerously near the war vessels that the acts of the insurgents were construed by Capt. Miller as an attack on the vessels, and a battery fire was opened on the revolutionists. This was followed by the debarkation of 400 marines and bluejackets from the Columbia and Newark, who made a successful landing ashore; they dislodged the insurgents from their position and chased them into the country; after accomplishing this the marines and bluejackets took again to their boats and returned to their ships. One man, a bugler named Painter, was seriously wounded by the accidental explosion of his firing piece; there were no other casualties. Authoritatively it is stated that the question of the annexation of Santo Domingo to the United States never has received the slightest consideration on the part of the administration.

El Herald-Advance publica la confirmación oficial del bombardeo de Pajarito: la nota confirma el informe del bombardeo de los insurgentes por los buques de guerra en una posición cerca de la ciudad capital, el desembarco de infantes de marina y marineros para castigar a los revolucionarios y su subsiguiente reembarque. Según el despacho del capitán Miller, el hecho ocurrió en un río a veinte millas de Santo Domingo, presumiblemente en Pajarito, el lugar mencionado en los despachos de prensa como la localidad donde ocurrió el bombardeo. Los insurgentes, que estaban apostados en tierra firme, dispararon contra un buque mercante de los Estados Unidos, que se cree era el New York, que estaba en el río; los disparos cayeron tan peligrosamente cerca de los buques de guerra que los actos de los insurgentes fueron interpretados por el capitán Miller como un ataque a los buques, y se abrió fuego de batería contra los revolucionarios. A esto siguió el desembarco de 400 infantes de marina y marineros del Columbia y el Newark, que efectuaron un desembarco exitoso en la costa; desalojaron a los insurgentes de su posición y los persiguieron hacia el interior; tras lograr esto, los infantes de marina y los marineros volvieron a sus botes y regresaron a sus buques. Un hombre, un corneta llamado Painter, resultó seriamente herido por la explosión accidental de su pieza de fuego; no hubo otras bajas. Se ha afirmado autorizadamente que la cuestión de la anexión de Santo Domingo a los Estados Unidos nunca ha recibido la más mínima consideración por parte de la administración.

The Madison Daily Leader

The Leader prints a dispatch of February 27 from San Domingo

February 27, 190427 de febrero de 1904

The Leader prints a dispatch of February 27 from San Domingo: government forces attacked San Pedro de Macoris last Saturday; they went in small boats with the intention of landing, but were received with a fusillade from the insurgents which resulted in the killing or wounding of many. The gunboat Presidente then bombarded the town and inflicted much damage; the attack and the bombardment were renewed on Sunday; the rebels fired on the Presidente and damaged it about the water line, which obliged the gunboat to return to San Domingo.
