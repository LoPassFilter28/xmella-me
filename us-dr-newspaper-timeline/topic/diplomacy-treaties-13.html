The Birmingham Age-Herald

Reporting on diplomatic representations, The Birmingham Age-Herald states

September 19, 190319 de septiembre de 1903

Reporting on diplomatic representations, The Birmingham Age-Herald states that the United States government will not accept the neutralization of Dominican waters or opening of free ports, given that San Domingo is a neighboring state. Washington declares it will not allow European coaling stations, territorial cessions, or exclusive foreign naval access in Dominican waters in peace or war. Furthermore, no Dominican territory may be classed as neutral if it conflicts with the Clyde Line concession exempting its vessels from foreign port dues. The paper notes that the protest has excited considerable comment, highlighting Minister Powell's determination to see American rights respected by the government of Dominica.

Al informar sobre representaciones diplomáticas, The Birmingham Age-Herald señala que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos no aceptará la neutralización de las aguas dominicanas ni la apertura de puertos francos, dado que Santo Domingo es un estado vecino. Washington declara que no permitirá estaciones carboneras europeas, cesiones territoriales ni el acceso naval exclusivo de extranjeros en aguas dominicanas en tiempos de paz o de guerra. Asimismo, ningún territorio dominicano podrá considerarse neutral si entra en conflicto con la concesión de la línea Clyde, la cual exime a sus buques de derechos portuarios extranjeros. El periódico añade que la protesta ha despertado considerables comentarios, destacando la determinación del ministro Powell de lograr que los derechos estadounidenses sean respetados por el gobierno de Dominica.

Omaha Daily Bee

The Omaha Daily Bee reports that United States Minister Powell delivered

September 25, 190325 de septiembre de 1903

The Omaha Daily Bee reports that United States Minister Powell delivered a vigorous reply rejecting the Dominican government's bill to neutralize Dominican waters and create free ports, calling the foreign minister's response highly objectionable. Meanwhile, the Dominican foreign office informed Powell that it will pay the long-disputed claim of J. Sala & Co. within a few days under the American minister's adjustment proposal. Originating from approximately 1215,000 in loans and munitions supplied by Sala to late President Heureaux of the Dominican Republic, the claim passed to Sala's American widow and was awarded $523,000 with interest by an international tribunal. After Salvador declared the award void—a finding Secretary Hay refused to recognize—a compromise settlement was reached at 67H percent of the awarded amount.

El Omaha Daily Bee informa que el ministro de los Estados Unidos Powell presentó una enérgica respuesta rechazando el proyecto del gobierno dominicano para neutralizar las aguas dominicanas y crear puertos francos, calificando la respuesta del ministro de relaciones exteriores de sumamente objetable. Mientras tanto, la cancillería dominicana comunicó a Powell que liquidará la prolongada reclamación de J. Sala & Co. en pocos días conforme a la propuesta de ajuste del ministro estadounidense. Originada en aproximadamente 1215,000 en préstamos y pertrechos suministrados por Sala al difunto presidente Heureaux de la República Dominicana, la reclamación pasó a su viuda estadounidense y un tribunal internacional le concedió $523,000 con intereses. Luego de que Salvador anulara el laudo —medida que el secretario Hay rehusó reconocer—, se alcanzó un acuerdo transaccional al 67H por ciento de la suma concedida.

The Beatrice Daily Express

Insurgent forces under General Rodriguez captured Santiago

October 27, 190327 de octubre de 1903

Insurgent forces under General Rodriguez captured Santiago, The Beatrice Daily Express reports, after hours of severe combat with several men wounded, advancing a northern revolution triggered by customhouse frauds and the misconduct of the ministers of war and finance. The rebellion established a provisional government at Puerto Plata under General Morales, arresting supporters of President Gil, while residents of Monte Cristo, La Lignan, and Loca united to attack Santiago and cut telegraphic links to La Vega. Observers view President Gil's government as lost, and Vice President Deschamps has arrived by small sailboat from Monte Cristo after abandoning the administration.

Fuerzas insurgentes bajo el mando del general Rodríguez tomaron Santiago, informa The Beatrice Daily Express, tras horas de encarnizado combate con varios hombres heridos, consolidando una revolución norteña provocada por fraudes aduaneros y desmanes de los ministros de guerra y hacienda. La sublevación estableció un gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata presidido por el general Morales, arrestando a los partidarios del presidente Gil, mientras habitantes de Montecristi, La Lignan y Loca se unían para atacar Santiago y cortar el telégrafo hacia La Vega. Los observadores dan por perdido al gobierno del presidente Gil, y el vicepresidente Deschamps llegó en un pequeño velero desde Montecristi tras haber abandonado la administración.

The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution

October 28, 190328 de octubre de 1903

The Republic prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution and a Washington item of October 27 headed 'Explanation Due': the leaders of the revolution are stated not to be in need of supplies, and it is the general opinion that the government of Woss-y-Gil is lost. The Washington item says a full explanation of the stopping of the American mail steamer Cherokee at Porto Plata by a Dominican warship will to-morrow be demanded of the Dominican Republic by the State Department; an American cruiser or gunboat will meantime be ordered to Porto Plata and prevent further arbitrary interference with commerce, either by so-called blockades or otherwise. Up to the time a Republic dispatch stating the Cherokee had been turned back at Porto Plata was shown to a State Department official to-night the department had received no advices regarding this occurrence; the only advices received to-day came from Minister Powell at Santo Domingo City and stated that Porto Plata was in a state of revolution. From this it is inferred the Dominican government has instituted a blockade with the idea of cutting off the revolutionists' source of supplies. According to international law, however, a blockade, to be recognized by other powers, must be preceded by a formal notification of its institution. The tendency of the Central American and West Indian governments, particularly Haiti and Santo Domingo, has been so arbitrary in this regard that no blockade not preceded by formal notification long enough to exempt ships sailing before notice is given will be tolerated by the United States government.

El Republic publica las palabras finales de un despacho sobre la revolución y una nota de Washington del 27 de octubre titulada «Explanation Due»: se afirma que los líderes de la revolución no necesitan suministros, y es la opinión general que el gobierno de Woss-y-Gil está perdido. La nota de Washington dice que mañana se exigirá a la República Dominicana, por conducto del Departamento de Estado, una explicación completa de la detención del vapor correo estadounidense Cherokee en Porto Plata por un buque de guerra dominicano; entretanto, un crucero o cañonera estadounidense será enviado a Porto Plata para impedir nuevas interferencias arbitrarias con el comercio, ya sea mediante supuestos bloqueos o de otro modo. Hasta el momento en que esta noche se mostró a un funcionario del Departamento de Estado un despacho del Republic que afirmaba que el Cherokee había sido obligado a retroceder en Porto Plata, el departamento no había recibido informes sobre este suceso; los únicos informes recibidos hoy provinieron del ministro Powell en la ciudad de Santo Domingo y afirmaban que Porto Plata estaba en estado de revolución. De ello se infiere que el gobierno dominicano ha instituido un bloqueo con la idea de cortar la fuente de suministros de los revolucionarios. Según el derecho internacional, sin embargo, un bloqueo, para ser reconocido por otras potencias, debe ir precedido de una notificación formal de su institución. La tendencia de los gobiernos centroamericanos y de las Indias Occidentales, particularmente Haití y Santo Domingo, ha sido tan arbitraria a este respecto que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos no tolerará ningún bloqueo que no haya sido precedido por una notificación formal con antelación suficiente para eximir a los buques que zarparon antes de que se diera el aviso.

The Tribune prints the closing part of a New York item on the revolution:

November 1, 19031 de noviembre de 1903

The Tribune prints the closing part of a New York item on the revolution: Señor Galvan said 500,000 cartridges are held at the order of the government and will be shipped by special steamer, probably to reach San Domingo to-morrow; the government has now a supply on hand of 1,000,000 cartridges, but the additional supply is sent, Señor Galvan said, 'so they don't run short.' General Carlos Morales, who heads the movement, has been governor of Puerto Plata under the present government, having fought against President Vasquez to place General Woss y Gil in power; in fact, General Morales has rebelled against every government under which he has at various times served; he was formerly a priest, but renounced the church for politics, and it has long been his ambition to be president. General Juan Isidro Jiminez, ex-president of San Domingo, who left this city a short time ago and is now at Mayaguez, Porto Rico, is also anxious to get back to power; President Woss y Gil appointed him fiscal agent to the United States and Europe, with a salary of $12,000 a year, to keep him quiet, it is said; he resigned this office, however, that he might take part in the new revolutionary movement; and it is not considered likely that he will be selected as chief executive, even in the event of rebel success, which is regarded as improbable.
