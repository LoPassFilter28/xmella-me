Springfield Weekly Republican

The Republican comments on the report of Assistant Secretary of State

July 24, 191324 de julio de 1913

The Republican comments on the report of Assistant Secretary of State Osborne, who was weeks in Santo Domingo and who declares that the bones of Christopher Columbus, instead of resting at Seville in Spain, are in the cathedral of Santo Domingo, capital of the Dominican Republic: Mr. Osborne knows this because the Dominicans told him; what they told him about their finances, which he went to investigate, will appear later. In 1898, following the Spanish war, when the bodies of Columbus and his son, Diego, were supposed to have been returned from Havana to Seville, their original resting place, only the body of Diego was actually sent, that of Columbus being secretly retained by the Dominicans. On the bottom of the lead casket is the inscription, 'Christobal Colon, first admiral and discoverer of America.' At this rate the shade of the great mariner must be in an unhappy state, uncertain where he saw the light of day and where he was last buried. But Mr. Osborne's report has been received with doubt, very respectfully of course.

El Republican comenta el informe del subsecretario de Estado Osborne, que estuvo semanas en Santo Domingo y que declara que los huesos de Cristóbal Colón, en lugar de descansar en Sevilla, España, están en la catedral de Santo Domingo, capital de la República Dominicana: el Sr. Osborne lo sabe porque los dominicanos se lo dijeron; lo que le dijeron sobre sus finanzas, que fue a investigar, aparecerá más tarde. En 1898, tras la guerra hispana, cuando se suponía que los cuerpos de Colón y de su hijo, Diego, habían sido devueltos desde La Habana a Sevilla, su lugar de descanso original, en realidad solo se envió el cuerpo de Diego, pues el de Colón fue retenido secretamente por los dominicanos. En el fondo del féretro de plomo está la inscripción «Cristóbal Colón, primer almirante y descubridor de América». A este paso, el espíritu del gran navegante debe estar en un estado desdichado, incierto de dónde vio la luz del día y dónde fue enterrado por última vez. Pero el informe del Sr. Osborne ha sido recibido con duda, muy respetuosamente, por supuesto.

The Washington Herald

The Herald prints an item: an automobile containing eight members of the family of the Dominican minister

August 1, 19131 de agosto de 1913

The Herald prints an item: an automobile containing eight members of the family of the Dominican minister was stranded during the storm of Thursday at the summer camp and unofficial legation of the Dominican Republic at the crest of the Palisades of the Potomac. One of three tents which compose the camp of the Minister of the Dominican Republic and Mme. Francesco Peynado was blown down by the storm. When the rain and hail and wind first became unusual, members of Dr. Peynado's family became frightened; they entered the automobile and attempted to start for a neighboring farmhouse, but the rain had short-circuited the engine, and the automobile would not start. So bad was the storm that the passengers were afraid to alight from the car; they remained exposed during the storm and later went to a farmhouse. Reconstruction of the camp, which was damaged seriously, began yesterday. The camp of Dr. Peynado is the most palatial the District has contained; although immensely wealthy, Dr. Peynado expressed a preference for camp life during the summer. He had built a camp composed of three tents, and a staff of servants were taken with the family of the minister.

El Herald publica una nota: durante la tormenta del jueves, ocho miembros de la familia del ministro dominicano quedaron varados en un automóvil en el campamento de verano y legación no oficial de la República Dominicana en la cresta de las Palisades del Potomac. Una de las tres tiendas que componen el campamento del ministro de la República Dominicana y la Sra. Francesco Peynado fue derribada por la tormenta. Cuando la lluvia, el granizo y el viento se volvieron inusuales, los miembros de la familia del Dr. Peynado se asustaron; entraron en el automóvil e intentaron partir hacia una granja vecina, pero la lluvia había cortocircuitado el motor y el automóvil no arrancaba. Tan mala era la tormenta que los pasajeros temían bajar del automóvil; permanecieron expuestos durante la tormenta y más tarde fueron a una granja. La reconstrucción del campamento, que fue seriamente dañado, comenzó ayer. El campamento del Dr. Peynado es el más palaciego que ha contenido el Distrito; aunque inmensamente rico, el Dr. Peynado expresó su preferencia por la vida de campamento durante el verano. Había construido un campamento compuesto de tres tiendas, y una plantilla de sirvientes fue llevada con la familia del ministro.

The Fargo Forum and Daily Republican

The Forum prints a Washington dispatch of September 8: Puerto Plata

September 8, 19138 de septiembre de 1913

The Forum prints a Washington dispatch of September 8: Puerto Plata is being shelled by Dominican gunboats, with great danger to American lives and property, according to a report from J. M. Estevan, American vice consul. The gunboat Des Moines is on the way there. The Des Moines is at Guantanamo, coaling for the run to Puerto Plata, where it is due tomorrow. Puerto Plata has been in the hands of Dominican rebels ever since the latest revolution started with the announcement that the governor of that state had seceded from allegiance to the central government. The present danger to Americans and property comes from an attempt of the Dominican government to force the rebel stronghold to capitulate. Until the receipt of today's dispatches, the State Department officials had not thought the flare-up serious. It is supposed the leader of the uprising is Hector Velasquez, one time provisional president of the republic. The American interests in Puerto Plata are small. It is thought a number of Porto Ricans recently entered the business community there.
