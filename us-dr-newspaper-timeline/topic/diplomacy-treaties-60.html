Grant County Herald

The Herald prints a portion of Mr. Reader's statement of his dealings

April 22, 190522 de abril de 1905

The Herald prints a portion of Mr. Reader's statement of his dealings with Mr. Cromwell: Mr. Cromwell also promised that on his return with these papers properly executed he would take him to Washington and introduce him to Mr. Hay; he explained to him, especially with respect to the desire of the United States to procure the bay of Samana as a coaling station, what authorizations and instructions he should specially see that he got from the Dominican government so as to enable him, with full and complete credentials, to negotiate with the United States government for the sale of that bay to it. 'With these papers I left New York, accompanied by Mr. Perez, on December 21, 1904. These papers provided for Mrs. Reader to be the fiscal agent of the Dominican government in the United States, and authorized and empowered her to negotiate with the United States government for the sale of the bay of Samana and for all differences between the Dominican government and the United States and other foreign governments, with full power of attorney to represent the Dominican government.' It was arranged with Mr. Cromwell that as soon as these papers were signed by President Morales the signatures should be verified by the United States minister at Santo Domingo, and that, as Mrs. Reader would then be the authorized fiscal agent of the Dominican government in the United States, they should be cabled to her.

El Herald publica una porción de la declaración del Sr. Reader, acerca de sus tratos con el Sr. Cromwell: el Sr. Cromwell prometió también que a su regreso con estos papeles debidamente ejecutados lo llevaría a Washington y lo presentaría al Sr. Hay; le explicó, especialmente respecto al deseo de los Estados Unidos de procurar la bahía de Samaná como estación carbonera, qué autorizaciones e instrucciones debía procurar especialmente obtener del gobierno dominicano para habilitarlo, con credenciales plenas y completas, a negociar con el gobierno de los Estados Unidos la venta de esa bahía a este. «Con estos papeles salí de Nueva York, acompañado por el Sr. Pérez, el 21 de diciembre de 1904. Estos papeles proveían que la Sra. Reader fuera la agente fiscal del gobierno dominicano en los Estados Unidos, y la autorizaban y facultaban para negociar con el gobierno de los Estados Unidos la venta de la bahía de Samaná y todas las diferencias entre el gobierno dominicano y los Estados Unidos y otros gobiernos extranjeros, con poder notarial pleno para representar al gobierno dominicano». Se había arreglado con el Sr. Cromwell que tan pronto como estos papeles fueran firmados por el presidente Morales las firmas fueran verificadas por el ministro de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo, y que, como la Sra. Reader sería entonces la agente fiscal autorizada del gobierno dominicano en los Estados Unidos, se le cablegrafiaran a ella.

The Sun prints an item on the new Dominican minister's presentation to President Roosevelt: Señor Joubert made a brief speech when he handed to the President a letter from President Morales of Santo Domingo accrediting him to this country as a minister extraordinary and envoy plenipotentiary. In closing, Señor Joubert said he had been especially charged to convey the best wishes of the President of his country. President Roosevelt responded in the usual manner, thanking Señor Joubert as the representative of Santo Domingo for the action which had been taken and the earnest wishes expressed: 'I desire for my part to assure you of the great interest felt by this Government and people in the welfare of the Dominican Republic and to declare that it is the wish and intention of this Government to maintain firmly and cordially the bonds of friendship which now unite the two countries. El Sun publica una nota sobre la presentación del nuevo ministro dominicano al presidente Roosevelt: el Sr. Joubert hizo un breve discurso cuando entregó al Presidente una carta del presidente Morales de Santo Domingo que lo acreditaba ante este país como ministro extraordinario y enviado plenipotenciario. Dijo que al exaltar el rango de su establecimiento diplomático en este país su gobierno deseaba dar expresión a la intensa simpatía que lo atrae a la nación estadounidense, cuya amistad Santo Domingo desea encarecidamente cultivar. Evening Star

The Star prints a correspondent's letter from Santo Domingo

November 30, 190530 de noviembre de 1905

The Star prints a correspondent's letter from Santo Domingo, under the head 'Discourtesy to American Commanders': commanders of American warships stationed at Monte Cristi or visiting that port on police duty have repeatedly paid official visits of courtesy to the governor of the province, which have not been returned. A short time ago Admiral Bradford, commanding operations in these waters, when at Monte Cristi desired to pay an official visit to the governor; fearing to be treated with the same discourtesy as his captains, he requested the American consul there to notify the governor of his intention and to inquire if the visit would be returned, but the governor categorically refused to return the visit, so the admiral did not call on him. The governor of Puerto Plata and President Morales were immediately notified of the fact, and the former wired the governor of Monte Cristi requesting him to be diplomatic in his relations with the Americans and desired him to receive and return the visit of Admiral Bradford, but the governor of Monte Cristi simply ignored the request and continued to boldly exhibit his hostility to American officials. United States officials have received gross and repeated insults from the Monte Cristi authorities, which have so far been tolerated with stoicism. Anti-American sentiment in Santo Domingo has reached fever heat.

El Star publica una carta de un corresponsal desde Santo Domingo, bajo el título «Discourtesy to American Commanders»: los comandantes de los buques de guerra estadounidenses estacionados en Monte Cristi o que visitan ese puerto en servicio de policía han pagado repetidamente visitas oficiales de cortesía al gobernador de la provincia, que no han sido correspondidas. Hace poco, el almirante Bradford, que manda las operaciones en estas aguas, deseó, estando en Monte Cristi, pagar una visita oficial al gobernador; temiendo ser tratado con la misma descortesía que sus capitanes, pidió al cónsul estadounidense allí que notificara al gobernador su intención y preguntara si la visita sería correspondida, pero el gobernador se negó categóricamente a corresponderla, por lo que el almirante no lo visitó. El gobernador de Puerto Plata y el presidente Morales fueron informados de inmediato del hecho, y el primero telegrafió al gobernador de Monte Cristi pidiéndole que fuera diplomático en sus relaciones con los estadounidenses y deseando que recibiera y correspondiera la visita del almirante Bradford, pero el gobernador de Monte Cristi simplemente ignoró la petición y continuó exhibiendo audazmente su hostilidad hacia los funcionarios estadounidenses. Los funcionarios de Estados Unidos han recibido insultos groseros y repetidos de las autoridades de Monte Cristi, que hasta ahora han sido tolerados con estoicismo. El sentimiento antiestadounidense en Santo Domingo ha llegado a un punto febril.

Evening Star

The Star prints a further portion of the correspondent's letter

November 30, 190530 de noviembre de 1905

The Star prints a further portion of the correspondent's letter: most of the information sent from Santo Domingo to the United States, unless sent by competent American officials, is misleading in the extreme, and the very statistics furnished are far from being correct; the furnishers of information are either afraid to give correct data, are unable to obtain them, or are incompetent men and do not know their business. Under the head 'Morales' Power Melting Away', the letter says that further information just received tends to show that President Morales' power is melting like snow in the sun; he is now practically a broken reed leaning on Minister Dawson for support, and were it not for this American official not only would Morales be no longer president, but it is more than probable that he would be at this moment a dead man. His few supporters are daily abandoning him, and it is extremely likely that within a very short time he will need more than Minister Dawson's moral support; American military support will probably be needed soon. Ex-President Horacio Vasquez is practically the ruler of four-fifths of the Dominican republic.

El Star publica una segunda porción de la carta del corresponsal: la mayor parte de la información enviada desde Santo Domingo a Estados Unidos, a menos que la envíen funcionarios estadounidenses competentes, es engañosa en extremo, y las propias estadísticas suministradas distan mucho de ser correctas; los que suministran la información o temen dar datos correctos, o son incapaces de obtenerlos, o son hombres incompetentes que no conocen su oficio. Bajo el título «Morales' Power Melting Away», la carta dice que información adicional recién recibida tiende a mostrar que el poder del presidente Morales se está derritiendo como la nieve al sol; ahora es prácticamente una caña quebrada que se apoya en el ministro Dawson para sostenerse, y de no ser por este funcionario estadounidense, Morales no solo ya no sería presidente, sino que es más que probable que en este momento sería un hombre muerto. Sus pocos partidarios lo abandonan a diario, y es sumamente probable que dentro de muy poco tiempo necesite más que el apoyo moral del ministro Dawson; probablemente pronto se necesitará apoyo militar estadounidense. El expresidente Horacio Vásquez es prácticamente el gobernante de las cuatro quintas partes de la República Dominicana.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints a dispatch from Santo Domingo: Perico Lasala

December 27, 190527 de diciembre de 1905

The Call prints a dispatch from Santo Domingo: Perico Lasala has marched on La Vega with a force of about 100 men; General Deschamps, former vice president of Santo Domingo, will leave here shortly on board a sailing vessel to operate against Puerto Plata; it is rumored that President Morales is a prisoner in irons at the capital, San Domingo. Under the head 'America Holds Aloof', the paper prints a Washington dispatch of December 26: for the present there will not be any interference by the United States in the difficulty which has arisen in Santo Domingo; the trouble is regarded by the State Department officials as entirely an internal one, and so long as outside interests are not menaced this attitude of non-intervention will be maintained; if, however, conditions should change materially and lawless acts should be committed against Americans and American interests or involve the collection of the Dominican customs by this Government, or other violence occur which in the opinion of the officials here would make it proper for this Government to interfere, this step will be taken and measures will be adopted to quell the trouble.
