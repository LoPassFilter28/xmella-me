The Washington Herald

The Washington Herald prints a dispatch: James M. Sullivan

December 12, 191412 de diciembre de 1914

The Washington Herald prints a dispatch: James M. Sullivan, minister to Santo Domingo, welcomed the inquiry to be made into his official conduct in dispatches received by President Wilson and Secretary Bryan yesterday. Minister Sullivan declared that his only interest was to serve his government, but that such service had been made difficult by 'sinister influences' in the Dominican republic. In his dispatch to Secretary Bryan he asked when and where the investigation would take place, and urged that it be begun as soon as possible. Secretary Bryan said he had not yet decided anything connected with the investigation.

The Washington Herald publica un despacho: James M. Sullivan, ministro en Santo Domingo, dio la bienvenida a la investigación que se hará sobre su conducta oficial en despachos recibidos por el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan ayer. El ministro Sullivan declaró que su único interés era servir a su gobierno, pero que tal servicio se había hecho difícil por «influencias siniestras» en la república dominicana. En su despacho al secretario Bryan preguntó cuándo y dónde tendría lugar la investigación, e instó a que comenzara lo antes posible. El secretario Bryan dijo que aún no había decidido nada relacionado con la investigación.

The Washington Herald

The Washington Herald prints an item: President Wilson has ordered

December 13, 191413 de diciembre de 1914

The Washington Herald prints an item: President Wilson has ordered an investigation into charges that James M. Sullivan, of New York, now minister to the Dominican republic, had been guilty of misconduct in office, mainly through alleged use of influence to obtain contracts from the Dominican government for a relative and friends, and by having Dominican revenue collections transferred to a bank in Santo Domingo City, which New York financial interests, friendly to Mr. Sullivan, were said to control. In making known that he had ordered the investigation, President Wilson said that his action had been taken at the cabled request of Minister Sullivan.

The Washington Herald publica: El presidente Wilson ha ordenado una investigación sobre las acusaciones de que James M. Sullivan, de Nueva York, ahora ministro en la república dominicana, había sido culpable de mala conducta en el cargo, principalmente por el presunto uso de influencia para obtener contratos del gobierno dominicano para un pariente y amigos, y por haber hecho transferir las recaudaciones de ingresos dominicanos a un banco en la ciudad de Santo Domingo, que intereses financieros de Nueva York, amigos del Sr. Sullivan, se decía que controlaban. Al dar a conocer que había ordenado la investigación, el presidente Wilson dijo que su acción había sido tomada a petición cablegráfica del ministro Sullivan.

Evening Star

The Evening Star prints an item under the head 'MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO': J. D. Phelan

December 27, 191427 de diciembre de 1914

The Evening Star prints an item under the head 'MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO': J. D. Phelan, senator-elect from California, appointed several days ago to investigate charges against James M. Sullivan, American minister to the Dominican republic, announced yesterday he might visit the island in connection with his inquiry. The State Department, at Mr. Phelan's request, asked Mr. Sullivan whether he desired to appear in person or be represented by counsel at hearings which Mr. Phelan said he thought would start early in January. Persons familiar with affairs in the Dominican republic have been asked to be present at the hearing. Their appearance will be voluntary, as there is no authority to subpoena witnesses or to compel them to testify. Some of the hearings will be here and some in New York. In accordance with a suggestion of Secretary Bryan, in his letter authorizing Mr. Phelan to make the inquiry, the hearings will be public.

The Evening Star publica una nota bajo el título «MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO»: J. D. Phelan, senador electo de California, nombrado hace varios días para investigar acusaciones contra James M. Sullivan, ministro americano en la república dominicana, anunció ayer que podría visitar la isla en relación con su investigación. El Departamento de Estado, a petición del Sr. Phelan, preguntó al Sr. Sullivan si deseaba comparecer en persona o ser representado por un abogado en audiencias que el Sr. Phelan dijo que creía que comenzarían a principios de enero. Se ha pedido a personas familiarizadas con los asuntos de la república dominicana que estén presentes en la audiencia. Su comparecencia será voluntaria, pues no hay autoridad para citar testigos ni para obligarlos a declarar. Algunas de las audiencias serán aquí y algunas en Nueva York. De acuerdo con una sugerencia del secretario Bryan, en su carta que autorizaba al Sr. Phelan a hacer la investigación, las audiencias serán públicas.

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer prints an item: The continuation of the inquiry

January 15, 191515 de enero de 1915

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer prints an item: The continuation of the inquiry to determine the fitness of James M. Sullivan, American minister to the republic, heard Farnham, of the National City Bank, as its first witness today before Senator-elect Phelan, of California. The National City Bank, it has been testified, was represented in negotiating the loan by Santiago Michelena, a Santo Domingo banker whose institution was depository of the republic until they were transferred, at the instance of Sullivan, it is alleged, to the Banco Nacionale. Michelena was not in favor with Secretary of State Bryan, it was testified, because Mr. Bryan 'was obsessed with the idea that Michelena represented the money trust.' Farnham began his testimony by saying the National City Bank had no interest in Santo Domingo in any way other than lending $1,500,000 to the government two years ago. This loan, by arrangement with the United States State and War Departments, was being paid off at the rate of $80,000 a month out of the government customs receipts. Farnham denied that there were any plans contemplated by the bank to exploit the island in any way. It was Secretary of State Knox who approved the loan, the witness said. Knox cabled the bank proposal to the then American minister, who presented it to the Dominican government. A copy went to Michelena. The minister and Michelena reported that the government favored their proposition in preference to propositions of other bankers.
