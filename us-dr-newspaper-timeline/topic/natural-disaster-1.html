Anti-Slavery Bugle

General Quitman's speech, quoted by The Anti-Slavery Bugle

July 5, 18565 de julio de 1856

General Quitman's speech, quoted by The Anti-Slavery Bugle, surveys Nicaragua, Cuba, and San Domingo, in which he characterizes the Haitian government as a "strange and grotesque power" under whose rule a strategic Caribbean island is relapsing into barbarism while supported by European influences in attempting to exterminate the "small white Dominican Republic" that still holds part of the island. Observing that the rest of "insular America" is European or African, Quitman warns American statesmen that a "world-startling drama" is approaching in which the development, greatness, and safety of the United States are deeply concerned. In commentary following the excerpt, the Bugle ridicules the phrase "Insular America" as alien to the history and traditions of the American people, contrasting it with the "Continental America" that resisted George III.

A dispatch reports that a Danish envoy supported by a war vessel

April 7, 18597 de abril de 1859

A dispatch reports that a Danish envoy supported by a war vessel peremptorily demanded $150,000 from the Dominican government as indemnity for two Danish ships seized by President Baez during the recent revolution in Dominica. President Santana offered to submit the dispute to arbitration, but the Danish official refused and delivered a twenty-four-hour ultimatum backed by the threat of a naval blockade. The Dominicans resolved to resist and immediately began manning their coastal batteries against the single Danish warship in port. Un despacho informa que un enviado danés respaldado por un buque de guerra exigió perentoriamente al gobierno dominicano 150,000 dólares como indemnización por dos embarcaciones danesas incautadas por el presidente Báez durante la reciente revolución en Dominica. El presidente Santana propuso someter la disputa a un arbitraje, pero el funcionario danés lo rechazó y dio un ultimátum de veinticuatro horas bajo amenaza de bloqueo naval. Los dominicanos decidieron resistir y comenzaron de inmediato a artillar sus baterías costeras frente al único buque danés surto en el puerto. Una nota local añade que, antes del ultimátum danés, las autoridades navales francesas en puerto habían exigido concesiones sobre todas las minas de oro, plata y cobre de la República Dominicana.

Daily National Intelligencer

The Daily National Intelligencer describes the geography of the island of Haiti

April 3, 18613 de abril de 1861

The Daily National Intelligencer describes the geography of the island of Haiti, noting that the western third forms the Republic of Haiti with its capital at Port-au-Prince, while the more mountainous eastern division constitutes the Dominican Republic (also referred to as the "Republic of Dominica" and "San Domingo"), with San Domingo as its capital. Pointing out that the island is separated from Cuba by the twenty-five-mile Windward Passage and from Puerto Rico by the twenty-five-mile Mona Passage, the paper observes that any power holding all three islands would command the ocean gateways to the Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean Sea, and Central America. The editors explain that they detail this geography because intelligence from Havana states that Spain has seized the Dominican Republic and is about to re-annex it to the Spanish Crown, a report they consider not at all improbable.

El Daily National Intelligencer describe la geografía de la isla de Haití, señalando que el tercio occidental forma la República de Haití con su capital en Puerto Príncipe, mientras que la división oriental, mucho más montañosa, constituye la República Dominicana (también denominada «República de Dominica» y «Santo Domingo»), con Santo Domingo como capital. Al destacar que la isla está separada de Cuba por el Paso de los Vientos, de veinticinco millas, y de Puerto Rico por el Canal de la Mona, también de veinticinco millas, el periódico observa que cualquier potencia que posea las tres islas dominaría las vías marítimas de acceso al Golfo de México, el Mar Caribe y Centroamérica. Los editores explican que detallan esta situación geográfica porque informes procedentes de La Habana indican que España se ha apoderado de la República Dominicana y está a punto de reanexarla a la Corona española, noticia que consideran nada improbable.

Conflicting reports of Cabral's defeat and Báez's proclamation

December 24, 186724 de diciembre de 1867

Conflicting reports of Cabral's defeat and Báez's proclamation are printed in the Herald's St. Domingo telegrams of December 23–24. Later advices from Santiago de Cuba report that well-informed persons there doubt the news of the defeat of General Cabral's forces, as well as the announcement of a pronunciamiento in favor of ex-President Báez. From Santo Domingo City the paper also reports that Señor Pujol, still at Santiago, was expected there and is to offer the lease of Samana for ninety-nine years; that in the Legislative Chamber the ten-million-franc loan recently negotiated in Paris was approved; and that Consul Savage, on a telegram from the American consul at Santiago de Cuba, has telegraphed Secretary Seward advising him to defer further proceedings in relation to the St. Thomas purchase.

Informes contradictorios sobre la derrota de Cabral y la proclamación de Báez se publican en los telegramas del Herald sobre Santo Domingo, de fechas 23 y 24 de diciembre. Avisos posteriores de Santiago de Cuba reportan que personas bien informadas allí dudan de la noticia de la derrota de las fuerzas del general Cabral, así como del anuncio de un pronunciamiento en favor del expresidente Báez. An account reprinted in the Avis, beginning in the middle of the report

August 4, 18684 de agosto de 1868

The Evening Telegraph

The Evening Telegraph reprints a Havana despatch: all the Dominican leaders

November 26, 186926 de noviembre de 1869

The Evening Telegraph reprints a Havana despatch: all the Dominican leaders are anxious for annexation to the United States in consideration of a reasonable amount of money. Baez, Pimentel, and Cabral, however much they may disagree on other points, are united on this, and the only question is which shall get the money: Baez is the constitutional President of the republic, and it is stated that he is ready to open negotiations whenever the Congress of the United States shall make the necessary appropriations; his rivals, however, are equally well disposed, the partisans of Pimentel scattering among the people handbills favoring annexation with the United States, and Cabral hoping to be in power when the bargain is concluded, so that he may get the money. In Hayti, the despatch adds, the only opponents to selling out to the United States are parties under European influence, while Salnave and Saget, the rival Presidents, both are willing and anxious to dispose of their claims; the entire island, it is said, can be purchased for $2,000,000, where the window ends. The despatch is set against Spain's refusal to sell Cuba, which the paper says we really have a longing for, on any terms.

New York Journal and Advertiser

A dispatch from Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, dated August 10

August 11, 189911 de agosto de 1899

A dispatch from Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, dated August 10, in The New York Journal and Advertiser states that the full severity of the cyclone on the island cannot yet be determined because communication with the interior is cut off and even the railway from Puerto Plata to San Domingo City has been disabled. The report asserts that the northern part of the Dominican Republic was largely laid waste and predicts that accurate accounts, once received, will likely show heavy loss of life, ruined growing crops, and families left homeless and starving. With apparently diminishing violence, the storm passed from Cape Haytien toward eastern Cuba on a track pointing toward the southern coast of the United States.

El New York Journal and Advertiser publica un despacho desde Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, fechado el 10 de agosto, según el cual aún no es posible determinar la magnitud de los estragos del ciclón en la isla porque la comunicación con el interior está cortada e incluso el ferrocarril desde esa localidad hasta San Domingo City ha quedado inutilizado. El informe sostiene que la parte norte de la República Dominicana quedó en gran medida arrasada y prevé que, cuando lleguen reportes precisos, probablemente se comprobará la pérdida de muchas vidas, la destrucción de cultivos en pie y familias sin hogar y hambrientas. Con violencia aparentemente decreciente, la tormenta pasó desde Cape Haytien hacia el extremo oriental de Cuba en dirección a la costa sur de los Estados Unidos.

Tranquillity prevails throughout the Dominican Republic, a Santo Domingo dispatch

August 16, 189916 de agosto de 1899

Tranquillity prevails throughout the Dominican Republic, a Santo Domingo dispatch in the Omaha Daily Bee states, according to government officials. The assassins of President Heureaux, reported to number sixteen, remain at large, although steps have been taken to capture them. The dispatch adds that the recent storm inflicted severe damage in the districts around Samana and Puerto Plata, and that the U.S. cruiser New Orleans has departed Santo Domingo for Porto Rico.

Reina la tranquilidad en toda la República Dominicana, informa un despacho fechado en Santo Domingo en el Omaha Daily Bee, según funcionarios del gobierno. Los asesinos del presidente Heureaux, que según se dice integran una banda de dieciséis hombres, siguen prófugos, aunque se han adoptado medidas para capturarlos. El despacho añade que la tormenta causó graves daños en las zonas de Samaná y Puerto Plata, y que el crucero estadounidense New Orleans zarpó de Santo Domingo rumbo a Puerto Rico.
