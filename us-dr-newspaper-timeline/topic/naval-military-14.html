New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union

Doubtless take steps to protect their interests is they deem it

July 29, 191429 de julio de 1914

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrasted unrest in the Caribbean, noting that while Dominican disturbances were likely to prompt U.S. armed intervention, the country presented fewer military and diplomatic difficulties than neighboring Haiti. European nations maintained smaller financial interests in Santo Domingo, whereas Haitian instability threatened foreign defaults. The Wilson administration sought guarantees that Haitian customs revenues would be managed to satisfy foreign debts in a manner analogous to the Dominican receivership.

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrastó las revueltas en el Caribe, observando que, si bien era probable que las perturbaciones dominicanas provocaran una intervención militar estadounidense, dicho país planteaba menores dificultades bélicas y diplomáticas que el vecino Haití. Las naciones europeas conservaban intereses financieros más reducidos en Santo Domingo, al tiempo que la inestabilidad haitiana ponía en riesgo moratorias con el exterior. La administración Wilson demandaba garantías de que los ingresos de las aduanas de Haití fueran administrados para cancelar deudas foráneas de modo análogo a la receptoría dominicana.

Custer Weekly Chronicle

The Custer Weekly Chronicle prints an editorial: The tremendous operations of this administration

August 1, 19141 de agosto de 1914

The Custer Weekly Chronicle prints an editorial: The tremendous operations of this administration in the field of foreign affairs, such as the Mexican matter, the 125,000,000 Colombian surrender and the already discredited Nicaraguan agreement, have obscured for the time the mess into which we have slipped in the island republic. 'Pressed by European powers,' the dispatches say, 'the U. S. has about lost patience with Haiti and San Domingo.' The policy in Santo Domingo of course is 'watchful waiting.' Puerto Plata, one of the chief seaports, is held by the rebels and has been declared under blockade by the Dominican government. Secretary Bryan has recognized the blockade, pronounced the port closed to American vessels and warned commanders of U. S. warships to wait watchfully for the outcome. As a result, American trade has almost ceased and American property interests are in constant danger. Meanwhile, European nations are pursuing a vigorous policy. A short time ago a German merchant vessel steamed into the 'blockaded' port and landed her cargo under protection of the guns of a German cruiser. No one can charge that the Wilson administration is responsible for the revolution, but it is undeniable that some of the deplorable conditions now existing are due to the prostitution of the diplomatic service, under the present regime, to the basest uses of partisan politics.

The Custer Weekly Chronicle publica: Ha reconocido el bloqueo el secretario Bryan; declaró el puerto cerrado a los buques americanos y advirtió a los comandantes de los buques de guerra de los EE. UU. que esperaran vigilantes el resultado. Puerto Plata, uno de los principales puertos marítimos, está en manos de los rebeldes y ha sido declarado bajo bloqueo por el gobierno dominicano. Como resultado, el comercio americano ha cesado casi por completo y los intereses de propiedad americanos están en peligro constante. Mientras tanto, las naciones europeas siguen una política vigorosa. Hace poco, un buque mercante alemán entró al puerto «bloqueado» y desembarcó su carga bajo la protección de los cañones de un crucero alemán. Nadie puede acusar a la administración Wilson de ser responsable de la revolución, pero es innegable que algunas de las condiciones deplorables ahora existentes se deben a la prostitución del servicio diplomático, bajo el régimen actual, para los usos más bajos de la política partidista.

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: Former Governor Fort recounts

September 17, 191417 de septiembre de 1914

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: Former Governor Fort recounts the peace commission's work in Santo Domingo. Since going there less than two months ago, four distinct revolutions and a number of insurrections of lesser weight have been settled and the leaders brought together in peace and harmony. The first refusals of the government of President Jose Bordas to surrender and submit to a provisional government were met by further demands by former Governor Fort. Bordas, finally realizing that this country meant business, yielded to the demands of the United States after two weeks of parley. The most serious condition found by Governor Fort when he reached the republic was the siege of Puerto Plata, which was held by rebels. President Bordas personally headed the federal troops besieging the city, and government gunboats had effected a full blockade of the port. Negotiations for a discontinuance of hostilities were opened at once, and after two days proved successful. The leaders of the warring factions at Puerto Plata were then taken aboard the steamer Prairie, which cleared for Santo Domingo, where another rebel force under General Vidal was besieging the city. Upon the demand of Mr. Fort hostilities were closed in order that the peace negotiations might be carried on. These were entered into also by President Bordas, who had gone to Santo Domingo in his private yacht. The election will be held under the observation of the United States, with slight modifications over the old Dominican electoral law, he said.

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser publica: Cuatro revoluciones distintas se han resuelto desde que el ex gobernador Fort fue a Santo Domingo hace menos de dos meses, junto con un número de insurrecciones de menor peso, y los líderes se han reunido en paz y armonía. Las primeras negativas del gobierno del presidente José Bordas a rendirse y someterse a un gobierno provisional fueron respondidas con nuevas exigencias del ex gobernador Fort. Bordas, al darse cuenta finalmente de que este país hablaba en serio, cedió a las exigencias de los Estados Unidos tras dos semanas de negociaciones. La condición más grave que encontró el gobernador Fort al llegar a la república fue el sitio de Puerto Plata, que estaba en poder de rebeldes.
