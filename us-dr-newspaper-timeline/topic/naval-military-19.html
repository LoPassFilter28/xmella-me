The Maryville Times

The Maryville Times prints a survey of the West Indies in wartime

May 14, 189814 de mayo de 1898

The Maryville Times prints a survey of the West Indies in wartime: the island's two republics, it says, are countries in the West Indies whose form of government is like the United States' own, except, of course, the republic of Cuba. Toussaint L'Ouverture's successful struggle against France and his capture by treachery, followed by the complete expulsion of the French, is one of liberty's favorite tales; the Spanish invaded San Domingo in 1861, and their defeat left the islanders free. The Haytians have experimented with kings and discarded them; the treasure of one of these despots, Christophe, remains buried under the rank vegetation, for which Hayti is unsurpassed, among the ruins of a stronghold he built; the story is that the architect, a European, and all of the workmen were thrown from the parapets, so that the secrets of the citadel they have just completed should never be divulged. There are quite a million people on the island, but they dally on the way to civilization; there is no army, or fortification worthy the name, and Hayti's only use to the United States would be in lending it a harbor: there are no harbors on the north coast, but Port au Prince, in the republic of Hayti, is a well-protected port lying behind the island of Gonave; in the Dominican Republic there are the harbors of Santo Domingo and Puerto Plata, which would make convenient bases for operations in the Caribbean. The Mona Passage, only 60 miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico.

El Maryville Times publica un estudio de las Indias Occidentales en tiempo de guerra: las dos repúblicas de la isla, dice, son países de las Indias Occidentales cuya forma de gobierno es como la de los propios Estados Unidos, excepto, por supuesto, la república de Cuba. La exitosa lucha de Toussaint L'Ouverture contra Francia y su captura por traición, seguida de la completa expulsión de los franceses, es uno de los relatos favoritos de la libertad; los españoles invadieron San Domingo en 1861, y su derrota dejó libres a los isleños. Los haitianos han experimentado con reyes y los han desechado; el tesoro de uno de esos déspotas, Christophe, permanece enterrado bajo la espesa vegetación, por la que Haití no tiene igual, entre las ruinas de una fortaleza que construyó; el relato dice que el arquitecto, un europeo, y todos los obreros fueron arrojados desde las murallas, para que los secretos de la ciudadela que acababan de completar no fueran divulgados jamás. Hay en la isla cerca de un millón de personas, pero se demoran en el camino de la civilización; no hay ejército, ni fortificación digna de ese nombre, y el único uso de Haití para los Estados Unidos sería prestarles un puerto: no hay puertos en la costa norte, pero Puerto Príncipe, en la república de Haití, es un puerto bien protegido situado detrás de la isla de Gonave; en la República Dominicana están los puertos de Santo Domingo y Puerto Plata, que harían bases convenientes para las operaciones en el Caribe. El paso de la Mona, de solo 60 millas de ancho, separa a Haití de la hermosa isla de Puerto Rico.

The Saint Paul Globe

Under the head 'Pretty Naval Problem,' the Globe sets out the positions of the fleets

May 15, 189815 de mayo de 1898

Under the head 'Pretty Naval Problem,' the Globe sets out the positions of the fleets: Admiral Sampson, with that portion of his fleet which took part in the bombardment of San Juan, is at Puerto Plata, the principal seaport of the Dominican Republic, situated on the north coast of the island; the auxiliary cruiser Yale sailed this morning from St. Thomas, going in a westerly direction, and the Montgomery, according to the report, is still at that place; the Harvard is at St. Pierre for repairs and will not be available for some days; the flying squadron under Schley, and the Oregon, with the Marietta and the Nictheroy as consorts, do not at present seem to be active factors in the problem. That the object of the Spanish commander is to reach the coast of Cuba, and perhaps Cienfuegos, is indicated by the latest reports of his movements, the Globe judges; if the Spanish fleet can be bottled up in a Cuban port, Sampson will be capable of taking care of it without other aid.

Bajo el título «Pretty Naval Problem», el Globe expone las posiciones de las flotas: el almirante Sampson, con la parte de su flota que tomó parte en el bombardeo de San Juan, está en Puerto Plata, el principal puerto marítimo de la República Dominicana, situado en la costa norte de la isla; el crucero auxiliar Yale zarpó esta mañana de Santo Tomás, en dirección oeste, y el Montgomery, según el informe, permanece aún en ese lugar; el Harvard está en St. Pierre en reparaciones y no estará disponible por algunos días; la escuadra volante al mando de Schley, y el Oregon, con el Marietta y el Nictheroy como consortes, no parecen por el momento factores activos en el problema. Que el objeto del comandante español es llegar a la costa de Cuba, y quizá a Cienfuegos, lo indican los últimos informes de sus movimientos, juzga el Globe; si la flota española puede ser embotellada en un puerto cubano, Sampson será capaz de encargarse de ella sin otra ayuda.

New-York Tribune

Among the Tribune's war dispatches is one from Puerto Plata

May 16, 189816 de mayo de 1898

Among the Tribune's war dispatches is one from Puerto Plata, in the Dominican Republic, dated May 15: all of the wounded of the United States fleet are doing well; there has been no firing by the American warships since the bombardment of the forts at San Juan de Porto Rico; the cable connecting San Juan de Porto Rico with the outside world has been cut at St. Louis; and the Spanish torpedo-gunboat Terror is being docked at Fort de France, Martinique. The same columns carry a Curacao dispatch of May 15 reporting that the Spanish cruisers Maria Teresa, Vizcaya, Almirante Oquendo and Cristobal Colon and the torpedo-boat destroyers Pluton and Terror, which arrived off that harbor the morning before, are still there, and noting the conflict over whether the destroyer with Admiral Cervera's squadron should read Furor instead of Terror.

Entre los despachos de guerra del Tribune hay uno de Puerto Plata, República Dominicana, fechado el 15 de mayo: todos los heridos de la flota de los Estados Unidos se encuentran bien; no ha habido fuego de los buques de guerra americanos desde el bombardeo de los fuertes de San Juan de Puerto Rico; el cable que conecta a San Juan de Puerto Rico con el mundo exterior ha sido cortado en St. Louis; y la cañonera torpedera española Terror está siendo reparada en dique en Fort de France, Martinica. Las mismas columnas traen un despacho de Curazao del 15 de mayo que informa que los cruceros españoles María Teresa, Vizcaya, Almirante Oquendo y Cristóbal Colón y los destructores torpederos Plutón y Terror, que llegaron frente a ese puerto la mañana anterior, permanecen allí, y anota el conflicto sobre si el destructor que acompaña a la escuadra del almirante Cervera debe leerse Furor en lugar de Terror.

The Indianapolis Journal

A Haitian deputy, who professes to be well informed on all

May 16, 189816 de mayo de 1898

The Indianapolis Journal reported from Haiti regarding proposed constitutional changes concerning foreign property rights. Haitian Foreign Minister Brutus St. Victor denied claims that the government intended to repeal Article 6 of the constitution, which prohibited alien ownership of real estate, stating the public was not prepared for such reform. St. Victor also declined to disclose specific government plans to address the rising premium on paper currency.

The Indianapolis Journal informó desde Haití sobre las propuestas de reforma constitucional relativas a los derechos de propiedad de los extranjeros. El ministro de Asuntos Exteriores haitiano, Brutus St. Victor, desmintió las versiones de que el gobierno pretendiera derogar el artículo 6 de la constitución, que prohibía a los extranjeros la propiedad de bienes inmuebles, afirmando que la opinión pública no estaba preparada para tal cambio. St. Victor declinó asimismo revelar planes gubernamentales específicos para hacer frente al creciente sobreprecio del papel moneda.

The Record-Union

The Record-Union reports the Haytien refusal to sell the gunboat

May 16, 189816 de mayo de 1898

The Record-Union reports the Haytien refusal to sell the gunboat Crete A. Pierrot: the report has been circulated that the United States negotiated with the Haytien Government for the purchase of the gunboat Crete A. Pierrot, one of the finest vessels in Hayti's navy, and Admiral Killock, the highest naval officer of Hayti, told the correspondent of the Associated Press that it was true and that the Haytien Government had refused the offer, for the express reason that it could not spare the vessel; the craft has an imposing ram and her armament is excellent, her guns being of the latest type with an entire complement of rapid-fire ordnance, and her officers would like to serve under the American flag, her second officer being an American who was in the Federal navy during the civil war in the United States. The same columns carry the dispatch from Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic, May 15: all the wounded of the United States fleet are doing well, and there has been no firing by the American warships since the bombardment of the fortifications at San Juan de Porto Rico.

El Record-Union informa de la negativa haitiana a vender el cañonero Crete A. Pierrot: ha circulado el reporte de que los Estados Unidos negociaron con el gobierno haitiano la compra del cañonero Crete A. Pierrot, uno de los mejores buques de la marina de Haití, y el almirante Killock, el oficial naval de mayor rango de Haití, dijo al corresponsal de la Associated Press que era cierto y que el gobierno haitiano había rechazado la oferta, por la expresa razón de que no podía prescindir del buque; la nave tiene un imponente espolón y su armamento es excelente, siendo sus cañones del tipo más moderno con un complemento entero de artillería de tiro rápido, y a sus oficiales les gustaría servir bajo la bandera americana, siendo su segundo oficial un americano que estuvo en la marina federal durante la guerra civil en los Estados Unidos. Las mismas columnas traen el despacho de Puerto Plata, República Dominicana, del 15 de mayo: todos los heridos de la flota de los Estados Unidos se encuentran bien, y no ha habido fuego de los buques de guerra americanos desde el bombardeo de las fortificaciones de San Juan de Puerto Rico.

The Jersey City News

The Jersey City News prints the closing passage of the Associated Press dispatch

June 2, 18982 de junio de 1898

The Jersey City News prints the closing passage of the Associated Press dispatch on the steamer Fanita: she was to touch at Monte Cristi, Dominican Republic, or at the keys just outside, to fill up the empty portion of her hold, and she has on board Chris Roberts, of Great Inagua, the most famous pilot of the West Indies. The captain of the Fanita, when that vessel was here, said the portion of her cargo which was not landed consisted of coal, but there is good reason to suppose that it is something very different from coal; and proof that the Fanita is under serious suspicion was afforded this morning by the excited conduct of the Spanish consul here, who denounces the taking on board the steamer of Spanish-speaking roustabouts and the sudden sailing of the vessel without the permission of the Dominican consul, classes this as a great outrage and an act of piracy, and has entered a formal protest with the Haytien authorities and telegraphed to his diplomatic chief at Santo Domingo this morning about the Fanita. There are large numbers of Cubans in Santo Domingo who eagerly desire to return to Cuba and take part in the war, and the correspondent here of the Associated Press is informed that the Spaniards of this place believe that it may be part of the mission of the Fanita to afford these Cubans transportation, at the same time furnishing them with arms and ammunition.
