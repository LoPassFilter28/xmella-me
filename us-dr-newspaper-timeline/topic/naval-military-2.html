January 6, 18566 de enero de 1856

From Dominica the papers announce that Soulouque, so recently at the head of a formidable force against the Dominicans, has returned without firing a shot at his adversaries. It says a courier from a French man-of-war was dispatched to the Emperor with sealed dispatches, on perusal of which he immediately gave orders to turn right about and march back again, and that it is rumored and generally believed that France will not permit the Haitians to invade or molest the Dominicans.

Desde Dominica los periódicos anuncian que Soulouque, que recientemente estaba al frente de una fuerza formidable contra los dominicanos, ha regresado sin disparar un tiro contra sus adversarios. Dice que un correo de un buque de guerra francés fue enviado al emperador con despachos sellados, tras leer los cuales ordenó de inmediato dar media vuelta y marchar de regreso, y que se rumora y se cree en general que Francia no permitirá que los haitianos invadan o molesten a los dominicanos.

Canton Weekly Register

Reprinting an item from the Chicago Press, the Canton Weekly Register explains

January 31, 185631 de enero de 1856

Reprinting an item from the Chicago Press, the Canton Weekly Register explains that the eastern portion of the island, called "St. Domingo or Dominica," was settled by Spaniards, whereas the people of Haiti descend from slaves held by the French on the western coast. Although the Dominicans are described as nearly all of mixed blood, including "Gen. Santander," the paper states that they call themselves Spaniards, cling tenaciously to their separate independence, and rely on steep mountain barriers to defend their population of about 175,000 against a Haitian population more than double that size. The Register also cites advices brought to the Philadelphia Exchange by Captain Burnaby of the schooner Ellen, which left Port-au-Prince on January 1, confirming the complete defeat of Emperor Faustin's 30,000-man march against the eastern side of the island, with Haitian losses of 200 to 300 soldiers, many officers, munitions, provisions, and the Emperor's military chest.

Al reproducir un artículo del Chicago Press, el Canton Weekly Register explica que la porción oriental de la isla, llamada «Santo Domingo o Dominica», fue colonizada originalmente por españoles, mientras que los habitantes de Haití descienden de los esclavos mantenidos por los franceses en la costa occidental. Aunque se describe a los dominicanos como casi todos de sangre mestiza, incluido el «general Santander», el periódico señala que se llaman a sí mismos españoles, se aferran con tenacidad a su independencia separada y cuentan con escarpadas barreras montañosas para defender a su población de unos 175,000 habitantes frente a una población haitiana que supera el doble de esa cifra. El Register cita además informes llevados al Philadelphia Exchange por el capitán Burnaby, de la goleta Ellen, que zarpó de Puerto Príncipe el 1 de enero, los cuales confirman la completa derrota de la marcha de 30,000 hombres del emperador Faustin contra la parte oriental de la isla, con bajas haitianas de entre 200 y 300 soldados, numerosos oficiales, municiones, provisiones y la caja militar del emperador.

The New York Herald

The New York Herald reports news brought by the schooner Mary Jane

November 11, 185711 de noviembre de 1857

The New York Herald reports news brought by the schooner Mary Jane from Puerto Plata, reprinting items from the Turks Islands Gazette of October 7, regarding hostilities in San Domingo. A translated September 17 bulletin from the official paper at Santo Domingo announces that the English schooner Merillen arrived from Samaná with dispatches from a government general reporting that his forces attacked and routed the Cibao garrison at Savannah la Mar, inflicting heavy losses in killed and wounded while suffering only one soldier wounded and a slight shoulder contusion to the general himself. The official bulletin hails this victory as proof that Providence protects the loyal armies from Mojarra to this latest engagement and adds that the northern flotilla opened fire on the 11th against a group of men assembled near Matanzas. The Turks Islands Gazette remarks that although civil war is raging in the Dominican Republic, it has not yet been able to ascertain the aims of the contending parties.

Al reproducir notas de la Turks Islands Gazette del 7 de octubre, el New York Herald informa sobre las noticias llevadas desde Puerto Plata por la goleta Mary Jane acerca de las hostilidades en Santo Domingo. April 20, 186120 de abril de 1861

Continuing its report on the Spanish takeover, a correspondent signed "E. D." in the New-York Dispatch states that Spain had been secretly landing men and supplies for three weeks while the entire population was disarmed before anyone suspected the cause. In the towns of Neyba, Azua, and San Cristobal, single officers collected all weapons under the pretext of replacing them with a better class, and popular patriotic leaders were lured inside the walls of Santo Domingo and detained as prisoners. At six in the morning on the 18th, the President, the three members of his cabinet, and a half-dozen high officers who had managed the affair raised the Spanish flag over the forts and proclaimed Isabella II Queen of the Dominican territory. The correspondent reports that the people received the notification in silent consternation and that scarcely a single Dominican outside the paid officials responded to the Spaniards' shout of "long live the Queen," despite double pay and lavish promises distributed overnight to the garrison and police, adding that desperate resistance is expected in the interior even though Dominicans say Spain will send 20,000 soldiers to crush the republic.

La nota se publica con un extracto del Tidende de Santo Tomás que anuncia la llegada del vapor de guerra De Soto con el secretario de Estado a bordo.

The New York Herald

Conflicting reports of Cabral's defeat and Báez's proclamation

December 24, 186724 de diciembre de 1867

Conflicting reports of Cabral's defeat and Báez's proclamation are printed in the Herald's St. Domingo telegrams of December 23–24. A press telegram from Santo Domingo of December 20 announces that Cabral's government has been defeated, owing to the defective organization of his troops; that a pronunciamiento of the people against him has been issued and Báez proclaimed President; and that a commission is going to Washington to complete arrangements for the transfer of the bay of Samana to the United States. Later advices from Santiago de Cuba report that well-informed persons there doubt the news of the defeat of General Cabral's forces, as well as the announcement of a pronunciamiento in favor of ex-President Báez. From Santo Domingo City the paper also reports that Señor Pujol, still at Santiago, was expected there and is to offer the lease of Samana for ninety-nine years; that in the Legislative Chamber the ten-million-franc loan recently negotiated in Paris was approved; and that Consul Savage, on a telegram from the American consul at Santiago de Cuba, has telegraphed Secretary Seward advising him to defer further proceedings in relation to the St. Thomas purchase.

Informes contradictorios sobre la derrota de Cabral y la proclamación de Báez se publican en los telegramas del Herald sobre Santo Domingo, de fechas 23 y 24 de diciembre. Un telegrama de prensa de Santo Domingo, del 20 de diciembre, anuncia que el gobierno de Cabral ha sido derrotado, a causa de la defectuosa organización de sus tropas; que se ha emitido un pronunciamiento del pueblo en su contra y Báez ha sido proclamado presidente; y que una comisión va a Washington a ultimar los arreglos para la transferencia de la bahía de Samaná a los Estados Unidos. Avisos posteriores de Santiago de Cuba reportan que personas bien informadas allí dudan de la noticia de la derrota de las fuerzas del general Cabral, así como del anuncio de un pronunciamiento en favor del expresidente Báez. Desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo el periódico informa además que el señor Pujol, todavía en Santiago, era esperado allí y va a ofrecer el arrendamiento de Samaná por noventa y nueve años; que en la Cámara Legislativa fue aprobado el empréstito de diez millones de francos negociado recientemente en París; y que el cónsul Savage, a raíz de un telegrama del cónsul americano en Santiago de Cuba, ha telegrafiado al secretario Seward aconsejándole diferir las gestiones relativas a la compra de Santo Tomás.

The Gazette reports that the steamer Montezuma has arrived at Santiago

January 8, 18688 de enero de 1868

The Gazette reports that the steamer Montezuma has arrived at Santiago from Puerto Plata, St. Domingo, with news that the Dominican Government had fled to Turks Island, that Báez had been proclaimed President, and that the capital had been blockaded by his brother.
