March 24, 186824 de marzo de 1868

The Ottawa Free Trader

The Free Trader recounts the history of the guano claims at Alto Velo.

May 9, 18689 de mayo de 1868

The Free Trader recounts the history of the guano claims at Alto Velo. Americans who had been taking guano from the island were ejected by the Dominican government, which asserted its jurisdiction, and the parties then sought a contract with that government for permission to ship guano and claimed damages. Secretary of State Jeremiah S. Black refused to notice the claim for damages until the United States agent at St. Domingo should have ascertained and reported the grounds upon which the Dominican government had asserted its jurisdiction; before the answer was received, Black had ceased to be Secretary of State and had been succeeded by Mr. Seward, and the rebellion and the war, various revolutions in the Dominican Republic, and war between that government and Hayti postponed any consideration of the claims. When the question was raised again, the department answered that two parties of Americans had claimed the island as discoverers, that St. Domingo had asserted a title and ejected the claimants, and that it declined to give any encouragement to Americans to resort there for guano without first obtaining the consent of St. Domingo.

El Free Trader relata la historia de las reclamaciones por el guano de Alto Velo. Los estadounidenses que extraían guano de la isla fueron expulsados por el gobierno dominicano, que afirmó su jurisdicción, y los interesados buscaron entonces un contrato con ese gobierno para obtener permiso de embarcar guano, reclamando además daños y perjuicios. El secretario de Estado Jeremiah S. Black se negó a atender la reclamación de daños hasta que el agente de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo averiguara e informara sobre los fundamentos en que el gobierno dominicano había basado su jurisdicción; antes de que llegara la respuesta, Black había cesado como secretario de Estado y había sido sucedido por el señor Seward, y la rebelión y la guerra, varias revoluciones en la República Dominicana y la guerra entre ese gobierno y Haití pospusieron toda consideración de las reclamaciones. Cuando la cuestión se planteó de nuevo, el departamento respondió que dos grupos de estadounidenses habían reclamado la isla como descubridores, que Santo Domingo había afirmado un título y expulsado a los reclamantes, y que se negaba a dar estímulo alguno a los estadounidenses para acudir allí por guano sin obtener primero el consentimiento de Santo Domingo.

Springfield Weekly Republican

An editorial in the Republican reviews the Alto Velo guano claim

May 9, 18689 de mayo de 1868

An editorial in the Republican reviews the Alto Velo guano claim: parties from Baltimore say they discovered guano upon the island of Alto Velo, lying about twenty miles south of the southernmost promontory of the island of San Domingo or Hayti, claimed it as not at the time the property or under the sovereignty of any government, and began to open and remove the valuable deposits, until in October an armed vessel of the Dominican Republic removed them. Their attorney, Judge Jeremiah S. Black, has ever since been urging their claims at Washington, either to obtain compensation or to induce the government to take forcible possession of the island, in defiance of the weak and distracted republic which assumes sovereignty over it. The Republican attributes Black's break with President Johnson — his withdrawal from the President's defense in the impeachment trial, and his present war on Mr. Seward — to the refusal of both to interfere in his behalf in this claim; it notes that the Court of Claims has likewise decided that the case is without any merit, and warns that four of the impeachment managers have committed themselves to a recommendation of what it calls this preposterous claim.

Un editorial en el Republican examina la reclamación del guano de Alto Velo: interesados de Baltimore dicen que descubrieron guano en la isla de Alto Velo, situada a unas veinte millas al sur del promontorio más meridional de la isla de Santo Domingo o Haití, la reclamaron como si en aquel momento no fuera propiedad ni estuviera bajo la soberanía de gobierno alguno, y comenzaron a abrir y extraer los valiosos depósitos, hasta que en octubre un buque armado de la República Dominicana los retiró. Su abogado, el juez Jeremiah S. Black, ha urgido desde entonces las reclamaciones en Washington, ya sea para obtener una indemnización o para inducir al gobierno a tomar posesión forzosa de la isla, en desafío de la débil y atribulada república que asume la soberanía sobre ella. El Republican atribuye la ruptura de Black con el presidente Johnson — su retiro de la defensa del presidente en el juicio político y su guerra actual contra el señor Seward — a la negativa de ambos a intervenir en su favor en esta reclamación; observa que la Corte de Reclamaciones ha decidido igualmente que el caso carece por completo de mérito, y advierte que cuatro de los fiscales del juicio político se han comprometido a recomendar lo que califica de reclamación disparatada.

The Herald's Havana advices of November 30 report that all was quiet

December 1, 18681 de diciembre de 1868

The Herald's Havana advices of November 30 report that all was quiet on the Haytian frontier. Drawn from government sources, the advices add that the rebel General Ogando was reported severely wounded in a recent skirmish; that Colonel Hevia, another rebel, was a prisoner and had been sentenced to be shot at Azua; that the government troops continued to pursue with great activity the dispersed bands of the Ogando faction and had taken many rebel prisoners; that the elections were progressing quietly throughout the country; that Santa Anna intended to proceed from Puerto Plata to the capital; that Colonel Fabens and Messrs. The New York Herald

A further Herald account, in a passage that opens in mid-discussion of the Cabinet

March 31, 186931 de marzo de 1869

A further Herald account, in a passage that opens in mid-discussion of the Cabinet, relates that it is known to everybody in St. Domingo that Minister Fabens was instructed to obtain the co-operation of the United States in making Samana a great commercial centre, and the Dominican Cabinet seems perfectly satisfied with his course. He had written for certain official statistics to lay before the American people, but not receiving them in season, he came on in person and found them just ready to be forwarded to him. The papers thus prepared are in singular contradiction, the account says, to the estimate of the public debt made by Mr. Angenard: he estimated it at from $12,000,000 to $16,000,000, while it is in fact less than $1,000,000, and even if the French loan is accepted it will still be under that figure, and the republic has ten times that value in the public domain and gold fields, which are not burdened as yet by a single dollar of debt. There is not probably a single state in all America so free from debt and so rich in the means of securing her creditors as the Dominican Republic. Her whole revenue is some hundreds of thousands of dollars per annum, of which $400,000 is derived from her custom house receipts; these would inure to the United States Treasury in the event of annexation, but the Dominicans would have the desired equivalent in peace, protection, the development of her means and a vast enhancement of general prosperity.

Un relato adicional del Herald, en un pasaje que se abre en mitad de la discusión sobre el gabinete, cuenta que es conocido por todos en Santo Domingo que el ministro Fabens recibió instrucciones de obtener la cooperación de los Estados Unidos para hacer de Samaná un gran centro comercial, y que el gabinete dominicano parece perfectamente satisfecho con su actuación. Había pedido ciertas estadísticas oficiales para presentarlas ante el pueblo estadounidense, pero al no recibirlas a tiempo, vino en persona y las encontró ya listas para serle enviadas. Los documentos así preparados están en singular contradicción, dice el relato, con la estimación de la deuda pública hecha por el señor Angenard: él la estimó entre $12.000.000 y $16.000.000, mientras que en realidad es de menos de $1.000.000, y aun si se acepta el empréstito francés seguirá por debajo de esa cifra, y la república posee diez veces ese valor en el dominio público y los campos de oro, que aún no están gravados por un solo dólar de deuda. Probablemente no hay un solo estado en toda América tan libre de deudas y tan rico en medios de asegurar a sus acreedores como la República Dominicana. Sus rentas totales ascienden a algunos cientos de miles de dólares al año, de los cuales $400.000 proceden de los ingresos de sus aduanas; estas pasarían al Tesoro de los Estados Unidos en caso de anexión, pero los dominicanos tendrían el equivalente deseado en paz, protección, el desarrollo de sus recursos y un vasto aumento de la prosperidad general.

The Evening Telegraph

The Telegraph recounts how the steamship Tybee came to be searched

June 8, 18698 de junio de 1869

The Telegraph recounts how the steamship Tybee came to be searched at New York. The West Indies line was advertised by the firm of Spofford, Tileston & Co., No. 29 Broadway; the pioneer vessel was to be the first-class steamship Tybee, which the advertisement stated would leave Pier No. 4, North River, on Wednesday evening, going first to St. Thomas, proceeding thence to Santo Domingo City, Samaná, and Puerto Plata, and returning from the last-named place to New York. The vessel was advertised as for freight and passengers, and a notice on the steamship bulletin board at the General Post Office stated that a mail would be despatched by the Tybee. A dispatch from Port-au-Prince in the New National Era reports that the San Domingo Commission arrived from Azua on March 2 in excellent health, awaiting Commissioner White's arrival from Puerto Plata aboard the Nantasket alongside two scientific expeditions under Professors Blake and Ward. The correspondent notes that questions of public debt and the disputed boundary remain foremost, with Haitians opposing annexation though receiving the commissioners civilly. Reports questioning the seaworthiness of the Tennessee were met with indignation, with the party expecting to reach home between late March and April 1. In addition, Commissioner Wade is preparing a preliminary report and a letter to the President to be dispatched to New York aboard the steamer Port-au-Prince.

A Senate speech charges that Baez was sustained in power by the United States Navy while the annexation voting proceeded, citing a dispatch of March 19, 1870 placing the Severn at Santo Domingo, the Swatara sent to Samana, the Nantasket to Puerto Plata and the Yantic in the river, and an official's sworn testimony that the navy's acts were protecting Baez from the citizens of St. Domingo. It also tells how the American citizen Davis Hatch was arrested on a trumped-up charge and, even after his formal pardon, kept in prison at the instance of Cazneau and with Fabens's connivance to prevent his opposition to the treaty, as a Dominican minister's note admitted his imprisonment was only to prevent his hostile action in New York.
