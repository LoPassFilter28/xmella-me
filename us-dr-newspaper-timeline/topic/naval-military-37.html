Pullman Herald

19.— The government finally refuses to sot at liberty the officials

January 23, 190423 de enero de 1904

The Pullman Herald reported that insurgent General Navarro seized cattle from an American-owned estate in the Dominican Republic, but Minister Powell was unable to provide military protection due to limited forces. Powell reported that the small complement of the gunboat Newport was insufficient to defend rural properties or prevent general pillage if insurgents breached the capital. The dispatch also noted the refusal of the Haitian government to release French bank officials implicated in a major bond fraud case.

El Pullman Herald informó que el general insurgente Navarro confiscó ganado en una hacienda de propiedad estadounidense en la República Dominicana, pero el ministro Powell no pudo brindar protección militar debido a la escasez de tropas. Powell informó que la reducida dotación del cañonero Newport era insuficiente para defender propiedades rurales o prevenir un saqueo generalizado si los rebeldes irrumpían en la capital. El despacho mencionó también la negativa del gobierno haitiano a liberar a funcionarios bancarios franceses implicados en un cuantioso fraude de bonos.

New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints a Washington item on Mr. Powell's recognition of the Morales government

January 24, 190424 de enero de 1904

The Tribune prints a Washington item on Mr. Powell's recognition of the Morales government: Mr. Powell has sent to the State Department confirmation of the press report that he has recognized the provisional government of General Morales and that Puerto Plata is now in the government's possession; the recognition took place on the 20th, and it is expected that the other diplomatic representatives in San Domingo will follow Mr. Powell's initiative, thus terminating the chaotic condition of affairs which has caused suspension of business in San Domingo; Mr. Sanchez, the Secretary of State under the Morales government, who is on his way to Washington, will be recognized here in his official capacity, thus confirming Mr. Powell's action.

El Tribune publica una nota de Washington sobre el reconocimiento del gobierno de Morales por el Sr. Powell: el Sr. Powell ha enviado al Departamento de Estado la confirmación de la noticia de prensa de que ha reconocido al gobierno provisional del general Morales y de que Puerto Plata está ahora en posesión del gobierno; el reconocimiento tuvo lugar el día 20, y se espera que los demás representantes diplomáticos en Santo Domingo sigan la iniciativa del Sr. Powell, poniendo así fin a la caótica situación que ha causado la suspensión de los negocios en Santo Domingo; el Sr. Sánchez, secretario de Estado bajo el gobierno de Morales, que está de camino a Washington, será reconocido aquí en su calidad oficial, confirmando así la acción del Sr. Powell.

Evening Star

The Star prints the closing part of an account from the republic

January 27, 190427 de enero de 1904

The Star prints the closing part of an account from the republic: the inhabitants of the city of Santo Domingo have declared that they will stand by President Morales and will under no consideration permit Jimenez to assume the presidency; the greatest suffering prevails throughout the entire republic, and at the time the French steamer left Puerto Plata for San Juan on the 10th one of the Dominican gunboats was shelling Cangrejo, a few miles from Puerto Plata. A Dominican colonel now residing in San Juan received a letter by the French steamer from his mother in Puerto Plata, telling him that the greater part of the town was destroyed by the bombardment of the Dominican gunboat on the 4th instant; that all letters coming into the republic are subject to the severest censorship from the post office authorities, who deliver none of those bearing unfavorable information or instructions, and have such persons to whom they are addressed immediately arrested, and that the greatest want and misery prevail, as the people are actually starving.

El Star publica la parte final de un relato procedente de la república: los habitantes de la ciudad de Santo Domingo han declarado que apoyarán al presidente Morales y que bajo ninguna consideración permitirán que Jiménez asuma la presidencia; reina el mayor sufrimiento en toda la república, y cuando el vapor francés salió de Puerto Plata hacia San Juan el día 10, una de las cañoneras dominicanas estaba bombardeando Cangrejo, a pocas millas de Puerto Plata. Un coronel dominicano que reside ahora en San Juan recibió, por el vapor francés, una carta de su madre en Puerto Plata, en la que le decía que la mayor parte de la ciudad fue destruida por el bombardeo de la cañonera dominicana el día 4 del mes en curso; que todas las cartas que llegan a la república están sujetas a la más severa censura por parte de las autoridades de correos, que no entregan ninguna de las que contienen información o instrucciones desfavorables, y hacen arrestar de inmediato a las personas a quienes van dirigidas, y que prevalecen la mayor necesidad y miseria, pues la gente literalmente se muere de hambre.

The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of January 28: the Presidente

January 29, 190429 de enero de 1904

The Republic prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of January 28: the Presidente, a Dominican gunboat, arrived last Tuesday, and her captain asked the Haytien Government for munitions of war; President Nord made no response. After the installation of the new Dominican consul here, the Presidente sailed for Monte Cristi to attempt to prevent a German steamer from entering Monte Cristi. Word has been received from General Deschamps, a partisan of General Jiminez, who was taken to Guantanamo, Cuba, by the United States cruiser Hartford for the shipping of munitions of war; he and numerous followers of Jiminez will soon return to Santo Domingo. Leopold Espaillat, formerly Minister of War, has arrived at Monte Cristi to help Jiminez.

El Republic publica un despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 28 de enero: la Presidente, una cañonera dominicana, llegó el martes pasado, y su capitán pidió al Gobierno haitiano municiones de guerra; el presidente Nord no dio respuesta. Tras la instalación del nuevo cónsul dominicano aquí, la Presidente zarpó hacia Monte Cristi para tratar de impedir que un vapor alemán entrara en Monte Cristi. Se ha recibido noticia del general Deschamps, partidario del general Jiménez, que fue llevado a Guantánamo, Cuba, por el crucero estadounidense Hartford por el envío de municiones de guerra; él y numerosos seguidores de Jiménez regresarán pronto a Santo Domingo. Leopold Espaillat, ex ministro de Guerra, ha llegado a Monte Cristi para ayudar a Jiménez.

Advices received at St.

February 2, 19042 de febrero de 1904

Advices received at St. Thomas report hard fighting in the Dominican Republic: the rebel General Jiminez was at Monte Cristi on January 29 preparing to resume the contest, having received a large supply of ammunition by the steamer Cherokee from New York. A gunboat bombarded Monte Cristi on the 19th with slight damage, an armed schooner later exchanged shots with the shore, and there has been hard fighting around Monte Cristi and Santiago de los Caballeros, with hundreds of men killed and business paralyzed.

Informes recibidos en Saint Thomas reportan duros combates en la República Dominicana: el general rebelde Jiminez estaba en Monte Cristi el 29 de enero preparándose para reanudar la lucha, tras recibir un gran suministro de municiones por el vapor Cherokee desde Nueva York. Una cañonera bombardeó Monte Cristi el día 19 con daños leves, una goleta armada intercambió disparos después con la costa, y ha habido duros combates alrededor de Monte Cristi y Santiago de los Caballeros, con cientos de hombres muertos y los negocios paralizados.

Daily Kennebec Journal

The Journal prints the closing part of a Washington item and a Monte Cristi dispatch

February 10, 190410 de febrero de 1904

The Journal prints the closing part of a Washington item and a Monte Cristi dispatch under the head 'Blow to Jiminez': at the close of the discussion, it was decided that the best solution of the problem was to turn affairs over to the navy and have Admiral Wise insure the absolute protection of American interests; it is believed here that he has already taken steps to obtain apology and full reparation for the firing on the New York. The dispatch, dated Saturday, February 6, reports: the Clyde Line steamer New York, from New York, January 30, arrived here this morning, followed by the Dominican gunboat Presidente; lighters were sent alongside the New York to bring ashore some ammunition which formed part of her cargo; when the lighters had nearly reached the steamer the gunboat fired on them and the revolutionists on shore replied with cannon and Mausers. The New York, being in danger, thereupon left Monte Cristi for Porto Plata; the firing continued for half an hour, after which the gunboat sailed for Porto Plata; she was hit twice by round shot and apparently by hundreds of bullets, for she stopped and lowered boats evidently to plug the holes made in her hull. The failure to land the ammunition is a serious blow to General Jiminez, for whom it was intended, and may cause him to lose the ground he had already gained; an attack on Santiago de los Caballeros had been fixed for Sunday. The German steamer Aiternberg was here on Monday; the revolutionists did not permit her to discharge at this port.

El Diario publica la parte final de una nota de Washington y un despacho de Monte Cristi bajo el titular «Blow to Jiminez»: al cierre de la discusión, se decidió que la mejor solución del problema era entregar los asuntos a la marina y hacer que el almirante Wise asegurara la protección absoluta de los intereses estadounidenses; se cree aquí que ya ha tomado medidas para obtener disculpas y reparación completa por los disparos contra el New York. El despacho, fechado el sábado 6 de febrero, informa: el vapor New York, de la línea Clyde, procedente de Nueva York, del 30 de enero, llegó aquí esta mañana, seguido por la cañonera dominicana Presidente; se enviaron lanchones junto al New York para traer a tierra algunas municiones que formaban parte de su cargamento; cuando los lanchones casi habían llegado al vapor, la cañonera disparó contra ellos y los revolucionarios en la costa respondieron con cañones y fusiles Máuser. El New York, al estar en peligro, salió entonces de Monte Cristi hacia Puerto Plata; los disparos continuaron durante media hora, tras lo cual la cañonera zarpó hacia Puerto Plata; fue alcanzada dos veces por balas de cañón y aparentemente por cientos de balas, pues se detuvo y bajó botes, evidentemente para tapar los agujeros hechos en su casco. El fracaso en desembarcar las municiones es un golpe serio para el general Jiménez, a quien estaban destinadas, y puede hacerle perder el terreno que ya había ganado; un ataque a Santiago de los Caballeros había sido fijado para el domingo. El vapor alemán Aiternberg estuvo aquí el lunes; los revolucionarios no le permitieron descargar en este puerto.

The Age-Herald prints the closing part of an editorial: the administration

February 11, 190411 de febrero de 1904

The Age-Herald prints the closing part of an editorial: the administration has recognized to some extent the Morales crowd, but Jiminez has still, it is said, a following. The island is about 450 miles long, Hayti holding the western one-third, and the Dominicans the balance; it seems a mass of mountains from the sea, but the island contains many beautiful valleys. In Hayti the French tongue is spoken, but the Dominicans speak Spanish; as a rule the Dominicans are a mixture of Spaniards, Indians and negroes in about equal proportions. The republic was established in 1843, and it has been the scene of many revolutions and insurrections since that time. The United States minister, Mr. Powell, considers the present situation chaotic and an invitation to this country to intervene and restore order. As the United States will not permit any other country to become peacemaker and order restorer, the responsibility seems to rest upon it to do something in Santo Domingo, and doubtless the murder of the machinist will be all the excuse it needs for a degree of intervention that will restore order, or at least enough authority to grant it reparation for the firing on an American gunboat, and the killing on it of an American citizen.
