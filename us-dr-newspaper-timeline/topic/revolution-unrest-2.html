New-York Daily Tribune

The editorial looks back on the eleven-month civil war just ended in the Republic of San Domingo, describing it as…

July 1, 18581 de julio de 1858

The editorial looks back on the eleven-month civil war just ended in the Republic of San Domingo, describing it as largely a geographical contest between the northern and southern sections, led by the rival cities of Santiago and San Domingo under Pedro Santana. It sketches the two cities and the republic's history: independence declared in 1821, twenty-two years united with Haiti under Boyer, the proclamation of the Dominican Republic in 1844, Haitian invasions beaten back by Santana, who received the title of Liberator, and the dissatisfaction that followed under President Jimenez.

El editorial repasa la guerra civil de once meses que acaba de terminar en la República de Santo Domingo, describiéndola como en gran parte una contienda geográfica entre las secciones norte y sur, encabezada por las ciudades rivales de Santiago y Santo Domingo bajo Pedro Santana. Esboza las dos ciudades y la historia de la república: la independencia declarada en 1821, veintidós años unida a Haití bajo Boyer, la proclamación de la República Dominicana en 1844, las invasiones haitianas rechazadas por Santana, quien recibió el título de Libertador, y el descontento que siguió bajo el presidente Jiménez.

The National Era

A Washington letter discusses European diplomatic policy on the West Indies

December 9, 18589 de diciembre de 1858

A Washington letter discusses European diplomatic policy on the West Indies and the preservation of European equilibrium in the Western Hemisphere. The writer cites statements that the Dominican Republic must be submerged by the Haitian empire because an independent republic with American sympathies endangers European colonial supremacy in the region. The correspondent further reports claims that an Anglo-French alliance is pressing Spain to alter Cuba's status, possibly through a confederation with Puerto Rico and St. Domingo, to place it beyond the reach of the United States. Although European ministers in Washington deny these plans, the writer insists that concerted European efforts aim to block American influence across the Antilles.

Una carta desde Washington analiza la política diplomática europea sobre las Antillas y la preservación del equilibrio europeo en el hemisferio occidental. El autor cita declaraciones según las cuales la República Dominicana debe ser absorbida por el imperio haitiano, debido a que una república independiente con simpatías estadounidenses pone en peligro la supremacía colonial europea en la región. El corresponsal informa además sobre versiones de que una alianza anglo-francesa presiona a España para alterar el estatus de Cuba, posiblemente mediante una confederación con Puerto Rico y Santo Domingo, a fin de colocarla fuera del alcance de los Estados Unidos. Aunque los ministros europeos en Washington niegan dichos planes, el redactor insiste en que los esfuerzos concertados europeos procuran cerrarle el paso a la influencia estadounidense en las Antillas.

January 19, 185919 de enero de 1859

The paper gives an account of Geffrard's revolution: Geffrard, joined by the military and town people, marched to a palace of Soulouque, a deputation proclaimed the republic of Haiti with Geffrard as provisional head, and on Thursday, December 23, the republic was publicly proclaimed in the grand square and Geffrard named provisional President, which office he accepted. It says the fortified town of St. Marc was taken without opposition, the insurrection was spreading north and south, and decrees had been issued reviving the constitution of 1816, calling a legislature, releasing all political prisoners, increasing soldiers' rations, ordering the destruction of the Fort of Gonaives, and depriving Soulouque of all power.

El periódico ofrece un relato de la revolución de Geffrard: Geffrard, acompañado por los militares y la gente de la ciudad, marchó a un palacio de Soulouque; una diputación proclamó la República de Haití con Geffrard como jefe provisional, y el jueves 23 de diciembre la república fue proclamada públicamente en la gran plaza y Geffrard fue nombrado presidente provisional, cargo que aceptó. Dice que la ciudad fortificada de Saint-Marc fue tomada sin oposición, que la insurrección se extendía al norte y al sur, y que se habían emitido decretos que restablecían la constitución de 1816, convocaban una legislatura, liberaban a todos los prisioneros políticos, aumentaban las raciones de los soldados, ordenaban la destrucción del Fuerte de Gonaïves y privaban a Soulouque de todo poder.

A dispatch reports that a Danish envoy supported by a war vessel

April 7, 18597 de abril de 1859

A dispatch reports that a Danish envoy supported by a war vessel peremptorily demanded $150,000 from the Dominican government as indemnity for two Danish ships seized by President Baez during the recent revolution in Dominica. President Santana offered to submit the dispute to arbitration, but the Danish official refused and delivered a twenty-four-hour ultimatum backed by the threat of a naval blockade. The Dominicans resolved to resist and immediately began manning their coastal batteries against the single Danish warship in port. A local postscript adds that prior to the Danish ultimatum, French naval authorities in port had demanded concessions over all gold, silver, and copper mines in the Dominican Republic.

The New-York Daily Tribune, commenting on United States relations

June 4, 18614 de junio de 1861

The New-York Daily Tribune, commenting on United States relations with Spain, remarks that there remains the chance of a collision, or rather of a diplomatic coolness and alienation, resulting from Spain's recent "reannexation" of the Dominican Republic (referred to in the passage as "Dominica"). The editorial characterizes the Spanish move as a rapacious and foolish act, though not nearly so unjustifiable as the United States' own kindred appropriation of Texas. The paper argues that had the United States ever treated the Dominican Republic with common decency or formally recognized its existence, Washington might now with reason interpose an energetic protest against the new Spanish blunder. As matters stand, the Tribune asks how the United States can protest when "Dominica," so far as appears on the surface, has chosen to merge its independent existence into a return to colonial dependence on Spain.

Al comentar las relaciones de los Estados Unidos con España, el New-York Daily Tribune señala que subsiste la posibilidad de un choque, o más bien de un enfriamiento y distanciamiento diplomático, como resultado de la reciente «reanexión» española de la República Dominicana (denominada en el pasaje «Dominica»).
