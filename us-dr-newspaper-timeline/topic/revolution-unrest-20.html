The San Francisco Call

A Washington dispatch dated November 1 states that rebels in Santo Domingo

November 2, 19122 de noviembre de 1912

A Washington dispatch dated November 1 states that rebels in Santo Domingo are causing severe unrest, making United States naval intervention—comparable to the occupation of Nicaragua by marines and bluejackets—appear certain within ten days. The United States government is also expected to assume full control over Dominican finances until the government proves capable of responsibility, following State Department reports of misappropriated funds and grave financial disorder under the Victoria administration. In addition, dispatches from Puerto Plata report that insurgents have besieged Monte Cristi since Sunday under near-continuous bombardment, endangering American lives and property.

Un despacho de Washington fechado el 1 de noviembre afirma que los rebeldes en Santo Domingo están provocando graves disturbios, por lo que una intervención naval estadounidense —comparable a la ocupación de Nicaragua por infantes de marina y marineros— parece segura en un plazo de diez días. Se prevé además que el gobierno de Estados Unidos asuma el control total de las finanzas dominicanas hasta que su gobierno demuestre capacidad de gestión, tras informes del Departamento de Estado que denuncian malversación de fondos y grave desorden financiero bajo la administración de Victoria. Asimismo, reportes de Puerto Plata indican que los insurgentes han sitiado Monte Cristi desde el domingo bajo un bombardeo casi incesante, amenazando vidas y propiedades estadounidenses.

Advices forwarded to the State Department from Puerto Plata report

November 2, 19122 de noviembre de 1912

Advices forwarded to the State Department from Puerto Plata report that bombardment has been continuing since Sunday, prompting expectations that a United States warship has been sent there. The cruiser Baltimore sailed from Philadelphia at noon today for Santo Domingo City and the Yankton left New York yesterday for Puerto Plata, bringing the total United States naval presence in Dominican waters to four ships alongside the Prairie at Caldera Bay and the Wheeling at Santo Domingo City. Upon arrival, the Baltimore will embark a detachment of the 750 marines aboard the Prairie, while special commissioners continue negotiations with the Dominican government in Santo Domingo City.

Informes remitidos al Departamento de Estado desde Puerto Plata señalan que el bombardeo continúa desde el domingo, lo que hace presumir el envío de un buque de guerra estadounidense a dicho punto. El crucero Baltimore zarpó hoy al mediodía desde Filadelfia hacia la ciudad de Santo Domingo y el Yankton partió ayer de Nueva York rumbo a Puerto Plata, con lo que la presencia naval de Estados Unidos en aguas dominicanas ascenderá a cuatro naves junto al Prairie en la bahía de las Calderas y el Wheeling en la capital dominicana. A su llegada, el Baltimore embarcará a un contingente de los 750 infantes de marina que se encuentran a bordo del Prairie, mientras comisionados especiales continúan las negociaciones con el gobierno dominicano en la capital.

December 2, 19122 de diciembre de 1912

The New Haven Union reports that voting on the election of Archbishop Nouel proceeded favorably in both houses, though Dominican law requires the House to vote three times with no more than one ballot per day, requiring three days to finalize the result. The election carries out a compromise program proposed by United States commissioners and accepted by the now-resigned Victoria administration and the revolutionists, with a permanent presidential election planned once normal conditions return. In a Rome dispatch dated Dec. 3, it is reported that the Pope cabled congratulations and his apostolic blessing to Archbishop Nouel on his election as provisional president of the Dominican Republic, expressing hope that it will strengthen ties with the Holy See and noting that the prelate will likely be created a cardinal upon completing his term.

The New Haven Union informa que la votación para la elección del arzobispo Nouel avanzó favorablemente en ambas cámaras, aunque la ley dominicana exige que la Cámara vote tres veces a razón de no más de un sufragio por día, por lo que se requerirán tres días para finalizar el resultado. La elección responde a un programa de compromiso propuesto por los comisionados de los Estados Unidos y aceptado tanto por el ya dimitido gobierno de Victoria como por los revolucionarios, previéndose una elección presidencial permanente tan pronto como se restablezcan las condiciones normales. En un despacho de Roma con fecha del 3 de diciembre, se informa que el Papa telegrafió sus felicitaciones y su bendición apostólica al arzobispo Nouel por su elección como presidente provisional de la República Dominicana, expresando la esperanza de que fortalezca los lazos con la Santa Sede y señalando que es probable que el prelado sea nombrado cardenal al concluir su mandato.

Following the departure of United States marines from Santo Domingo city aboard the cruiser-transport Prairie for Philadelphia, the Evening Star reports that Archbishop Adolfo Alejandro Nouel has been unanimously elected provisional president of the Dominican Republic. The fifty-year-old prelate, Archbishop of Santo Domingo and Primate of America, was consecrated by his Roman classmate Cardinal Merry del Val and received special permission from Pope Pius to assume the presidency. El prelado de cincuenta años, arzobispo de Santo Domingo y primado de América, fue consagrado por su compañero de estudios en Roma, el cardenal Merry del Val, y recibió un permiso especial del papa Pío para asumir la presidencia. Descrito como amigo íntimo del cardenal Farley, quien le obsequió el anillo episcopal que lleva puesto, el arzobispo Nouel fue seleccionado como la única figura de consenso capaz de restablecer la paz tras la renuncia del presidente Victoria. Former Provisional President Vasquez, whose fortunes are cast with Cespedes in the present attempt to overthrow the regular government, commands equally great influence and resources in the rich farming countries in the interior. As it would be almost impossible to send an army overland to put down this uprising, the authorities in Santo Domingo City are depending altogether on the successful bombardment of Puerto Plata.

Supposed to lie In the American receivership Is that the customs

The Raymer Enterprise reviewed the history of the Dominican customs receivership, noting that removing customs revenues from political grasp had ended chronic revolutions and established domestic peace between 1905 and 1912. The article recalled that President Roosevelt intervened in 1905 to prevent European creditor nations from seizing Dominican customs to collect $20,000,000 in defaulted debts. Newly appointed Receiver Walter W. Vick was charged with maintaining strict administrative neutrality and safeguarding revenue security.

The Wheeling Intelligencer prints a dispatch: the revolution

October 9, 19139 de octubre de 1913

The Wheeling Intelligencer prints a dispatch: the revolution headed by General Horatio Vasquez against the government of Provisional President Jose Bordas Valdez is at an end. The American minister's action is the first successful application of the United States government's new Latin American policy with regard to the Dominican Republic, which is to the effect that the force of arms can never again settle any question in this country. Mr. Sullivan has made it known that the last civil war in this Republic has been fought. In the future any uprising, he says, will be stamped out as criminal without a conference being held between the opposing factions, the United States government supporting the constitutional authority against force regardless of the merits of the issue. The people of Santo Domingo are being instructed that the remedy for future grievances lies in the free ballot at a future election. The American policy, as announced by Mr. Sullivan, has been accepted joyously by all parties, regardless of political affiliations. The American minister arrived at Santo Domingo City from the United States on September 17 and immediately brought about an armistice between the rebels and the government troops fighting in the northern department. Since then negotiations had been in progress continuously with the result that what promised to be the most sanguinary revolution in the history of the Dominican Republic has been averted.
