The Watchman and Southron

The Watchman and Southron prints a dispatch: the action of the minister

October 11, 191311 de octubre de 1913

The Watchman and Southron prints a dispatch: the action of the minister is the first successful exemplification of the American government's new Latin American Policy, which is to show that the force of arms can never again settle any question in this country. Mr. Sullivan has made it known that the last civil war in this republic has been fought. In future any uprising, he says, will be stamped out as criminal without a conference being held between the opposing factions, the United States government supporting the constitutional authority against force, regardless of the merits of the issue. The people of Santo Domingo are being instructed that the remedy for future grievances lies in the free ballot at a future election. The American policy as announced by Mr. Sullivan has been accepted with apparent gladness by all parties, regardless of political affiliation. The American minister arrived September 17, at Santo Domingo City. He immediately brought an armistice between the rebels and the government troops. Since then negotiations have been in progress continuously, with the result that what promised to be the most sanguinary revolution in the history of the Dominican republic has been averted.

The Watchman and Southron publica un despacho: en Santo Domingo, la acción del ministro es la primera ejemplificación exitosa de la nueva Política Latinoamericana del gobierno estadounidense, cuyo fin es mostrar que la fuerza de las armas nunca más podrá resolver cuestión alguna en este país. El Sr. Sullivan ha dado a conocer que la última guerra civil de esta república ya se ha librado. En el futuro, dice, todo levantamiento será tachado de criminal sin que se celebre conferencia alguna entre las facciones opuestas, con el gobierno de los Estados Unidos en apoyo de la autoridad constitucional contra la fuerza, sin importar los méritos del asunto. Se está instruyendo al pueblo de Santo Domingo de que el remedio para agravios futuros está en el voto libre en una elección futura. La política estadounidense, tal como la anunció el Sr. Sullivan, ha sido aceptada con aparente alegría por todos los partidos, sin importar la afiliación política. El ministro estadounidense llegó el 17 de septiembre a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. Inmediatamente logró un armisticio entre los rebeldes y las tropas del gobierno. Desde entonces las negociaciones han avanzado continuamente, con el resultado de que se ha evitado lo que prometía ser la revolución más sangrienta de la historia de la República Dominicana.

November 21, 191321 de noviembre de 1913

The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints a dispatch of November 21: a five-fold increase in the foreign trade of San Domingo has been one of the beneficial results of the six years' operations of the 'Dominican convention' under which the United States supervises the customs collection of that country, Professor Jacob Hollander, of Johns Hopkins University, told the Clark University conference on Latin America today at Worcester, Mass. Professor Hollander was financial adviser of the Dominican republic from 1908 to 1910. 'The foreign trade of San Domingo for 1911-12, the latest fiscal year for which figures are available,' he said, 'aggregated nearly $20,600,900, as compared with some $6,000,000 for the year preceding the convention. Little short of a revolution, social, political and economic, has been wrought in the country. The people of the island, protected from rapine and bloodshed, are fairly on the way to becoming a decent peasantry, an industrious and stable one, as sub-tropical conditions are likely to evolve.' Professor Philip Brown, of Princeton, former minister to Honduras, advocated a union of the Central American republics and urged that the United States take the initiative in bringing it about. Professor Hiram Bingham, of Yale, and George K. Tucker spoke on the Monroe Doctrine.

The Sun prints a dispatch: the Dolphin probably will go to Porto Rico

December 6, 19136 de diciembre de 1913

The Sun prints a dispatch: the Dolphin probably will go to Porto Rico and take aboard there a number of American officials in Porto Rico who are to join the 'observing' representatives. It is not the present intention that Mr. Bryan's representatives, attached to the Latin American division of the State Department, shall go to Santo Domingo on the Dolphin. They probably will proceed by way of Key West, Havana and Guantanamo, and thence by the gunboat Nashville over to Santo Domingo. Secretary Bryan was most uncommunicative regarding his plans for action in the Dominican republic. It was admitted, however, that this Government is trying to insure the fairness of the elections as a result of assurances given last summer, when through the efforts of the United States the revolution was brought to an end. The plan of having 'observers' has been adopted as the most expedient procedure in view of the short time between now and election day. The fact that the Assembly to be elected is to revise the Constitution, especially with reference to the provisions for elections of a President, gives the coming elections more than usual importance. It is realized that the placing of a few 'observers' in the Dominican provinces will not effect anything like a supervision of the elections by the United States.

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer prints a dispatch: the Washington government

December 8, 19138 de diciembre de 1913

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer prints a dispatch: the Washington government decided to send some observers speaking the language of San Domingo to act as impartial witnesses but not as referees or officials, so that in case of dispute it would have persons to turn to for impartial testimony. The United States does not intend to supervise or interfere with the approaching elections in the Dominican Republic. A special commission from Washington will merely observe the election as 'friendly and impartial spectators.' That was the way President Wilson today outlined informally the mission of State Department officials now on their way to San Domingo. President Wilson made it clear that the United States had used its good offices in persuading the revolutionists to lay down their arms on the promise that everything possible within international right would be done by the United States to insure fair elections. It was pointed out that the commission was unofficial even to the extent of not desiring recognition from the government authorities of the island. The protest from San Domingo, it was explained at the White House today, was founded on the belief that the United States intended a supervision of the election, and now that the notion has been dispelled by the State Department the administration here is confident that there will be no objection to the presence of the American observers.

En todo caso, aceptó un armisticio por unos días mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de San Domingo comunicaban sus demandas al presidente Bordas por el inalámbrico de los buques de la marina americana.

Bismarck Daily Tribune prints: Bordas's hold upon the capital

July 19, 191419 de julio de 1914

Bismarck Daily Tribune prints: Bordas's hold upon the capital, San Domingo City, in the south is weakening, according to reports to the State Department, while President Bordas besieges the northern rebels at Puerto Plata with a scanty army. Washington, July 18. The southern insurgents already are in a position to dictate terms, which may mean abdication of Bordas. The American charge d'affaires, White, in the absence of Minister Sullivan, has been active in trying to prevent a clash near the capital, as that would be disastrous to the foreigners and noncombatants who have flocked into the city.

Bismarck Daily Tribune publica una nota: Washington, 18 de julio. El dominio de Bordas sobre la capital, la ciudad de San Domingo, en el sur se debilita, según informes al Departamento de Estado, mientras el presidente Bordas sitia a los rebeldes del norte en Puerto Plata con un escaso ejército. Los insurgentes del sur ya están en posición de dictar términos, lo que puede significar la abdicación de Bordas. El encargado de negocios americano, White, en ausencia del ministro Sullivan, ha estado activo tratando de impedir un choque cerca de la capital, pues eso sería desastroso para los extranjeros y no combatientes que han acudido en masa a la ciudad.
