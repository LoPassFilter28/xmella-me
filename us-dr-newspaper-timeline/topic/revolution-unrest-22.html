Evening Star

The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Charge White had taken counsel

July 19, 191419 de julio de 1914

The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Charge White had taken counsel with the federal officials left in temporary command by Bordas and was prepared to offer terms for at least a temporary suspension of hostilities. The fact that Mr. White has behind him two powerful American warships, with their marine contingents, probably had its effect upon the insurgent leader. At any rate, he agreed to an armistice for a few days while the federal officers in Santo Domingo City communicated his demands to President Bordas. The wireless outfits of the American naval vessels were used in transmitting this information to President Bordas. They afford the only means of quick communication between the north and the south at present, owing to the destruction of the telegraph wires in the interior. At Puerto Plata, Capt. Eberle of the cruiser Washington submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. Originally it was intended no forward movement should be made by these marines until reinforced by 400 additional men from the Philadelphia navy yard, but as these cannot reach there for two weeks it is probable, if a crisis is reached, the Hancock will be dispatched immediately to Puerto Plata or Santo Domingo City. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan.

The Evening Star publica: El encargado White había tomado consejo con los oficiales federales dejados en mando temporal por Bordas y estaba preparado para ofrecer términos para al menos una suspensión temporal de las hostilidades. En todo caso, aceptó un armisticio por unos días mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de Santo Domingo comunicaban sus demandas al presidente Bordas. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques navales americanos fueron utilizados para transmitir esta información al presidente Bordas. Ofrecen el único medio de comunicación rápida entre el norte y el sur en la actualidad, debido a la destrucción de los cables telegráficos en el interior. En Puerto Plata, el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. Originalmente se pretendía que ningún movimiento de avance se hiciera con estos infantes de marina hasta ser reforzados por 400 hombres adicionales del arsenal naval de Filadelfia, pero como estos no pueden llegar allí por dos semanas es probable que, si se llega a una crisis, el Hancock sea despachado inmediatamente a Puerto Plata o a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan.

The Anderson Intelligencer

The Anderson Intelligencer prints a dispatch: Two powerful American warships

July 21, 191421 de julio de 1914

The Anderson Intelligencer prints a dispatch: Two powerful American warships with their marine contingent backed Charge White, who secured an agreement to an armistice while the federal officers in San Domingo City communicated Vidal's demands to President Bordas. The adjutant general, Vidal, has occupied San Cristobal and Bani after a successful uprising at La Romana, about 50 miles east of the capital, last week, and he was marching toward the capital when met by Charge White. The wireless outfits of the American vessels were used and at Puerto Plata Captain Eberle, of the cruiser Washington, submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. So far Mr. White has received no answer, but it is believed that Bordas will either make a desperate effort to capture Puerto Plata or to abandon the siege and return south to protect his own capital. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan regarding the Dominican situation. It will be determined at this conference whether the Hancock's marines shall be landed and a virtual, though temporary, protectorate established.

The Anderson Intelligencer publica una nota: El encargado White, respaldado por dos poderosos buques de guerra americanos con su contingente de infantes de marina, logró un acuerdo de armisticio mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de San Domingo comunicaban las demandas de Vidal al presidente Bordas. El ayudante general, Vidal, ha ocupado San Cristóbal y Baní después de un exitoso levantamiento en La Romana, a unas 50 millas al este de la capital, la semana pasada, y marchaba hacia la capital cuando fue encontrado por el encargado White. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques americanos fueron utilizados y en Puerto Plata el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. Hasta ahora el Sr. White no ha recibido respuesta, pero se cree que Bordas hará un esfuerzo desesperado por capturar Puerto Plata o abandonará el sitio y regresará al sur para proteger su propia capital. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan sobre la situación dominicana. En esta conferencia se determinará si los infantes de marina del Hancock serán desembarcados y se establecerá un protectorado virtual, aunque temporal.

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: liquidation of its obligations

July 22, 191422 de julio de 1914

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: liquidation of its obligations was to be insured by American action in Haiti, demanded to force some control of the island government's finances. If intervention were ordered in Haiti, it was suggested that the United States would attempt to establish a financial trusteeship over the republic, by which American officials not only would collect taxes, but also would require proper appropriation of the funds. If intervention in Haiti became necessary, it was possible that the American troops might be used to put down the long revolt that has harassed the Dominican government. Such action, it was suggested, might be accompanied by extension of the authority which the United States now exercises in the collection of Dominican customs. Representations that Haiti's credit was good and that the government had met international obligations have been made to President Wilson by Minister Menos. San Juan, Porto Rico, July 22. The United States revenue cutter Algonquin arrived here last night from Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic, with 166 refugees, mostly Porto Ricans, on board. One woman died during the voyage from the effects of starvation. A report was brought here by the Algonquin that an American bluejacket on board the gunboat Machias had been shot in the leg by a bullet.

September 17, 191417 de septiembre de 1914

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: Former Governor Fort recounts the peace commission's work in Santo Domingo. Since going there less than two months ago, four distinct revolutions and a number of insurrections of lesser weight have been settled and the leaders brought together in peace and harmony. The first refusals of the government of President Jose Bordas to surrender and submit to a provisional government were met by further demands by former Governor Fort. Bordas, finally realizing that this country meant business, yielded to the demands of the United States after two weeks of parley. The most serious condition found by Governor Fort when he reached the republic was the siege of Puerto Plata, which was held by rebels. President Bordas personally headed the federal troops besieging the city, and government gunboats had effected a full blockade of the port. Negotiations for a discontinuance of hostilities were opened at once, and after two days proved successful. The leaders of the warring factions at Puerto Plata were then taken aboard the steamer Prairie, which cleared for Santo Domingo, where another rebel force under General Vidal was besieging the city. Upon the demand of Mr. Fort hostilities were closed in order that the peace negotiations might be carried on. These were entered into also by President Bordas, who had gone to Santo Domingo in his private yacht. The election will be held under the observation of the United States, with slight modifications over the old Dominican electoral law, he said.

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser publica: Cuatro revoluciones distintas se han resuelto desde que el ex gobernador Fort fue a Santo Domingo hace menos de dos meses, junto con un número de insurrecciones de menor peso, y los líderes se han reunido en paz y armonía. Las primeras negativas del gobierno del presidente José Bordas a rendirse y someterse a un gobierno provisional fueron respondidas con nuevas exigencias del ex gobernador Fort.
