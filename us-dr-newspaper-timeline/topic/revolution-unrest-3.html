Baltimore Daily Commercial

A narrative of the crisis at the capital describes how the ministers

January 6, 18666 de enero de 1866

A narrative of the crisis at the capital describes how the ministers, in the confusion, abandoned their seats and sought protection in the houses of the French, Italian and English consuls. Cabral appeared in public and declared that if the popular voice and welfare demanded the rule of Baez he would gladly give his consent; the popular feeling then turned in favor of Cabral, and he retained power for several weeks. The mysterious man who had frightened the Congress proved to be General Pedro Guillermo, who managed to have himself appointed dictator until Baez should arrive; and an election held on the 14th of December by the Constitutional Assembly unanimously appointed Buenaventura Baez President of the Dominican Republic.

Una narración de la crisis en la capital describe cómo los ministros, en la confusión, abandonaron sus escaños y buscaron protección en las casas de los cónsules de Francia, Italia e Inglaterra. Cabral apareció en público y declaró que si la voz y el bienestar del pueblo demandaban el gobierno de Báez, él daría gustoso su consentimiento; el sentimiento popular se volvió entonces a favor de Cabral, y este retuvo el poder durante varias semanas. El hombre misterioso que había asustado al Congreso resultó ser el general Pedro Guillermo, quien logró hacerse nombrar dictador hasta que Báez llegara; y una elección celebrada el 14 de diciembre por la Asamblea Constitucional nombró por unanimidad a Buenaventura Báez presidente de la República Dominicana.

Worcester Daily Spy

The Spy, discussing the sister republic of St. Domingo, holds

December 2, 18672 de diciembre de 1867

The Spy, discussing the sister republic of St. Domingo, holds that the spirit of the Dominican population is decidedly opposed to American aggression, on account of the past treatment of the colored race in this country, and that on that account alone the efforts of the State Department to purchase the bay of Samana have proved abortive: the financial difficulties of the Dominican government would long ago have induced it to dispose of a portion of its territory but for a public opinion that would at once become a deadly enemy of the administration, and even the cause of rebellion against it. In this dilemma, it relates, the Dominican cabinet entered into negotiation with the Paris banking house of Erlanger & Co.: the firm was to open a subscription in France for a loan of two millions and a half of dollars in gold, one tenth of the amount to be guaranteed by the house if the subscription had little success in Europe, its advances to be guaranteed in securities on the custom-house duties of the republic. The government of Cabral indignantly refused to accept the proposition, though the country's financial difficulties may yet compel the administration to come to terms.

El Spy, al tratar de la república hermana de Santo Domingo, sostiene que el espíritu de la población dominicana se opone decididamente a la agresión americana, a causa del trato pasado dado a la raza de color en este país, y que solo por esa razón han resultado infructuosos los esfuerzos del Departamento de Estado por comprar la bahía de Samaná: las dificultades financieras del gobierno dominicano lo habrían inducido hace mucho a desprenderse de una porción de su territorio si no fuera por una opinión pública que se convertiría de inmediato en enemiga mortal de la administración, e incluso en causa de rebelión contra ella. En este dilema, refiere, el gabinete dominicano entró en negociación con la casa bancaria parisina Erlanger & Co.: la casa abriría una suscripción en Francia para un empréstito de dos millones y medio de dólares en oro, cuya décima parte garantizaría la propia casa si la suscripción tuviera poco éxito en Europa, y sus anticipos serían garantizados con valores sobre los derechos de aduana de la república. El gobierno de Cabral se negó indignado a aceptar la proposición, aunque las dificultades financieras del país podrían aún obligar a la administración a llegar a un acuerdo.

Worcester Daily Spy

Under the head 'What San Domingo Is,' the Spy describes the island of San Domingo

February 9, 18699 de febrero de 1869

Under the head 'What San Domingo Is,' the Spy describes the island of San Domingo and its resources. It has an area, with the smaller ones under its jurisdiction, of about 30,000 square miles, and is in extent about the size of Scotland. Every product of tropical and temperate climes can be grown there; it is known to be enormously rich in mineral wealth — for many years, while under the dominion of Spain, its annual tribute from the gold mines, consisting of one-fourth of the product, was about $5,000,000 — and in timber it is exceedingly rich, having every variety of cabinet woods; the flora includes the most valuable medicinal plants known to man. The Dominican territory could sustain in comfort at least ten million inhabitants, probably twice as many; there is an abundant water power; and it is geographically in the very centre of the Antilles. Commercially speaking, its position is worth everything to us, the writer says: we acquire Samana, one of the only half dozen harbors in the world in which the Great Eastern can ride, and secure the key and controlling position in the Gulf. The outlines given are, in the writer's judgment, what seems now the most honest in effort and most promising in result of all annexation schemes lately had before the public.

Bajo el encabezado «Lo que es Santo Domingo», el Spy describe la isla de Santo Domingo y sus recursos. The bay of Samana, the feature continues, is the outlet of the Yuna River, and is fifty miles long and from fifteen to twenty miles wide; the ocean entrance to it is only a mile wide, and is commanded by an old fort, while high mountains enclose both shores. Twenty-five miles up the bay, upon an inlet to the north, is the town of Samana, the inlet very deep and almost land-locked; the bay has been compared both to the Bay of Naples and to the Chesapeake, to the former for its dazzling tropical and mountain scenery, to the latter for its innumerable wild fowl. This bay has been pronounced the key to the Gulf of Mexico; fine beds of coal surround it, and navigators say that the future route to Darien and Mexico will be by the Mona Passage, just outside of Samana. Samana town, the passage adds, is no town at all, a mere semi-detached peninsula with town vestiges upon it, and the site is swampy; Bonaparte planned a city near Samana, to be called Napoleon, and had the site surveyed. An unnamed speaker tells the Chicago Tribune, in its final window

November 30, 186930 de noviembre de 1869

An unnamed speaker tells the Chicago Tribune, in its final window, that "we have been besieged from the Spanish end of the island these four or five years, to annex Domingo, bodily, and twice or thrice during the same period we have been asked to extend our protectorate over Hayti." Baez has offered to use the whole influence of his party to make the United States proprietors of Dominica, accepting it as a territory of the United States, to be, in course of time, a candidate for admission into the Union as a State, and for no other price than the assumption of its small debt; this offer is open to us at present. With a foothold established there, the Haytian end of the island would be soon anxious for annexation, and Salnave would be only too glad to be the instrument of consigning Hayti to a place among the United States. Asked whether there is any party in either end of St. Domingo violently opposed to annexation, the speaker answers: none; the Jacmel party of Hayti have been, at times, incensed with us for permitting our Consuls and Agents to take sides with Salnave, and we have reason to believe that the enemies of Baez are the enemies of the United States; but everybody in St. Domingo who owns a foot of land wants us to come. The offer, the speaker adds, is not entirely from the natives: there are many Americans, with large interests, in both republics — merchants, planters, and lumber-sawyers — whose property would appreciate immensely if the United States shielded the land, where the window ends.

«Hemos sido asediados desde el extremo español de la isla estos cuatro o cinco años, para anexar a Domingo corporalmente, y dos o tres veces durante el mismo período se nos ha pedido extender nuestro protectorado sobre Haití», dice un orador sin nombre al Chicago Tribune en la ventana de cierre de su reportaje descriptivo. Báez ha ofrecido usar toda la influencia de su partido para hacer a los Estados Unidos propietarios de Dominica, aceptándola como territorio de los Estados Unidos, para ser, con el tiempo, candidata a la admisión en la Unión como estado, y por ningún otro precio que la asunción de su pequeña deuda; esta oferta nos está abierta al presente. Con un punto de apoyo establecido allí, el extremo haitiano de la isla pronto estaría ansioso por la anexión, y Salnave estaría más que contento de ser el instrumento de consignar a Haití a un lugar entre los Estados Unidos. Preguntado si hay algún partido en cualquiera de los extremos de Santo Domingo violentamente opuesto a la anexión, el orador responde: ninguno; el partido de Jacmel, en Haití, ha estado, en ocasiones, indignado con nosotros por permitir que nuestros cónsules y agentes tomen partido con Salnave, y tenemos razones para creer que los enemigos de Báez son los enemigos de los Estados Unidos; pero todo el que en Santo Domingo posee un pie de tierra quiere que vengamos. La oferta, añade el orador, no procede enteramente de los nativos: hay muchos estadounidenses, con grandes intereses, en ambas repúblicas — comerciantes, plantadores y aserradores de madera — cuya propiedad se apreciaría inmensamente si los Estados Unidos protegieran la tierra, donde termina la ventana.

The closing window of the Herald's long essay on the island

December 22, 186922 de diciembre de 1869

The closing window of the Herald's long essay on the island gives the terms on which, it says, the United States is to assume the possession of the Dominican republic: a Territorial government identical with the other Territorial governments within the jurisdiction of the United States, the Dominican people first deciding by vote to allow such possession, the United States assuming the public debt and other obligations of the Dominican government, amounting to one million dollars, and paying for their State property one million dollars more. The essay recalls that our government sent out commissioners in July last, who returned in October expressing their belief that the Dominicans are desirous of annexation and that the island is worth possessing, and predicts that, 'Dominica once a Territory of the United States, Hayti will soon seek our protection,' Salnave already looking with favor upon annexation and having several times sought the protection of the American flag, while the newly accredited Minister from Hayti, General Alexander Tate, is said to favor annexation — the man who, in 1861, offered his services to Mr. Lincoln to aid in the organization of colored troops. The window ends in mid-sentence, on the peace that possession will bring 'to a people long distracted.'

La ventana final del largo ensayo del Herald sobre la isla ofrece los términos en que, dice, los Estados Unidos han de asumir la posesión de la república dominicana: un gobierno territorial idéntico a los demás gobiernos territoriales bajo la jurisdicción de los Estados Unidos, decidiendo primero el pueblo dominicano por voto permitir tal posesión, y asumiendo los Estados Unidos la deuda pública y demás obligaciones del gobierno dominicano, que ascienden a un millón de dólares, y pagando un millón de dólares más por sus propiedades estatales. El ensayo recuerda que nuestro gobierno envió comisionados en julio pasado, que regresaron en octubre expresando su convicción de que los dominicanos desean la anexión y de que la isla merece ser poseída, y predice que, «siendo Dominica territorio de los Estados Unidos, Haití pronto buscará nuestra protección», pues Salnave ya mira con favor la anexión y ha buscado varias veces la protección de la bandera americana, mientras que el recién acreditado ministro de Haití, el general Alexander Tate, según se dice, favorece la anexión: el hombre que, en 1861, ofreció sus servicios al Sr. Lincoln para ayudar en la organización de tropas de color. La ventana termina a mitad de frase, con la paz que la posesión traerá «a un pueblo largo tiempo agitado».

A description of the Bay of Samaná, whose opening lines are cut

January 5, 18705 de enero de 1870

A description of the Bay of Samaná, whose opening lines are cut in the window, gives the bay as eight miles broad and receiving at its west end the Yuna, the largest river in the Dominican Republic; it forms one of the finest harbors in the world, and may be regarded as a most important maritime position in reference to the trade of the Gulf of Mexico and the inter-oceanic routes across Central America, in both a commercial and a military point of view. On the north shore is the small town of Santa Bárbara, which lies in a land-locked bay and has natural facilities for repairing vessels, a dilapidated Roman Catholic church, a neat Wesleyan chapel and a custom house; the peninsula of Samaná is thirty-two miles long, its highest peak, Sugar Loaf Hill, 1,936 feet, and Devil's Hill 1,309 feet above the level of the sea, its soil extremely fertile and to a great extent covered with timber suited both for ship-building and cabinet-work, and containing gold, copper and bituminous coal, its population at the last census 1,721. The news of the occupation of Samaná by the United States, the description closes, created great excitement among the people of San Domingo, and the revolutionary party were making redoubled efforts to drive out Báez, who had effected the negotiations and declares that he will annex the whole republic to the United States if he is forced to that alternative.

Una descripción de la bahía de Samaná, cuyas líneas iniciales están cortadas en la ventana, presenta la bahía con ocho millas de ancho, recibiendo en su extremo occidental el Yuna, el río más grande de la República Dominicana; forma uno de los mejores puertos del mundo, y puede considerarse una posición marítima de la mayor importancia con referencia al comercio del golfo de México y a las rutas interoceánicas a través de América Central, tanto en el punto de vista comercial como en el militar. La noticia de la ocupación de Samaná por los Estados Unidos, cierra la descripción, creó una gran agitación entre el pueblo de Santo Domingo, y el partido revolucionario redoblaba sus esfuerzos por expulsar a Báez, que había efectuado las negociaciones y declara que anexará toda la república a los Estados Unidos si se ve forzado a esa alternativa.

The Tribune's narrative of Báez's career: Pimentel elected President, then forced to give way to Cabral when a pronunciamiento broke out in San Domingo City on the 4th of August, Cabral being commissioned Protector of the Republic; Báez presented himself as a candidate in the following December and, though not a military hero and notwithstanding that his sympathies and support were given to the Spaniards during their invasion of the island, by his tact and consummate management defeated Cabral and became President; Pimentel and Cabral entered his Cabinet, but in 1866 a revolution headed by Pimentel drove him out of San Domingo, and another revolution the same year brought him back into power; Cabral succeeded him, but in the latter part of 1867 the office was again vacated, and after Cabral and his Cabinet fled at night from the capital, Báez was formally proclaimed President in 1868; Cabral remained in the field, with Polanco, Luperón, Moreno, Castillo, Ogando and Adó, opposed to the President with arms or otherwise, and Salnave lent Báez a helping hand in those trying times, without which he could never have maintained his position — the window closing mid-sentence, on the President's notoriety for shrewdness.

La narración del Tribune sobre la carrera de Báez: Pimentel electo presidente, luego obligado a ceder el paso a Cabral cuando estalló un pronunciamiento en la ciudad de Santo Domingo el 4 de agosto, quedando Cabral nombrado Protector de la República; Báez se presentó como candidato en diciembre siguiente y, aunque no era un héroe militar y a pesar de que sus simpatías y su apoyo estuvieron con los españoles durante su invasión de la isla, derrotó a Cabral con su tacto y consumada habilidad y se convirtió en presidente; Pimentel y Cabral entraron a su gabinete, pero en 1866 una revolución encabezada por Pimentel lo expulsó de Santo Domingo, y otra revolución, ese mismo año, lo devolvió al poder; Cabral lo sucedió, pero a fines de 1867 el cargo quedó de nuevo vacante y, después de que Cabral y su gabinete huyeron de noche de la capital, Báez fue proclamado formalmente presidente en 1868; Cabral se mantuvo en campaña, con Polanco, Luperón, Moreno, Castillo, Ogando y Adó, opuesto al presidente con las armas o de otro modo, y Salnave le tendió una mano a Báez en aquellos tiempos difíciles, sin la cual nunca habría podido mantenerse en su posición — y la ventana se cierra a media frase, en la notoriedad de astucia del presidente.
