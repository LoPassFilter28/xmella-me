The Elbert County Tribune

The Tribune prints part of an editorial item on the San Domingo Improvement Company

August 10, 189910 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints part of an editorial item on the San Domingo Improvement Company: the American interest at stake in the republic, the item says, is due to a concession which a company incorporated in New Jersey and known as the 'San Domingo Improvement Company of New York' has acquired in the republic; in 1888 the fiscal agents of the Dominican republic entered into convention with an Amsterdam firm for a loan, and to meet the payments on the loan the Holland company was given the right to collect directly the receipts from all custom houses in the republic, and the indebtedness was made a mortgage upon all the revenues of the country; in 1893 the rights of the Amsterdam bankers were transferred to the New York company, and by act of the Santo Domingo Congress the right of the Americans to collect the customs and port dues was reaffirmed; and in the event of a revolution it will require the presence of several American warships to preserve the interest of the American company, and as a civil war would seriously reduce the revenues the administration may feel warranted to extend its protection to the extent of exerting force to maintain peace.

El Tribune publica parte de un artículo editorial sobre la San Domingo Improvement Company: el interés estadounidense en juego en la república, dice el artículo, se debe a una concesión que una compañía incorporada en Nueva Jersey y conocida como la «San Domingo Improvement Company of New York» ha adquirido en la república; en 1888 los agentes fiscales de la república dominicana celebraron un convenio con una firma de Ámsterdam para un préstamo, y para atender los pagos del préstamo se dio a la compañía holandesa el derecho de cobrar directamente los ingresos de todas las aduanas de la república, y la deuda quedó hipotecada sobre todas las rentas del país; en 1893 los derechos de los banqueros de Ámsterdam fueron transferidos a la compañía de Nueva York, y por ley del Congreso de Santo Domingo se reafirmó el derecho de los estadounidenses de cobrar las aduanas y los derechos portuarios; y en caso de una revolución se requerirá la presencia de varios buques de guerra estadounidenses para preservar el interés de la compañía americana, y como una guerra civil reduciría seriamente los ingresos, la administración podría sentirse justificada para extender su protección hasta ejercer la fuerza para mantener la paz.

The Age-Herald

THEY JOINED THE REBELS’ News from all Ports of the Dominican

August 10, 189910 de agosto de 1899

The Age-Herald reported that the Dominican revolutionary movement in favor of Juan Isidro Jimenes was expanding rapidly, with the province of La Vega in revolt. Government troops commanded by General Francisco Tiriano deserted en masse upon confronting rebel General Ramon Pacheco at Dajabon, giving the insurgents control of the Yaque River crossings and isolating Monte Cristi. The paper also noted cordial relations between Haiti and the United States, marked by approval for an American meteorological station at Cape Haitien.

El Age-Herald informó que el movimiento revolucionario dominicano en favor de Juan Isidro Jimenes se extendía con rapidez, encontrándose la provincia de La Vega en rebelión. Tropas del gobierno comandadas por el general Francisco Tiriano desertaron en masa al enfrentarse al general rebelde Ramón Pacheco en Dajabón, otorgando a los insurgentes el control de los pasos del río Yaque e incomunicando a Monte Cristi. El diario destacó además las cordiales relaciones entre Haití y los Estados Unidos, marcadas por la aprobación de una estación meteorológica estadounidense en Cabo Haitiano.

The Indianapolis Journal

The Journal prints the closing part of the interview with General Jiminez

August 10, 189910 de agosto de 1899

The Journal prints the closing part of the interview with General Jiminez: in reply to these offers he had asserted that he had neither need nor desire to organize expeditions; to-day he said he had received advices that two more Dominican generals had declared themselves in his favor, with followings of 800 men; Dominican government paper, General Jiminez says, is unsalable; men of all colors in Santo Domingo are joining his cause, but most of his generals are colored, the proportion of black to white being two to one; when questioned regarding a dispatch from Cape Haitien published by the Discusion, representing that the Haitien government is unfavorable to his pretensions and is prepared to prevent him from landing on Haitien territory, General Jiminez replied that the Haitien government was in a very rotten condition; he estimates that the revenue of the Dominican republic under a good government should be more than enough to meet governmental expenses and the interest on the public debt; when he attains to the presidency he hopes to go ahead promptly with public works and to carry on other necessary improvements, but he insists that 'the first necessity of Santo Domingo is honest men in her administration'; and as to the possibility that the existing government may prevent his entering the country he ridicules it, and, to quote his words, 'Santo Domingo is practically mine.'

El Journal publica la parte final de la entrevista con el general Jiménez: en respuesta a esas ofertas había asegurado que no tenía necesidad ni deseo de organizar expediciones; hoy dijo que había recibido noticias de que dos generales dominicanos más se habían declarado a su favor, con partidarios de 800 hombres; el papel del gobierno dominicano, dice el general Jiménez, no tiene venta; hombres de todos los colores en Santo Domingo se están uniendo a su causa, pero la mayoría de sus generales son de color, siendo la proporción de negros a blancos de dos a uno; al ser interrogado sobre un despacho de Cabo Haitiano publicado por la Discusión, que representa que el gobierno haitiano es desfavorable a sus pretensiones y está preparado para impedirle desembarcar en territorio haitiano, el general Jiménez respondió que el gobierno haitiano estaba en una condición muy podrida; estima que los ingresos de la república dominicana bajo un buen gobierno deberían ser más que suficientes para cubrir los gastos del gobierno y los intereses de la deuda pública; cuando alcance la presidencia espera avanzar prontamente con las obras públicas y llevar a cabo otras mejoras necesarias, pero insiste en que «la primera necesidad de Santo Domingo son hombres honestos en su administración»; y en cuanto a la posibilidad de que el gobierno existente le impida entrar al país se burla de ella, y, para citar sus palabras, «Santo Domingo es prácticamente mío».

The Age-Herald

The Age-Herald's reprinted editorial from the Chicago Times-Herald continues

August 10, 189910 de agosto de 1899

The Age-Herald's reprinted editorial from the Chicago Times-Herald continues: San Domingo's political history has been varied almost beyond parallel; an uninhabited, wild region separates it from Hayti, and the political hostility between the two governments is a fixed and ineradicable fact; Hayti has been growing and ascending, while San Domingo has been declining and lapsing into poverty; within less than a century San Domingo has been successively under Spanish, French, Haytian, independent, Spanish and again independent flags; in 1865 Spain withdrew its authority for the last time, and San Domingo has been independent ever since; the present republic was founded earlier, however, in 1844, and its constitution is modeled to a considerable extent upon that of the United States; there is a congress of twenty-two deputies, chosen under a restricted suffrage, while the executive is chosen by an electoral college for a term of four years; the country is divided into ten districts, each administered by a governor appointed by the president, and the governors in turn appoint prefects over the communes and cities; and under President Heureaux there has been comparative quiet and a revival of prosperity, the insurrection in Cuba leading to emigration which San Domingo was glad to welcome.

Commenting editorially on Dominican affairs, The Age-Herald describes

Commenting editorially on Dominican affairs, The Age-Herald describes the island between Cuba and Porto Rico as fertile, well watered, and suited to tropical crops, while characterizing its population in racial terms as divided between the Black inhabitants of Hayti and the mulatto population of San Domingo, claiming it would become one of the richest countries in the world if occupied exclusively by white people.
