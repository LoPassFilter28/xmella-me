Evening Times-Republican

The Times-Republican prints a Washington item of March 1: President Morales of the Dominican republic

March 1, 19041 de marzo de 1904

The Times-Republican prints a Washington item of March 1: President Morales of the Dominican republic has been refused permission to blockade his own ports to keep out the revolutionists, as shown by the following report to the Navy Department by Captain James E. Miller, commanding the cruiser Columbia: 'This morning (February 8), the American schooner W. R. Perkins came in from the eastward. The captain had informed me he had been warned off Macoris and not allowed to enter by the gunboat Presidente, and was told that he must proceed to this port. Immediately upon receipt of this information I communicated with the minister, requesting him to communicate with President Morales and inform him I would not recognize his right to blockade Macoris and refuse entrance to vessels flying the United States flag. The minister informs me the president demurred at first, but finally agreed to the fact.' The item appears under the head 'Morales Called Down'.

El Times-Republican publica una nota de Washington del 1 de marzo: al presidente Morales de la República Dominicana se le ha negado permiso para bloquear sus propios puertos para mantener fuera a los revolucionarios, según muestra el siguiente informe al Departamento de Marina del capitán James E. Miller, al mando del crucero Columbia: «Esta mañana (8 de febrero), la goleta estadounidense W. R. Perkins vino del este. El capitán me había informado que había sido advertido de que se alejara de Macorís y no se le había permitido entrar por la cañonera Presidente, y se le había dicho que debía proceder a este puerto. Inmediatamente al recibo de esta información me comuniqué con el ministro, pidiéndole que se comunicara con el presidente Morales y le informara que no reconocería su derecho a bloquear Macorís y negar la entrada a buques que enarbolan la bandera de los Estados Unidos. El ministro me informa que el presidente vaciló al principio, pero finalmente aceptó el hecho». La nota aparece bajo el titular «Morales Called Down».

The News-Herald

The News-Herald prints a magazine excerpt on Santo Domingo

March 3, 19043 de marzo de 1904

The News-Herald prints a magazine excerpt on Santo Domingo, drawn from 'Santo Domingo, A Turbulent Republic', by Charles S. Solomon, in the American Monthly Review of Reviews for March: the absence of cold weather, the excerpt says, makes it possible for the insurrectionists to fight season in and season out, sleep in the open, and subsist, if necessary, on the fruit which grows in great variety and abundance in their country. In the Dominican republic, revolutions take the place of elections: not because the people are too impetuous to permit a president to complete his tenure of four years, and not because the conditions have ever been improved as the result of a victorious campaign by some ultra 'patriotic' leader. Solomon, who says he has watched for ten years with political disinterestedness every step made in the politics of Santo Domingo, puts the reasons for the many uprisings that have taken place there in that period in two words, 'political ambition': some supposed misconduct on the part of the government is made the pretext for fighting, he says, done to mislead the people, who blindly follow the standard of the ambitious politician and the manifesto he spreads over the country setting forth the faults of the president whom he wishes to depose and incidentally succeed in office.

El News-Herald publica un extracto de revista sobre Santo Domingo, tomado de «Santo Domingo, A Turbulent Republic», de Charles S. Solomon, en la American Monthly Review of Reviews de marzo: la ausencia de frío, dice el extracto, hace posible que los insurrectos peleen en toda estación, duerman al aire libre y subsistan, si es necesario, de la fruta que crece en gran variedad y abundancia en su país. En la República Dominicana, las revoluciones ocupan el lugar de las elecciones: no porque el pueblo sea demasiado impetuoso para permitir que un presidente complete su período de cuatro años, ni porque las condiciones hayan mejorado alguna vez como resultado de una campaña victoriosa de algún caudillo ultra «patriótico». Solomon, que dice haber observado durante diez años con desinterés político cada paso dado en la política de Santo Domingo, resume en dos palabras las razones de los muchos levantamientos ocurridos allí en ese período, «ambición política»: alguna supuesta mala conducta del gobierno se convierte en pretexto para pelear, dice, hecho para engañar al pueblo, que sigue ciegamente la bandera del político ambicioso y el manifiesto que difunde por el país exponiendo las faltas del presidente a quien desea deponer y, de paso, suceder en el cargo.

The Journal prints the end of a Washington item and a New York item of March 2

March 3, 19043 de marzo de 1904

The Journal prints the end of a Washington item and a New York item of March 2 on Santo Domingo: the Washington item's closing lines say it does not care to create a strong government on the island by taking sides in the factional struggles there, and the whole subject is under serious consideration by the administration, which realizes that foreign interests are looking to the United States to relieve their own governments of the necessity of taking hold in Santo Domingo. The New York item reports that Gen. Francisco Vasquez, formerly Dominican consul here and a brother of former President Horacio Vasquez, asserts that he has documentary proof that the Jiminez revolutionists are deliberately making war upon Americans in the island in order that the United States government may be moved to interfere and assume a protectorate over the country; one letter shown by General Vasquez purports to have been written by General Arias, governor of Monte Cristi, to Jiminez, and declares that they would prefer to cause the loss of Santo Domingo's sovereignty and autonomy than submit to the government in this struggle. General Vasquez, who has just returned from Santo Domingo, asserts that this policy of the revolutionists explains the killing of the machinist Johnston, of the United States gunboat Yankee, and the firing on the Clyde line steamer New York by the revolutionists.

El Journal publica el final de una nota de Washington y una nota de Nueva York del 2 de marzo sobre Santo Domingo: las líneas finales de la nota de Washington dicen que no le importa crear un gobierno fuerte en la isla tomando partido en las luchas de facciones allí, y que todo el asunto está bajo seria consideración de la administración, que se da cuenta de que los intereses extranjeros miran hacia los Estados Unidos para que alivien a sus propios gobiernos de la necesidad de tomar las riendas en Santo Domingo. La nota de Nueva York informa que el general Francisco Vásquez, antes cónsul dominicano aquí y hermano del expresidente Horacio Vásquez, afirma que tiene pruebas documentales de que los revolucionarios de Jiménez están haciendo deliberadamente la guerra a los estadounidenses en la isla para que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos se vea movido a intervenir y asumir un protectorado sobre el país; una carta mostrada por el general Vásquez pretende haber sido escrita por el general Arias, gobernador de Monte Cristi, a Jiménez, y declara que preferirían causar la pérdida de la soberanía y la autonomía de Santo Domingo antes que someterse al gobierno en esta lucha. El general Vásquez, que acaba de regresar de Santo Domingo, afirma que esta política de los revolucionarios explica la muerte del maquinista Johnston, de la cañonera estadounidense Yankee, y los disparos contra el vapor New York, de la Clyde line, por los revolucionarios.

Okolona Messenger

The Messenger prints an editorial on Santo Domingo: the United States

March 9, 19049 de marzo de 1904

The Messenger prints an editorial on Santo Domingo: the United States has many grievances, it says, and the result of the interference will doubtless look to establishing a stable and peaceful government, which will respect the rights of other nations. United States Minister Powell's reports to the State Department for the last six months have been a series of accounts; at Samana, last month, a band of insurgents attacked the United States consulate and removed two refugees who had sought shelter there; at Santo Domingo, the insurgents fired deliberately upon American seamen and killed Machinist Johnson; telephone and telegraph lines belonging to Americans have been cut in various parts of the island, and Americans who have ranches in the Dominican Republic have been persecuted by insurgents, who have killed cattle and offered every possible insult to American subjects.

El Messenger publica un editorial sobre Santo Domingo: los Estados Unidos tienen muchos agravios, dice, y el resultado de la intervención sin duda buscará establecer un gobierno estable y pacífico, que respete los derechos de las demás naciones. Los informes del ministro estadounidense Powell al Departamento de Estado durante los últimos seis meses han sido una serie de relatos; en Samaná, el mes pasado, una banda de insurgentes atacó el consulado de los Estados Unidos y se llevó a dos refugiados que habían buscado albergue allí; en Santo Domingo, los insurgentes dispararon deliberadamente contra marineros estadounidenses y mataron al maquinista Johnson; líneas telefónicas y telegráficas propiedad de estadounidenses han sido cortadas en varias partes de la isla, y los estadounidenses que tienen ranchos en la República Dominicana han sido perseguidos por los insurgentes, que han matado ganado y ofrecido todo insulto posible a súbditos estadounidenses.

The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints an official letter on a bombardment: the letter

March 9, 19049 de marzo de 1904

The Republic prints an official letter on a bombardment: the letter, received from San Pedro de Macoris, Santo Domingo, and dated February 29, gives details of the bombardment of San Pedro de Macoris on February 27 by the Dominican warships Presidente and Estrella: the captain of the Presidente, a Spaniard, was killed, and of those on board her only six of the crew escaped without injuries; in all, 74 men were killed and 23 were wounded on the two ships, and the insurgents lost 36 men killed or wounded. The Presidente was driven away at daybreak, February 28, the insurgents having brought up a field piece in the night and unexpectedly opening fire on her. Several defeats of the troops of President Morales, with heavy losses, have been reported lately. General Jose Amador, who was captured at San Antonio de Guerra with quantities of provisions and clothing and $2,000 in cash, is now a prisoner at San Pedro de Macoris.

El Republic publica una carta oficial sobre un bombardeo: la carta, recibida de San Pedro de Macorís, Santo Domingo, y fechada el 29 de febrero, da detalles del bombardeo de San Pedro de Macorís el 27 de febrero por los buques de guerra dominicanos Presidente y Estrella: el capitán del Presidente, un español, murió, y de los que iban a bordo solo seis tripulantes escaparon sin heridas; en total, 74 hombres murieron y 23 resultaron heridos en los dos buques, y los insurgentes perdieron 36 hombres entre muertos y heridos. El Presidente fue ahuyentado al amanecer del 28 de febrero, pues los insurgentes habían emplazado durante la noche una pieza de campaña y abierto fuego sobre él inesperadamente. Últimamente se han informado varias derrotas de las tropas del presidente Morales, con grandes pérdidas. El general José Amador, que fue capturado en San Antonio de Guerra con cantidades de provisiones y ropa y $2,000 en efectivo, está ahora prisionero en San Pedro de Macorís.

Deseret Evening News

The News prints a signed description of Santo Domingo, its harbors

March 12, 190412 de marzo de 1904

The News prints a signed description of Santo Domingo, its harbors, towns and past: Santo Domingo is one of the beautiful islands of the West Indies, lying between Cuba and Porto Rico; in Samana Bay it possesses one of the finest natural harbors in the world, the port President Grant was especially anxious to secure for a coaling station, at present little used. There are a number of small cities in the republic: Santo Domingo the capital, and Puerto Plata the principal port, at about the same number of inhabitants; La Vega about 10,000, Santiago 8,000 and Samana 2,000. The oldest settlement made in the New World was on this island, Columbus himself having founded it, and here the great discoverer was buried for a number of years; it was the capital of the Spanish possessions in the New World. At the end of the eighteenth century Spain ceded the eastern half of the island to France, which held the western half; soon after, the description says, the whole country under Toussaint Louverture gained its independence, and there followed the rebellion of the eastern portion of the island, resulting in the formation of the present Dominican Republic. Outside of the commercial element, there is said to be much opposition to the idea of annexation to the United States.

El News publica una descripción firmada de Santo Domingo, sus puertos, sus poblaciones y su pasado: Santo Domingo es una de las hermosas islas de las Antillas, situada entre Cuba y Puerto Rico; en la bahía de Samaná posee uno de los mejores puertos naturales del mundo, el puerto que el presidente Grant estaba especialmente ansioso por asegurar para una estación carbonera, en la actualidad poco usado. Existen varias poblaciones pequeñas en la república: Santo Domingo, la capital, y Puerto Plata, el puerto principal, con más o menos el mismo número de habitantes; La Vega, unos 10,000; Santiago, 8,000, y Samaná, 2,000. El asentamiento más antiguo del Nuevo Mundo estuvo en esta isla, fundado por el propio Colón, y aquí estuvo enterrado el gran descubridor durante varios años; fue la capital de las posesiones españolas en el Nuevo Mundo. A fines del siglo XVIII, España cedió la mitad oriental de la isla a Francia, que poseía la mitad occidental; poco después, dice la descripción, todo el país bajo Toussaint Louverture obtuvo su independencia, y siguió la rebelión de la porción oriental de la isla, que dio por resultado la formación de la actual República Dominicana. Fuera del elemento comercial, se dice que existe mucha oposición a la idea de la anexión a los Estados Unidos.

The Call prints the closing part of an item on a naval visit

The Call prints the closing part of an item on a naval visit, and a San Domingo dispatch of March 13: the item reports that the failure to visit caused considerable surprise at the time; there was fighting in progress in the vicinity on the day the party was there, and for that reason the admiral did not deem it advisable to make an official visit; during the forenoon there were several casualties in San Domingo City from the fighting then in progress, the shots crossing the river through which the boats must pass, and more fighting was expected during the afternoon; the admiral, however, sent his respects to President Morales by United States Minister Powell. The Star prints an item on the Columbia accident: it was learned

March 19, 190419 de marzo de 1904

The Star prints an item on the Columbia accident: it was learned today that the triple-screw cruiser Columbia, in Dominican waters, struck an uncharted pinnacle rock in the harbor of Macoris, March 4; the damage to the vessel, so far as it has been ascertained, consists of two dents in her bottom, and the leaks were not extensive and have been stopped temporarily.

El Star publica una nota sobre el accidente del Columbia: hoy se supo que el crucero de triple hélice Columbia, en aguas dominicanas, chocó contra una roca pináculo no registrada en las cartas en el puerto de Macorís, el 4 de marzo; el daño al buque, hasta donde se ha podido averiguar, consiste en dos abolladuras en su fondo, y las filtraciones no fueron extensas y han sido contenidas temporalmente.

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinion

March 25, 190425 de marzo de 1904

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinion, and the Atlanta Journal, the Buffalo Express, the Macon Telegraph, the Philadelphia Ledger, the New Orleans Times-Democrat and the New York Globe all denounce the annexation idea. The Philadelphia Ledger says that a 'military occupation of Santo Domingo, purely as an exercise of international police power, is the measure suggested by present conditions.' The Philadelphia Record thinks that if we attempt to police the island it will gradually become a ward of our government: 'A government cannot play the role of a big policeman within the limits of another state without becoming the protector of the latter. The sovereignty of a region resides where the power to preserve order resides, and when one takes over the sovereignty the territory usually goes with it.' Russia understood this fully, the Record says, when she undertook to establish settled conditions in and suppress the banditti of Manchuria. A military administrator put 'temporarily' in charge of the administration of Santo Domingo would be likely to undergo a slow and gradual transformation into a subordinate of the bureau of insular affairs in the war department. The Brooklyn Eagle thinks that there is little hope for any of the West Indies 'except as wards of this nation', and the St. Louis Globe-Democrat, which has advocated annexation of Santo Domingo for some time, says that nothing short of American supervision, which will amount to a virtual protectorate, will end the misrule of the island's ambitious politicians and their followers.

El Herald publica el final de un examen de la opinión editorial, y el Atlanta Journal, el Buffalo Express, el Macon Telegraph, el Philadelphia Ledger, el New Orleans Times-Democrat y el New York Globe denuncian todos la idea de la anexión. La crónica recordó cómo Báez encarceló a opositores políticos para asegurar la aprobación del tratado hasta que un político estadounidense lo convenció de que una pequeña votación disidente resultaría menos sospechosa. The Montgomery Advertiser

The Advertiser prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of April 3: a dispatch

April 4, 19044 de abril de 1904

The Advertiser prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of April 3: a dispatch received here from Monte Cristi, San Domingo, reports that General Epifanio Rodrigues and Ciena Navarro attacked that place, and that General Jiminez, being without ammunition, abandoned the town and embarked on the German steamer for St. Thomas; this news is confirmed by the Dominican consul here. It is rumored that General Rodriguez and Navarro, having abandoned President Morales, are trying to start a movement in favor of former President Horatio Vasquez; they are now believed to be marching on Santiago de los Caballeros and Porto Plata. A score of Dominican refugees arrested here were sent back to Santo Domingo by the Dominican consul. The dispatch appears under the head 'Dominican Revolt'.

Se rumora que el general Rodriguez y Navarro, habiendo abandonado al presidente Morales, están tratando de iniciar un movimiento a favor del expresidente Horacio Vásquez; se cree que ahora marchan sobre Santiago de los Caballeros y Puerto Plata.
