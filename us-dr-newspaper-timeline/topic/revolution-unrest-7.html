Worcester Morning Daily Spy

An editorial on the Spanish-American republics: they are republics

June 5, 18985 de junio de 1898

An editorial on the Spanish-American republics: they are republics only in name, in the throes of revolutions, liberty is nominal, and the presidents are more like military dictators than anything else, the inhabitants speaking French in the republic of Hayti and Spanish in the republic of Santo Domingo. An attempt to overturn President Heureaux's government in Santo Domingo has recently been foiled, and its leading instigators have been executed, and this is only one of a long series of similar revolts on the island; and the editorial, rating the native people of Cuba as considerably superior in intelligence and ability to the Haytians and Dominicans, doubts whether a stable government can be secured for the Cubans in a short space of time without the aid and protection of the United States.

Un editorial sobre las repúblicas hispanoamericanas: son repúblicas solo en el nombre, en medio de revoluciones, la libertad es nominal y los presidentes son más parecidos a dictadores militares que a otra cosa, hablando los habitantes francés en la república de Haití y español en la república de Santo Domingo. Un intento de derrocar el gobierno del presidente Heureaux en Santo Domingo ha sido frustrado recientemente, y sus principales instigadores han sido ejecutados, y esto es solo una de una larga serie de revueltas similares en la isla; y el editorial, que estima a los nativos de Cuba como considerablemente superiores en inteligencia y habilidad a los haitianos y los dominicanos, duda que pueda asegurarse un gobierno estable para los cubanos en poco tiempo sin la ayuda y protección de los Estados Unidos.

New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints the assassination rumor from Cape Haytien

June 5, 18985 de junio de 1898

The Tribune prints the assassination rumor from Cape Haytien: it is rumored there that President Heureaux of San Domingo has been assassinated, and the Associated Press correspondent at Monte Cristi, San Domingo, gives further confirmation of the failure of the attempted revolution there. The son of Señor Jiminez, the wealthy merchant who took part in the revolution in San Domingo, at 11 o'clock this morning was still without news from the revolutionists as to the result of the attempt to overthrow President Heureaux, but he is disposed to believe the worst; he ascribes the supposed defeat of the revolutionists to the lack of opportunity to land from the Fanita at Monte Cristi, to the failure to seize Señor Galito, the Governor of the province, as had been planned, and to the death at the first fire of the two foremost military leaders.

El Tribune publica el rumor de asesinato desde Cabo Haitiano: se rumora allí que el presidente Heureaux de Santo Domingo ha sido asesinado, y el corresponsal de la Associated Press en Monte Cristi, Santo Domingo, da confirmación adicional del fracaso del intento de revolución allí. El hijo del señor Jiminez, el rico comerciante que tomó parte en la revolución en Santo Domingo, a las 11 de esta mañana aún estaba sin noticias de los revolucionarios en cuanto al resultado del intento de derrocar al presidente Heureaux, pero está dispuesto a creer lo peor; atribuye la supuesta derrota de los revolucionarios a la falta de oportunidad para desembarcar de la Fanita en Monte Cristi, al fracaso en capturar al señor Galito, el gobernador de la provincia, como se había planeado, y a la muerte al primer fuego de los dos principales jefes militares.

The Press prints a dispatch on the aftermath of the Monte Cristi landing:

June 8, 18988 de junio de 1898

The Press prints a dispatch on the aftermath of the Monte Cristi landing: three prisoners were taken in the recent abortive revolutionary attempt headed by Jiminez and Morales, and Commodore Jansen landed to receive the report of the Dominican consul on the illegal shipments of men from this point by the two leaders to participate in the landing at Monte Cristi. The warships left Monte Cristi at eleven o'clock this morning, and a rumor is current that after they left President Heureaux was made a prisoner in a new uprising and was shot, the report having it that Joaquin Taveras, Lieutenant Commander at Monte Cristi under Governor Galito, led the new movement; the story is not believed, although no explicit reply is forthcoming from Monte Cristi to cabled inquiries. It appears that General Jiminez was transferred from the Fanita, on which he escaped from Monte Cristi, to another steamer before landing at Great Inagua, and it is said that he has been released on bail of 200 pounds; the Fanita did not venture within the jurisdiction of the island authorities on her return voyage.

El Press publica un despacho sobre las consecuencias del desembarco de Monte Cristi: tres prisioneros fueron tomados en el reciente intento revolucionario abortado encabezado por Jiminez y Morales, y el comodoro Jansen desembarcó para recibir el informe del cónsul dominicano sobre los embarques ilegales de hombres desde este punto por los dos líderes para participar en el desembarco en Monte Cristi. Los buques de guerra salieron de Monte Cristi a las once de esta mañana, y corre el rumor de que después de su salida el presidente Heureaux fue hecho prisionero en un nuevo levantamiento y fusilado, sosteniendo el reporte que Joaquín Taveras, teniente comandante en Monte Cristi bajo el gobernador Galito, encabezó el nuevo movimiento; la historia no es creída, aunque no llega de Monte Cristi respuesta explícita alguna a las indagaciones cablegráficas. Parece que el general Jiminez fue transferido de la Fanita, en la que escapó de Monte Cristi, a otro vapor antes de desembarcar en Gran Inagua, y se dice que ha sido liberado bajo fianza de 200 libras; la Fanita no se aventuró dentro de la jurisdicción de las autoridades de la isla en su viaje de regreso.

The Wilmington Daily Republican

The Republican prints the June 10 dispatch from Cape Haytien

June 10, 189810 de junio de 1898

The Republican prints the June 10 dispatch from Cape Haytien: President Ulysses Heureaux, of the Dominican Republic, believes he has discovered proof that the steamer Fanita, formerly of the Clyde line, which was used by Señor Jiminez and Morales in their unsuccessful attempt to overthrow the Dominican government, left the United States with the countenance of the American government, under a mere pretense of taking munitions of war to Cuba. President Heureaux arrived there yesterday on the Dominican gunboat Restauracion, from Great Inagua; while there he personally made a charge of piracy against Señor Jiminez, who had previously been released on bail under a charge of having violated the navigation laws, and a Dominican agent was left at Matthew Town to look after the case until an advocate can be sent there from San Domingo to push the prosecution. General Heureaux talked with the greatest apparent freedom about the recent revolutionary attempt, ridiculing Jiminez and the preparations the latter had made; he told how the revolution was nipped in the bud, and how he had executed nearly all the prisoners, reserving only those who, he thought, might be useful in obtaining further information about the conspiracy; and he said distinctly that he could make no charge against the United States government, believing that the government had been misled, and that a feeble country could not enforce a demand against so strong a neighbor.

El Republican publica el despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 10 de junio: el presidente Ulysses Heureaux, de la República Dominicana, cree haber descubierto prueba de que el vapor Fanita, antes de la línea Clyde, que fue usado por el señor Jiminez y Morales en su infructuoso intento de derrocar el gobierno dominicano, salió de los Estados Unidos con la connivencia del gobierno americano, bajo el mero pretexto de llevar municiones de guerra a Cuba. El presidente Heureaux llegó allí ayer en el cañonero dominicano Restauracion, procedente de Gran Inagua; mientras estuvo allí presentó personalmente un cargo de piratería contra el señor Jiminez, quien había sido anteriormente liberado bajo fianza bajo un cargo de haber violado las leyes de navegación, y un agente dominicano quedó en Matthew Town para atender el caso hasta que pueda enviarse allí un abogado desde Santo Domingo para impulsar la acusación. El general Heureaux habló con la mayor aparente franqueza sobre el reciente intento revolucionario, ridiculizando a Jiminez y los preparativos que este había hecho; contó cómo la revolución fue cortada de raíz, y cómo había ejecutado a casi todos los prisioneros, reservando solo a aquellos que, según pensaba, pudieran ser útiles para obtener información adicional sobre la conspiración; y dijo distintamente que no podía presentar cargo alguno contra el gobierno de los Estados Unidos, creyendo que el gobierno había sido engañado, y que un país débil no podía hacer cumplir una demanda contra un vecino tan fuerte.

Springfield Weekly Republican

The Republican sums up the past week's reports of the San Domingo revolution:

June 10, 189810 de junio de 1898

The Republican sums up the past week's reports of the San Domingo revolution: President Heureaux has been reported assassinated, and the report denied; however, the revolutionary attempt appears to have been unsuccessful, and its originators are now fugitives. The two principal leaders of the revolutionary attempt in the Dominican republic, J. I. Jiminez and General Agustin Morales, are well known in this country, particularly in New York city and Washington, says the New York Tribune; both of them have been conspicuous in their efforts in favor of the Cuban cause, which they rightly considered as intimately connected with that of the liberal party in their native country, San Domingo; while General Heureaux, president of that republic, ever evidenced strong pro-Spanish proclivities, for which he was rewarded with a decoration by the Madrid government.

El Republican resume los informes de la semana pasada sobre la revolución en Santo Domingo: el presidente Heureaux ha sido reportado asesinado, y el reporte desmentido; sin embargo, el intento revolucionario parece haber sido infructuoso, y sus originadores son ahora fugitivos. Los dos principales líderes del intento revolucionario en la República Dominicana, J. I. Jiminez y el general Agustin Morales, son muy conocidos en este país, particularmente en la ciudad de Nueva York y en Washington, dice el New York Tribune; ambos han sido conspicuos en sus esfuerzos en favor de la causa cubana, que consideraban íntimamente conectada con la del partido liberal en su país natal, Santo Domingo; mientras que el general Heureaux, presidente de esa república, siempre evidenció fuertes inclinaciones pro-españolas, por las que fue recompensado con una condecoración por el gobierno de Madrid.

New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints part of its correspondent's account of President Heureaux

June 24, 189824 de junio de 1898

The Tribune prints part of its correspondent's account of President Heureaux at Cape Haytien: Heureaux entertained a good deal of suspicion of the Haytian authorities, which may not be entirely allayed, and the correspondent judges his ambition not unequal to an attempt to consolidate the whole island under his sway, if ever a fair excuse and a fairer opportunity offered, rating Hayti, though a little stronger in the armament of her gunboats and in the paper strength of her army, greatly inferior to Santo Domingo in the warlike qualities of her people, as also in their capacity for culture and industrial and commercial advancement. One of the notable traits of Heureaux's conversation while in the port of Cape Haytien the second time, last Thursday, the 9th, was his contempt, not the least bit concealed, for the Haytians; nevertheless, on this visit he ordered the regulation salute of twenty-one guns to be fired, and it was returned after the usual lazy fashion by the Haytians from the two or three small field-pieces of ancient type which constitute about the whole of the serviceable artillery there. Under the head 'Night Excursions in Disguise,' the account adds that Heureaux is a man of mystery and of mystification, as well as of promptitude and blood, and that after each of his departures from Cape Haytien it was reported he had been ashore in the evening in disguise, and thus, for purposes of his own, had made a tour of the city.

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Dispatch prints the close of the Cape Haytien exiles item and a Washington item of July 28 on the warships: the death of President Heureaux was hardly credited by the people generally until last evening, when undoubted confirmation was received, and then there was great rejoicing among the Dominican exiles, many of whom live in and around Cape Haitien; these exiles, who were driven out of their country by Heureaux, are now preparing to arm themselves and enter Santo Domingo, where they expect their chief, Don Juan Isidoro Jiminez, to join them. Under the head 'To Protect American Interests,' the Washington item says Secretary Long to-day issued orders detaching the cruiser New Orleans from the North Atlantic squadron at Newport, and directing her to proceed at once to Santo Domingo city; orders were issued also to the Machias, now at St. Thomas, to proceed to the same point, via San Juan; these orders were made at the instance of the State Department, and as a precautionary measure, owing to the disturbed condition following the assassination of President Heureaux; neither the State nor the Navy Department has received any advices of an alarming character, but owing to the large American interests on the island it was deemed advisable to take every precaution; and it is understood that American capitalists have an arrangement with the Dominican authorities by which practically the entire machinery of the island administration is under their direction.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo and the foreign interests at stake: the vessels are being sent solely as a precautionary measure; the press dispatches indicate political intrigues which may result in revolution; and, as American interests in the little republic are paramount to those of any other country, it is the part of prudence to have ample force at hand to see that full protection is given to those interests. As to the possibility of annexation as an immediate outcome of the assassination, Secretary Hay and Secretary Long think that it is going too fast to expect such a result and rather discourage this kind of talk; they do not deny, however, that the United States may be forced to serious responsibilities in connection with the future government of San Domingo. While no detailed information has been received regarding the plot which resulted in the assassination of President Heureaux, it is believed to be probable that it was planned and executed by partisans of Jiminez, the revolutionary leader; it is known that Heureaux had lived in fear of assassination for many months, and had frankly announced that he proposed to continue the Presidency as long as he lived. German interests in this republic are considerable, and President Heureaux was strongly backed by them; the future of the republic may depend largely upon the course taken by the foreign element in this crisis, and the course of the German Government in the present crisis will be watched with considerable interest by the American Government.

El Call publica una nota de Washington sobre la crisis en Santo Domingo y los intereses extranjeros en juego: los buques están siendo enviados únicamente como medida precautoria; los despachos de prensa indican intrigas políticas que pueden resultar en revolución; y, como los intereses americanos en la pequeña república son primordiales frente a los de cualquier otro país, es propio de la prudencia tener amplia fuerza a mano para ver que se dé plena protección a esos intereses. En cuanto a la posibilidad de la anexión como resultado inmediato del asesinato, el secretario Hay y el secretario Long piensan que es ir demasiado rápido esperar tal resultado y más bien desalientan esta clase de conversación; ellos no niegan, sin embargo, que los Estados Unidos puedan verse forzados a serias responsabilidades en conexión con el gobierno futuro de Santo Domingo. Aunque no se ha recibido información detallada respecto al complot que resultó en el asesinato del presidente Heureaux, se cree probable que fue planeado y ejecutado por partidarios de Jiminez, el líder revolucionario; se sabe que Heureaux había vivido en temor de un asesinato por muchos meses, y había anunciado francamente que se proponía continuar la Presidencia mientras viviera. Los intereses alemanes en esta república son considerables, y el presidente Heureaux estaba fuertemente respaldado por ellos; el futuro de la república puede depender en gran medida del curso que tome el elemento extranjero en esta crisis, y el curso del Gobierno alemán en la crisis presente será vigilado con considerable interés por el Gobierno americano.
