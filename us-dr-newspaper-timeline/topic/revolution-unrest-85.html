Evening Star

The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Charge White had taken counsel

July 19, 191419 de julio de 1914

The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Charge White had taken counsel with the federal officials left in temporary command by Bordas and was prepared to offer terms for at least a temporary suspension of hostilities. The fact that Mr. White has behind him two powerful American warships, with their marine contingents, probably had its effect upon the insurgent leader. At any rate, he agreed to an armistice for a few days while the federal officers in Santo Domingo City communicated his demands to President Bordas. The wireless outfits of the American naval vessels were used in transmitting this information to President Bordas. They afford the only means of quick communication between the north and the south at present, owing to the destruction of the telegraph wires in the interior. At Puerto Plata, Capt. Eberle of the cruiser Washington submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. Originally it was intended no forward movement should be made by these marines until reinforced by 400 additional men from the Philadelphia navy yard, but as these cannot reach there for two weeks it is probable, if a crisis is reached, the Hancock will be dispatched immediately to Puerto Plata or Santo Domingo City. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan.

The Evening Star publica: El encargado White había tomado consejo con los oficiales federales dejados en mando temporal por Bordas y estaba preparado para ofrecer términos para al menos una suspensión temporal de las hostilidades. El hecho de que el Sr. White tiene detrás de sí dos poderosos buques de guerra americanos, con sus contingentes de infantes de marina, probablemente tuvo su efecto sobre el jefe insurgente. En todo caso, aceptó un armisticio por unos días mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de Santo Domingo comunicaban sus demandas al presidente Bordas. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques navales americanos fueron utilizados para transmitir esta información al presidente Bordas. Ofrecen el único medio de comunicación rápida entre el norte y el sur en la actualidad, debido a la destrucción de los cables telegráficos en el interior. En Puerto Plata, el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. Originalmente se pretendía que ningún movimiento de avance se hiciera con estos infantes de marina hasta ser reforzados por 400 hombres adicionales del arsenal naval de Filadelfia, pero como estos no pueden llegar allí por dos semanas es probable que, si se llega a una crisis, el Hancock sea despachado inmediatamente a Puerto Plata o a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan.

The Anderson Intelligencer

The Anderson Intelligencer prints a dispatch: Two powerful American warships

July 21, 191421 de julio de 1914

The Anderson Intelligencer prints a dispatch: Two powerful American warships with their marine contingent backed Charge White, who secured an agreement to an armistice while the federal officers in San Domingo City communicated Vidal's demands to President Bordas. The adjutant general, Vidal, has occupied San Cristobal and Bani after a successful uprising at La Romana, about 50 miles east of the capital, last week, and he was marching toward the capital when met by Charge White. The wireless outfits of the American vessels were used and at Puerto Plata Captain Eberle, of the cruiser Washington, submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. So far Mr. White has received no answer, but it is believed that Bordas will either make a desperate effort to capture Puerto Plata or to abandon the siege and return south to protect his own capital. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan regarding the Dominican situation. It will be determined at this conference whether the Hancock's marines shall be landed and a virtual, though temporary, protectorate established.

The Anderson Intelligencer publica una nota: El encargado White, respaldado por dos poderosos buques de guerra americanos con su contingente de infantes de marina, logró un acuerdo de armisticio mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de San Domingo comunicaban las demandas de Vidal al presidente Bordas. El ayudante general, Vidal, ha ocupado San Cristóbal y Baní después de un exitoso levantamiento en La Romana, a unas 50 millas al este de la capital, la semana pasada, y marchaba hacia la capital cuando fue encontrado por el encargado White. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques americanos fueron utilizados y en Puerto Plata el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. Hasta ahora el Sr. White no ha recibido respuesta, pero se cree que Bordas hará un esfuerzo desesperado por capturar Puerto Plata o abandonará el sitio y regresará al sur para proteger su propia capital. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan sobre la situación dominicana. En esta conferencia se determinará si los infantes de marina del Hancock serán desembarcados y se establecerá un protectorado virtual, aunque temporal.

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: liquidation of its obligations

July 22, 191422 de julio de 1914

Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: liquidation of its obligations was to be insured by American action in Haiti, demanded to force some control of the island government's finances. If intervention were ordered in Haiti, it was suggested that the United States would attempt to establish a financial trusteeship over the republic, by which American officials not only would collect taxes, but also would require proper appropriation of the funds. If intervention in Haiti became necessary, it was possible that the American troops might be used to put down the long revolt that has harassed the Dominican government. Such action, it was suggested, might be accompanied by extension of the authority which the United States now exercises in the collection of Dominican customs. Representations that Haiti's credit was good and that the government had met international obligations have been made to President Wilson by Minister Menos. San Juan, Porto Rico, July 22. The United States revenue cutter Algonquin arrived here last night from Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic, with 166 refugees, mostly Porto Ricans, on board. One woman died during the voyage from the effects of starvation. The condition of those remaining in Santo Domingo was reported to be pitiable, as it was almost impossible to obtain foodstuffs, the prices of which were prohibitive. A report was brought here by the Algonquin that an American bluejacket on board the gunboat Machias had been shot in the leg by a bullet.

Evening Star

The Evening Star prints a report: President Wilson then will discuss

July 25, 191425 de julio de 1914

The Evening Star prints a report: President Wilson then will discuss the entire situation with the cabinet. Decision as to what further steps shall be taken by the American government in the cases of Haiti and Santo Domingo has been deferred until Tuesday. Under the head 'Disorders in Santo Domingo': At the request of Acting Secretary of State Phillips, the Navy Department today ordered the gunboat Marietta, at Sanchez, Dominican Republic, to proceed at once to San Pedro de Macoris, where new disorders have broken out, resulting in the deaths of two native employes of an American-owned sugar plantation. The extent of this latest outbreak is not known, but the request for American protection came to the State Department from the New York attorneys of the plantation owners. San Pedro Macoris is the principal port of the southwestern Dominican province of that name. James Mark Sullivan, American minister to Santo Domingo, who was called to Washington to report on conditions in the island, will return soon to his post.

Doubtless take steps to protect their interests is they deem it

July 29, 191429 de julio de 1914

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrasted unrest in the Caribbean, noting that while Dominican disturbances were likely to prompt U.S. armed intervention, the country presented fewer military and diplomatic difficulties than neighboring Haiti. European nations maintained smaller financial interests in Santo Domingo, whereas Haitian instability threatened foreign defaults. The Wilson administration sought guarantees that Haitian customs revenues would be managed to satisfy foreign debts in a manner analogous to the Dominican receivership.

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrastó las revueltas en el Caribe, observando que, si bien era probable que las perturbaciones dominicanas provocaran una intervención militar estadounidense, dicho país planteaba menores dificultades bélicas y diplomáticas que el vecino Haití. Las naciones europeas conservaban intereses financieros más reducidos en Santo Domingo, al tiempo que la inestabilidad haitiana ponía en riesgo moratorias con el exterior. La administración Wilson demandaba garantías de que los ingresos de las aduanas de Haití fueran administrados para cancelar deudas foráneas de modo análogo a la receptoría dominicana.

Custer Weekly Chronicle

The Custer Weekly Chronicle prints an editorial: The tremendous operations of this administration

August 1, 19141 de agosto de 1914

The Custer Weekly Chronicle prints an editorial: The tremendous operations of this administration in the field of foreign affairs, such as the Mexican matter, the 125,000,000 Colombian surrender and the already discredited Nicaraguan agreement, have obscured for the time the mess into which we have slipped in the island republic. 'Pressed by European powers,' the dispatches say, 'the U. S. has about lost patience with Haiti and San Domingo.' The policy in Santo Domingo of course is 'watchful waiting.' Puerto Plata, one of the chief seaports, is held by the rebels and has been declared under blockade by the Dominican government. Secretary Bryan has recognized the blockade, pronounced the port closed to American vessels and warned commanders of U. S. warships to wait watchfully for the outcome. As a result, American trade has almost ceased and American property interests are in constant danger. Meanwhile, European nations are pursuing a vigorous policy. A short time ago a German merchant vessel steamed into the 'blockaded' port and landed her cargo under protection of the guns of a German cruiser. No one can charge that the Wilson administration is responsible for the revolution, but it is undeniable that some of the deplorable conditions now existing are due to the prostitution of the diplomatic service, under the present regime, to the basest uses of partisan politics.

The Custer Weekly Chronicle publica: Ha reconocido el bloqueo el secretario Bryan; declaró el puerto cerrado a los buques americanos y advirtió a los comandantes de los buques de guerra de los EE. UU. que esperaran vigilantes el resultado. Puerto Plata, uno de los principales puertos marítimos, está en manos de los rebeldes y ha sido declarado bajo bloqueo por el gobierno dominicano. Como resultado, el comercio americano ha cesado casi por completo y los intereses de propiedad americanos están en peligro constante. Mientras tanto, las naciones europeas siguen una política vigorosa. Hace poco, un buque mercante alemán entró al puerto «bloqueado» y desembarcó su carga bajo la protección de los cañones de un crucero alemán. Nadie puede acusar a la administración Wilson de ser responsable de la revolución, pero es innegable que algunas de las condiciones deplorables ahora existentes se deben a la prostitución del servicio diplomático, bajo el régimen actual, para los usos más bajos de la política partidista.
