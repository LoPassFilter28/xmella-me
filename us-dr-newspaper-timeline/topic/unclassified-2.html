Pine Bluff Daily Graphic

The Graphic reprints the closing passage of a Chicago Times-Herald item

December 10, 189710 de diciembre de 1897

The Graphic reprints the closing passage of a Chicago Times-Herald item on the coinage the United States mint makes for foreign governments: happily, the coins are not intended for circulation in this country; the work is being done for the Dominican Republic, and the dollars will be sent to Santo Domingo; the design for the coins was furnished by the Dominican treasury department and in no respect, excepting shape, resembles the dollar of the United States. It has long been the custom of the United States to make the money of several South American countries, and as an act of comity and good will the work is done at the actual cost of labor and material; the present order from Santo Domingo also includes 800,000 half dollars, 375,000 quarter dollars and 750,000 dimes. The mint bureau has just filled an order from Costa Rica for a large quantity of gold coins, and Costa Rica, so pleased with the coinage, has ordered through its minister at Washington 40,000 more coins of gold; and the first work of this kind done by the United States was in 1876, when $100,000 worth of minor coins were turned out for the government of Venezuela.

El Graphic reproduce el pasaje final de una nota del Chicago Times-Herald sobre la acuñación que la casa de moneda de los Estados Unidos hace para gobiernos extranjeros: afortunadamente, las monedas no están destinadas a circular en este país; el trabajo se está haciendo para la República Dominicana, y los dólares serán enviados a Santo Domingo; el diseño de las monedas fue suministrado por el departamento del tesoro dominicano y en nada, excepto en la forma, se parece al dólar de los Estados Unidos. Hace mucho que es costumbre de los Estados Unidos hacer el dinero de varios países sudamericanos, y como un acto de cortesía y buena voluntad el trabajo se hace al costo real de la mano de obra y el material; el pedido actual de Santo Domingo incluye también 800,000 medios dólares, 375,000 cuartos de dólar y 750,000 monedas de diez centavos. La oficina de la moneda acaba de cumplir un pedido de Costa Rica de una gran cantidad de monedas de oro, y Costa Rica, tan complacida con la acuñación, ha ordenado, por medio de su ministro en Washington, 40,000 monedas de oro más; y el primer trabajo de esta clase hecho por los Estados Unidos fue en 1876, cuando se produjeron $100,000 en monedas menores para el gobierno de Venezuela.

Worcester Morning Daily Spy

The Spy prints part of a survey of the West Indies: there are no harbors

April 18, 189818 de abril de 1898

The Spy prints part of a survey of the West Indies: there are no harbors on the north coast, but Port au Prince, in the Republic of Hayti, is a well-protected port lying behind the island of Gonave; in the Dominican Republic there are the harbors of Santo Domingo and Puerto Plata, which would make convenient bases for operations in the Caribbean. The Mona Passage, only sixty miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico; St. John's is an ancient town, its old buildings curious and interesting, especially the palace erected by Ponce de Leon, who conquered the island for Spain; Ponce, the principal city on the southern coast, has 40,000 inhabitants, situated three miles inland, and Mayaguez, on the west coast, contains 30,000 people; the island's harbors are rapidly filling up with silt and are dangerous to heavy vessels; and in the group of the Virgin Islands, to the east, only the two Danish islands, St. Thomas and St. Croix, are probable — the passage is cut off there.

El Spy publica parte de un estudio de las Indias Occidentales: no hay puertos en la costa norte, pero Puerto Príncipe, en la República de Haití, es un puerto bien protegido situado detrás de la isla de Gonave; en la República Dominicana están los puertos de Santo Domingo y Puerto Plata, que harían bases convenientes para las operaciones en el Caribe. El paso de la Mona, de solo sesenta millas de ancho, separa a Haití de la hermosa isla de Puerto Rico; San Juan es una ciudad antigua, con edificios viejos curiosos e interesantes, especialmente el palacio erigido por Ponce de León, que conquistó la isla para España; Ponce, la principal ciudad de la costa sur, tiene 40,000 habitantes, situada tres millas tierra adentro, y Mayagüez, en la costa oeste, contiene 30,000 personas; los puertos de la isla se están llenando rápidamente de sedimentos y son peligrosos para los buques pesados; y en el grupo de las islas Vírgenes, al este, solo las dos islas danesas, Santo Tomás y Santa Cruz, son probables — el pasaje se corta ahí.

The Enterprise

The Enterprise prints the Fanita's story, a filibustering voyage

June 8, 18988 de junio de 1898

The Enterprise prints the Fanita's story, a filibustering voyage: so far from being engaged in peaceful traffic, she had been for two weeks on a filibustering expedition, and from Tampa the Fanita was ordered to Mobile, ostensibly to take an expedition to Cuba under the charge of Jiminez and Morrillo, both Dominicans, but strongly sympathetic with the movement for Cuban independence. Monte Cristi, San Domingo, was reached on June 2; on casting anchor the captain was surprised to see Jiminez come on deck in uniform, fully armed, and the others similarly equipped; they ordered 37 rifles and two cases of ammunition to be put aboard the boat that was to row them ashore, and then commanded the ten alleged laborers on board to take their seats in the boat; the captain understood that something was wrong, but the boat was rowed ashore.
