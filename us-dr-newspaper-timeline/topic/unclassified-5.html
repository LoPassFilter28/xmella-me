Following years of campaigning, the Fifth Pan-American Conference

September 22, 192822 de septiembre de 1928

Following years of campaigning, the Fifth Pan-American Conference at Santiago, Chile, adopted a resolution in 1923 recommending that the governments and peoples of the Americas cooperate to erect a memorial lighthouse in the Dominican Republic, leading the governing board of the Pan-American Union to organize an architectural competition. The competition specifications require the reinforced-concrete and structural-steel monument to stand on a mound or terrace nearly 40 feet high, not exceed 600 feet in total height, support at least one great lantern, and include both a museum and a chapel large enough to house the Columbus monument currently in the cathedral at Santo Domingo.

Tras años de campaña, la Quinta Conferencia Panamericana en Santiago de Chile adoptó una resolución en 1923 recomendando que los gobiernos y pueblos de América cooperaran para erigir un faro conmemorativo en la República Dominicana, lo que llevó al consejo directivo de la Unión Panamericana a organizar un concurso arquitectónico. Las especificaciones del concurso exigen que el monumento de hormigón armado y estructura de acero se levante sobre un montículo o terraza de casi 40 pies de altura, no supere los 600 pies de altura total, sostenga al menos una gran linterna e incluya tanto un museo como una capilla lo suficientemente grande para albergar el monumento a Colón que se encuentra actualmente en la catedral de Santo Domingo.

An architectural competition directed by the Pan-American Union

November 6, 19286 de noviembre de 1928

An architectural competition directed by the Pan-American Union has invited architects from all nations to submit designs for a monumental lighthouse near Santo Domingo, with a winning entry to be chosen in the coming months. Estimated to cost about $4,000,000 and situated in a park donated by the Dominican Republic, the structure will house the world's largest and most powerful beacon, an adjoining museum for Columbus memorabilia, and a chapel to receive the monument holding Columbus's bones in the Cathedral of Santo Domingo. The Pan-American Union's foreword urges participating artists to adopt a universal perspective encompassing five centuries of world history since the discovery of America.

Un concurso arquitectónico dirigido por la Unión Panamericana ha invitado a arquitectos de todas las naciones a presentar diseños para un faro monumental cerca de Santo Domingo, cuyo diseño ganador será seleccionado en los próximos meses. El prefacio de la Unión Panamericana insta a los artistas participantes a adoptar una perspectiva universal que abarque cinco siglos de historia mundial desde el descubrimiento de América.
