Opening mid-sentence, this article states that during its military occupation

November 24, 192024 de noviembre de 1920

Opening mid-sentence, this article states that during its military occupation the United States has put the republic of Santo Domingo on its feet, restored order, rehabilitated finances, established public sanitation, and begun training Dominicans to carry on the government when the American Navy withdraws. It quotes an October 3 report by Commander Reynolds Hayden of the U.S. Navy Medical Corps, a member of the staff of the military governor of Santo Domingo, noting that the Dominican government account was overdrawn by $14,234.33 when the temporary government was installed, whereas the military government has since paid all 1910 indebtedness, met all government expenses, and held $3,200,000 in the treasury on August 1, 1920. Hayden's report adds that the country's $20,000,000 bonded debt due in 1938 is expected to be paid in 1925, and that the military government is extending education and sanitation while creating communications, roads, mills, telegraphs, and port improvements.

Iniciándose a mitad de oración, este artículo afirma que durante su ocupación militar Estados Unidos ha puesto en pie a la república de Santo Domingo, restablecido el orden, rehabilitado las finanzas, establecido la sanidad pública y comenzado a capacitar a dominicanos para dirigir el gobierno cuando se retire la Armada estadounidense. Cita un informe del 3 de octubre del comandante Reynolds Hayden, del Cuerpo Médico de la Armada de Estados Unidos y miembro del estado mayor del gobernador militar de Santo Domingo, en el que se señala que la cuenta del gobierno dominicano tenía un sobregiro de $14,234.33 cuando se instaló el gobierno temporal, mientras que desde entonces el gobierno militar ha pagado todo el endeudamiento de 1910, cubierto todos los gastos gubernamentales y mantenía $3,200,000 en el tesoro el 1 de agosto de 1920. El informe de Hayden añade que se espera que la deuda en bonos del país de $20,000,000, con vencimiento en 1938, sea pagada en 1925, y que el gobierno militar está ampliando la educación y la sanidad al tiempo que crea comunicaciones, caminos, molinos, telégrafos y mejoras portuarias.

El plan prevé una iglesia «institucional», una escuela industrial, un hospital y una librería en la ciudad capital de Santo Domingo, instalaciones similares de proselitismo en Santiago, e iglesias «institucionales» y hospitales en otros cinco lugares. Opening mid-sentence on making known to the world the truth of events

December 26, 192026 de diciembre de 1920

Opening mid-sentence on making known to the world the truth of events in Santo Domingo before and after the American occupation, the article reports that La Manana quotes the former Dominican president as saying the isolation of the Dominican Republic is so great that not even official circles in the United States know the details of a situation brought about without the American people's consent. He states that only a limited number of diplomatic functionaries in direct contact with the Navy Department possess knowledge of the question, and that without a casual allusion by President-elect Harding, the "tombstone of silence" would still weigh on the Dominican people. The former president and his nephew assert that reports of ill treatment of Dominicans by United States marines are "absolutely true," express conviction that the liberty and sovereignty of Santo Domingo will be restored, and announce plans to visit Rio Janeiro, Buenos Aires, and Pacific coast cities.

Afirma que solo un número limitado de funcionarios diplomáticos en contacto directo con el Departamento de Marina posee conocimiento de la cuestión, y que de no ser por una alusión casual del presidente electo Harding, la «lápida de silencio» seguiría pesando sobre el pueblo dominicano.
