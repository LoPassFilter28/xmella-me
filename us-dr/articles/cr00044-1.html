Juror in Frank's Trial Excused for Hearing TV

By PHIL THOMAS

A juror in the District Court trial of Washington Attorney John Joseph Frank was excused from further service this morning because she "vaguely" heard conversation on a television program last night.

Judge James R. Kirkland excused Mrs. Robert Lyons of 178 Joliet street S.W., when she said she had heard snatches of conversation of the WRC television program "Suspicion" last night.

The plot of the program had to do with the spiriting away of an old man from the United States to a South American country ruled by a dictator.

In the District Court trial Mr. Frank is charged with acting illegally as an agent for the Dominican Republic and Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo.

Judge Kirkland polled the jury to find out if any one of them had seen the program after Edward L. Carey, attorney for Mr. Frank, asked that this be done.

Mrs. Lyons told Judge Kirkland that she did not relate anything that she heard over television to the Frank case. However, the judge, saying that he is a "conservative person," excused her just to be on the safe side.

He reminded the jury of his often repeated admonition that they should not read newspaper accounts, listen to radio programs or watch television shows having any bearing on the Frank case.

"Though this program did not bear directly on the case before us, it apparently had an indirect bearing, and the court of its own motion excuses (Mrs. Lyons)," Judge Kirkland said.

Ill Juror Also Excused

The judge also excused another juror because of illness. This leaves only 12 persons in the jury box, since every jury has 12 regular members and two alternates who hear a given case.

In testimony yesterday Air Force Sergt. Harold French told the jury Mr. Frank was introduced to him by Gerald L. Murphy, an American pilot for the Dominican government airline. Mr. Murphy has been missing since December, 1956.

Sergt. French said the date of his meeting with Mr. Frank—introduced as "Mr. Kane"—was March 6, 1956, at the Linden (N. J.) airport.

Six days later Dr. Jesus de Galindez, a Columbia University professor and Trujillo foe, disappeared.

Officials Present

The sergeant, answering questions of William Hundley, Justice Department attorney, said he and Mr. Murphy, who had been friends since they met in May, 1955, went to Linden to rent a twin-engined airplane.

He said Mr. Frank and two Dominican officials were there at the time. He identified the officials as Arturo Espaillat, then Dominican consul general in New York, and Felix Bernardino, a member of the Dominican Congress.

Sergt. French said he helped Mr. Murphy equip the plane with extra gas tanks, blankets, pillows and "survival pistol" and other material for an over-water flight.

He said he later saw Mr. Frank and Mr. Murphy in New York's Pennsylvania Station.

This testimony by the airman is contrary to testimony previously given by a Federal Bureau of Investigation agent in the trial.

The agent said Mr. Frank had told him in an interview here that he (Mr. Frank) had met Mr. Murphy only twice—once in a bar in Ciudad Trujillo, the Dominican capital, and once in Washington when Mr. Murphy asked his professional help about patenting a new identification card process.

Asked about the last time he saw the aircraft and Mr. Murphy, Sergt. French said this was on March 12, 1956, at an airport in Amityville, Long Island. This was the same day Dr. de Galindez was reported to have disappeared.
