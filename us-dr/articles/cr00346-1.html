Teddy Roosevelt vs. Mellon and Eisenhower

By MILES ROMNEY

In THE WESTERN NEWS

Back in the Harding Administration it was discovered by Senator LaFollette that national oil reserves at Teapot Dome and other places were being handed over to the big oil companies. An investigation conducted by Montana's Senator T. J. Walsh uncovered the rascals. Two of Harding's cabinet members were involved. Resource assets worth hundreds of millions were saved. Some people went to jail, some went into exile, more escaped with blackened reputations. Some people think the mess contributed to Harding's untimely death.

During the contemporary Eisenhower Administration some of the off-shore oil reserves of the nation have been handed over to the Oil Trust, most valuable hydroelectric sites are in process of being given to favored corporations, timber reserves of the nation are being delivered to the Lumber Barons and the potential of atomic energy is being sought by the privateers of Industry.

The Al Sarena timber deal is only one of a number of stinking attempts to filch the nation's timber resources.

The gift of Hells Canyon to the Idaho Power Co., if permitted to be consummated, is a loss to the nation far greater than Teapot Dome. The nation's taxpayers invested billions of dollars in developing the atomic techniques. Now it is proposed by the smart boys of the Eisenhower Administration to hand this development financed by the nation's taxpayers and designed and perfected by scientists in the employ of Uncle Sam, over lock, stock and barrel to private industry—so that the general public can be exploited and the rich made richer. When those running the government today will figure out a scheme for turning over exploitation of the air Americans breath to some giant corporation we can expect the Eisenhower Administration to abet it.

Apparently Modern Republicans, like those of the Mellon era, are most happy when they are gathering for the rich the nation's natural resources.

It was not always the case that Republicans first thought was to give public resources to a privileged few for exploitation at the expense of the mass of Americans. It was none other than a Republican President, Theodore Roosevelt, who, in 1908, issued an executive order which set aside the Hells Canyon area of the Snake River as part of the public forest preserves so that its waterpower potential could not be exploited for private profit. A year later Theodore Roosevelt warned the nation that "the great corporations are acting with foresight, singleness of purpose, and vigor to control the waterpower of the country". He declared that "to give away, without conditions, this one of the greatest of our resources, would be folly."

Thus Theodore Roosevelt, a Republican President, was the author of the "giveaway" charge which ought to be echoing in the Eisenhower ears today in connection with offshore oil, with Hells Canyon, with Al Sareno, with Dixon-Yates, with the green sugar deals of the President's brother-in-law with Dictator Trujillo of the Dominican Republic, with the threatened gift of atomic energy to private exploitation.

It is difficult to conceive Theodore Roosevelt handing over a tax amortization certificate that will cost American taxpayers $83.5 million dollars to the Idaho Power Co., which will benefit by such a gift several times as much!

There is a lot of fresh air which separates the Old Guard Republicans of the Harding-Coolidge-Hoover-Mellon era and their Knowland compatriots of today, and the Modern Eisenhower Republicans—who approve of private exploitation of the nation's natural resources for the benefit of a favored few—and Teddy Roosevelt and Republicans of his type, who considered natural resources the heritage of all American citizens.
