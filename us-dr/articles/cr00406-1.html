Original in English.Original en inglés.

Questionable Assistance to Exiles Use of a Former Batista Police Aide To Interview Anti-Castroites Hit

Gen. Swing, meet Secretary of State Rusk.

Secretary Rusk is in charge of a foreign policy whose aim respecting Cuba is to encourage underground operations there. Many of the Cuban exiles now fleeing to the United States are experienced guerrillas who fought the Batista dictatorship, became disillusioned with Fidel Castro and form the main hope for a successful anti-Castro underground.

The Immigration and Naturalization Service headed by Gen. Swing has employed as one of its interpreters interviewing these exiles Col. Mariano Faget, a high secret police official in the Batista regime. Col. Faget's presence at the Cuban screenings has shocked and bewildered the exile leaders.

They declare it will turn anti-Castro fighters against the United States, complicate underground operations in Cuba and discourage defections from Castro.

But there is more to the Faget story than that. The Latin-American volcano now in the process of eruption is the Dominican Republic. The policy of this government, working through the Organization of American States, is to encourage the departure of the family of the late Dominican Dictator Rafael Trujillo from the scene. This is believed to be the only method of preventing total explosion and anarchy.

Col. Faget fled to the Dominican Republic with Gen. Batista when Castro forces triumphed in January, 1959. They were given asylum by the late Dictator Trujillo, the sort of fact which would impress itself upon any potential rebel against a Trujillo regime.

Such rebels are not likely to find encouragement in the employment policies followed by Gen. Swing's immigration service. The service is trying to play down Col. Faget's role, insisting that while he is part of the Cuban screening and interrogation process, he is only an interpreter.

He is being used, according to a service spokesman, because he is an expert anti-Communist. On this theory all the Batistas and Trujillos could find employment with Gen. Swing; they are, among other things, anti-Communist. Of course, the Batista and Trujillo families don't need the money.

The President cannot escape all responsibility for these operational boo-boos. Gen. Swing is an Eisenhower appointee, often under fire, not only in policy matters but for his abuse of some of the prerogatives of his office. Democrats had looked forward to his replacement when his term expired last summer.

It is understood that he was rescued by his friend, Representative Walter, chairman of the House Un-American Activities Committee, and a powerful voice on the Joint Committee on Immigration policy. Mr. Kennedy needs, of course, every House vote he can get.

It was then pleaded for Gen. Swing that he needed slightly more government service in order to get certain retirement privileges. He has had it or soon will have it. He serves at the pleasure of the President and under the President's brother, Attorney General Kennedy.
