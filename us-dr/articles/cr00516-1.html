TROOPS LEAVE THEIR GENERAL. Dominican Commander Left to Fight It Alone---Jiminez Is Gaining Strength. Haytien Government Not Friendly to the Insurgents---President Sam Has His Enemies---Amunition Wanted.

By The Silver State's Leased Wire.

CAPE HAYTIEN (Hayti), Aug. 9.—General Francisco Liriano, sent from Monte Christo by the Dominican government to attack General Ramon at Dajabon, the headquarters of the revolutionists, was abandoned when he arrived in front of the enemy, by his troops, who, without firing a shot, deserted and entered the camp of General Pacheco. The advanced posts of the latter are at [illegible], and the revolutionists are masters of the fords across the river Yaque, thus cutting off communication between Monte Christo and the interior. The forces of Don Juan Isidro [illegible], the leader of the revolution, are being augmented every day, and news from every part of the Dominican Republic is favorable to him.

ARMS SEIZED.

PUERTO PLATA, Aug. 9.—The Haytien government has seized at Fort Liberte arms and ammunition destined for Dominican insurgents. Several Dominicans in Hayti have been arrested for violating the neutrality laws. Among them is Francisco Deetjen, in whose keeping arms were found. President Sam of Hayti has wired the Dominican government that insurgents will not be permitted to use Haytien territory as a base of operations.

The force for the government under the command of Minister Cordero have encountered insurgents led by General Pablo Reyes.

DON'T LIKE SAM.

PHILADELPHIA, Aug. 9.—A morning paper says:

Three officers representing the insurgents of San Domingo and two civilians, representing those who do not like the manner in which President Sam manages affairs in Hayti, have been here three days endeavoring to find vessels to carry munitions of war recently purchased in New Haven. To avoid suspicion all the members of the party crossed the Windward passage in small boats and embarked on a steamship at Guantanamo, landing last week in New York. Those from San Domingo were delayed by the capsizing of a boat. They claim that there is little danger in landing contraband war material anywhere in the coast of either country, except near the principal ports, as the coasts are not patrolled, because neither of the governments have war vessels to do the work. What they claim are needed are small tugs to take out their purchases and remove cargoes landed on the eastern shores of Cuba some months ago. The tugs have been selected, and the only thing that prevents their sale is a difference as to the price, which it is thought will soon be settled.
