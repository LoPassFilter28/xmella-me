MORALE IMPROVES AT SANTO DOMINGO AS FOOD ARRIVES Bodies Removed, Relief Groups Coordinated—U. S. Sailors Guarding Transportation of Foodstuffs

Santo Domingo, Sept. 9—(AP)—For the first time since the hurricane struck this charming old city last Wednesday, the morale of the people today showed marked improvement as great supplies of foodstuffs and medicines came from Cuba, Haiti, Porto Rico and the United States, along with great numbers of doctors, nurses and relief workers.

Definite knowledge that the outside world responded quickly and sympathetically to the needs of the storm-sufferers had a decided effect in turning the spirit of the Dominican capital from despair to hope.

Fresh Water Available

President Rafael L. Trujillo expanded his already heroic efforts toward sanitation with a view to preventing possible epidemic of tropical diseases. His work was greatly facilitated by an ample supply of fresh water — the first available since the storm destroyed the city's water plant. Clearing of bodies and debris from the narrow streets also showed definite results, and the tropical sun lent its cheering influence.

The value of his training as a general was shown in President Trujillo's organization of relief and his coordination of the efforts of all groups working toward that end. The units distributing food rations among the populace today worked with greater efficiency despite the fact more persons came to receive their succor.

The American Red Cross, assigned to the city itself, opened several rationing stations, and Director Ernest J. Swift estimated that 10,000 persons now are receiving rations daily. At one time today the crowd at a Red Cross food station became so large and so unmanageable for the civilians that Dominican soldiers were compelled to drive the crowd back before order could be restored.

Food Supply Augmented

Tonight for the first time in a week there were few among the city's homeless who were hungry.

Imported food made possible the feeding of the thousands who had not only lost their homes and their loved ones, but also their sources of income. The food supply was greatly augmented by trucks bringing provisions from other parts of the Dominican republic.

Surgical attention for the injured was greatly facilitated today with the opening of the Roosevelt emergency tent hospital, which has 1000 beds.

Comdr L. W. Johnson of the United States army was placed in charge of all the medical work in the city.

Many Vessels in Port

So many vessels arrived today that Capt John Davis, chief of the port at Port-au-Prince, was delegated to the task of discharging their cargoes in an organized manner. At the same time, bluejackets from the United States ship Grebe were sent ashore to form an armed guard over food wagons passing from the wharves to the general warehouses from which relief is distributed. This latter emergency action was taken following yesterday's attempt by hungry citizens to wrest food from the handlers at the wharves.

Comdr John B. Pollard of the medical department, United States navy, today said no epidemic existed at Santo Domingo, but that the need of surgical dressings in unlimited quantities was most urgent.

Relief funds began to arrive today and the banks turned over considerable money for the government work. One of the unsolicited donations received was $531.50 from passengers and crew of the steamship San Lorenzo, from New York, collected on the ship during her voyage to this port.
