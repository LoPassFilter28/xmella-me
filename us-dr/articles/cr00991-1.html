Dominican Ex-Presidents Join Forces; Seek Support

By VIRGINIA PREWETT

WASHINGTON (NANA) — Ex-President Juan Bosch's forces in the Dominican Republic for the first time are acquiring partial army support. This has come through a quiet agreement with former president Dr. Joaquin Balaguer, who has a respectable army following and who now swings his influence to the Bosch cause.

Till this development, the Bosch camp lacked a real handhold on the power apparatus in Santo Domingo, though Bosch won the 1962 election by more than 60 per cent of the popular vote. The apparatus consists mainly of the militarized police the army and the air force.

The pro-Balaguer officers include a few at top levels and many lower officers in control of troops. Balaguer was made president of his country by the late dictator Rafael Trujillo in Aug. 1960, but tried to become independent of the Trujillo dynasty after the dictator's assassination in May, 1961.

Balaguer's army friends asked him to send them an advisor on ways to effectively support the "constitutional" cause led by Bosch. On Oct. 17, Balaguer, who is in New York, sent to Santo Domingo by air the secretary general of his reform party, Nicolas Silfa. Silfa was turned back from the airport because his name is blacklisted by the junta, but the two maintain contact with the Dominican Republic.

Serves As Link

Silfa was ex-Pres. Bosch's U.S. representative for many years before he broke with Bosch and realigned with Balaguer. Now he serves as link between the two ex-presidents.

The new Balaguer-Bosch alignment comes at a time when the power apparatus in Santo Domingo is splitting up. Brig. Gen. Elias Wessin y Wessin is challenging for supreme control a combination headed by Gen. Antonio Imbert, one of the men who assassinated Trujillo, and Gen. Belisario Peguero, commander of the Dominican militarized police.

The Dominican police, involved in the grim chores of political repression at present, number from 13,000 to 15,000 men. The army is stronger than the police only because it has planes and tanks. General Peguero has a palace guard who include many former low-echelon Trujillo enforcers. He is not popular however, with his own higher officers.

Line-Up Of Power

Wessin Y Wessin controls more officers than General Imbert, who attained his rank only last year as a reward for eliminating Trujillo. However, Imbert has a long-standing pact with Peguero which gives him a strong leverage in the power game.
