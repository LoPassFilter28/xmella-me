Caribbean: New Storms?

Cuba's bearded revolutionary leader Fidel Castro, in the first flush of victory after Dictator Fulgencio Batista's overthrow last January, warned other Latin American dictators that they were next. But last week Premier Castro's troubles in his own Cuba were multiplying and the dictators he threatened were making threats of their own in the direction of Havana.

Since the Castro triumph, Cuba's already shaky economy has grown steadily weaker. Unemployment, chronic in any sugar-growing region, has increased significantly. Foreign investment in Cuban industry, agriculture and real estate has slowed to a trickle. The Castro land reform decrees have brought widespread resistance and sparked a potential counter-revolution. Tourism, once a major source of income, has dwindled to almost nothing.

Amid persistent reports of preparations throughout the big island for an anti-Castro revolt, Dominican Republic and Haitian forces were said to be co-operating in military measures called "defensive" in Ciudad Trujillo, "aggressive" in Havana. Dominican Dictator Rafael Trujillo Molina has formed an "Anti-Communist Foreign Legion," led by Dominican army officers and reportedly strengthened by veterans of Franco Spain's "Blue Division" of World War II.

New Moves on Suez?

The rapidly deteriorating Caribbean situation is causing increasing concern in Washington. State Department officials, watching the situation closely, are ready to invoke the security agreements of the Organization of American States should actual hostilities explode the Caribbean powder keg.

But the determined Mr. Castro, despite his increasing domestic troubles, warned Friday that Cuba would tolerate no "interference" from anywhere, including the OAS and the United States. He denied, in the course of a five-hour television harangue that Cuba intended to attack the Dominican Republic. Diplomatic relations between the two nations were broken off by Mr. Castro a week ago, after Dominican charges that Cuba sponsored an abortive invasion of Trujillo's country last June 10.

In London, the Cuban Ambassador to Britain charged that Dictator Trujillo, working with ex-Dictators Juan Peron of Argentina, Marcos Perez Jimenez of Venezuela, and Gen. Batista, was planning an assault on Cuba. The Ambassador cited stepped-up purchases of arms, planes, and military equipment by Dominican agents in Europe to back up his charges.
