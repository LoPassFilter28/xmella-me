Haskin's Answers To Readers' Questions

By Frederic J. Haskin.

A reader can get the answer to any question of fact by writing The Evening Star Information Bureau, Frederic J. Haskin, director, Washington, D. C. Please inclose stamp for reply.

Q. Is it known what bar is referred to in Tennyson's "Crossing the Bar"?—L. M.

A. Tennyson had in mind the bar across the harbor at Farringford on the Isle of Wight, where he was making his home. The poem was written after a stormy night passage.

Q. Is it true that "Stanley" was not the true name of the famous British explorer?—H. T.

A. Stanley was baptized John Rowlands. Coming to New Orleans at an early age, he obtained a situation through the good offices of Henry Morton Stanley. Later, when adopted by Mr. Stanley, Rowlands took his name.

Q. Please tell me if it is proper to typewrite a personal letter.—L. M. P.

A. It is perfectly proper to typewrite a personal letter, and even preferable if the letter is long.

Q. What steps would be necessary for Alaska to become a State in the Union?—L. J. S.

A. No steps for admitting a State have been fixed, but a substantially uniform procedure has grown up. Ordinarily the process is set in motion by the people of the Territory sending a petition to Congress for admission as a State. If this is regarded with favor, Congress passes an enabling act, authorizing the territorial officials to arrange for a popularly elected convention to frame a State constitution. After the constitution is prepared it is submitted to the people and, if adopted, is sent to Congress for approval. If it is accepted, a resolution is passed declaring the Territory a State.

Q. Which are the "Poetical Books" of the Bible?—F. L.

A. The so-called "Poetical Books" are: Job, the Psalms, the Proverbs, Ecclesiastes and the Song of Songs.

Q. Please give me information on how to grow rosebushes from cuttings.—B. R.

A. To get a hardwood cutting from roses, good strong shoots of the past summer's growth should be selected. They should be cut between the time leaves fall and freezing weather. Cut into pieces of 5 or 6 inches with upper cut just above a bud; tie into bundles and bury in moist sand, tops down. They should be placed in a cool cellar and should be planted in the open ground in the spring, so that one or two eyes are above the ground.

Q. When and where does the the Mummers' parade take place?—J. W. P.

A. The Mummers' parade takes place in Philadelphia, Pa., on New Year Day. It comes directly up Broad street from South Philadelphia.

Q. Should a question mark be placed inside or outside quotation marks?—S. P.

A. A question mark is placed inside quotation marks if it is an integral part of the quotation. When it punctuates an entire sentence ending in a quoted phrase, the question mark is placed after the second quotation marks.

Q. Which is the largest planet?—A. R. O.

A. Jupiter is the largest planet, being greater than all the others put together.

Q. Please tell me if caracul wool comes from sheep or goats, and also if the wool from the young is called Persian lamb.—R. R.

A. Both caracul wool and Persian lamb come from sheep, but they are of two different types, both of which are found in Asia. Persian lamb has a longer and flatter curl than that of caracul.

Q. How does the United States compare with other countries in the production of rayon?—G. P.

A. The United States produces 15 per cent of the world's rayon. Japan produces 30 per cent, Germany 24 per cent, and Italy 14 per cent. These are the leading producing countries.

Q. What is the nickname of Washington, D. C.?—L. P.

A. Several nicknames have been given to Washington, notably Capital City, City of Magnificent Distances, Federal City, Executive City. Daniel Webster is said to have called it the Great Dismal and Mrs. John Adams the Wilderness City.

Q. How may I clean an heirloom picture frame with pure gold leaf edging and trimming?—D. D. M.

A. The National Bureau of Standards says that the frame may be cleaned by going over it (including the gilded portion) with a small sponge that has been moistened with alcohol or spirits of turpentine. The sponge should only be wet enough to take off the dust and fly specks and the frame must not be wiped, but permitted to dry of itself. Otherwise the gold leaf may be damaged.

Q. Which is the oldest European settlement in America?—L. O.

A. Santo Domingo (now Cuidad Trujillo) in the Dominican Republic, founded in 1496, is the oldest.

Q. Where may I obtain a songbook containing Christmas carols?—E. L. H.

A. See Haskin advertisement on Page A-2 today.
