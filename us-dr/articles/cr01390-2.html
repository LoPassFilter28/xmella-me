Haitian President's Exile Expected Soon

By FRANCIS L. McCARTHY

(UPI Latin American Editor)

Diplomats said Tuesday that time was running out for Haitian President Francois Duvalier. They said he could go into exile, voluntarily or otherwise, as early as the next day or so.

Haitian foes of the Duvalier regime were reported to have massed in the neighboring Dominican Republic for a long-expected big push to topple Duvalier. They were said to include Clement Barbot, former Haitian security chief and Duvalier's most feared foe.

Venezuela called home its ambassador to Haiti. The United States said it was discussing a joint rupture of diplomatic relations with Haiti in talks with Latin American governments.

CENSORSHIP TIGHTENS

Censorship in Port Au Prince virtually isolated Haiti from the outside world.

The U. S. Navy attack carrier Shrangri-la was reported at sea in the Caribbean near Haiti “undergoing training.” A second American task force with a 2,000-man Marine landing unit, and icluding the aircraft carrier Boxer, has been standing off Haiti for a week.

All these developments indicated an imminent showdown in the 18-day crisis which has enveloped the Caribbean island of Hispaniolaniola, on which both Haiti and the Dominican Republic are located.

In addition, there were new rumors of Duvalier arrangements to flee Haiti with his family similar to those current last week when he sought permission from Dutch authorities to land a Haitian military plane on the nearby island of Curacao.

TERM ENDS WEDNESDAY

With Duvalier's six-year term of office scheduled to expire Wednesday, his enemies reportedly were determined at all costs to prevent him from starting a second six-year term to which he arbitrarily declared himself elected some time ago.

In Santo Domingo, Haitian exile leader Louis Dejoie, the man Duvalier beat for the presidency in 1957 - mapped plans for hemispheric recognition of a newly formed government-in-exile. He predicted Duvalier “will go down very soon.”

With Daniel Fignole, a president of Haiti ofr 19 days a half dozen years ago, Dejoie represents exiles' political reformation hopes.
