Costa Rica Civil War May Be Taken to U. N. By Latin Americans

LAKE SUCCESS, N. Y., Mar. 27. (CDN).—Two of the larger Latin American nations, at least, are ready to seek United Nations investigation of Costa Rica's civil war if the strife takes a more serious turn.

They are holding back, anonymously, until they can ascertain more facts about the part being played by the Communist leader, Manuel Mora, and his influence over President Teodoro Picado.

In general, Latin American delegates here fear that the trouble threatens peace in all Central America and might conceivably develop into a tropical testing ground for Communist encroachment south of the border.

Ulate Victory Over-ruled.

The trouble reached the shooting stage a fortnight ago.

Otilio Ulate, a rightwing editor, appeared to have won the presidential election last month by a margin of some 10,000 votes out of 100,000 votes cast.

An independent election tribunal reviewed the election and found for Mr. Ulate. The government-controlled Congress nullified Mr. Ulate's claim.

Mr. Ulate took to the field in revolution. Jose Figueres, a planter educated at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, became military commander of the Ulatistas.

Somoza Aids Government.

Nicaragua's "strong man," Anastasio Somoza, sent troops and planes to help the government. The "strong man" of Honduras, Tiburcio Carias, and the Dominican Republic's dictator, Rafael Trujillo, also are supporting the leftist group.

Help for the Ulatistas has come from President Juan Jose Arevalo of Guatemala.

It is in no sense a comic-opera war. It has all the sparks of a dispute that could spread, as many observers here see it, into a dangerous situation far beyond the Caribbean area.
