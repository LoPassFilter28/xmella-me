There's Much Excitement These Days Around The Dominican Embassy; Lavishly Decorated, It May Be Home for Generalissimo Trujillo

Washington society has spent enough time conjecturing about one man during the past 10 days to settle one of the world's tougher decisions.

When the Iron Man of the Dominican Republic, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, sent his warlike yacht steaming into the port of Washington a week ago—so soon after purchasing and decorating another house on Benton place back to back to the lavish embassy he bought last year—and just a few days after blowing into town himself and announcing Dominican Ambassador Manuel de Moya's resignation—the tongues of Capitalites began to wag.

Was the Generalissimo planning to live in Washington? Was he himself going to be the next ambassador? What would it be like to have a real live dictator kicking around town complete with strong bodyguard protection? Was he coming here to seek public vindication for having warned us about communism in South America eight years ago? He broke off relations with Guatemala in 1946. Would he be tossing some peppy parties in that dreamy Embassy or on that sleek, all-white yacht with its crew of 80?

Some Answers

One guess was as good as another until some answers came this week end from Ambassador de Moya, whose resignation won't take effect till the end of November.

For one thing, Trujillo will not make himself the next envoy to Washington. "The Generalissimo is a very busy man and consequently could not be ambassador," said the handsome Manuel. "But he loves Washington very much and will spend more time here." He will be coming up, it seems, "every once in a while."

Why the new house when that big beautiful embassy on Edgevale Terrace is available? "The idea of buying the property was for important guests," said Manuel very seriously, "—maybe he and his son—"

"Wouldn't it seem more natural for the ambassador to take the smaller house and leave the Generalissimo the big one?" Manuel de Moya's grin indicated it would. After all, he and his wife moved out in deference to the Generalissimo.

So don't be surprised if the house on Benton place becomes the new Dominican Embassy, and the present embassy—the house that Perle Mesta originally wanted — becomes the permanent Washington residence of the Dominican Republic's Iron Man.

The yacht was up from Florida in case the Generalissimo wanted to take some cruises around here, said Ambassador de Moya, but it will return to Florida this week, having, apparently, taken none. The Generalissimo, his wife, his 15-year-old daughter Angelita and 11-year-old son Radames will also leave this week, but not on their private frigate. They are going to Louisiana and Texas and visit some cattle ranches before embarking on the yacht in Florida and heading for home.

His Yacht

Any whing-dings on the yacht will have to be tossed this winter when both the Generalissimo and the frigate are expected back. Trujillo loves winter weather, says his retiring representative here.

As for what's in store for Ambassador de Moya, he still insists he doesn't know which post he's getting though it's been announced that it will be a cabinet one. If feminine intuition means any thing, there's more to that than meets the eye, too. A favorite of Trujillo's, maybe he's being groomed for an even bigger post.

There is no news on his successor except that it will definitely not be former Ambassador to Washington Godoy, who's due to spend some time in his home country now.

And to complete this Dominican round-up—the Massachusetts avenue house they've owned ever since Flor Trujillo lived there, the one next to Buzzy Hewes' house, was sold three weeks ago to Bob Rodenberg. . . . And finally, Ambassador de Moya's glamorous wife, Anna Maria; his sister, Claudina de Moya, and his cousin, Maria Tavares, set out from Washington Friday—while the Trujillos were still here—for a two or three month stay in Europe.
