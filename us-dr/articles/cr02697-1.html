Heavy Dominican Vote Reported Trouble Free

SANTO DOMINGO, Dominican Republic, Dec. 20 (AP).—Dominicans marched by the thousands to vote today in their first free election in 38 years. The early turnout appeared to dash predictions of a light vote.

The fledgling democracy was picking leaders to guide it in the next four years. The nation has been ruled by a provisional government since the fall of the Trujillo dictatorship in 1961.

Nearly perfect weather prevailed.

Most Dominicans had hopes the election results, expected to be known by Christmas, would give the nation a stable representative government after long years of dictatorship.

There had been predictions of voter apathy in the absence of the fear of official pressures which sometimes resulted in hard labor for failing to vote for the government party in the elections held during the dictatorship.

Despite trouble in the final days of the campaign, few police were on hand to maintain order. There are 500 electorial units throughout the county.

National Police Chief Belisario Peguero said reports from around the country indicated the balloting was progressing peacefully.

Luis Augusto Dubuc of Venezuela, one of the foreign officials invited to observe the elections, reported the turnout of voters was surprisingly large. The number of women in the voting lines was impressive.

The closing campaign hours were marked last night by a brief outbreak of rioting in the capital. Police quickly restored order after three persons were injured by rocks. There were no arrests.

Young agitators from the 14th of June Movement, a pro-Castro group, were in the middle of the rock-throwing, pistol-firing melee with followers of the Conservative National Civic Union.

Dr. Viriato A. Fiallo, 67, of the Civic Union, and Juan Bosch, 53, of the left-of-center Dominican Revolutionary Party, were leading candidates for president in a five-man contest.
