Mack-Whiteside, Teamster Cases to Highlight '60 Docket

By CARL IRVING

Star Staff Writer

The retrial of former Federal Communications Commissioner Richard A. Mack and Miami lawyer Thurman A. Whiteside on charges of trying to rig the award of a television channel will be one of several nationally watched cases scheduled for Washington's District Court during 1960.

The two men are accused of conspiring to give the Miami TV channel to a subsidiary of National Airlines. Mr. Whiteside is alleged to have funneled about $14,000 to Mr. Mack to influence his vote.

Last year they were the subject of a 13-week trial, longest here for many years. The jurors were discharged after they deliberated more than 30 hours without reaching a verdict.

Last month, the Supreme Court refused to block a retrial now scheduled for January 25.

Teamsters Litigation

The Teamsters Union is expected to be the subject of continuing litigation here. Judge F. Dickinson Letts set up a board of monitors early in 1958 to police the union after rebel union members had charged that the election of James Hoffa was rigged.

Judge Letts let Hoffa take office as provisional president and appointed the monitors. The union has been protesting the scope of the monitors' investigation before Judge Letts, the Court of Appeals for the District and the Supreme Court.

A major civil antitrust case is scheduled for trial beginning Tuesday. Riss & Co., Inc., is suing 23 railroads and five trade associations for $90 million. Riss, a trucking firm, charges that the railroads and associations conspired to eliminate it as a competitor.

Linked To Trujillo

Alexander L. Guterma, international financier and former president of the Mutual Broadcasting System, is scheduled for trial March 14 on charges involving spreading propaganda in the United States for the Dominican Republic.

Mr. Guterma and two codefendants—Hal Roach, jr., former board chairman of MBS, and Garland L. Culpepper, jr., former MBS director—are specifically charged with failing to register with the Government as agents for a foreign concern. The indictment accuses them of receiving $750,000 from Dominican Dictator Rafael L. Trujillo to broadcast propaganda as if it were news.

Litigation is expected to continue here this year in another international case—one dealing with disposition of property owned by a Swiss firm which the United States claims was a cloak for the German chemical trust, I. G. Farben.

Interhandel Co., a Swiss holding company, claims shares valued at $200 million in the General Aniline and Film Corp. The United States seized these shares in 1942. The case was returned to the United States courts last year when the International Court of Justice at The Hague rejected Interhandel's application for a ruling that the United States must release the property or submit the case to arbitration.

Conflict of Interest Case

An Eisenhower appointee to the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission, who resigned last August, is scheduled for trial February 15 on conflict of interest charges.

The defendant, Henry J. Clay, a New York attorney, prominent in State and national Republican politics, is accused of representing a group of citizens in an attempt to obtain a Government loan while he was a member of the commission.

Two contempt of Congress cases are scheduled for 1960 here. They involve Martin Popper, New York attorney, and Frank Grumman, a radio operator.

Mr. Popper will be tried March 7 on a charge stemming from his refusal to tell the House Un-American Activities Committee whether he was a Communist.

Precedent Setting Case

Mr. Grumman is scheduled to be tried March 14. He refused to tell the same committee whether he was a Communist and declined to give information about the Communist affiliations of others.

The Court of Appeals for the District is soon expected to decide on a precedent setting case involving a civilian employe who was fired from her job at the Naval Weapons Plant when her security badge was taken away without a hearing.

The woman, Mrs. Rachel M. Brawner, was a cafeteria cook at the plant, formerly known as the Naval Gun Factory. The Government has argued that it can exclude anyone from a military installation in the interest of security without a hearing.

Counsel for Mrs. Brawner, of 2342 Pomeroy road S.E., who is mother of nine children, has contended that she should have been granted a hearing.

Also awaited from the appellate court is an opinion on a wiretapping issue involving three men charged with gambling violations in the District.

A third opinion is expected from the appellate court soon in the case of Willie Lee Stewart, who is under a death sentence. Stewart, 36, was convicted for the third time in November, 1958 for the shooting of Washington grocer Harry Honikman during a holdup in the 700 block of East Capitol street in 1953.

Two previous convictions of Stewart have been set aside by appeals court.
