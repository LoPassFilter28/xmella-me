La Verita, Corriere del Connecticut · September 8, 19398 de septiembre de 1939

Original in English.Original en inglés.

Many Nationalities In CBS Studios

Across the Atlantic Germany is at war with Poland, England and France. Many other European nations fear they may be drawn into the conflict. Hate and terror are taking root abroad.

No such animosity or nervousness, however, is evident in CBS' own "Little League of Nations." The 17 members of this "League" comprise Columbia's foreign-language staff.

Working side by side, they translate foreign-language broadcasts from abroad for the benefit of CBS' nationwide audience on this side of the ocean and keep foreign listeners apprised of latest war developments with short-wave broadcasts sent out over Columbia's international stations W2XE and WCAB.

These are beamed in French, Italian, German, Polish, Spanish and Portuguese in addition to English. During the war crisis, W2XE and WCAB are on 24-hour schedules, the former directing its programs to Europe and the latter to Latin-America.

Pierre Bedard, Beverly Thurman and Albert Olivier are the three French experts of Columbia's staff. Notwithstanding Bedard's more Gallic-sounding name, Olivier is actually the only Frenchman of the trio. He was formerly French consul at Norfolk, Va.

Bedard and Thurman are both native Americans. The former, whose forbears came to Canada in 1666, was born in Lynn, Mass. He got his A.. at Harvard, lived in France for seven years and was interpreter for Gen. Bliss at the Versailles Treaty conference after the World War. He broadcasts a program over the French network every Wednesday from Columbia's New York studios, in addition to his work as CBS translator and French news-reporter. Bedard occupies a high position in Franco-American circles, being director of the French Institute of New York.

Thurman, who hails from Memphis, Tenn., brought a brilliant scholastic record in languages with him when he quit the Havas Agency's New York office to join CBS' staff three weeks ago. He studied at Williams and Mary College, North Carolina University and N. Y. U. before going abroad for further studies at the Sorbonne.

He taught French at North Carolina U. for six years. Thurman boasts B.A. and M.A. degrees, and has won many scholarships at school. In addition to French, Thurman also speaks German, Spanish and Italian. His first experience in radio was gained as a singer on a Norfolk station.

Columbia's German staff consists of two news-reporters and translators, Field Horine and Gary Culeman, and four men who monitor the programs sent out over German stations and picked up by CBS' short-wave receivers. The men who do the monitoring are Paul Vitali, who was born in Turkey; Henry Kranz, an Austrian, and Harry Wolfson and Karl Vaselow, Germans.

Culeman is a German, too, but Horine is an American, native of Louisville, Kentucky. Horine took up the study of German at Davidson College and later at Louisville University because he wanted to know what his father was talking about when German-speaking visitors came to the house. Horine, Sr., had picked up the language while studying medicine in Vienna.

The younger Horine became so proficient in the new tongue that he continued his studies at the Universities of Heidelberg and Bonn. Last year he joined the faculty at Columbia University Extension as an instructor in German, a position he still holds. He does the bulk of Columbia's German translating and newscasting, interpolating speeches by Hitler and other prominent Reich officials.

Mario Capelli, a native of Genoa, and Angelo Lanza di Trabia, who was born in Sicily, are CBS' Italian experts. Capelli came to this country in 1930 and worked in the Havas Agency's New York office before coming to Columbia a year ago. Lanza is a graduate of the University of Florence and the Sorbonne and holds a doctorate in the social and political sciences.

The Polish experts of Columbia's "Little League of Nations" are Jan Drohojowski and Alexander Maksel. Drohojowski was born in Tarnow, Poland, the town recently reported wiped out by German bombers. He has perhaps the longest international record of any member on CBS' foreign language staff — and one of the most brilliant scholastic records as well.

Drohojowski graduated from the University of Lausanne in Switzerland, taking first honors and a gold medal at the Ecole des Sciences Politiques and then studied at the Academy of International Law in The Hague. In 1917, he worked for the Polish Press Agency in Switzerland, and later spent several years with various Polish diplomatic missions in Paris, The Hague, Warsaw, Danzig, Rome and Berlin. He also managed Poland Magazine before coming to the United States in 1926 Drohojowski is now American correspondent for the Kurjer Warszawski, leading Polish daily, in addition to his CBS duties. Drohojowski speaks five languages besides Polish and English. They are German, French, Italian, Dutch and Spanish.

Maksel has been in newspaper work most of his life. Educated in Poland, he now is connected with the Polish Daily Courier and the Catholic Leader in New Britain, Conn.

During the war crisis, CBS' Spanish and Portuguese news-casters are working out of Philadelphia, where station WCAB is located. Alberto Zalamea, dean of the Latin-American experts, Amerigo Lugo Romero and Adrian Rubio are the Spanish language news-reporters. Luiz Lopez Correa sends out the Portuguese language broadcasts to Latin America.

Zalamea brought a splendid background of Latin American newspaper work when he joined CBS' staff in March, 1937. He was night editor of the United Press' foreing service and before that was for three years cable editor and translator for "El Tiempo" of Bogota, principal Colombia newspaper. He is a graduate of Jesuits College in Bogota and traveled extensively in South America as salesman for American and British commercial houses before becoming a journalist.

Romero was born in Puerto Plata in the Dominican Republic but was educated almost entirely in Europe, studying at the College Rollin, Sainte Barbe College and Schola Cantorum in Paris. He learned to speak and write French, German and English as fluently as Spanish and to write Italian. Later he worked for many South American newspapers and magazines.

Rubio is from Cuba. He teaches languages at the Berlitz Schools in Philadelphia and is particularly noted in Spanish-speaking circles as a sports expert.

Correa was born in Sao Paulo, Brazil, and educated at the Gymnasio de Carmo in that city. He served as editor of the Portuguese edition of the American Exporter from 1928 to 1931 and then became motion picture translator for the RKO and Metro-Goldwyn Mayer studios before joining Columbia in 1934.
