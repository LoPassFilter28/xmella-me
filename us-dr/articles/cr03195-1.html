Richmond Dispatch. Richmond, Va., August 5, 1899. Page 1. View the originalVer el original

The Dispatch prints part of an interview with General Jiminez and an item headed 'Funeral of Heureaux': 'I did hear,' he continued, 'that Heureaux was sending men to Cuba to kill me, but I knew nothing whatever regarding the plot to kill him. I had no connection with Ramon Caceres, who did the shooting. I do not believe that the United States will interfere with my government in San Domingo, which will be a government peaceful and helpful to all, for the United States did not interfere with the government of Heureaux, who robbed and murdered for fourteen years.' General Jiminez admits that he has often been to the United States incognito, but says he has not had interviews with official persons; he considers that there is no need of promoting expeditions to land in San Domingo, as there are already a thousand men waiting for him, a force which he deems quite sufficient for his purpose; referring to the future relations of San Domingo with the United States, he expressed a desire to be on friendly terms with Washington, and said he believed he could count upon American sympathy; Gomez he referred to as 'esteemed by all classes of Dominicans, but not sought for as President'; and the people of San Domingo, he declares, are impetuous, and are urging him to come to them at once, but for a time he prefers delay, as 'each day is worth a battle won.' The funeral item, from San Domingo, August 4, says solemn funeral services in memory of the late President Heureaux were held in the Cathedral to-day, and the diplomatic and consular bodies were present.El Dispatch publica parte de una entrevista con el general Jiminez y una nota titulada 'Funeral of Heureaux': 'Sí oí', continuó, 'que Heureaux enviaba hombres a Cuba para matarme, pero no supe absolutamente nada del complot para matarlo a él. No tuve ninguna relación con Ramón Cáceres, que hizo los disparos. No creo que los Estados Unidos vayan a interferir con mi gobierno en San Domingo, que será un gobierno pacífico y útil para todos, porque los Estados Unidos no interfirieron con el gobierno de Heureaux, que robó y asesinó durante catorce años'. El general Jiminez admite que ha estado a menudo en los Estados Unidos de incógnito, pero dice que no ha tenido entrevistas con personas oficiales; considera que no hay necesidad de promover expediciones para desembarcar en San Domingo, ya que ya hay mil hombres esperándolo, una fuerza que juzga bastante suficiente para su propósito; al referirse a las relaciones futuras de San Domingo con los Estados Unidos, expresó su deseo de mantener relaciones amistosas con Washington y dijo que creía poder contar con la simpatía americana; de Gómez se refirió como 'estimado por todas las clases de dominicanos, pero no buscado como presidente'; y el pueblo de San Domingo, declara, es impetuoso y lo insta a ir junto a ellos de inmediato, pero por un tiempo prefiere la demora, ya que 'cada día vale una batalla ganada'. La nota fúnebre, desde San Domingo, el 4 de agosto, dice que hoy se celebraron en la Catedral solemnes servicios fúnebres en memoria del difunto presidente Heureaux, y que estuvieron presentes los cuerpos diplomático y consular.
