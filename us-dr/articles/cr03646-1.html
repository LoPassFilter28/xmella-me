PARLEY WILL SEEK GRAN CHACO PEACE

Pan-American Delegates Ready for Conference Opening Today.

By the Associated Press.

MONTEVIDEO, December 2. — A spirit of good will and willingness to cooperate prevailed tonight among the 100 delegates from North, Central and South American nations, who tomorrow, will begin the Pan-American Conference on economic and peace questions.

Among the subjects expected to be taken up are the Gran Chaco war between Bolivia and Paraguay and the monetary and debt situations.

Two suggestions have already been offered regarding the months-long Chaco struggle: One by Foreign Secretary Puig Casauranc of Mexico, that Bolivia and Paraguay declare a truce for the period of the conference, and the other by the American Secretary of State, Cordell Hull, that all delegations work on behalf of a settlement.

The foreign ministers of Chile, Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Nicaragua, Guatemala, Panama and Uruguay are also personally in attendance.

Montevideo newspapers did not appear today because printers and newsboys declared a 24-hour strike in sympathy with 40 Communists, who were deported for agitating in favor of a strike against the parley.

Communists Hit Hull.

A number of Communist pamphlets were sold on the street today. They attacked the deportations and the conferees, including Secretary Hull. The public, however, seemed apathetic regarding the agitation.

The Cuban delegation told the Associated Press that Cuba's program would be based on these policies: The integral independence of all American countries and freedom from intervention or admonition; absolute freedom economically, and support for "the revolution of these times."

An intra-American commission of women laid plans for conference action in the direction of equality, social and political rights and uniform legislation regarding nationality.

For the first time women are delegates to a Pan-American gathering. Sophronisba Breckenridge is an American representative and Senora Vignole Demichelli is a delegate from Uruguay.

To Seek Place.

It was understood that Paraguay will oppose any Chaco truce without guarantees of arbitration in the entire case. Bolivian delegates have made no public announcement of their stand.

The peace theme was generally expected to be advanced by efforts seeking additional signatories to a non-aggression and anti-war pact recently signed by Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Uruguay and Paraguay.

Possibilities regarding economic projects were said to center apparently in the American and Mexican delegations.

The latter group has been active in promoting monetary and debt proposals envisaging the linking of gold and silver in a modified bi-metallic project, details of which were not given out.

It was understood the United States delegation was prepared to advance economic proposals along different lines.

Costa Rica, the only one of 21 Pan-American nations so far not represented by delegates, decided at the last minute to send a representative who will arrive by airplane at 5 p.m. tomorrow, an hour before the conference is scheduled to open.

Although several participants are known to seek the presentation of particular problems to the conference, there was increasing confidence tonight among leading delegations that the agenda will be confined to broad subjects applicable to all countries represented, with emphasis on economic matters.

Charging that for "18 years Haiti has been supporting unjustifiable intervention by the United States," Pierre Paul, delegate to the conference, said today Haiti's presence was not justified unless an obligatory principle of non-intervention is adopted.

In Haiti, Pierre Paul said, the United States "without reason has occupied territory, seized customs and taken control of the army and finances."

Haiti Paid Debts.

The United States is withdrawing Marines from Haiti in October, 1934, but will retain control of customs for 11 years.

When European nations demanded the United States drastically reduce their debts and many repudiated them, the delegate said, Haiti faithfully fulfilled its obligations although virtually all families were the hardest hit of any country by the depression.

"The entire country is succumbing under the weight of new taxes imposed by the imperialist policy of the United States," he continued. "Our view is that financial control, if not the most violent, is the most humiliating and vexatious form of intervention.

"A financial dictatorship has no other motive than to protect private interests."

Pierre Paul said he would introduce a non-intervention resolution at the earliest possible time.

Members of the various delegations exchanged informal calls throughout today. The Mexican foreign minister, Puig Casauranc, paid a long call on Secretary of State Hull. He was followed by a delegation from Haiti, then a Paraguayan emissary.

The head of the Haitian delegation, Pierre Paul, who earlier announced that Haiti would offer to the conference a resolution establishing the principle of non-intervention, said after his call that he and Secretary Hull had discussed United States financial control in Haiti. He said Hull had indicated the Washington administration was disposed to deal in a neighborly spirit, but that he had made no promises, for which reason, he said, the Haitians would continue disposed to present the resolution to the conference.

Secretary Hull visited the heads of delegations from Argentina, Nicaragua, Paraguay, Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Honduras and the Dominican Republic.
