Hotel 'Bugging' Probe Ends With 5 Indicted

By JACK LANDAU

Star Staff Writer

After 14 months of secret sessions which yielded five indictments, the United States Attorney's Office has announced the end of the secret grand jury investigation into multi-million dollar commercial espionage at the Mayflower Hotel.

As the grand jury wove its investigative web, it came up with a well-known retired police inspector, a veteran police detective, two private investigators; and a soldier of fortune previously implicated with the Trujillo dictatorship in the Dominican Republic.

The special grand jury was hearing testimony on who was behind the electrical "bugging" of the Mayflower Hotel suite of a San Francisco lawyer, Malcolm T. Dungan. Mr. Dungan represented the El Paso Natural Gas Co. in proceedings before the Federal Power Commission in March 1962, for a multi-million-dollar franchise to pipe natural gas into the rich Southern California market.

The bizarre affair started the night of March 31 when Mr. Dungan was having a supposedly secret conversation.

Mr. Dungan called a private detective who searched his suite and found a 4-inch transmitter taped to the bottom of a coffee table.

The next day, Mr. Dungan obtained an anti-bugging injunction from Judge George H. Hart of the United States District Court.

Marshals and detectives broke into the next door suite and discovered a man Oliver W. Angelone — an unidentified blond and a small FM receiver.

Angelone is a private detective of the 4000 block of MacArthur Boulevard N.W.

The room was registered under the name of John J. Frank, a soldier-of-fortune attorney.

Also implicated was another private detective, John W. Leon.

Mr. Dungan started a civil suit against the trio, but they took the Fifth Amendment rather than answer questions at pre-trial depositions.

Detective Testifies

Wayne G. Barber, a private detective of the 2700 block of Nicholson street, West Hyattsville, Md., was given immunity to testify before the grand jury.

Mr. Barber's testimony led to the first indictments on November 14, 1962. They were against Police Inspector Joseph W. Shimon, 56, who had retired the previous July after 33 years of service with Washington police and Detective John A. Jones, 50, who had retired after 21 years.

The two policemen were charged with conspiring to obstruct justice in attempting to influence Mr. Barber to change his story to the grand jury.

In February, three more indictments were issued this time against Angelone, Frank and Leon. All three were charged with housebreaking into Mr. Dungan's suite and with violating the Federal Communication Commission's requirements of obtaining a license prior to broadcasting from an FM set.

The trial of the five indicted persons is scheduled for the fall.
