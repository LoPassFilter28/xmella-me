Dominican Legation Scene Yesterday Of Large Reception Gen. Rafael Trujillo Left City Last Night After Being Feted

Gen. Rafael Trujillo, former President of the Dominican Republic, left last evening for New York, to sail next week for France, where he will join Senora de Trujillo and see for the first time their baby daughter, born only a few weeks ago in Paris. The general left immediately after the delightful reception which the Dominican Minister and Senora de Pastoriza gave as a farewell courtesy for their distinguished visitor.

The Dominican Legation, where the fete was held, was a delightful setting for a midsummer party, the hostess having turned the place into a veritable summer garden with tall vases and enormous baskets holding flame color and white gladioli, or pink roses and blue delphinium, or bunches of salmon color gladioli, and with smaller bowls of other flowers in every available spot. The garden itself was opened for the use of the guests, with chairs and tables being set out in cool spots.

Senora de Pastoriza wore a cool chiffon gown of aquamarine color, with a shirred bodice and long full skirt, and a wine color kid belt to add that necessary touch of contrast. Her corsage was of white orchids touched with maroon on the tips.

Lilies and Roses Form Table Centerpiece.

In the dining room the tea table was lovely with a centerpiece of white lilies and red roses, and the cakes, which added greatly to the attractiveness of the table, were iced in the Dominican colors of red, white, blue and green.

Senora Brea Messena, daughter of Gen. Trujillo, was among the guests. She flew from Santo Domingo Wednesday with her husband and last evening went with her father to New York, where she will remain after Gen. Trujillo sails and will visit the fair.

In the group which gathered yesterday to bid farewell to the former Dominican President were quite a number of notables, despite the inroads which summer absences have made upon Washington society.

Secretary and Mrs. Hull Among the Guests.

The Secretary of State, Mr. Cordell Hull, was present, accompanied by Mrs. Hull, who wore a black dress with a red, green and white design and a black straw hat trimmed with green ribbon. Many from the congressional circle were there, including Mrs. Arthur H. Vandenberg, Mrs. William E. Borah, Mrs. Claude Pepper, Mrs. Pete Jarman and Mrs. Luther Johnson.

A great number of the diplomats called during the afternoon, including the Ambassador of Ecuador, Capt. Colon Eloy Alfaro; the Lithuanian Minister, Mr. Povilas Zadeikis; the Chadge d'Affaires of Germany and Frau Thomsen, the Counselor of the Mexican Embassy and Senora de Fuentes, the Cuban Counselor and Senora de Valdes Rodrigues, the Minister-Counselor of Cuba, Dr. Jose Baron; the Minister of Honduras and Senora de Caceres and the Finnish Minister, Mr. Hjalmar Procope.

The Commandant of the Marine Corps and Mrs. Thomas Holcomb were there, as were Mr. and Mrs. Warren Pierson, Maj. Parker West, Dr. and Mrs. Frederick Benton, the Counselor of the State Department, Mr. R. Walton Moore, and his two sisters, the Misses Jennie and Margaret Moore; Mrs. Elsa Lopez McGuire, Dr. Oscar Albertini, Mrs. George Oakley Totten, Gen. and Mrs. Calvin Matthews and Dr. and Mrs. George Trible.

Before the reception began Gen. Trujillo held a press conference at the Legation and chatted informally with reporters until the formal reception began.
