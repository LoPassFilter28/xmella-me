LBJ's Junket

State Department officials were mum about it, but they were worried about Vice President Johnson's trip to the Dominican Republic. There had been a lot of bitterness in that dictator-ruled country over the manner in which junketing congressmen and members of the Eisenhower administration had scratched Trujillo's back and fed out of his hand.

LBJ's flight to the Dominican Republic for President Bosch's inauguration was the first trip since the stream of congressmen who came at Trujillo's expense. So State Department officials crossed their fingers for fear of demonstrations.

However, here is the restricted cablegram received by the State Department from the U. S. Embassy in the Dominican Republic:

"Embassy considers Vice President's visit outstanding success both from point of view public relations and impact on new GODR (Government of Dominican Republic) reception accorded him by crowds of ordinary Dominicans and his response thereto constituted in Embassy's opinion a triumph for U. S. in Dom. Rep. (Dominican Republic).

"Press made much of fact that Vice President embraced Bosch in the Latin American manner. . . President Bosch was pleased and obviously impressed by gift of ambulance presented by Vice President in name of American people to Dom. people . . . . Mrs. Johnson's visits to school for blind and maternity hospital were thoroughly covered by television and press . . .

"6 or 7 months ago would have been unlikely that Vice President of United States could drive through city without arousing demonstration of hostility. Johnson's unaffected mixing with crowds caused strong and favorable impact."
