Dr. Bonnelly Confident

Of ‘True Democracy’

SANTO DOMINGO, Jan. 20 (AP).—Dr. Rafael Bonnelly, scholar, lawyer and diplomat, is confident he can guide his country along the path of democracy.

The 57-year-old Bonnelly took over as chairman of the ruling Council of State Thursday night after a whirlwind sequence of events that led to the resignation of President Joaquin Balaguer. But Dr. Bonnelly had been slated for the job anyway and the week’s upheavals only served to advance the date.

The trim, erect Dr. Bonnelly —of Spanish, Italian and French ancestry — considers himself a “liberal in the truly democratic sense.” He says he is opposed to extremism of either right or left.

Dr. Bonnelly brings to the chairmanship of the council, which is to prepare the nation for general elections later this year, a profound knowledge of his country’s history—some of which he learned from inside the government.

Long in Government

The late Dominican dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, exerted various forms of economic pressure to bring the country’s influential citizens into his service. For the first 10 years of the Trujillo regime, Dr. Bonnelly stayed clear of any association with the government, but in the 1940s he joined the official family.

Among the high posts he held were Secretary of the Interior, Ambassador to Spain and to Venezuela. He was also rector of Santo Domingo University, a job in which he still takes pride since it gave him a chance to teach and direct what Dominicans consider the hemisphere’s oldest institution of learning.

During this career, Dr. Bonnelly says he was able to avoid identification with the regime except in a purely professional capacity.

His family ran into trouble with the Trujillo dynasty in January, 1960, when Dr. Bonnelly’s youngest son, Rafael Francisco, was among hundreds of youths arrested as a result of an anti-government plot.

The elder Bonnelly himself spent the next 17 months under house arrest at his son-in-law’s home—with a guard posted outside. But the time was not wasted. The house had an ample library, and Dr. Bonnelly studied history and political science.

Civic Union Leader

After Gen. Trujillo’s assassination in May of last year, Dr. Bonnelly became one of the founders of the National Civic Union, the biggest of the opposition parties campaigning for a democratic government. His son joined the 14th of June group, a movement which drew much support from youths who had suffered physically under Gen. Trujillo.

Born in Santiago, Dr. Bonnelly has three other children, a son and two married daughters. His wife, Aida, was wounded slightly last fall when police hurled noise bombs at demonstrators. His father, a merchant, served a number of terms as Mayor of Santiago.

Dr. Bonnelly is optimistic that the council will be able to pave the way for democratic rule.

“I believe,” he says, “that with good will, unselfishness and authentic patriotic spirit the government will carry out the transitional job of establishing a government of law.”
