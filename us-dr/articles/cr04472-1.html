Southern Christian Advocate. Charleston, S.C., March 20, 1846. Page 3. View the originalVer el original

Based on accounts from St. Domingo up to February 10, Dominicans were courageously preparing for an expected attack by the Haytiens amid reports of various regional conflicts and naval incidents, including the protection of a Dominican vessel by the English governor at Turk's Islands. Additionally, it was believed in St. Domingo that the United States government had recognized the Dominican republic and that a Spanish squadron from Cuba was coming to protect the Dominicans against the Haytiens.Con arreglo a noticias procedentes de Santo Domingo que alcanzan hasta el 10 de febrero, los dominicanos se disponían con denuedo a repeler una previsible acometida de los haitianos, en medio de informes de diversas pugnas regionales e incidentes marítimos, incluyendo el amparo dispensado a un buque dominicano por el gobernador inglés en las islas Turcas. Circulaba asimismo en Santo Domingo la creencia de que el gobierno de Estados Unidos había formalizado el reconocimiento de la República Dominicana y que una escuadra española despachada desde Cuba acudiría a proteger a los dominicanos frente a los haitianos.

Here we have accounts from St. Domingo, the city, to the 10th of February. The Dominicans were expecting the attack of the Haytiens, with good courage and preparations. There was a report current that Acaau had been moving again and had massacred some mulattoes at the Anse d' Hainault. Also that a Dominican vessel, the Trinidad, had been chased to Turk's Islands by the French man-of-war Virginie, but that the English Governor of the islands had given her protection. Also, that a Haytien war schooner had been wrecked in going from Turk's Islands to Cape Haytien.Aquí tenemos noticias de Santo Domingo, la ciudad, hasta el 10 de febrero. Los dominicanos esperaban el ataque de los haitianos, con buen valor y preparativos. Corría el informe de que Acaau había estado actuando de nuevo.

It was believed at St. Domingo that the U. S. Government had recognized the Dominican republic, and that a U. S. ship of war, with a consul on board, would soon arrive at St. Domingo.Se creía en Santo Domingo que el Gobierno de los Estados Unidos había reconocido la república dominicana, y que un buque de guerra estadounidense, con un cónsul a bordo, pronto llegaría a Santo Domingo.

The story prevailed, also, that a Spanish squadron from Cuba was coming to protect the Dominicans against the Haytiens.Prevalecía también la historia de que una escuadra española de Cuba venía a proteger a los dominicanos contra los haitianos.
