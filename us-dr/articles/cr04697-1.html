Pine in Santo Domingo. Nearly as Good as the Long-Leaf Southern Variety.

The pine in the Dominican Republic is said to be at least as good as the long leaf yellow pine of the Southern States. There is a pine forest estimated to contain about 4,000,000 feet in the central part of the republic. The timber extends from about eight miles to the southwest of San Jose de Las Matas to a short distance east and southwest of Jarabacoa. The main pine forest is from five to twenty miles long. There are, however, scattering pines to the west of San Jose de Las Matas as far as the Haitian border, and also to the east of Jarabacea. San Jose de Las Matas is about twenty-five miles from Santiago de los Caballeros, which is connected by railroad with its port, Puerto Plata.

Jarabacea is about fifteen miles from La Vega, which is joined to its port Sanchez by railroad. There is not, however, a railroad into the pine belt, which would have to be built in order to get out the timber.

The pine there is a very sound, free from windshakes, and rich turpentine. The trees are straight, clean and hold their thickness well up to the first limbs. While the trees do not run very thick there are some forty-two inches in diameter. There are few other varieties of trees in the belt.
