18 Nations Ratify Pact On World Food Unit; Two More Needed

By the Associated Press.

The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization provided for at the Hot Springs (Va.) Conference of 1943 will come into being with the ratification of only two more nations, it was revealed today. Eighteen nations have approved the agreement. Twenty ratifications are required.

Ambassador L. B. Pearson of Canada, chairman of the United Nations interim commission on food and agriculture, said that acceptances of membership had been received from the following countries:

Australia, Belgium, China, Czechoslovakia, Dominican Republic, Egypt, Guatemala, Haiti, Honduras, Iceland, India, Mexico, the Netherlands, Nicaragua, Norway, Philippines, Poland and the United Kingdom.

Acceptance for Poland came from the Polish government in exile in London, Mr. Pearson told a questioner. He added: "This occurred last October."

The plan has not yet been submitted to Congress for approval, although President Roosevelt indicated in his February 2 message on the Bretton Woods proposals that it soon will be.

Mr. Pearson said action is "pending" in "a number of other countries."

All 44 United Nations participating in the Hot Springs Conference a year and a half ago have been represented on Mr. Pearson's interim commission.

The proposed constitution provides for the FAO to "become a part of any general world organization which may be established," Mr. Pearson declared.
