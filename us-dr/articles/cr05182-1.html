Crisis Alerts Armies Of Betancourt, Trujillo

CARACAS, Venezuela, June 19 (AP).—The bitterly opposed fighting forces of Venezuela and the Dominican Republic were reported on the alert today as the result of a new Caribbean crisis over the attempt on the life of Venezuelan President Romulo Betancourt last Friday.

Mr. Betancourt's government has accused the Dominican regime of Rafael Trujillo of aiding the assassins. Mr. Betancourt suffered burns and two persons were killed when a parked carload of dynamite exploded as the President rode by to a military review.

The Venezuelan Army yesterday was ordered to be "ready to defend the fatherland." Gen. Regulo Pacheco Vivas, chairman of the Venezuelan Joint Chiefs of Staff, conferred with military commanders at the Presidential Palace.

An alert was broadcast from Ciudad Trujillo to Dominican forces. The broadcast said authority to mobilize Dominican militiamen was being sought because a virtual state of war exists.

Caracas Claims Proof

Foreign Minister Ignacio Luis Arcaya told American diplomats last night Venezuela has proof the explosives used against Mr. Betancourt came from the Dominican Republic. Most of the authors and accomplices of the plot in Venezuela have been arrested, he said.

A large section of the Venezuelan press has been demanding war against Generalissimo Trujillo following the attempt on Mr. Betancourt's life. The President was reported making a rapid recovery from burns on his hands and face. Two persons were killed in the blast.

Mr. Betancourt and Generalissimo Trujillo long have been foes.

Story of Plot Told

Here is the Venezuelan version of the alleged conspiracy, as attributed to reliable sources:

In the Dominican Republic the leader was a former Navy captain, Eduardo Morales Luengo, who was retired and expelled from Venezuela five months ago for suspected complicity in a plot to overthrow Mr. Betancourt's year-old government.

He collected the electronic gear to be used to explode the dynamite charge in the path of Mr. Betancourt's car.

Eduardo's brother Angel was still on active service in the Venezuelan Navy. Last week Angel's wife went to Carlos Chevez, her brother-in-law, who owned the Ransa Airlines, which flies freight between Caracas and Miami. She asked the loan of a plane to take members of the family on a visit to Eduardo.

The airline operator was quoted as being reluctant because flights to the Dominican Republic have been prohibited since Venezuela broke relations with the island dictatorship last year. He finally consented, however, and the plane took off, ostensibly for Miami, with Angel as a passenger.

It flew to Ciudad Trujillo where it remained but a few hours, returning with both the Morales brothers and the implements to explode the dynamite.

The airline pilot, Juvenal Zabala, landed not at Maiquetia Airport, but, at an airstrip on a cattle ranch. Near Caracas the conspirators rehearsed by setting off dynamite blasts close to two automobiles in a stone quarry where blasting was the usual thing. The cars were blown upside down and the plotters were satisfied they were ready for the real thing.

The owner of the quarry, Manuel Vicente Yanes, borrowed an old car from a friend and drove it in the night to the spot Mr. Betancourt was to pass next day. He parked it with a flat tire to avert suspicion.
