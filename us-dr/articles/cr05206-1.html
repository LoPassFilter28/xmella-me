Original in English.Original en inglés.

U. S. MARINES TAKE BIG FORT AT HAITI Ready to Repel Rebels, Who Again Threaten Trouble—Santo Domingo Is Uneasy.

Port au Prince, Haiti, Aug. 6.—The regiment of marines from the battleship Connecticut have completed their occupation of the city and are in possession of the barracks at Dessalines. They have occupied the national fort which dominates the town without having to fire a single shot.

Americans Control Two Cities.

Washington, Aug. 6.—With American forces in occupation of the capital of Haiti, and also in possession of the second most important city, Cape Haitien, the situation in the negro republic is still most confused.

According to official advices received here, General Bobo, the rebel leader in Haiti, is now marching with considerable forces in the direction of Port au Prince.

All the northern region has been won by him to his support, and it seems certain that on arrival at Port au Prince he will demand the presidency.

On the other hand the administration has not yet made any decision as to what is to be done in Haiti.

Meantime, pending a decision as to Haiti, there are indications that the disturbances will spread across the island into Santo Domingo.

General Blot, who commanded the forces of the former government in the north, has started for Monte Cristi, Dominican republic. It is believed here that he will attempt to obtain arms and men in the Dominican republic in the hope of returning to Haiti and resuming the contest for control of the government.

Unrest in Santo Domingo.

Officials fear that the result of his expedition to Santo Domingo may be demoralizing. The political unrest in Santo Domingo is growing, and riots have broken out in some parts of the republic.

Six weeks ago there was a threat of trouble in Santo Domingo City, and the cruiser Nashville was sent there.

The disturbance apprehended did not occur at that time, but since then the Nashville has been withdrawn to take part in the operations in Haiti.

At present Santo Domingo is governed by a compromise government, set up through Washington influence, and all reports agree that the coalition is not a success.
