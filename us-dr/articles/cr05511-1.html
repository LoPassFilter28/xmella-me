Cafritzes Honor Lily Pons At Official-Studded Dance

By Betty Beale

Capital society, Republicans and Democrats alike, turned out in full-dress fashion last evening for the dance Mr. and Mrs. Morris Cafritz gave for Miss Lily Pons. Old and new Senators decked out in white tie and tails escorted their besatined and bejeweled wives to the brightly lighted Foxhall road residence of the noted Washington realtor and his wife.

British Ambassador and Lady Makins were among the new faces at the 10:30 affair, which drew Vice President Barkley and twoscore ambassadors and Senators. Mr. Barkley, minus his wife who is away, was the No. 1 guest at the Cafritz dinner preceding the dance. Others seated around the same table were the dean of the diplomatic corps, Norwegian Ambassador and Mme. Morgenstierne; Brazilian Ambassador Walther Moreira Salles, the chairman of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee and Mrs. Alexander Wiley and Senator and Mrs. William F. Knowland.

Arrive From Gotham

Miss Pons came down from New York for the gay party that climaxed a friendship that developed in St. Moritz last summer and continued in the fall when the Cafritzes were entertained by the Metropolitan Opera star in San Francisco. Miss Pons returned to New York today to give a benefit concert at Carnegie Hall this evening.

The colutura soprano will return to Washington to sing at the inaugural ball immediately following Lauritz Melchoir. She will sing the bell song from "Lakme."

A full-blooming mimosa tree and birds-of-paradise flowers decorated the scene that greeted the guests as they stepped across the Cafritz threshold last evening into the foyer of the modern dwelling. Elsewhere on the first floor—in the dining room and drawing rooms—shades of pink, red and white flowers added color to the setting and complemented the gowns worn by the hostess and honor guest. Pink camellias cascaded from the mantel and vases were filled with antherium or red roses or white calla lilies.

Red Velvet; White Satin

Miss Pons wore a dramatically styled red velvet gown that formed a striking contrast, as she stood beside her hostess to receive, with the white satin, ermine-trimmed sheathlike Balenciaga dress of Gwen Cafritz.

In the ballroom downstairs where Sidney's red-coated orchestra held forth until the small hours, gardenias and other white flowers, white balloons and greens formed a background for the dancing couples. A three-piece orchestra played in the adjoining spacious bar where guests relaxed at tables in the miniature "night club" setting.

Glimpsed in the official-studded throng were four bachelors from the upper house—Senators Theodore F. Green, John Sherman Cooper, Jack Kennedy and "Scoop" Jackson; also Senator and Mrs. James Duff, Senator and Mrs. Mike Monroney, Senator and Mrs. Robert Hendrickson, Senator and Mrs. Russell Long, and Senator and Mrs. Clinton Anderson.

Malone's Dinner

The President Pro Tem of the Senate and Mrs. Styles Bridges and the new Senator from Arizona, Barry Goldwater, and his wife were with the group that were entertained at dinner beforehand by Senator and Mrs. George Malone, Held at the 1925 F Street Club, their party also included Senator and Mrs. Bourke Hickenlooper, Mrs. Robert R. McCormick, Mr. C. E. Tuttle of Baltimore, Mr. and Mrs. Garvin E. Tankersley, Mr. and Mrs. Oliver Presbrey, Mr. and Mrs. William Randolph Hearst, jr., Mr. and Mrs. Lawrence Spivak and Mrs. Cobina Wright, who has arrived from Beverly Hills to remain until after inauguration.

Mr. Herbert May of Pittsburgh was host at a dinner at the F Street Club, and others who gave parties preceding the dance were Mr. and Mrs. Cornelius Bretsch, Mrs. Rahel Davies Fitch, Mrs. Harold Coolidge, Mr. John Logan, Mrs. Richard Cowell, and Mr. and Mrs. Charles Farrell who came down from New York for the party.

Former Officials Present

Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo of the Dominican Republic and Senora de Trujillo arrived with the Dominican Ambassador and Senora de Thomen, and others mingling were former Ambassador and Mrs. George Garrett, Mr. and Mrs. Corrin Strong, Mr. and Mrs. Clark Clifford, the Swedish Ambassador and Mme. Boheman, former Ambassador and Mrs. Joseph Davies, former Senator and Mrs. Millard Tydings, the Peruvian Ambassador and Senora de Berckemeyer, Lt. Gen. and Mrs. Anthony McAuliffe, the Danish Ambassador and Mme. de Kauffmann, the Turkish Ambassador and Mme. Erkin, the Philippine Ambassador and Mrs. Romulo, the Spanish Ambassador and Senora de Lequerica and the Netherlands Ambassador and Mme. van Roijen who had come from Mr. and Mrs. Eugene O'Dunne's dinner for the departing Swedish Minister and Mme. Bagge.

Mr. and Mrs. Russell Havenstrite of Beverly Hills, Calif., flew here in their own plane in time for last night's dance and will remain through inauguration. And numerous other out-of-towners were present and being introduced to Capital celebrities.
