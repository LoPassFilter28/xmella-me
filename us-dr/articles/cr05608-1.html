Trujillo Departs, Promising Great Things for Republic

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

Well, our dictator visitor, Gen. Rafael Trujillo, who has been touring in this country, off and on, since early July, slipped away rather quietly yesterday. Bound for Miami, the general will board his yacht there and cruise to Havana, pick up his wife and family, and thence go to Trujillo City, where, no doubt, a riotous and joyous welcome awaits him.

As you know, the general had never ventured from his native island before. But once away, he didn't miss a thing.

Yesterday, before attending a huge luncheon at the Carlton, Trujillo held a press conference and got a lot of things off his chest.

Most interesting to Washington is the announcement that the Dominican Legation will soon have a residence all its own. Senor and Senora de Pastoriza have been living in a big apartment, but the general decided at first sight he wanted something better for his envoy. So-o-o—within the next few months building will start on Massachusetts-av, where an advantageous property has been purchased. It's not far from the new Venezuelan Embassy, right in the heart of "embassy row."

PLANS RACING PLANT

Next the general announced that during his recent trip to Kentucky, where he visited at the governor's mansion with Mrs. "Happy" Chandler, wife of Kentucky's new senator, and a gay house party, his idea to build a race track in Trujillo City ripened. He attended races at Lexington, looked over hundreds of fine horses, and the outcome will be a course and Jockey Club which, he says, will rival the finest in the Caribbean countries.

The general looks very well since his sojourn at the Mayo Clinic. Yesterday he wore a light brown suit, natty striped shirt of beige and brown, and a foulard tie to match. He's rather shy with the press, but with the aid of an interpreter, he answered all questions. Yes, the race track will be built with Dominican money and will be run by the government—like most things in that country. No, he didn't buy any horses here. He has a breed on the island which he hopes may work out as race horses, and he hopes that private owners will bring their strings to his track.

NEW HOTEL, TOO

There's going to be a new and modern hotel at Trujillo City, and since the harbor has been dredged from 14 feet to 35, cruise ships will now include Trujillo City as a port of call on Caribbean trips.

When the tourist trade down there begins to boom, I can personally tell you it's a fine place for a vacation.

The general further elaborated on his refugee plans which were outlined last year. Refugees will not have to pay $500, a sum established by the Dominican law of immigration. Because they will not be immigrants. They will be considered as refugees working on colonization plans. In accordance with plans which are being worked out by the Committee for Refugees in New York, 500 families will be admitted to the Dominican Republic.

And for two years the general will be personally responsible for their upkeep and education. After that they should have a good start and be able to support themselves.

CONCERNING WAR RUMORS

And, finally, the general got around to answering all those rumors about Dominican ships aiding and refueling German submarines. And, because I recently picked on the Dominican government about that same thing, I'll quote the general: "I consider that the statements made in this regard by Secretary of State Hull, as well as the categoric reports made by the French Legation and Santo Domingo and the British Admiralty, are ample denials of the charge. Furthermore, they all expressed their complete satisfaction with the manner in which the Dominican government maintains its neutrality.

"Our government was the first to request the co-operation of the United States for the patrol of the surrounding seas."
