Story of a Massacre

NOTE: The United Press on Nov. 10 carried an exclusive copyrighted dispatch containing the first authoritative account from an American neutral source on the Haitian side of the border of the killing of Haitian nationals on Dominican soil early in October. Today the United Press is able to present another exclusive account of these killings obtained from American and neutral sources on the Dominican side of the border. For obvious reasons the identity of all informants has been withheld but all references made in this dispatch were taken from original communications, the authenticity of which is beyond question.

By LOUIS JAY HEATH

Copyright, 1937, by United Press

On three successive nights in early October bands of Haitian men, women and children were herded down to the sea in the Dominican Republic, knocked on the head and killed by Dominican soldiers and police.

These killings, characterized by Haitian officials as "mass murders," caused the Haitian government on Nov. 12 to request the governments of Cuba, Mexico and the United States to tender good offices in the interests of continental peace.

Dominican President Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo has not accepted the tender. Instead he has designated special envoys to all three capitals. Dr. Manuel de Jesus Troncoso de la Concha, special Dominican envoy to Washington, is due here today for conferences with State Department officials.

PRESIDENT IN AREA

For the first time in these reports the name of Gen. Rafael Trujillo was brought prominently into accounts of the alleged mass slayings.

Two reports state that the Dominican President spent the night of Oct. 2 in the neighborhood where the first of the wholesale killings occurred. They substantiate statements by Americans in Haiti that over 5000 Haitians were slaughtered on successive days and nights.

The reports assert that the killings were not border clashes but occurred over a period of several days, well within Dominican territory and extended over a wide area.

HERDED TO BORDER

The first of several communications written from the Dominican Republic under date of Oct. 16, 1937, states in part:

"While on a trip to the Cibao District, the Chief Executive (President Trujillo) attended a function given in his honor and during a dance was informed of certain cattle stealing by Haitians from Dominican ranches. This prompted a strong speech in which it was indicated that drastic steps would be taken to stop it.

"We have been told that during the past week Haitian families were visited by members of the Dominican National Police and the army for the purpose of serving them deportation orders. It was understood that these Haitians were herded to the border by hundreds and whether they refused the demand to travel quickly or whether there was cause to put them out of the way we have been unable to ascertain.

LOOKED LIKE A BATTLE FIELD

"From authentic sources the dead reported to have been seen is a large number and reaches as high as 1300 including men women and children.

"Travelers who were in the District of Dajabon say that it was a terrible sight and that in places it looked like a battlefield and that particularly in the vicinity of Dajabon and north as far as Monte Cristi. Haitians have also been reported as having been killed at San Francisco de Macoris and Santiago and families have disappeared.

SYSTEMATIC CAMPAIGN

Another report dated Oct. 11 from another American on the Dominican side of the border and based on personal conversations with an American who had visited Dajabon and Monte Cristi stated flatly:

"Apparently with the approval of President Trujillo, a systematic campaign of extermination was directed against all Haitian residents in an area from some 30 kilometers south of Dajabon to Monte Cristi. The drive was conducted with ruthless efficiency by the national police and army. The technique used is of special importance in that it was designed to give the Dominican government an opportunity to disclaim all responsibility for the killings.
