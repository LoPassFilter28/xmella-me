Original in English.Original en inglés.

Though personally in favor of annexation, he had agreed to suspend the judgment until the report was made. It had been thought best to dismiss the matter from politics until that report was made; but this course had not been followed by some, though he had declared for one his willingness to drop the subject if the Commissioners' report was unfavorable. Certain men had kept the question before the country, and presented it constantly in an exaggerated and distorted form, as if to anticipate the report, and 'prejudice the public mind against it. Whatever might be its character, good taste and propriety required that the Senator from Massachusetts should have deferred his speech until the report was made. While he did not know anything of its character, he (Morton) felt confident that many of the charges against the President, and a large part of the Senator's speech, would be answered by the report, aud that the vindication of the President. upon all the charges he made, would be complete upon the statement of all the facts. We had heard to-day of a war made by the usurpation of Presidential power, but after all it was the most peaceful war ever heard of. Not a gun had been fired nor an act of violence committed upon a single Haytien, so far as the evidence went, and there was no evidence of the slightest complaint by Hayti. Not one word of complaint had ever come from that country, and it had remained for the Senator from Massachusetts to bring in a complaint.

Mr. Morton then had read by the Secretary a dispatch to United States Minister Bassett, from the Haytien Secretary of Foreign Affairs, assuring him that Hayti had no disposition or desire to interfere with the free will of the Dominicans. As a further reply to Mr. Sumner, he reminded the Senator, as evidence, that the Administration had not propsed to take any immediate action upon annexation; that Commission had no authority to negotiate a treaty, and that no one went with them who could take a single tep in that direction. The Haytien Government, in violation of the law of nations, had given free passage across its territory to Cabral, and had furnished him with war vessels to attack Dominica, and the United States had merely notified, Hayti that while our negotiations were pending with Dominica we would not allow him to make war on the island. This the United States had an undoubted right to do, because our Government had information of organizations on foot in Hayti to defeat annexation. No one need tell him (Morton) that the Senator from Massachusetts was a friend of liberty or the colored man when he so industriously spent his time in an effort to put the Democratic party in power, for to destroy the President is to destroy the great party of which he is the head (applause), and we know the terrible effect upon the liberties of this country if the Democratic party comes into power.
