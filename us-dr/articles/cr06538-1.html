Robinson, Bankhead, Campanella Look Good With Dodgers, Royals

CUIDAD TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic — (NNPA) — There are strange doings in the Brooklyn Dodgers' training camp, but they all point to Jackie Robinson becoming the regular second baseman and Roy Campanella of the Montreal Royals sharing the catching chores with Bruce Edwards.

When Branch Rickey, Dodger president, traded Eddie Stanky to the Boston Braves, Leo Durocher, Brooklyn Manager, named Eddie Miksis as his probable successor.

But Pete Reiser and Dick Whitman, outfielders, were tried out at first base, and Jackie was made a candidate for the keystone position, a base that he plays naturally.

To top it off, Campanella, the Montreal catcher who was reputed to be the best in the minor last season, was played in right field and at third base, and Dan Bankhead, the pitcher Rickey bought from the Memphis Red Sox, was played in the outfield and didn't do so bad.

By the time the club reaches Ebbets Field, however, it's a cinch that Jackie will be regularly installed at second and Campanella will be a member of the catching staff.

In the game last Tuesday against the Royals, which the Dodgers won, 13 to 4, for their fourth straight, Jackie batted in the lead off spot and refuted the charge that he isn't hustling.

COLLECTS TWO HITS

Up twice in the first inning, he beat out a hit to short to start the Dodger rally and singled o right the second time.

Jackie had been told by Durocher that he was acting as if he had his first base job cinched.

"I didn't know I was acting that way," said Robinson. "If that's the way I looked, I'm glad Durocher spoke to me about it."

Campanella made one of the four hits the Royals scored. In the last inning he walked, stole second and scored on a single. He caught his second straight game but played third base from the fifth inning to the end.

Durocher took public notice of Jackie's overweight at morning practice on March 16. He had Robinson work out at second base and sent him to the outfield to snag flies.

"I've never worked harder to get down to playing weight in my life," Jackie said.

When Jackie reported he was weighing 220. He's now down to 203. Robinson also has been plagued with a sore arm and blisters on his feet, but he apparently is rounding into condition.
