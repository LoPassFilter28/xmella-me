Pro-Trujillo Mobs Rout Bishop, Wreck Home

CIUDAD TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic, April 14 (AP).—Pro-Trujillo mobs have driven a Roman Catholic bishop to this capital after wrecking his Diocesan residence 100 miles west of here. Nuns and priests also are fleeing the district.

Bishop Thomas J. Reilly, 52, in trouble before with Dominican Dictator Rafael Trujillo, said police and soldiers put him in protective custody for a while yesterday after terrorists ransacked his home.

"I am going to deal with the government over these acts of terrorism," the prelate from Boston said after arriving in Ciudad Trujillo to take refuge in a Catholic school.

He said he will demand that the government guarantee the safety of priests and nuns in the Western part of the country, where his diocese of San Juan de la Manguana is located. Attacks on parishes there are forcing priests and nuns to come to the capital, the bishop said.

The mobs smashed doors and windows, destroyed furniture and records and battered three automobiles at the bishop's office and residence. Police and soldiers ended the attack.

"About all that was saved at my home were my sacred vestments," Bishop Reilly said. "No one can live there now."

The newspaper El Caribe said the attacks started after a priest got into a heated argument aver a pro-Trujillo business shutdown in San Juan de la Maguana. The shutdown was to protest "the machinations of the bishop against national sovereignty," El Caribe said.

The newspaper said pickets marched with banners proclaiming "We're with Trujillo" and "Down with Yankee terrorism." Two priests reportedly were attacked and their robes ripped off, but the bishop said he understood no one was seriously hurt.

The Trujillo-church dispute started last year with a pastoral letter in which the bishops urged Gen. Trujillo to ease what they called repressive measures. The hostility increased when the bishops rejected a Trujillo demand that the title "benefactor of the church" be added to the long list of titles he holds.

Bishop Reilly, a member of the Redemptorist Fathers, was consecrated a bishop in 1956. His 86-year-old mother and a sister live in Boston. His diocesan area is, like the entire Dominican Republic, about 90 per cent Catholic.

Aides Ousted as Aliens

In Washington, the National Catholic Welfare Conference said two aides of the bishop—Fathers John Schomber of Buffalo, N. Y., and John Kelly of Pittsburgh—have been ordered expelled from the Dominican Republic as undesirable aliens.

Bishop Reilly, whose brother Gerard D. Reilly is a Washington attorney, was assigned as an assistant pastor of the Annapolis church conducted by the Redemptorist Fathers in 1937 and 1938.

He had been a graduate student at Catholic University after his ordination, and was awarded a doctor of sacred theology degree in 1937.
