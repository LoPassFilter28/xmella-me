LET THE TRUTH BE KNOWN

THE DOMINICAN REPUBLIC IS MILITANTLY ANTI-COMMUNIST. IT IS NOT RULED BY THE USE OF TERROR AND SECRET POLICE. IT IS A PROSPEROUS NATION. ITS PEOPLE ARE PROUD AND PATRIOTIC.

DECLARATION

On the part of foreign newspapermen residing in the Dominican Republic, in connection with the recent publication in the United States of misrepresentations against this country and against Generalissimo Doctor Rafael L. Trujillo Molina.

1) Foreword

THE PRESENT declaration, drawn up and signed by foreign newspapermen residing in the Dominican Republic, constituting a complete denial of misrepresentations published by Mr. Edward C. Burks in a recent series of three articles in "The New York Times," and to the wide publicity given by the United States press to the statements and accusations made by certain exiled Dominicans living in that country, is meant to constitute a description and explanation of the present situation in the Dominican Republic as we, impartial and fairminded observers, have been observing it for many years, and continue to observe it daily.

By so doing, we believe that we are fulfilling a moral duty, not only towards the Dominican Republic and its Government, but also to the people of the United States, so often deceived by their own reporters and by news coming from biased sources.

We believe, in fact, that many reports as to the truth about the Dominican Republic are mistaken, erroneous and improvised. It is physically impossible to become judges and censors, as many newspapermen do after short visits to a foreign country as to situations and conditions about which, in most cases, they are totally ignorant in regard to the historical roots and to the present implications and consequences of these, be they political, social or economic.

These gratuitous judges and censors, in our modest opinion, through their extravagant improvisations, greatly prejudice the situations they would remedy; they contribute in no small measure to destroying the already tottering though necessary unity of the Hemisphere and, what is still more unforgivable, they become the irresponsible and persistent promoters of ills, ruin and chaos.

Furthermore, we who have appended our signature to this declaration, wish to place on record that we are active anti-Communists and that not one of us, in principle, is anti-American. We have always understood the United States to be the bulwork of our common defense and of our common anti-Communist ideals and it is for this reason that we are so anxious for the great people of that country to know the truth.

2) Historic and economic synthesis

The Dominican Republic won its independence from Spain in 1865 but was again occupied by a foreign power, the United States, in 1916. In the course of those 51 years of independence, there were some fifty civil wars and other outbreaks of violence.

Until 1930, when Trujillo was elected to the Presidency through the unanimous and democratic vote of the people, the country had been sunk in misery and disorder; illiteracy reigned; public health was abandoned to its own devices; the national treasury was practically non-existent and was still having to bear the burden of a huge foreign debt. Business was poor. The system of agriculture was rudimentary. Industry was negligible. Most of the roads were no better than municipal pathways. Irrigation canals were primitive and few and far between.

Sickness, despair and poverty was the panorama and patrimony inherited by Trujillo in 1930 and these, with the strength and tenacity of the pioneer, he hastened to mold and transform without dismay, into the new Dominican reality.

In order to achieve this unprecedented miracle two factors were essential: peace and internal unity and the complete backing of all the people. Trujillo achieved these and has known how to preserve them. Had this not been the case, it would have been impossible for him to retain the leadership of his country for 30 years, nor would his people have bestowed upon him the outstanding titles of Benefactor of the Nation and Father of the New Nation. These are not titles that are given to a tyrant nor to anyone who tortures his people, as the yellow press would have it of Trujillo. In a country with no Iron, Bamboo or even Sugar Cane Curtain, a country which any foreigner may enter and leave without hindrance and in which he may circulate freely, such as the Dominican Republic, it is stupid to say that Trujillo has dominated his people through the use of terror and the secret police. It is stupid since it is absolutely contrary to the facts.

3) Disproofs

It is, of course, only natural that every government should have the right to defend itself, and it is its duty to do so for its loyal and law-abiding citizens, against its detractors when these have recourse, to demonstrate their opposition, to methods that might disturb public order and, what is still worse, to terrorism.

Every regime, every government in the world has its opponents. Why then should the Dominican government not have them?

The world daily press speaks of conflicts in practically every country in the world; from the United States to South Africa; from France to Korea. Conflicts of ideas, interests, attitudes, principles.

In practically all these cases, the Dominican press has maintained an exemplary attitude of dignity and moderation, avoiding that sensationalist dramatization which, when it refers to the Dominican government's opponents, the press of the United States, and principally The New York Times, delights in offering its readers.

From the point of view of statistics, supposing the Dominican government to have 1,500 opponents—this is the maximum figure mentioned in the United States press—this would mean one half of one per thousand of the inhabitants of the Republic, the total number of which is close to three millions.

This percentage, contrary to what the Dominican Republic's and Trujillo's detractors would have the people of the United States believe, constitutes dramatic proof of the universal backing and consent of the Dominican people which accompany all of Trujillo's works and achievements.

Frankly, we do not believe there is another government in the whole world which has such a small percentage of opponents and such an immense percentage of supporters.

In the series of articles alluded to, Mr. Edward C. Burks delights in speaking of the "many" secret police in the employ of the Dominican Government, as if there were any mystery in the fact that every civil government in the world employs secret police and government agencies for criminal and political investigation. In this connection, the United States has the privilege of being the creator of organizations, the "pattern" of which has served as a model for a number of other countries, even though some of the latter may not have attained the degree of efficiency of the former.

It is not our objective, point by point to refute the statements made by Mr. Burks in the series mentioned; nevertheless, and despite our intention not to enter into any controversy, we find ourselves unable to ignore one of the many contradictions he has fallen into, which is as follows:

Burks affirms on the one hand, that all our laborers and peasants revere the Chief and love him dearly because from him they have received protection, houses, land and adequate wages; that there is only a minority of malcontents with Trujillo's regime and that these are to be found among the professional classes. Then he goes on to ask himself, with pretended candor: "How can Trujillo be deposed with so many people in his favor?"

Now it is our turn to ask Mr. Burks, Who is it then that wants to depose Generalissimo Trujillo and why, if, as he himself says, Trujillo has done everything for the Dominican Republic and even his own detractors admit that—and these are Mr. Burks own words—there would be no fatherland without Trujillo.

In his anxiety to invent plots and intrigues after the style of Agatha Christie, Mr. Burks places in the mouths of eminent members of the clergy of the Dominican Republic, subtly insidious declarations which are frankly offensive to the dignitaries of the Catholic Church to whom he attributes them, to later affirm that the Church has been "indulged" and generously favored by Trujillo.

In effect, the Catholic, Apostolic and Roman Church, in the course of Trujillo's 30 years of power, apart from favors and protection, has received the sum of 30 million Pesos (a Peso is equal to one U.S. Dollar) for expenses from the government and from Trujillo's own pocket. Furthermore, it has benefited from exoneration of customs duties on a large number of its own imports, including automobiles.

In order properly to evaluate these 30 million Pesos, one must remember the economic and demographic aspects of the Dominican Republic whose national budget, in 1930, scarcely reached seven million Dollars and whose inhabitants, as we have mentioned earlier, total just under three millions at the present moment.

4) The present economic situation

As a comparison between the country's economic situation when Trujillo came to power, we are setting forth below certain of the present economic indexes of the Dominican Republic.

—Gross national production (the sum of the value of production and public services) is at present calculated in some $600 million per annum;

—Wholesale commercial sales (excluding exportable agricultural products) have been calculated over the past few years at an average of $300 million per annum;

—The sale of industrial products has continued to increase greatly year after year and the same has occurred with national production, every year more diversified, and to this must be added the greater purchasing power of the people;

—The value of exports, principally agricultural products, and every year more diversified although still led by sugar, has increased to a yearly average of close on $150 millions over the past three years;

—The value of exports, principally, agricultural licenses, has continued to spiral up to 1959, reaching approximately $140 millions during the last year.

—The inflationary index of the Dominican Peso is about the lowest in the world;

—More than 60% of the Dominican Republic's imports come from the United States, despite the fact that our best client for sugar at world market prices, is Great Britain.

To paraphrase a statement made by a foreign newspaperman who recently visited this country, we can say that if it were necessary to destroy everything done by Trujillo, throughout the whole of the Republic there would not remain standing one single house, hospital, efficient industry, bridge, kilometer of roadway, irrigation channel or anything else.

5) Trujillo's democracy

No one can doubt "a priori" Trujillo's sincere democratic nature nor his generosity. To make him out to be a man of implacable hatred is, to say the very least, puerile. Less than a month ago this "cruel tyrant" spontaneously freed the last survivors of the frustrated invasions of the Dominican Republic of June 1959; he received a number of the relatives of some of them in the National Palace and he even gave them considerable sums of money to help them to rebuild their lives.

There is not one single home, one single family in the whole of the Republic that has not received some favor or personal aid from Trujillo. This statement cannot be refuted even by the most ardent of the Dominican leader's detractors.

Here are a few other facts that reveal Trujillo's sincere democratic feelings:

—The campaign commenced in 1952 for complete literacy throughout the country. Since then, more than a half a million people have been taught to read and write, apart from the normal scholar population which at the present time is around 450 thousand;

—Free distribution of land, farm implements and seeds to every agricultural worker of poor financial means, throughout the country;

—The allocation of free housing, or housing with maximum purchase rates spread over 20 years, to thousands of employees of reduced financial means;

—The construction of healthy dwellings for poor agricultural workers, each with its individual title to the property;

—The establishment of minimum wage rates by Law which are obligatory in every business and industry throughout the country;

—Similarly, the State has proved itself to be the best "boss" in the Dominican Republic, paying its civil servants considerably higher salaries than those paid by private enterprises;

—The institution of an obligatory Social Insurance to cover every illness of the worker, be he laborer, agricultural worker or employee, his hospital treatment, be he an in or an out patient, and all necessary medicines;

—Pensions in respect of old age, inability to work and death in favor of workers and other families;

—The creation and establishment of medical centers and hospitals of which, thanks to their magnificent modern equipment and the ability of their medical staffs, the most advanced country would be proud.

In the strictly political field, Trujillo has just presented his resignation as a member and as the Supreme Leader of the Dominican Party, with the sole idea of allowing the creation and democratic functioning of other political parties with their diverse platforms. (One cannot allow a Mr. Burks to ridiculize and minimize an act of such political transcendency in this country, thereby casting doubt on the Dominican leader's sincerity.)

6) Conclusion

If what Mr. Burks says as to the enormous watch kept by a large number of secret police in the Dominican Republic, were true, the fact that he was allowed to circulate freely throughout the country, maintaining any number of contacts, obviously undisturbed, with numerous "enemies" of the regime; taking refuge with some of them even in an abandoned building; taking down their statements; carrying out a number of investigations of "check points"; copying out material and so on, can only be classified as a great show of presumably heroic reporting on his part.

We can even appreciate, for what it's worth, the fertility of Mr. Burks' imagination when, in the very few days he spent in the country, a country in which a life of serenity, peace and hard work is lived in a happy and progressive atmosphere, he was able to get to know about, and uncover, so many underground and secret items and situations: so secret in fact, that many of them might even be considered non-existent since those of us who live here, perhaps because we are consecrated to our work and to a placid and decent life, are totally unaware of them.

We might be able to appreciate his fertile imagination and that of the many detractors and reformers "sui generis," if this constant and systematic deformation and slanted presentation of the Dominican situation did not imply also, through its lack of responsibility, the bringing about of so many tragic and sorrowful consequences.

We honestly believe that Trujillo's anti-Communist attitude of unflinching intransigence has caused his enemies of every political hue, and those who bitterly resent his personal success, to lose even the smallest sense of proportion, causing them to become exasperated and impelling them to propagate even the basest of lies. At the same time, we recognize, of course, that many of Trujillo's detractors are people who have heard say this or that and have not even taken the trouble to find out the facts for themselves.

There is something extremely erroneous in the attitude of the majority of United States citizens regarding Trujillo and the Dominican Republic because they are unaware that by backing Trujillo, as we do wholeheartedly, instead of maligning him, they would be defending the best interests of the Free World and the victory of the democratic cause, something that can never be done by plotting, rebellion and terrorism, but only by loyal and sincere collaboration.

Why don't you try to aid Trujillo's democratic nature in the same way as we, the signatories of this declaration, do with dignity and respect for ourselves and for the Dominican people?
