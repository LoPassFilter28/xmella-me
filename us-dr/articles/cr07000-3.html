AMERICAN ENVOY RELATES DETAILS OF STORM'S FURY Minister To Dominican Republic Says Capital Demolished.

EDITOR'S NOTE: In the following dispatch, Charles B. Curtis, United States minister to Santo Domingo, describes the toll taken by the hurricane which swept the capital Wednesday. He said his estimate of 1500 dead is "conservative," and mentions the unroofing of his own legation.

By CHARLES B. CURTIS

United States Minister to the Dominican Republic

Written for the United Press

Copyright 1930 by United Press

SANTO DOMINGO, Dominican Republic, Sept. 6. (U.P.)—The ancient and historical city of Santo Domingo was devastated Wednesday by what appears to be the worst hurricane that ever swept the Caribbean.

After 24 hours of continuous storm a north wind of hurricane violence, with a velocity of 180 miles an hour, began about 3 o'clock that afternoon, unroofing the legation and destroying many homes.

A conservative estimate puts the loss of life at 1500, with the number of injured reaching 5000.

No loss of life was reported among continental Americans or Europeans here and all are believed safe.

President Trujillo has taken personal charge, the city is under martial law and good order prevails. The dead are being burned.

Governor Theodore Roosevelt of Porto Rico dispatched personal representatives by airplane Thursday and is sending in behalf of the people of Porto Rico all available relief in the form of food, shelter, clothing and medical aid.

Yesterday a marine corps plane arrived from Port Au Prince, Haiti, with representatives of Colonel Cutts and is returning immediately to bring medical officers and all possible medical supplies. No shipping can enter Santo Domingo port because of the swiftness of the current and the fact that a dredge has been sunk in the channel.

The capital is isolated on the land side by floods, fallen trees and wrecked bridges. Electric light and water plants are disabled. The old cathedral and colonial monuments were practically undamaged.

The storm was confined to a 40-mile strip along the coast but damage extends inland indefinitely in a northwesterly direction. The sugar area at Boca Chica was reported completely destroyed.

The American Red Cross has placed $15,000 at my disposal for emergency relief but this amount is insufficient for actual needs and additional clothing and other help are urgently needed.
