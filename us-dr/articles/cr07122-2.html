In the Interest of Jiminez

NEW YORK, Aug. 15.—Work of great importance, according to the men interested, is going on in this city in connection with the revolutionary movement in Santo Domingo. A junta has been formed, which has no official headquarters as yet, but whose members are in conference almost constantly and are supposed to be communicating daily by cable with General Juan Jiminez, the latter, who is still in Havana, is directing the work of the revolutionary party and arrangements are understood to be in progress for the sending of expeditions from points in the West Indies and directly from the United States, to aid in the overthrow of the existing government.

About fourteen months ago Adriano Grullon, an intimate friend of General Jiminez, came to New York as the representative of the revolutionary party and established relations with the Dominicans in this city. There are also some natives of Santo Domingo in Boston and Philadelphia whose interest has been enlisted in the cause.

A week ago Mr. Grullon's cousin, Maximillian Grullon, arrived here from Havana on a secret mission from General Jiminez. Both men held the most confidential relations with the revolutionary leader.

Father Charles Phillip Morales, a brother of the late General Morales, whom President Heureaux caused to be executed a year ago, arrived here from Santo Domingo, about the first of the month.

General A. A. Moscoso, a Dominican, has lived in this city several years. Messrs. Grullon, Morales and Moscoso have had several meetings and are understood to be in charge of the work being done in this country in aid of the schemes of General Jiminez.
