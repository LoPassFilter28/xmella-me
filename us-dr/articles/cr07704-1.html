Alcazar Palace of Columbus to be Opened to Public on October 12th

CIUDAD TRUJILLO.— (UP)—The Alcazar, palace of Columbus, has been restored to its original splendor by the Dominican Republic government and will be opened to the public Oct. 12, anniversary of the discovery of America.

Built by Diego, son of Christopher Columbus, the 447-year-old palace will be an international museum and repository of Columbiana relics.

Authentic furnishings and tapestries of the Spanish colonial period have been imported to furnish the palace.

Four prominent Spanish architects were commissioned by Generalissimo Rafael L. Trujillo to complete the reconstruction. In the early 1500's, using the Alcazar as a jumping-off base, Balboa discovered the Pacific, Ponce de Leon colonized Puerto Rico and discovered Florida, Pizarro conquered Peru and Cortez settled Mexico. The palace saw the rise and fall of the Spanish empire, the birth of liberty in the New World and the establishment of the Spanish colonies.

BEGUN IN 1910

The hotels Embajador and Jaragua are preparing for the influx of tourists and pilgrims who annually visit the republic for Columbus Day festivities. This capital city is the oldest permanent European settlement in the Western Hemisphere, founded in 1496 by Bartolome Columbus.

Construction of the Columbus palace began in 1510 under the direction of Viceroy and Second Admiral Don Diego Colon, son of Christopher Columbus, as a home for his wife, Dona Maria de Toledo, and the conquistadores who came from Europe to explore the new continent. The palace is regarded as the most eloquent example of the neo-Romanesque architecture transplanted by Spain in the New York.

Situated on a hill rising from the mouth of the Ozama River, the palace is a stone's throw from the trunk of the cieba tree to which Christopher and his brother, Bartolome, chained their caravels.

During its construction, the viceroy and Vicereine lived in the nearby Tower of Homage—first fort built by settlers in this hemisphere wich still stands unaffected by time and weather. Later the royal couple lived in the Casa de Cordon, the first adobe residence, built on this side of the Atlantic. It is now a tourist hotel, and restaurant.

TOMB TO BE OPENED

Built of massive coral rock stones, tile and brick by master workmen from Spain, the Alcazar has Romanesque windows, with stone Gothic seats or poyos built in the walls. Festooned arches and multiple Isabeline arches are throughout, supported by Doric columns whose capitals are tiled in Renaissance fashion.

Over the portals are frieze designs of vines and leaves. A winding staircase within a small cylindrical tower also has been replaced. A 16th century historian, Gonzalo Fernández de Oviedo, wrote that he had never seen anything in Spain to compare with the Alcazar.

Diego continued as governor of the Island of Hispaniola until 1523, with the exception of a five-year interval between 1515 and 1520. In 1523 he returned to Spain on official matters and died there three years later.

Dona Maria de Toledo went to Spain after his deah and claimed her husband's remains and those of Christopher Columbus. Under authority of King Phillip of Spain the remains of father and son were tranferred to Hispaniola and interred in the Cathedral of Santo Domingo.

The catedral, oldest in the New World, was erected during the reign of Diego Columbus. Ceremonies this Columbus Day also will include the annual opening of the Christopher Columbus tomb for the traditional verification of the existence of his remains.
