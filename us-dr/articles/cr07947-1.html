San Domingo Now. Story Current in Havana That Uncle Sam Will Intervene in the Republic. More Trouble Feared. Dominican Exile Predicts Revolution in Santo Domingo. Telegraph Wires Cut. San Domingo Insurgents Only Await the Arrival of Jiminez.

HAVANA, July 31.—The troubles of San Domingo absorb attention here. General Gomez refused to discuss the presidency of the Dominican republic, claiming to know nothing regarding the matter. When questioned concerning the rumors circulated in the cafes as to his aspiration regarding the Dominican republic, General Gomez' action proved his contempt for the stories, yet, in the clubs and cafes he is seriously accused of conspiring to bring both the island of Hayti and Cuba under the dominion of the United States. General Gomez' wife and family left Santo Domingo on board the steamer Maria Herrera and are expected to arrive in Havana on Monday next.

El Diarro de la Marina and La Lucha express the opinion that considering the present expansion policy, the United States may intervene in San Domingo.

MORE TROUBLE FEARED.

Dominican Exile Predicts Revolution in Santo Domingo.

NEW YORK, July 31.—General Abelardo A. Moscosso, an exiled leader of the Liberal party of Santo Domingo, now living in New York, said:

"The death of President Heureaux will, I think, surely be followed by a long state of disorder and revolution. I want to emphasize this prophecy—that the United States will eventually be compelled to interfere to establish peace in the island, just as this country did in Cuba.

"I favor the policy of peace. I shall be glad to return to my country and support such a policy if it is inaugurated by General Figuero, the vice president, who succeeds to the head of the government. But if something is not quickly done to establish order and peace, more revolutionary expeditions will follow that of General Jiminez and General Horales, who led a band from Cuba against Heureaux."

TELEGRAPH WIRES CUT.

San Domingo Insurgents Only Await the Arrival of Jiminez.

CAPE HAYTIEN, July 31.—Advices just received from the Dominican frontier say the insurgents have cut the telegraph wires in the neighborhood of Santiago de los Caballeros and also near Moca. The insurgents in the western part of Santo Domingo await the arrival of Don Juan Isidoro Jiminez, under whose leadership they expect to attack Santiago.
