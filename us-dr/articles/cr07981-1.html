The Indianapolis Journal · February 23, 189623 de febrero de 1896

Original in English.Original en inglés.

MANY CUBANS KILLED

SPANISH ACCOUNTS OF RECENT FIGHTS WITH INSURGENTS.

Several Hundred of the Iatter Said to Have Been Slaia and Wounded in Havana Province.

FORCED TO MOVE WESTWARD

THE PATRIOT ARMY UNABLE TO ENTER MATANZAS IN FORCE.

No Prisoners Yet Executed by Weyler —An American's Observations While Visiting the Island.

HAVANA, Feb. 22.—The Spanish columns which have been operating against the insurgents have been engaged with the enemy under Maximo Gomez and others commanding the main forces of the insurgent army. Several fights have taken place during the last few days on the limits of the province of Havana and the province of Matanzas. The insurgents have lost seventy killed and over three hundred wounded. It is asserted that only a small portion of the insurgent forces has been able to enter Matanzas. The main body, it is added, has been obliged to retreat westward. A dispatch from San Felipe says that a severe skirmish has taken place at the plantation of Aguacata, resulting in the insurgents being compelled to retire with the loss of twenty-five killed and thirty-seven wounded. Among the killed was an insurgent colonel. This body of insurgents is said to have lost during the recent fighting about two hundred men in killed and wounded. Advices just received from Aguacita say that the sound of continued rifle and artillery firing has been heard from there, and it is supposed that the column of Spanish troops under Colonel Hernandez has again been engaged with the insurgents.

Colonel Periz reports having been engaged with 1,200 insurgents between Penon and Dolorita. The insurgents left eleven killed, among them being Major Morejon. Three soldiers were seriously wounded. Major Cabelo, telegraphing from the front, announces that his column of troops has been engaged with the insurgents under Mestre and Rojas, at the plantation of Buena Vista. The insurgents lost five killed.

The insurgent leader Cartagena has attacked a detachment of Spanish troops at Tanamo, province of Santiago de Cuba. The enemy was repulsed. Cartagena was wounded and two of his folowers were killed. The insurgents have attacked the village of Casigua, province of Havana, but they were repulsed with loss.

MACEO'S DEFEAT.

A telegram from San Jose de las Lajas says that when the column of troops commanded by Colonel Tort was leaving that place, firing was heard in the direction of Zenea and Luz, and detachments were pushed in those directions, it being believed that the insurgents were retreating before Generals Linares and Aldecoa. When the troops reached Zenea it was found that Maceo and other inusrgent leaders had been defeated in an attempt to capture Jaruco, and General Linares reported that he had defeated the insurgents at Morales and was countermarching towards Catalina. Colonel Tort saw a great fire in the direction of Catalina, and, making a forced march to that place, he found that Colonel Hernandez had dislodged the insurgents there. Hernandez and Tort met at the passage of the river Ojo de Agua, and soon afterwards their columns exchanged shots for thirty minutes with the insurgents, six of whom were killed and many wounded.

At Catalina the insurgents encamped at the plantation of El Gato for forty-five minutes only before being dispersed by the troops. The insurgent advance guards advanced shortly upon the enemy, although the latter kept up a well-sustained fire from behind fences and ruined houses, which formed a semicircular defense. The insurgents were estimated to number about five thousand, but they were dislodged by the artillery, after defending their position with tenacity. Four companies of Spanish infantry and a squadron of the Pizarro cavalry charged the enemy after the artillery had done its work, and the insurgents retreated leaving ten killed and a great many wounded on the field. The main body of the insurgents retreated towards the Esperanza mountains, where Maximo Gomez was awaiting them. At Catalina the insurgents were under the leadership of Zavas, Cardenas and Miro. In the figthing at El Gato the Spaniards lost Capt. Antonio Serra, and eighteen privates wounded.

A telegram from Perdoso announces that a column of infantry, cavalry and civil guards has been engaged there with an insurgent force commanded by Gomez. The latter is said to have lost thirty, killed.

A detachment of civil guards, commanded by a lieutenant, has had a skirmish with the insurgents, commanded by Francisco Collado, at the plantation of Tinguaro, near Colon, in the province of Matanzas. The guards captured Callado, his lieutenant, Eliseo Campo, and nine privates.

JARUCO PARTLY BURNED.

Although the insurgents were defeated at Jaruco, it is now known that they burned 132 brick and fifty wooden houses, in addition to forty huts, and plundered all the buildings except those on Real street, which was fortified. The garrison of the fort and the firemen surrendered to the insurgents after having exhausted their ammunition. Three of the garrison elected to remain with the insurgents, but the rest refused to do so, and were set at liberty. Among the houses burned at Jaruco was the city hall and the fine residence of Pedro Rodriguez. The insurgents took all the money they could find in the town. The troops lost four wounded, and the insurgents left two killed on the field and retired with their wounded.

The insurgents have attacked the village of Rio Blanco and plundered the stores.

A dispatch from Bolondron, province of Matanzas, announces that a band of 200 insurgents passed there yesterday going in the direction of the swamps of the Grand Cienega Occidental de Zapata. On the other hand a dispatch from Sabanilla, in the same province, says that several insurgent bands have passed that place, going in the direction of the province of Havana.

Official advices from Madruga, on the borders of the province of Havana, say that a band of insurgents, comanded by Pedro Castillo, has crossed the frontier, coming from the province of Matanzas, and plundered the stores of a village, Santa Ana. The insurgents have also plundered a store between Palp and Vega and have burned two culverts and destroyed two bridges two miles from Guanabacoa, which is not far from this city. Near Matanzas, the insurgents have destroyed the railroad and at Banaguises they have cut the telegraph lines. In addition, the insurgents have burned a railroad bridge between Rodrigo and Liticeto la Sagua, and have also derailed an exploring engine.

In the province of Pinar del Rio, the insurgents have burned the villages of Santa Cruz de los Pinos, including the church.

A telegram received here from Matanzas, this evening, says that the insurgent leader, Tavares, has been killed near Cardenas, that province.

PARDONS FOR INSURGENTS.

General Weyler announces that he will pardon those insurgents who will surrender at once to the government officials. If those who surrender are found armed a second or third time, he gives warning in his statement just issued, they will be subject to proceedings which may result in their being punished for a capital offense.

The Spanish consul at Santo Domingo has succeeded in obtaining from the Dominican government the detention of a number of filibusters, who, it is claimed, intended to capture the Spanish steamer Maria Herrara, in order to embark on an expedition to this island.

The casino of the colored population has elected Captain General Weyler honorary president of that organization as a token of their gratitude for the kind words he uttered regarding the colored population on the day of his arrival here. It is reported that the Captain General is preparing a circular which will cause a great impression.

The insurgents have burned the railroad bridge at Jiqui, and have twice attacked Fort Monte Alto, at San Jose de Los Ramos, but they were repulsed with loss. The insurgent force commanded by the well-known leader, Perico Delgado, is reported to be moving in the vicinity of Cabanas, province of Pinar del Rio.

The insurgents have burned the house, machinery and plantation of Dolores, in the district of Santo Domingo, province of Santa Clara.

The Cayo Confiles police have captured a band of counterfeiters engaged in putting out spurious notes of the Bank of Spain.

Troops captured near Guines several important insurgent leaders, among them being General Ynglesito and Colonel Nunez. The insurgent officers captured were immediately court-martialed. Near Jaruco, the Spanish forces recently fought a battle with a band of insurgents. Eleven of the insurgents were left dead on the field of battle.

A band of 150 insurgents attacked the village of Hoye Colorado, eleven miles from Havana. Colonel Cavestany hurried to the assistance of the Spanish forces, and the insurgents retreated, leaving nine dead and wounded on the field of battle.
