New Italian Corps Hopes for Revival of Embassy Glory

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

"I DON'T imagine there'll be many parties like that just now. Italy is poor, very poor. Perhaps when we get lend-lease."

It was youthful Carlo Almagia, secretary of the Italian Embassy, speaking. We'd been talking about the generation-old fame of that embassy and the magnificent balls and cotillions given there in pre-war days.

Signor Almagia looks as you expect a young Italian diplomat to look. He is of medium size, dark-haired, brown-eyed, with an ingratiating smile and well-cut clothes. His manner differs little from that of hundreds of other young Italian attaches who have been appearing in Washington for many years.

Yet, he is the first member of the new Italian Embassy staff I've met. In fact, he is the first member of that post-Mussolini staff that I have even identified. When Carlo Almagia handed me his card I noticed that the former identification "Royal Italian Embassy" is now simply engraved "Italian Embassy."

* * *

SIGNOR ALMAGIA doesn't even think the King will return to his throne. Nor for that matter will Crown Prince Umberto replace him. There's just a chance that the small son of Umberto and his Belgian-born Crown Princess Marie Jose, may be chosen to reign under a regency. Who knows, there may even be a Republic of Italy!

Carlo Almagia can't work up much enthusiasm over any Italian who served under the Fascist regime. His great hero is Count Carlo Sforza, the grand old nobleman who renounced his fealty to King Victor Emmanuel because the King permitted Mussolini to exist. Another hero is his own chief, Ambassador Alberto Tarchiani, with whom Almagia has worked for several years in this country during the envoy's self-imposed exile.

* * *

ODDLY enough the new staff doesn't find the embassy in which they are now living very comfortable. All of which seems odd because Washington always considered the residence one of the best in town. Of course, the fact that it was planned by Benito Mussolini and was lived in principally by Ambassadors of Fascist Italy, may have something to do with it.

As a matter of fact, the last Italian Ambassador to Washington who had nothing to do with Mussolini was Prince Caetani, during whose tenure of office the residence was built. Nobile Giacomo de Martino was the first envoy to move into the embassy. And, tho nobody thought much about it, back in the early 1920s, I suppose de Martino could have been described as a Fascist envoy.

Anyhow, no matter what differences there will be in the beautiful big embassy, it is interesting to realize that soon again—after nearly four years—there will be some social contact with that embassy.

* * *

WELL, diplomatic Washington has been sympathizing for more than a year with pretty Betty Byrne and her fiance, Luis de Silva, Marques de Zaraha and first secretary of the Spanish Embassy. Sympathizing while Betty and Luis waited patiently for permission to marry from Generalissimo Francisco Franco. Then without much warning the permission came.

Meantime Betty and Luis had planned first one kind of wedding then another; a large reception; no, just a group of friends; all the relatives? In the end they eloped! And while they eloped they thought they might as well elope to some place.

Soooo—down in Trujillo City in the Dominican Republic Luis had an old and dear friend who is the Spanish Minister. Just the place. On April 26 they were married at the church in the Dominican Monastery with just a group of the Spanish colony present.

* * *

FOLLOWED a brief honeymoon at the magnificent new hotel—which the new Marquesa describes as unbelievably lovely—built at the ocean's edge. Says Betty, her only regret is a wonderful pink linen frock, hand-embroidered in navy blue for which she paid $2.75, but which she had to leave behind when the Pan American Airways finally came thru with reservations for home just before the frock was finished!

In Trujillo City you can buy radios, electric clocks, and all the excellent steaks you want! Sounds like paradise, whether you're on a honeymoon or not. The new Marquesa is the daughter of Mrs. Stanton J. Peelle, of Four Winds Farm, Chardon, O., and the late Col. Bernard Abert Byrne. She has lived most of her life in Washington.
