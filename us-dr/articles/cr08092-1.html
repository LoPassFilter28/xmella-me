Was He True to Trujillo? $25,000 Fee Probe 'Hooks' Ham Fish

Rep. Hamilton Fish (R., N. Y.) today was disclosed as being under official investigation by the Justice Department on the ground that he received a secret $25,000 feet in 1939 from Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo, military dictator of the Dominican Republic.

The fee was received in July, 1939, a week after Mr. Fish made an about-face, eulogizing the dictator for inaugurating a "golden age" in Dominican affairs, and a few weeks before he visited Germany where he was wined and dined by big shot Nazis on his way to Oslo, Norway. A clerk in his office recently was sentenced for perjury in connection with the Justice Department's investigation of isolationist and Nazi propaganda.

The fee came to the attention of the Justice Department about six weeks ago at the time the District grand jury on Axis agents was questioning George Jamhar Djamgaroff, a White Russian who claims to have turned down a $200,000 offer to handle Nazi propaganda here in 1935.

OLD FRIENDS

Mr. Djamgaroff and Rep. Fish had been old friends since the Congressman in 1932 interceded for the Russian when the latter's immigration standing was in question. With the passage of the foreign agent registration act, Mr. Djamgaroff three years ago registered with the American State Department as the $50,000-a-year agent of the Dominican Republic.

ASKED TO EXPLAIN

Rep. Fish, presumed to be in New York, currently is explaining to the Bureau of Internal Revenue how he came to "overlook" the alleged $25,000 fee in 1939 when his total reported income from all other sources was only $22,000, the department said. The sequence of events leading up to and following the alleged fee, were:

Dec. 21, 1937—Commenting on reports that several thousand Haitians had been massacred by Trujillo's soldiers, Rep. Fish told the House: "This is the most outrageous atrocity ever perpetrated on the American continent . . . I should like to see the Government of the United States withdraw our recognition of the Dominican Republic."

Jan. 4, 1938—Fish inserted a magazine story in the Congressional Record describing the massacre.

A GOODWILL TOUR

March, 1939—Mr. Djamgaroff, as Dominican agent, arranged to have several American newspapermen and congressmen make a "goodwill tour" of the Dominican Republic. Mr. Fish went along; discovered that Trujillo had retired as "president" but was keeping the reins of government as generalissimo of Dominican army.

July, 1939—Gen. Trujillo, "perpetrator of most outrageous atrocity," returned goodwill visit "as a private citizen," but was a guest of honor at a Pan American Society banquet in New York on July 12. While pickets paraded outside the Biltmore Hotel with banners repeating Gen. Hugh Johnson's description of Gen. Trujillo as a "blood-spattered bully," Rep. Fish was quoted as telling Gen. Trujillo:

"General, you have created a golden age for your country . . . and I am proud and happy to repeat at this time to a United States audience, you will go down in the history of your country as a builder greater than all the Spanish conquistadores together."

Sails for Europs a few days later—The $25,000 is alleged to have been deposited to Rep. Fish's order in the National City Bank of New York as part of a $300,000 credit established in this coutry for Gen. Trujillo thru the Dominican Legation in Washington.

A few weeks later—Rep. Fish sailed for Europe to attend the Interparliamentary Union at Oslo, pausing in Berlin for enthusiastic write-ups in German press, a conversation with the Foreign Minister Von Ribbentrop, whose private plane flew him to Oslo.

HID OIL COMPANY PROFIT

Sept., 1939 — Rep. Fish returned to America and became a leader of the isolationists. Justice Department operatives have been seeking to establish that while he then used his office as headquarters for the National Committee to Keep America Out of War, he instructed the National City Bank how to dispense the $25,000; that at this point the bank was instructed to transfer more than $2000 to his Washington bank to be followed by an equal sum a month later.

It is also alleged by the Justice Department that more than $8000 found its way to an oil company, the Nepaug Oil Co., of Houston, Tex., whose president, David D. McDaniels, is said to have had dealings with Rep. Fish.

At about the same time, the National City Bank is alleged to have been directed to transfer $12,500—half of the total fee—back to Gen. Trujillo.

Rep. Fish was (and is) ranking Republican member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, which passes on legislation affecting other governments.
