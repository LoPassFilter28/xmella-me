Morgan Asks Plotters Be Spared Firing Squad

HAVANA, Aug. 20 (AP).—Maj. William Morgan of Toledo, Ohio, who helped smash the recent anti-Castro conspiracy, said today he felt the plotters should not be executed but should serve hard labor terms on Cuban farms.

Maj. Morgan and Maj. Eloy Gutierrez Menoyo, who also posed as a counter-revolutionary to frustrate the plot, were interviewed on television. Under new laws, counter-revolutionary activities may be punishable by death before a firing squad.

Majs. Morgan and Menoyo, who both fought in Fidel Castro's revolution, reiterated that the conspiracy originated with the Dominican Republic's strongman, Generalissimo Rafael L. Trujillo. But Gen. Trujillo's immediate collaborators were so corrupt, Maj. Morgan declared, that the plot had no chance of success.

Got Only $4,000 of $10,0000

As an example, he said, he asked Gen. Trujillo for $10,000 and got only $4,000—that a Spanish priest, acting as go-between kept $1,000 and a Dominican consul took $5,000. The consul was not named.

Maj. Morgan said the consul arranged for the purchase of arms which were shipped from the United States aboard a private boat. He said he met the vessel on the high seas and brought it to Havana.

This varied from earlier unofficial reports published here. They said the boat had come from the Dominican Republic and Maj. Morgan, on board all the time, suddenly turned on the crew, captured them and brought the ship into port with the aid of other Castro men posing as conspirators.

Maj. Morgan said he made several trips to Miami and at one time was questioned at length by United States agents. He said he was able to elude them and contact the arms-bearing ship.

Asked Batista Surrender

Maj. Menoyo said he told Gen. Trujillo he would participate in the plot if Gen. Trujillo would turn over ex-Dictator Fulgencio Batista and former Gen. Eleuterio Pedraza. He said Gen. Trujillo replied he would surrender both men after the conspiracy had succeeded. Gen. Batista, who fled to the Dominican Republic last New Year's Day, left yesterday for new sanctuary in Portugal's Madeira Islands, off the Moroccan coast of Africa.

A check of those accompanying Gen. Batista did not list Gen. Pedraza, who has been described as one of the master minds of the recent anti-Castro conspiracy. Unconfirmed reports from Ciudad Trujillo have said Gen. Pedraza was imprisoned on Gen. Trujillo's order because the conspiracy had failed.
