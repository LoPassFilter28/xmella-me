$20,000,000 Loan To Brazil Will Help Cement Hemisphere Export-Import Bank Will Advance Money for New Steel Factory

By BLAIR BOLLES.

For the defense of the Americas and even closer amity between Brazil and the United States, this Nation has assigned a loan of $20,000,000 to Brazil for construction of a steel plant near Rio de Janeiro. The lending agency is the Export-Import Bank, and the borrower is the Brazilian Siderurgical Plan.

The loan was announced last night by Jesse Jones, Federal loan administrator, after months of negotiations with a group of Brazilians, headed by Guilherme Guinle, president of the executive commission of the Siderurgical Plan. The cost of the mill will be about $45,000,000, of which $25,000,000 has been advanced by the Brazilian government.

This example of the United States' economic co-operation with countries to the south indicates the type of undertaking the United States might finance under the special loan authority granted the Export-Import Bank in the bill signed by President Roosevelt yesterday, making available $500,000,000 for Western Hemisphere lending. The completion of the Brazilian deal was accomplished Wednesday.

Military Men Coming Here.

The United States is interested in developing the economies of nations to the South to as great an extent as is compatible with our own economic interests. Heavy industries are rare in South America. The new plant will be designed to turn out annually about 300,000 tons of steel rails, boiler plate and other steel products, many of which Brazil now imports.

At the same time the United States took this step for political and economic solidarity of the Americas, it looked forward to another aspect of hemisphere co-operation, more directly concerned with hemisphere defense—the arrival here Sunday and Monday of 20 distinguished military leaders and observers from nine South American countries at the invitation of the United States Army chief of staff, Gen. George C. Marshall. They will be in the United States until October 16.

Material for the Brazilian steel plant will be bought in the United States, according to the agreement reached through the exchange of the Jones-Guinle letters. The factory's construction is to be completed in about two and a half years. The project calls for the closest co-operation between the two countries primarily interested.

Setup Is Outlined.

Dr. Guinle stated in his letter:

"We will establish an office in Pittsburgh or other suitable center with a corps of engineers and executives to handle the design of the mill, purchase of equipment and construction of the plant.

"This organization will be composed of Americans and Brazilians acceptable to both parties. We will, if you think it advisable, establish a supplemental or consulting office in Washington or New York. All of this, of course, will be at our expense and become a part of the cost of the mill."

The mechanics of the loan calls for an initial commitment from the Export-Import Bank of $10,000,000, to be increased to $20,000,000 as the work advances. The loan will be payable in 20 semiannual installments, the first of which is to become due three years from the date of the first advance. Interest, payable semiannually, at 4 per cent, will run from the date each advance is made.

The military visitors coming this week end are coming from Bolivia, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Guatemala, Honduras, Panama, Peru and Uruguay. When they leave here they will go to the Panama Canal.

To Inspect Posts.

During their visit they will become acquainted with our national defense program and inspect military establishments here and some of the principal posts, such as Langley Field, Fort Benning, Ga., Barksdale Field, La., San Antonio, Fort Sill, Okla., Fort Knox, Ky., Detroit and New York.

Gen. Marshall will give a reception to the visitors at his quarters at Fort Myer Tuesday afternoon and a dinner that night at the Mayflower Hotel. Bolling Field will be inspected the following morning and the group will then fly to Langley Field.

A second group of Latin American officers from 11 other countries will begin a similar tour from Washington on October 16.

Members of the first group are: Gen. Felipe Rivera, chief of staff, Bolivia; Lt. Col. Victor Acosta, Bolivia; Gen. Luis M. Castaneda, chief of staff, Colombia; Lt. Col. Ernesto Buenaventura, chief of air corps, Colombia; Lt. Col. German Ocampo, superintendent of military academy, Colombia; Maj. Hernando Mora, Colombia; Col. Manuel Rodriguez Torra, deputy chief of staff, Costa Rica; Col. Guillermo Guardia Mora, military secretary to the President of Costa Rica; Lt. Col. Antonio Leyba Pou, Dominican Republic; Maj. Frank Felix Miranda, Dominican Republic; Maj. Gen. Rodolfo A. Mendoza, chief of staff, Guatemala; Lt. Col. Enrique Peralta, Guatemala; Gen. Leonidas Pineda, undersecretary of war, Honduras; Gen. Calixto Carias, Honduras; Lt. Col. Rogelio Fabrega, deputy chief of national police, Panama; Capt. German Lemm, Panama; Col. Felipe de la Barra, chief of staff, Peru; Lt. Col. Jose M. Tamayo, Peru; Gen. Marcelino Bergalli, commander general of army, Uruguay, and Lt. Col. Oscar D. Gestido, director of military aeronautics, Uruguay.
