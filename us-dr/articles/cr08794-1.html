For Trujillo: Troubles

Year 29 of the Era of Trujillo is undoubtedly the most disturbing ever experienced by the dictator of the Dominican Republic.

The year 1959, like practically everything else in that tiny Caribbean nation, bears the stamp of the sturdy, 67-year-old chief of state, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, who came to power in 1930.

But ever since the Castro brothers overthrew the Batista government in neighboring Cuba earlier this year, Gen. Trujillo has faced a mounting pile of troubles. The most serious challenge posed to his rule was a small-scale invasion of the country which was attempted several weeks ago.

Gen. Trujillo's well-trained military forces reportedly wiped out most of the tiny rebel band on the landing beach. But a handful of rebels apparently made it through to the hills, and may still be hiding out.

Gen. Trujillo knows well that Premier Castro led a similar band to power in Cuba following his near-disastrous "invasion" that was scarcely any more distinguished in the beginning.

Though news of the revolutionary attempt is sparse and clouded with propaganda, it now appears that at least a few of the invaders—who reportedly journeyed by boat from Cuba—were sons of prominent Dominicans. If that is true, Gen. Trujillo is confronted with the germ of a domestic problem that may be far more serious than invasion threats from abroad.

But it was just those alleged threats that were under consideration Friday by the Organization of American States. Charging before the OAS Council that certain elements in Cuba and Venezuela were plotting the downfall of their government, Dominican representatives asked for action by the organization.

On Friday, however, Virgilio Diaz Ordonez of the Dominican Republic withdrew the charge. The Council of the OAS then postponed until tomorrow consideration of a request to convene foreign ministers of the 21 member nations to consider the Caribbean turmoil.

Though some of the diplomats believe the charges are well founded, there is an underlying belief in the OAS that most member nations would not take part in any defense of the Dominican Republic.
