Insurgents of San Domingo Cut Telegraph Wires. Expect to Make an Attack on Santiago Under the Leadership of Jiminez

CAPE HAYTIEN, July 29.—Advices just received from the Dominican frontier say that the insurgents have cut the telegraph wires in the neighborhood of Santiago de Los Caballeros and also near Moca. The insurgents in the western part of San Domingo await the arrival of Don Juan Isidoro Jiminez, under whose leadership they expect to attack Santiago.

DEPEND UPON UNCLE

SAM FOR PROTECTION

WASHINGTON, July 29.—Upon inquiry at the State and Navy departments to-day I was informed that neither Germany nor any other foreign power, as far as is known, has dispatched a vessel to San Domingo. This is very gratifying to the authorities, for although no objection could be made to any foreign power having a man-of-war in these waters to protect its citizens in the event of a revolution they look upon the absence of such vessel as an indication that other countries are satisfied to depend upon the United States to look after foreign interests in case of an uprising.

Germany, I am told, has not a single man-of-war on this station, her North Atlantic squadron having recently been withdrawn to participate in the autumn maneuvers at Kiel. It was stated some time ago that a vessel was to be sent to Guatemala to demand the payment of debts due to certain German subjects, but the Navy Department has not been informed of her departure. The only possible fear of any undue foreign interference in affairs in San Domingo has been from Germany. It is known that she has some interests in San Domingo, and it has been stated that some of the German residents are in sympathy with Jiminez, who is said to be heading a movement antagonistic to a legitimate successor of Heureaux.

While, of course, keeping a careful lookout to see that there is no foreign interference, the immediate work of the two men-of-war to be sent to San Domingo will be to protect American citizens. If necessary marines will be landed to protect the American consulate and American property. The moral effect of the mere presence of these ships will, the authorities hope, be sufficient to put a damper on any revolutionary movements. Secretary Long said to-day that he expected that the Machias would reach San Domingo city by Monday. Her repairs have been expedited so that she will be able to sail from St. Thomas to-morrow evening. The New Orleans will sail from Newport to-day, and ought to reach the waters of the Dominican republic by next Wednesday night.
