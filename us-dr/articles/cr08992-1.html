Original in English.Original en inglés.

IMPORTANT FROM ST. DOMINGO.

Important intelligence from the Dominican Republic has been received and laid before the State Department. By the act of the Spanish Cortes of May 1, 1865, the military occupation of that island was formerly abandoned. The attempt to convert an American State into a European colony has been made a failure by the resistance of the Dominicans, and Spain leaves St. Domingo as she left her other American colonies. General Gandera, Queen Isabella's Governor of St. Domingo, demands of the Dominican Republican acknowledgment that Spain went to take possession of the Dominican soil and people at the spontaneous desire of the people; that her conduct while there was grandly generous, and that she leaves it entirely of her own free will. The Dominican Government refuses to sanction such an admission, and General Gandera has declared a blockade on all the coasts and harbors of the Dominican Republic. His officers, according to private accounts, talk in the streets of San Domingo of laying the city in ashes, but there is a considerable amount of foreign property within the precincts, and the indignant Captain General will probably take into consideration that France, England, and the United States might have a word to say in the matter.
