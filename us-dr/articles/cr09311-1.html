Dentists Close Successful Session; To Houston, Texas For 1952 Conclave Dr. W. M. Springer, Of Cincinnati, Is New President

BY LEWIS O. SWINGLER

For approximately 700 dentists who attended the Thirty-Eighth Annual Convention of the National Dental Association last week Memphis lived up to its far-flung, traditional reputation, "The Place of Good Abode." The six-day session, held from August 12th through the 17th at Booker T. Washington High School, was presided over by Dr. Martin Walton of Thomasville, Ga., who in appraising this first annual meeting this far below the Mason-Dixon line, declared:-

"The visit to the University of Tennessee College of Dentistry by the members of the NDA upon invitation of Dean James T. Ginn was an indication of the growing trend toward the practical application of the values of democratic ideals here in the deep South. All the officials of the dental college, and the entire student personnel showed our delegates every courtesy to make the visit to the college one of the highlights of the convention."

President Walton warmly commended Union Protective Assurance Company for its reception, referring to it as "one of our most enjoyable affairs . . and had equal words of praise for all other activities carried out under supervision of the Shelby County Dental Society, official host to the convention.

SECOND LARGEST REGISTRATION

The Memphis meeting represented the second largest registration in the history of the association, Dr. Walton added. "This was due in large part to the widely advertised reputation of Memphis as a great host city," he stated.

"The local dentists in Memphis set a record in their pre-convention planning," the president said. "Every detail was thoroughly prosecuted, and when the convention time rolled around everything was ready to go."

DR. SPRINGER NEW PRESIDENT

Dr. Walton was automatically succeeded by Dr. W. M. Springer, of Cincinnati, Ohio, who moved up from the position of president-elect. Other officers elected and installed during the closing session Friday afternoon included:-

Dr. A. M. Mackel, of Natchez, Miss., president-elect; Dr. C. L. Thomas, of Louisville, Ky., who defeated Dr. Millard R. Dean, of Washington, D. C. for position of vice-president; Dr. Charles Williams, of Chicago; Dr. Leroy Gates, of Philadelphia, Pa., and Dr. R. A. Jackson, of Kansas City, Mo., the latter three members of the Executive Board.

DR. CARRUTHERS PUBLIC SPEAKER

Dr. Ben Carruthers Department of Social Affairs, Division of Human Rights, United Nations Secretariat, was guest speaker at the opening public program Monday night, August 13th, at Booker Washington high school. The UN leader told the audience that "The United Nations has got to succeed as it is the last and best hope for solution of our present world dilemma." He pointed out several notable successes of the UN, including the settling of the Dutch-Indonesia controversy, the preparation of Libya for immediate independence and the Somaliland for their independence within the next ten years.

Other speakers on the public program were Mayor Watkins Overton, Dr. J. E. Walker, Dr. E. M. Wilkins, Dr. A. W. Jefferson, president of the Shelby County Dental Society; Dr. Charles E. Thompson, of Chicago; Dr. R. Q. Venson, who served as general chairman for the local convention committee and retiring member of the Executive Board of the NDA; Revs. J. A. McDaniel, Rev. H. C. Nabrit, Prof. B. T. Hunt, principal of Booker Washington High School; Mrs. Cooper E. Taylor, president of the Ladies Auxiliary, Shelby County Dental Society; Mrs. Harry E. Thomas, of Seneca, S. C., president of the National Ladies Auxiliary to the NDA; and Dr. James T. Ginn, Dean of Dentistry, University of Tennessee.

Dr. Charles E. Williams, of Chicago, chairman of the Executive Board, presented an oversize trophy as an award for meritorious service to Dr. Robert G. Kesel, of Chicago.

AUXILIARY HEARS MRS. ARRINGTON

Mrs. Alice B. Arrington, Executive Director of Madison Avenue Branch YWCA, Baltimore, Md., addressed a capacity house Tuesday morning, August 14th, at First Baptist Church, Lauderdale, under auspices of the Ladies Auxiliary to the National Dental Association. She spoke on the subject, "Today's Woman in Today's World." Other participants on the Welcome program of the Auxiliary were prominent Memphis women who extended greetings to the visiting ladies. Mrs. Bruce Alleyne, national program director of the Auxiliary from Chicago, presented Mrs. Arrington.

Mrs. Raymond L. Jackson, of Kansas City, Mo., was elected president of the Auxiliary to succeed Mrs. Thomas.

Other national women leaders on hand for the sessions were Mrs. John Carr, vice-president of the NDA, who moved up to the position of president-elect; Mrs. J. A. Jackson, secretary-treasurer of the Auxiliary; Mrs. W. B. Reed, and Mrs. H. W. Williamson, regional vice-presidents.

FULL SOCIAL CALENDAR

Memphis hospitality won the heart and unstinted praise of every delegate. There were rounds of social affairs, including a number of informal house parties. Scheduled receptions included the Smoker and Cocktail Party respectively for the men and ladies, Tuesday evening... a Dutch Supper by Union Protective Assurance Co. Wednesday evening, a Picnic outing in beautiful Fuller State Park, Thursday afternoon, and a Semi-Formal Dance Thursday evening at the Hippodrome Skating Rink.

Approximately twelve of the delegates, led by Dr. Charles Thompson, left Memphis Saturday morning for a cruise of the Caribbean countries barring stormy weather. The delegation was scheduled to stop in Miami, Florida where they are joined by a dozen more dentists for the continued journey. The trip to the Caribbean is being made over the Pan American World Airways and will include San Juan, Ciudad Trujillo, Dominican Republic, Port-au-Prince, Haiti, Kingston, Jamaica, and Camaguey, and Havana, Cuba.

Next Year's convention is scheduled for Houston Texas.
