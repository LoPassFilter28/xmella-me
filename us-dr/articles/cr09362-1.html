Evening Star · January 17, 191417 de enero de 1914

Original in English.Original en inglés.

TOTAL COLLECTIONS AT DOMINICAN PORTS Report Shows $4,109,294 Last Year, an Increase, Despite Political Disturbances.

Gen. McIntyre, chief of the insular bureau of the War Department, has received a long report of financial conditions in Santo Domingo from Walker W. Vick, general receiver for the United States of Dominican customs. It is shown that, despite political disturbances, the customs collections of the republic last year amounted to $4,109,294, being an increase of $463,319 as compared with the collections of the preceding year.

Review of the Year.

"A retrospect of the year," says the receiver general, "presents many satisfying examples of the very genuine character of the benefits resulting to the Dominican republic through the existence of the receivership. It demonstrates clearly the unlimited possibilities of the future with deterrent influences removed and the pathway to administrative effectiveness cleared of the restraining jungle growth. The process merely entails frank understanding and earnest co-operation by the officials of the Dominican republic. Any suspicion or misconception of the aims and purposes will render nil the effort to regenerate the customs service in keeping with the standard it should maintain. The very spirit and letter of the convention of 1907 forecasted a constructive achievement for the sole benefit of a sister republic, which can be accomplished to the honor and credit of the American government by the sincere conformance of the beneficiary party."

Credit for Progress.

"The American personnel, laboring under many discouraging handicaps, is responsible for the rehabilitation already evident and should be accorded full measure of credit. The vista of future progress is bright and the natural resources of Santo Domingo almost without parallel. The means of stimulation are readily available to the Dominican government. It is to be hoped the receivership, in its eagerness to accomplish completely its mission and purposes, will be substantially supported and encouraged."
