Costa Rica Asks Ministers To Scan Dominican Coup

SAN JOSE, Costa Rica, Sept. 27 (AP). — Costa Rica has called on the foreign ministers of the Americas to consider the military takeover of the Dominican Republic and joint action to prevent more such coups.

Foreign Minister Daniel Oduber issued the call last night after Dominican military chiefs who ousted President Juan Bosch installed a three-man provisional civilian government.

Costa Rica also broke off diplomatic relations with the Dominican Republic, adhering to a policy of not recognizing governments taking power in coups. The United States and Venezuela have suspended relations with the Dominican Republic, a step short of a full break.

Costa Rican President Francisco Orlich, who is visiting in Honduras, sent word that Honduras and El Salvador also had repudiated the coup.

Mexico Retains Ties

The Mexican Foreign Ministry said Mexico plans to maintain diplomatic relations with the new Dominican government. Mexican policy is that other nations have no right to judge the suitability of another's government.

Costa Rica proposed a foreign ministers' meeting to review Dominican affairs and discuss possible measures against military coups staged anywhere in the hemisphere. The Bosch regime was the third constitutional government to be overthrown in Latin America in six months.

Sources close to the Organization of American States predicted the United States would indorse a ministers' meeting if a majority of OAS members favored it. The United States, which generally deplores seizure of a government by power, withheld an immediate decision.

United States Senator Smathers, Democrat of Florida, urged President Kennedy to dispatch a team of Latin American experts to the Dominican Republic to study conditions.

In Santo Domingo, Mr. Bosch was kept a prisoner in the presidential palace but guards permitted his wife to visit him. Mrs. Bosch flew in from Puerto Rico.

She was accompanied by two leading Puerto Ricans—both American citizens—apparently in an effort to assure the security of the Bosch family. They were Santiago Polanco Abreu, speaker of the Puerto Rican House of Representatives, and Manuel Abreu Castillo, president of the bar association in San Juan.

The new provisional government apparently will determine Mr. Bosch's fate. It was believed the deposed president, accused by the military of coddling Communists and leading the country toward economic chaos, would be exiled.

The new civilian junta dedicated itself to weeding out communism. Its members are Dr. Ramon Tapia Espinal, 40, a lawyer who was secretary of the interim Council of State that ruled after the assassination of dictator Rafael Trujillo; Emilio de los Santos, 65, an independent who was president of the Central Electoral Junta, and Manuel Tavares Espaillat, about 40, an industrial engineer.

The military junta jailed 31 persons as suspected Communists. Gen. Antonio Imbert Barrera, leader of Wednesday's coup, said, "We are after a lot more Communists. We are looking for them all."
