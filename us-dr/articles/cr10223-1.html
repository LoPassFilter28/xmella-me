Latin Nations Deplore 'Oppression' by Trujillo

By JOHN V. HORNER

Star Staff Writer

Dictator Rafael Leonidas Trujillo Molina was under some of the heaviest pressure of his political career today as diplomats throughout the Americas delved into charges that he flagrantly violates human rights in the Dominican Republic.

The general committee of the Organization of American States met here to explore accusations brought to its formal attention yesterday by Venezuela.

For the United States, Secretary of State Herter added weight to the inquiry with a blunt comment that "we have been very much disturbed" by reports of the mass arrests of Dominican citizens oposed to the dictatorial regime.

2,000 Reported Imprisoned

Recent reports have indicated that up to 2,000 persons—some from the country's most prominent families—have been thrown into concentration camps after being accused of plotting to overthrow the Trujillo government.

At home, Gen. Trujillo is smarting from a pastoral letter read in all 624 Catholic churches in the Dominican Republic January 31 clearly indicating that the Roman Catholic Episcopate sympathizes with those arrested and their families.

The letter, which said "a combination of human potentates" cannot impede the free exercise of human rights, was read in full to the OAS Council yesterday as Venezuelan Ambassador Marcos Falcon Briceno pressed the case against the Dominican regime.

Cuba Backs Charges

He asserted Gen. Trujillo has been guilty of "patent, flagrant, ugly violations of human rights in the Dominican Republic recently and over a period of many, many years."

Enrique Perez Cisneros, the Cuban representative to the OAS, said during yesterday's debate his government gives "complete backing to the grave accusations" made by Venezuela against the Trujillo regime.

On behalf of the Dominican Republic, Ambassador Virgilio Diaz Ordonez denied that any violations have occurred. He suggested that Venezuela brought the charges at the special meeting of the council in order to divert attention from its own domestic troubles "and the fact it collaborated in an invasion of our country last year."

Denies Mass Arrests

The Dominican envoy rejected the idea that thousands of persons have been arrested. The actual total, he said, is 123, and all of these were "submitted to legal processes, in accordance with our laws and constitution, with defense guaranteed."

Ambassador Falcon Briceno declared "no one on earth" could question the good faith or the facts of the pastoral letter.

The 11-member general committee of OAS is considering the Venezuela charges in executive session. Officials at the Pan American Union were uncertain how long they would remain in session and various diplomats acknowledged they are not sure what specifically could be done about the situation in the Dominican Republic.
