APPOINTED CONSUL Dr. Charles M. Hathaway Naval Academy Instructor Named By President Taft FOR DOMINICAN REPUBLIC A Scholarly Man Whose Good Work As Instructor Has Been Felt Here Among The Midshipmen

The Associated Press despatches on Thursday announced that President Taft had appointed Dr. Charles M. Hathaway, Jr., as consul of the United States at Puerto Plata in the Dominican Republic on the island of Hayti, and the second city in the country in population and in foreign commerce.

The Dominican Republic (or Santo Domingo as it is often called) was originally a Spanish colony, like most of the other West India Islands, and Spanish is still the language of the Dominican people. It was in the city of Santo Domingo, the capital, that the bones of Columbus were buried, and it was Columbus who struck by the beauty of the fertile interior plain of the island called it vega real (royal plain.)

It is a district famous for its fertility to this day. It finds the outlet of its products by a railroad to Puerto Plata. The Island of Hayti is separated only by a strait from Porto Rico, now a possession of the United States, and Dr. Hathaway, though stationed in a foreign land, will not have far to go far to see the stars and stripes again.

The appointment of Dr. Hathaway meets with general approval. Dr. Hathaway received his early education in the public schools of Blakely, Pa. Then he attended the School of Lackawana in Scranton. From there he went to Yale University, where he graduated in 1899. He then took three years of post-graduate study, receiving the degree of Doctor of Philosophy in 1902.

Since then he has taught English in Adelphi College, Brooklyn, N. Y., one year; in Columbia University, New York city, two years, and in the United States Naval Academy, six years. He has also, since the death of his father in 1906, been a member of the insurance firm of C. M. Hathaway & Co.

Dr. Hathaway has published, besides various articles of a scholarly nature, a book, Ben Johnson's Alchemist, edited with introduction, notes, and glossary (New York, Henry Holt & Company, 1902). The book was printed at the Clarendon Press, England, and is an excellent example of the fine printing of that famous printery.

Dr. Hathaway was married September 1, 1904, to Miss Frances Warner, daughter of Gen. A. J. Warner, of Ohio, who served several terms in Congress. They have for several weeks been visiting his mother, Mrs. Eliza G. Hathaway, at the family home on Lincoln avenue, Olyphant, Pa.

Dr. Hathaway retains his citizenship in the Second ward of Blakeley, where he cast his first vote in 1895, though of course the nature of his occupation has kept him away from home most of the time for some years past. He has not yet received his instructions from the Secretary of State, but expects to be ordered to Washington soon to spend a month in the State Department before going to his post.

Dr. Hathaway has many friends in Annapolis, who regret to see him leave here. He has been a most successful instructor to the midshipmen the past six years. His successor at the Naval Academy has not been appointed, as no official information to that effect has been received at the Superintendent's office here.
