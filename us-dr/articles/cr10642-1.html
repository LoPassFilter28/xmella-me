Haiti Marshals Forces Against Rebel Bands

PORT AU PRINCE, Haiti, Aug. 17 (AP).—The government marshaled its regular army and civilian armed forces today to combat rebel bands reported entrenched in the mountains near the Dominican border 75 miles northeast of here.

There was no official communique on the latest insurgent activity and no extraordinary security measures were visible in Port au Prince. However, reliable sources reported the government yesterday started moving troops and militia north towards the mountainous Mont Organise area into which Haitian exile invaders moved in a stab across the Dominican border late Wednesday.

A flurry of rumors also swept the city about violent reprisals against disloyal civilians in the northern area by Tonton Macoutes, President Francois Duvalier's special security force.

Official sources remained tight-lipped on operations.

Rebel Activity Admitted

One report received here said invaders struck across the Dominican border at the frontier town of Capotille, then swung south into Mont Organise, known as the historic starting point of other insurgent movements.

This report said the raiders struck at the town of Vallieres, 15 miles west of Mont Organise but withdrew into the hills under fire.

In what was interpreted as an admission of rebel activity on the border, the government controlled Radio Haiti accused the government of the neighboring Dominican Republic of offering moral and material help to opponents of President Duvalier to "stage frontier aggressions."

Radio Haiti claimed Radio Santo Domingo was trying to incite the Haitians.

Haiti and the Dominican Republic share the island of Hispaniola, and the Duvalier government has repeatedly accused President Juan Bosch of aiding exiles trying to topple the Haitian government.

Strafing Attacks Reported

In Santo Domingo, Dominicans returning from the interior reported observing two Haitian air force planes making several strafing runs near the Mont Organise area in the last 48 hours.

Reliable reports said rebel leader Gen. Leon Cantave, and his force of 250 men escaped injury, but an undisclosed number of civilians, including women and children, were injured.

There have been persistent reports that Gen. Cantave and his band of exiled Haitians who landed in Haiti August 5 in an effort to topple the Duvalier government had captured Mont Organise. The Haitian government, however, has not confirmed the reports.

Sources reported Gen. Cantave was building up strength in Mont Organise, a town deep in the rich coffee growing region. Mont Organise is located just across the Dominican border and has a population of between 2,000 and 3,000 persons.
