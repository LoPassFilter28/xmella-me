Dominican Formula On Government Set

SANTO DOMINGO, Dec. 9 (AP).—Viriato A. Fiallo, leader of the opposition National Civic Union, said today agreement has been reached with the government on a formula to solve the Dominican Republic's political crisis.

Mr. Fiallo said the accord would be made formal later today when representatives from both sides sign it.

Mr. Fiallo, who heads the chief opposition movement, said the formula calls for establishment of a seven-man Council of State, with President Joaquin Balaguer at the helm for a limited period.

The agreement excludes from the council members of the board of the opposition faction as well as persons connected with the government.

Keep Long Vigil

Agreement was finally reached about 2:30 a.m., after John Calvin Hill, United States Consul General, and Arturo Morales Carrion, United States Deputy Assistant Secretary of State, kept an all-night vigil on the negotiations.

Principal mediator in the crisis was Nicholas E. Pichardo, president of the University Professors' Association and one of the leaders of a crippling general strike called 12 days ago to bring pressure on the Balaguer government.

The official newspaper El Caribe reported earlier that an agreement in principle had been reached.

The opposition radio network, Voz Del Tropico, also announced the general strike had ended.

Hold Long Session

The reports followed last night's 12-hour negotiating session between government and opposition representatives. After the session recessed last night, the National Civic Union, largest of the opposition groups, said it would continue negotiations today despite an ultimatum that it would withdraw from the talks unless a settlement was reached.

Meanwhile, the United States announced it has withdrawn a fleet of warships from the vicinity of the Dominican Republic. The ships appeared off the coast shortly after two brothers of Slain Dictator Rafael Trujillo returned to the country apparently to try to restore a rightist dictatorship. The Trujillos, Hector and Jose Arismendi, were forced out of the country a few days later by an Air Force coup.

In Washington, United States Government sources declined to discuss the significance of recalling the ships, saying only they supposed the vessels were not considered necessary at this time. Some have suggested, however, that the presence of the warships might be interpreted as giving support to the Balaguer regime.

Take Over 15 Firms

The government, meantime, has taken over operations and financial control of 15 more business concerns formerly owned by the Trujillos or their associates, placing a total of 50 companies now under government operation.

The government ordered the assets of the last 15, including any abroad, be transferred to national banks.

It was estimated the 50 firms account for 40 per cent of the national income.
