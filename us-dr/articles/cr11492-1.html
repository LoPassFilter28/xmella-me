Latin America vs. Trujillo

By Harold Preece

The second anniversary of this column falls in the same week as the fourth anniversary of Pearl Harbor. Two years ago, on December 1, I began the column as a "good neighbor" feature to help some 14,000,000 Negroes of the United States get acquainted with some 30,000,000 Negroes of Latin America.

As a Southern white man, I'm grateful to the editors who publish this column for giving me an opportunity to help bring blood brothers together. In this new age following Pearl Harbor, I feel that all of us need to remember the words of the Apostle Paul and "strengthen the bonds of love" between all the races and nations and tongues of mankind.

This week, I am able to report that those bonds are being strengthened between the Negro people of the Dominican Republic and their Negro-Indian kinsmen in the rest of Latin America. Throughout Latin America, the peoples are rallying to help their brothers of that colored nation of the West Indies to get rid of a Negro quisling who remained Hitler's ally long after Pearl Harbor—"His Excellency Generalissimo Dr. Rafael Leonidas Trujillo Molina, Honorable Chief of State, Benefactor of the Nation, President and Dictator of the Dominican Republic."

American and Cuban Negro delegates to the recent World Youth Conference in London joined hands with young people of all colors from the rest of the earth to exclude two stooge delegates of Dictator Trujillo. Those boys and girls at London, representing the awakening youth of the world, had lost brothers and friends in that greatest of all human tragedies which destroyed millions of their generation.

TRUJILLO BUTCHERED NEGROES

They had no use for the representatives of a traitor Negro who had butchered thousands of other Negroes from Haiti in the border massacre of 1937, who had taken direct bribes from Hitler and, who, while receiving lend lease funds from the United States, had cooperated with Hitler secretly to rig the Chilean presidential election in favor of a pro-Nazi candidate, who had even acted as paymaster for Nazi submarine spies seeking to blow up transportation and communication facilities in this country.

"As you can imagine, the two stooges who showed up from the Dominican Republic didn't want to be identified as Negroes," one of the American delegates to the Conference, Miss Esther V. Cooper, of the Southern Negro Youth Congress, wrote me from London. "Their boss who was born of poor Negro farmers, and who became a dictator with the connivance of the American marines after a long criminal record, has also ceased to be a Negro. He now proclaims himself to be a descendant of 'Spanish conquistadores.'"

"Had real representatives of the Dominican youth—Negro, white, or both—showed up, we would have welcomed them. As it was, admitting Trujillo's apprentice fuehrers would have been like admitting those Nazi war criminals who will be on trial for their lives at Nuremburg by the time that you receive this letter."

Well, those criminals are now facing curtains and coffins before the international tribunal of an outraged world there in Nuremburg. And, morally, Dictator Trujillo is on trial by the outraged nations of the Americas.

Venezuela, whose Negro oil workers furnished fuel for the ships which Nazi submarines operating from Dominican harbors tried to destroy, had practically broken all relations with Trujillo long before its recent revolution. The Venezuelan congress had passed a resolution condemning the bloody tyrant who helped Hitler organize the first unit of the Nazi party on North American soil as far back as 1930. A committee of Venezuelan senators and representatives was set up to help the people of the Dominican Republic win back their freedom.

The new president, Ramon Betancourt, realizing that anybody in his country who courts Trujillo courts destruction, said in the national capitol at Caracas, that Venezuela would not recognize this bloodthirsty Nero who has killed hundreds of his countrymen or his "assassins of liberty."

PLOTTED MURDER AND WAR

President Betancourt just wasn't being oratorical when he used the word "assassins." According to documentary evidence in my possession, Trujillo recently hired gangsters to plan the assassination of Haitian President Elie Lescot. If this could have been accomplished, Trujillo, anxious to own the whole island, would have sent his troops into Haiti backed up, so it is said, with Nazi money, Nazi guns, and Nazi equipment delivered to him by German submarines before the fall of Hitler.

In Cuba, a movement to help the Dominican people has developed under the leadership of one of the country's leading statesmen, Senator Eduardo Chibas. Senator Chibas, during a recent visit to Washington, said point-blank that the present Dominican regime "was an obstacle to democracy in the Americas."

In this country, liberals are demanding that Trujillo receive no more American subsidies and that we withdraw our recognition of the man who started his political career by acting as a stool pigeon for the U. S. marines against his patriotic countrymen.

On this fourth anniversary of Pearl Harbor, it looks like taps for Trujillo.
