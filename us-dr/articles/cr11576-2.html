Gay Legation Party Marks Dominicans' Anniversary

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

Well, yesterday another Republic on this side of the world celebrated its independence. The Minister of the Dominican Republic and Senora de Pastoriza outdid themselves in the celebration of the 95th anniversary of the day Spain lost another of its colonies.

Capital society whipped itself up in its regal best, with the women delighted for an excuse to sport new spring bonnets even in this first week of Lent. We do all try to be awfully good about the penitential season, and for about three weeks we put most functions down to officialdom. After that we just give up, and by Easter there is definitely an anti-climax for the "little season."

So officials, diplomats, and that great mass of people who would come under the banner of "smart resident society" wended their way to the legation on 16th-st where the Pastorizas occupy an entire floor of what used to be the old Warder House in the early days of the century when it stood on lower K-st, until the late Col. George Oakley Totten moved the mansion, wall by wall, to a more desirable location.

ALMOST DEPARTED

Then, too, the party yesterday was a sort of re-welcome for the Pastorizas and a gay way of returning all the farewell parties given for them several months back. You'll remember that during the autumn they were feted and feted because Senor Pastoriza had been ordered to London, and only on the very eve of his departure were his orders revoked—et voila, he is still here.

Now a lot of people who drank the excellent champagne, sipped the whisky sodas, nipped at the lavish buffet, or simply settled down to eat their supper from the selection of cold meats, salads and ices, didn't even know what they were celebrating. That's the way it goes here. We celebrate, congratulate and give thanks—for what? Well, most of the time we don't know. But just so you can be vaguely intelligent about the situation here's what it was:

On Feb. 27, 1844, Santo Domingo won its independence from Spain, thanks to the vision and idealism of Juan Pablo Duarte, the heroism of Francisco del Rosario Sanchez, and the valor of Ramon Mella. All of those men were instrumental in attaining the goal of emancipation. That little strip of land which is the Dominican Republic was first colonized by Bartolome, brother of Christopher Columbus, in 1496. Great boys, those Columbus lads.

Yesterday was also another anniversary, the first, of the inauguration of the Port of Trujillo City, a work instigated by the former President, Leonidas Trujillo.

Now we don't know much more about Santo Domingo, or perhaps they prefer it to be called the Dominican Republic, than you do. But there is a very strategic body of water right there called Samana Bay. And with our own President cruising around those waters, it is not unlikely that ere he returns to Washington next week, Mr. Roosevelt may put in at Samana Bay, and pay a visit to the island republic.

What for? Well, the Cuban government some time ago negotiated with us and in consequence we have the use of the famous Guantanamo Bay for a fleet harbor. Perhaps it might not be a bad idea if we could also manage the use of Samana Bay for just exactly that same reason. The Santo Dominicans couldn't mind the proximity of the United States Navy, nor could we object to another snug harbor for our ships.

FRIENDLY ATMOSPHERE

But none of this was mentioned yesterday, of course. It was purely a social rally. But there was a very friendly atmosphere which permeated all 800 of the guests who came and went from 5 until 7 o'clock; who shook the hand of Senora de Pastoriza as she stood, costumed in a black crepe frock embroidered with pale pink sequins, and with the smiling Minister, who drank those gay toasts to independence as some of the relatives of former President Trujillo (he was and perhaps still is the strong man of Santo Domingo) looked on, and smiled at the friendliness of relations. A lot of other envoys can't be so at ease when "official relatives" are on hand.
