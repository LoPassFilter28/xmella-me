How a Navy Reservist Spent the Summer

Jess Nettles, who served in the Navy, became a member of the Navy Reserve and a resident of Chapel Hill, and was a freshman in the University last year, went to New Orleans in June and boarded the U. S. destroyer Compton for a summer training cruise.

He worked in the radio room of the ship at the same job that he had done in his regular Navy service. The ship stopped for a day at the big base at Guantanamo, Cuba. It visited the port of Ciudad Trujillo, Dominican Republic. And it took part in maneuvers with three other destroyers on the way back to New Orleans. The crew had plenty of work to do.

Nettles returned to Chapel Hill to vote for Frank Graham in the run-off primary; went to western North Carolina for a week's vacation and picked up his car that he had left there; drove via Atlanta, Montgomery, New Orleans, Houston, and Laredo to Saltillo in Mexico, a mile-high mountain city of 70,000; enrolled in the summer school there to study Spanish for six weeks; studied not only in the school but an extra three hours a day under a tutor; visited Monterrey on a week-end excursion; and went on two other excursions into mountain country.

He saw a bull-fight which he describes as spectacular.

After completing his course of study at Saltillo he drove back to North Carolina and attended the annual homecoming celebration at his high school alma mater at Union Mills in the North Carolina mountains. Then he came back to Chapel Hill and picked up a Durham newspaper to read that he was one of the Navy reservists in this area who had been called to active duty.

Because of his absence another man had been sent in his place. "I will probably be with the next group called," he said yesterday. But there is uncertainty about that. If the call does not come before the beginning of the fall term he will go ahead and re-enter the University as he intended to do when he left here in June.
