Exclusively Yours—By Betty Beale Generalissimo Trujillo Entertains Fifty at Luncheon On His 300-Foot Yacht; Plans Big Party in January

Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, who admits he's a dictator—a benevolent dictator, that is—dispensed benevolent hospitality yesterday on his luxurious yacht that left his Washington guests wide-eyed.

Two of my columnist colleagues leaned back on a divan on the aft-erdeck. Overhead was a deep blue canvas to keep off the hot sun, underfoot was a deep pile specially designed carpet. Balmy river breezes swept through the glistening white 300-foot yacht and caressed their brows; tall drinks were carried to them from the super special mahogany bar.

"You two never had it so good," someone quipped. "No, and we're getting paid for it," they grinned back, looking at it from the esthetic side as usual.

The luncheon aboard the Canadian-built frigate, El Presidente Trujillo, drew about 50 guests down to Pier 5, Maine avenue, at the early hour of 11 a.m. Nobody objected though. Sitting on a yacht that sports a crew of 85 is so much better than sitting at a typewriter.

It was mostly newsmen and women who walked up the gangplank and were ushered by the Generalissimo's aides into the richly paneled dining lounge where he and Ambassador Manuel de Moya were receiving. The latter did the interpreting for his boss, who doesn't speak English as well as his wife, Mme. Trujillo arrived later and mingled with the guests on the deck.

They say she's not overly fond of the yacht—gets a bit of mal de mer on it. Their 15-year-old daughter Angelita and 11-year-old son Ramades were also on board. And there was a huge photograph of their 25-year-old son who is already chief of staff of the air corps.

Non-press people invited included General Trujillo's old friend, former Ambassador Joe Davies and his daughter, Rachel Fitch; Dr. William Morgan, for whom the hospital in Ciudad Trujillo is named, and his wife Elizabeth; and from the State Department—Chief of Protocol Jack Simmons, Director of the Middle American Division and Mrs. Robert Newbegin, his deputy, Jack Neal, and his wife, and Charles Whittaker, who has to do with Dominican affairs.

Gen. and Mrs. Wade Hampton Haislip, Mr. and Mrs. Samuel H. Kauffmann, Dennie Rumbough, the Robert LeBarons and the Bill Hapleys were also in the group that helped themselves to a buffet luncheon on the upper deck. Lamb chops, turkey, ham, vegetable salad, rice and quantities of other dishes ornamented the big table.

Yours truly wandered below to see what was cooking and found the Generalissimo and the Ambassador having a bite alone in the paneled dining lounge. They drew up another chair and next thing you knew there was merengue music emanating from the victrola and yours truly was twirling first with the Ambassador and then with Gen. Trujillo. Both are expert hoofers, even on a plush carpet. And who would have thought yesterday morning that by mid-day I'd be cutting a rug with a dictator?

Who would be the next ambassador here? It might be Joaquim Salazar, now consul general in New York and former Ambassador to the U. N. At least he would like to have the post, indicated his boss.

When he returns, Trujillo will stay at the embassy on Edgevale terrace, but his wife doesn't like it, he said; she wants to be closer to town. On January 27, he and Ambassador de Moya will have a huge, gay party at the Embassy. "You mean Manuel is not leaving the end of November after all but will still be here in January?" "Yes." . . . Believe me, dictators are as unpredictable as women are supposed to be.

Intermittent press conferences held before lunch was served informed us that a world's fair of free nations will open in Ciudad Trujillo on its namesake's birthday, October 24, 1955; that invitations to participate went out to envoys of other nations here this week; that the Dominican Republic owes nary a cent to any country in the world; that the Generalissimo's confidential files on communism in Latin America have been offered to the House Investigating Committee, and so forth.

There's no question about it. The boss of the little Caribbean republic is setting about to remove the American stigma on dictators. Another party on that yacht would probably do it.
