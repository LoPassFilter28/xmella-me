Foreign Notables Continue to Keep Capital on the Jump

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

Honestly there really never is a time for rest in Washington. Either things are hitting on all six or else things of interest are so imminent that you just don't dare relax for fear of missing some of the excitement. Now here we are, pushing right into the middle of June when everybody ought to be settling down for a calm life. If not for an actual vacation, well anyhow the easiest way to live thru the heat.

But not the Capital! We get so hardened to fame and fortune that anybody less than the head of a foreign government fails to rouse our emotions. Of course, after last week's visitors, most things—at least for a few years to come—will definitely be in the anti-climax case.

That was the peak. We worked up to it thru a visiting crown prince and couple of prime ministers and a neighboring president. Oh, yes, and we did also have a dictator. Now we are going to taper off in much the same manner. But it's still exciting, and we still glean a good deal from the visits.

PARIS DECREES CUT

We've seen history made and fashions set, such as parasols and longer skirts. Catch anybody who saw the Queen listening to a Paris decree for shorter and shorter hemlines! Try and find even the vaguest beauty who will toast herself to an unprotected coffee color. Not after that peaches and cream coloring we've just had a dose of! We've learned royal address and statesmen's parlance.

We're all equipped to receive bigger and better comers. Yet we've reached the apex of the social order. There's really no plaec to go from here. It is summer and we're pining for nothing more stimulating than a morning on the beach and a Tom Collins before luncheon.

Why only yesterday the President, weary with wining and dining, pulled himself together and gave a luncheon for a president-elect. One who's been in these parts for quite a while, and passed rather unnoticed until he got himself elevated to the presidency of Paraguay.

HOMEY AFFAIR

It was a nice, homey little luncheon given for Gen. Jose Felix Estigarribia, who has been serving as his country's envoy. Conversations, however desultory they may have been, certainly turned to Paraguay and her problems. Secretary of State Hull shook the weariness of royalty from his shoulders and chatted friendly relations. Secretary of the Treasury Morgenthau discussed high finance, perhaps even loans; the Secretary of Agriculture, inquired for the crops; Sen. Key Pittman and Rep. Sol Bloom were there, both of 'em well nigh ubiquitous; White House Secretary Steve Early, Sumner Welles—also on the ubquitous side; R. F. C. Chairman Jesse Jones, and the President's military and navy aides understanding better than anybody else the lingo of the General.

But on and on it goes. Next week we'll have the whole military works repeated for the Brazilian Chief of Staff, Gen. Jose Monteiro. The General will be here two days and to date the ladies don't have to worry about his coming. Entertainments in his honor are on a strictly military basis, with Gen. Malin Craig, our own Chief of Staff, playing the host.

Then again there comes word that another dictator, in the person of Generalissimo Trujillo, will soon be on his way here from the Dominican Republic. More Army activity, and also a detachment of Marines. Remember the General was once a Marine and still thinks they're a pretty swell bunch of fellas.

To climax what has really been a hell-bent-for-bigger-and-better-visitors-year the youthful Crown Prince and Crown Princess of Norway will round out their two-months tour of this country with a two-day visit to the Capital the end of June. Then, and only then, will society ease up on the reins, take the bit in its teeth and go wherever it pleases, willy-nilly, and protocol to the winds until next autumn.

TICKLISH SITUATION

One thing in the way of excitement which we have missed is the meeting of Senor Don Juan Francisco de Cardenas, envoy of Nationalist Spain, and Alvarez del Vayo, former Minister of Foreign Affairs of Republican Spain!

That meeting will probably take place some time this week aboard the good ship Normandie on which both men are sailing today for Europe. It's a big ship, but chances are that every ounce of the well-known French diplomacy will be at play either keeping those two fellows dodging each other, or seeing that no violence occurs when they meet.

Rep. and Mrs. Karl Stefan have as their guests at the Mayflower Mr. and Mrs. Donald Mapes, of Norfolk, Neb., who will go from here to Richmond, Va., and thence to New York.

Senhora Martins, wife of the Brazilian Ambassador, entertained at a small tea yesterday at the embassy. This afternoon the Naval Attache of the Embassy and Senhora de Araujo will entertain at a cocktail party in their home on Macomb-st.

The Minister of Portugal and Mme. de Bianchi are spending a few days in New York for the official opening of the Portugal Day ceremonies at the World's Fair.
