Inventory of Latin American Dictatorships

There is special inter-American political significance in the statements made by the Secretary of State of the United States, Christian Herter, with regard to the listing of the three remaining Latin American dictatorships, which he mentioned by name before the U. S. Congress in Washington: those of Nicaragua, Dominican Republic and Paraguay.

Disregarding any remarks made on the possibility that two of those dictatorships could "democratize", the fact that the Secretary of State said that the regimes existing in Nicaragua, Dominican Republic and Paraguay are dictatorial, has an importance perhaps greater than could be attributed to it at first sight, because it is the first time that in a categoric way the State Department mentiones with proper names the type of government existing in the those countries. And this, of course, should mean that the Washington Government will be exceedingly cautious in its relations with the Somoza, Trujillo and Stroessner regimes.

For sure, the White House envoys before those three governments must have taken due note of the classification made by the Secretary of State with reference to the ideology and system of those regimes.

The United States Ambassadors in those countries have to be very tactful in their official and personal relations with the rulers of those nations, to avoid that the excessive cordiality may create the impression that Washington supports dictatorships. And it must not be forgotten that, unfortunately, in some cases there has been and still there is excessive cordiality, which the dictatorships exploit politically. And those Envoys cannot say they are not officially informed that they are accredited before dictatorial regimes, for the Secretary of State himself was the one who, through his statements before Congress, told them so.
