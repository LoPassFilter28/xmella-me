Curtis Considers New Job; Brother-in-Law Loses One

Ex-Vice President Ponders Offer of Job to Represent Notorious 'Goat Gland Doctor' in Mexican Radio Case

By GEORGE ABELL

FORMER Vice President Charlie Curtis is considering a visit to Mexico City to represent Dr. William Brinkley, "goat gland doctor" and former candidate for the governorship of Kansas, in a radio litigation case.

If Charlie decides to go, he will leave some time next week.

"There's nothing definite yet. I won't know until Monday," he said yesterday. "People have been calling up all day to ask me about it."

Despite the fact that Charlie is now president of the New Mexico Gold Producers Corporation he is keeping up his law practice with great enthusiasm. Recently he confided that he had never enjoyed himself more.

"I like to be busy," he explained. "And now I always have a lot to do."

Dr. Brinkley, known thruout the U. S. as the "goat gland doctor," has attracted much notoriety because of his unorthodox political and medical views. His campaign for the governorship of Kansas, in 1932, was sensational. He ran up a huge number of votes and was very nearly elected, but lost his license to operate a radio station in this country.

At present the goat gland specialist operates a huge radio station in Mexico and the litigation which Charlie may participate in concerns Brinkley's efforts to strengthen his network. Stations as far north as New York have complained against Brinkley's tactics.

TRUJILLO TACTICS

CHARLIE'S brother-in-law, Mr. Edward Everett Gann, has lost one job because of the change in administration.

During the Hoover-Curtis regime Mr. Gann served (for a reported fee of $400 a month) as Washington representative of the Trujillo government at Santo Domingo. Dictator Trujillo is now said to be seeking a Democratic connection in the person of Joe Davies, Washington lawyer.

Davies served as Federal Trade Commissioner under the Wilson Administration, acted as counsel for the Mexican government in 1920 and for Peru in the Tacna-Arica controversy. He is now serving as counsel for Sen. James J. Davis in lottery charges against the Senator.

Davies recently returned from a visit to Santo Domingo, making the trip by plane. He was accompanied by his daughter and Senor Ulises Espaillat, counselor for the Dominican Legation here.

Newspapers in Santo Domingo made much of Davies' visit as "an influential Democrat at Washington", and of the reception given him by President Trujillo. They reported Davies as saying Trujillo has many friends in Washington, "especially among the Marines, having been at one time an officer in that branch of the U. S. military."

(Trujillo never was a U. S. Marine officer, but during the military occupation of the republic, while many Dominicans were resentful and humiliated, he enlisted in the Guardia Nacional Dominicana which was officered by the U. S. Marines.)

Diplomatic opinion here is that even if Counselor Espaillat is successful in the present instance, he will not be rewarded by the coveted post of Dominican Minister to Washington.

The recent reappointment of William Pulliam as Collector General of Customs in Santo Domingo—after Espaillat had declared him "persona non grata" there—has proved a severe blow to many political aspirations.
