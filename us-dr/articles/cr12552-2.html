Gen. Trujillo Expected To Resume Presidency

CIUDAD TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic, Feb. 8 (AP).

Generalissimo Rafael Leonidas Trujillo soon may return to the presidency of this Caribbean republic where he has held absolute power for 30 years, informed sources said today.

They believe the Generalissimo wants to return to the helm in name as well as in fact because of the recent discovery of a plot against the regime. His return would be designed as a psychological boost for his regime and a reassertion of his authority.

Gen. Trujillo's younger brother, Hector Trujillo, has been nominal President since 1951.

Attorney General Luis Suero says 123 persons were arrested in the plot and their cases are being tried normally, with the defendants being given the right of defense counsel. Sentences of 30 years have been handed out to 40 defendants so far.

Arrests Put at 2,000

(Well informed sources from the Dominican Republic say the number of those arrested is closer to 2,000. They say those held include engineers, doctors, former diplomats and others tired of the regime and worsening economic conditions.)

Col. John W. Abbes Garcia, chief of the military intelligence service, charged the plot originated with the Cuban regime of Prime Minister Fidel Castro, arch-foe of Gen. Trujillo.

Col. Abbes said the plotters planned to detonate about 200 bombs in buildings in this capital. He claimed the plot was crushed and both the bombs and the plotters were seized.

Pastoral Letter Read

A pastoral letter from the Roman Catholic Episcopate of the Dominican Republic, read in the 624 Catholic churches throughout the country last Sunday, was considered a declaration of human rights and a display of solidarity with the families of those arrested.

The letter mentioned "sufferings which afflict the hearts of so many Dominicans" and said "a combination of human potentates" cannot impede the free exercise of human rights.

Circulated abroad, the letter was published here for the first time yesterday in the interdiocesan weekly "Fides." With the letter was published this note:

"The archbishops and bishops of the Dominican Republic in the first episcopal meeting of 1960 . . . ask for all priests to abstain from any intervention of a political character or which could alter public order incompatible with the mission of the sacred priestly ministry or with religious life."

Emmissary Sent to Vatican

Possible concern over the impact of the letter on the predominantly Roman Catholic Dominican Republic was seen in the dispatch of Foreign Minister Porfirio Herrera Baez to Rome for a conference with the Vatican Secretariat of State.

(In Rome, however, Mr. Herrera denied yesterday he had talked to Vatican officials about the pastoral letter. He insisted there were no serious difficulties between the Dominican government and the church.)

The early return of Gen. Trujillo to the presidency was seen by observers in several events last week.

The press and radio embarked on a big campaign to persuade the 68-year-old strongman to run for the presidency in 1962. A giant rally to support him was called for February 21.
