The Times prints a Washington item: Minister Powell advisedEl Washington Times publica una nota de Washington sobre el avance de Jiménez

The Washington Times. Washington [D.C.], December 27, 1903. Page 2. View the originalVer el original

The Times prints a Washington item: Minister Powell advised the State Department yesterday that Jiminez and his followers are now moving upon Santo Domingo from Macoris, a city which is friendly to the Jiminez movement, and that the provisional president is still in charge of the government and is preparing to make a stubborn resistance to the movement against the Dominican capital. Jiminez is a disgruntled follower of General Morales; he assisted in the movement against Woss y Gil, who was overthrown several weeks ago; the provisional government was not organized to suit Jiminez and he left the capital and organized the forces which are now threatening Santo Domingo. Tourists from Santo Domingo say the republic is a hopeless mass of revolutions, which cannot be checked until a foreign power takes a hand; even in Puerto Plata, the home of General Morales, a strong Jiminez party has sprung up which is making trouble; Morales is threatening to send warships to check the movement against him, but it is so general that he will doubtless find much trouble in controlling it.El Washington Times publica una nota de Washington: el ministro Powell avisó ayer al Departamento de Estado que Jiménez y sus seguidores avanzan ahora sobre Santo Domingo desde Macorís, ciudad favorable al movimiento de Jiménez, y que el presidente provisional sigue a cargo del gobierno y se prepara para oponer una tenaz resistencia al movimiento contra la capital dominicana. Jiménez es un seguidor descontento del general Morales; ayudó en el movimiento contra Woss y Gil, que fue derrocado hace varias semanas; el gobierno provisional no se organizó al gusto de Jiménez, que abandonó la capital y organizó las fuerzas que ahora amenazan a Santo Domingo. Turistas procedentes de Santo Domingo dicen que la república es una masa desesperada de revoluciones, que no podrá contenerse hasta que una potencia extranjera intervenga; incluso en Puerto Plata, la tierra del general Morales, ha surgido un fuerte partido de Jiménez que está causando problemas; Morales amenaza con enviar buques de guerra para frenar el movimiento en su contra, pero este es tan general que sin duda le costará mucho controlarlo.
