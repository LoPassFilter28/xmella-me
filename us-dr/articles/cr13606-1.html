The Washington Times · December 27, 190327 de diciembre de 1903

Original in English.Original en inglés.

CANNOT KEEP TRACK OF SOUTHERN REVOLTUIONS

State Department officials have ceased to try to follow political movements in Santo Domingo. Revolutionary movements are so numerous and complex that it is impossible for one to understand them who is not in the turbulent republic.

Minister Powell advised the State Department yesterday that Jiminez and his followers are now moving upon Santo Domingo from Macoris, a city which is friendly to the Jiminez movement. Wos y Gil is still preparing to send an expedition from Porto Rico to overthrow the provisional government. General Morales, the acting president, is still in charge of the government, and is preparing to make a stubborn resistance to the movement against the Dominican capital.

Jiminez is a disgruntled follower of General Morales. He assisted in the movement against Wos y Gil, who was overthrown several weeks ago. The provisional government was not organized to suit Jiminez and he left the capital and organized the forces which are now threatening Santo Domingo.

Tourists from Santo Domingo say the republic is a hopeless mass of revolutions, which cannot be checked until a foreign power takes a hand. Even in Puerto Plata, the home of General Morales, a strong Jiminez party has sprung up which is making trouble. Morales is threatening to send warships to check the movement against him, but it is so general that he will doubtless find much trouble in controlling it.
