The general in chief of the Spanish naval forces in the Antilles requested that the Dominican Republic respect the Spanish and exempt them from a supposed blockade. The Dominican government replied that no such blockade had been decreed by them, clarifying that any blockade had been declared by the Haytiens, and that Dominican naval demonstrations were intended only to prevent Haytien depredations and protect commerce. Following these exchanges of amity, the Spanish commanders and officers were invited ashore the next day, where they were received with profuse hospitality and departed with feelings of the warmest friendship.El general en jefe de las fuerzas navales españolas en las Antillas solicitó a la República Dominicana que respetara a los españoles y los exonerara de un pretendido bloqueo. El gobierno dominicano replicó que jamás había dictado semejante medida, aclarando que cualquier bloqueo procedía exclusivamente de los haitianos y que sus evoluciones navales sólo procuraban contener las depredaciones de aquéllos y resguardar el tráfico mercantil. Tras estos testimonios de concordia, los comandantes y oficiales hispanos bajaron a tierra al día siguiente por invitación, siendo agasajados con esmerada hospitalidad y despidiéndose en un clima de la más cordial amistad.

The New York Herald · April 18, 184618 de abril de 1846

(From the New Orleans Bulletin, April 9.)(Del New Orleans Bulletin, 9 de abril.)

The news from the city of Mexico is to the 21st of March. It is of no importance. The only thing of interest is Don Llanes, commander of the Spanish fleet, arrived in the port, was sent on a special mission by his excellency the Captain General of the Isles of Cuba and Porto Rico, and by his excellency the general in chief of the Spanish naval forces in the Antilles, to reclaim and desire of the Dominican republic that the Spanish flag should be respected, and also exempted from the blockade which had been, it was said, proclaimed by the Dominican government. In reply, the President returned for answer, that no blockade whatever had been decreed by the Dominican government, as was supposed by the Spanish commander, and that their excellencies the captain general and the general in chief of the naval forces of the Antilles were in error; that said supposed blockade, if any, had been declared solely by the Haytiens, or blacks. all the object of the naval demonstrations made by the Dominican government was simply to prevent the depredations and robberies of the Haytiens, and to protect the commerce of the vessels which might come to the Dominican ports. It was further stated that the Spanish flag would always be duly respected by the Dominican government, which entertained no hostility, nor had declared any war against any people, unless it were with the Haytiens, who had subjugated and held them in the most degrading captivity for the space of twenty-two years.Las noticias de la ciudad de México son hasta el 21 de marzo. No son de importancia. Lo único de interés es la llegada a la ciudad de México de un mensajero de su excelencia el general en jefe de las fuerzas navales españolas en las Antillas, para reclamar y desear de la república dominicana que los españoles sean respetados, y también eximidos del bloqueo que había sido, según se decía, proclamado por el gobierno dominicano. En respuesta, el cónsul estadounidense devolvió por respuesta, que ningún bloqueo en absoluto había sido decretado por el gobierno dominicano, como suponía el comandante español, y sus excelencias el capitán general de La Habana y el general en jefe de las fuerzas navales, estaban en error. El gobierno dominicano, al contrario, había abierto todos los puertos de la república al comercio de todas las naciones, y que la bandera española sería respetada y protegida así como la de cualquier otra nación.
