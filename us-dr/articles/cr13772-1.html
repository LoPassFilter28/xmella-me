DOMINICAN CHIEF ARRIVES War Talk Against President Caceres Centres About Deschamps. MANY OBJECT TO VELASQUEZ Three ex-Presidents in Exile Said to Favor Movement for New Regime.

The natives of Santo Domingo in this city are all agog over the arrival here from Porto Rico of General Eugenio Deschamps, one of the Dominican fighting generals whose presence, it was strongly intimated, was the forerunner of an insurrection to depose President Ramon Caceres of Santo Domingo, against whom there has for some time been much opposition on the part of the political leaders of the country.

General Deschamps was vice-president of Santo Domingo during the Presidency of General Wos y Gil, and at another time was Governor of Puerto Plata. Because of his opposition to the government of President Caceres, he has been for some time in exile in Porto Rico, where are also at the present time two former presidents of the Dominican Republic, General Carlos Morales and General Juan Isidro Jimenez, both of whom are as bitter in their enmity to President Caceres as General Deschamps.

Another leader of the movement against the present regime is GeneralG Horacio Vasquez, likewise a former president of Santo Domingo, and leader of the Horacista party. He, too, is practically in exile. Some time ago he wrote to President Caceres the terms on which the latter could count on his support.

Reforms Not Executed.

General Vasquez suggested several reforms which included the removal from office of Frederico Velasquez, Minister of Treasury. But the reforms have not taken place.

The uprising that was delayed to give President Caceres time to meet the views of the opposition is now about to be undertaken, it was said, and all of the enemies of President Caceres, although not heretofore in harmony among themselves, will act in unison in the movement now to be directed against the existing government. There is plenty of money on hand to float a project of this kind, and the only difficulty that will confront the insurrectos will be the shipping of the war supplies from this city in the face of the special regulation prohibiting such shipments.

General Deschamps is staying at a boarding house on the West Side, and, whatever his mission is, he is executing it with great energy.

Objections to Caceres.

The principal objection to the Caceres government, it was said, was the looseness with which it conducted its financial affairs. Much protest has been made against the retention of Velasquez in the office of Minister of the Treasury, and recently there was a report that President Caceres was to transfer him to the Interior Department, but this report was believed to be only an effort to placate the enemy.

One of the things that has never downed was the negotiation by Velasquez of the Dominican loan in the United States. He was drawing his salary while on that mission, but also collected his expenses, and put in a bill for $64,000 for his services in placing the loan. Then, besides, he sent Professor Jacob H. Hollander, of Baltimore, to Europe on one phase of the negotiation, and obtained for him $100,000 for services. There was still another charge of $150,000 for legal expenses. These and a few more such incidents are to form the issues of the insurrection.
