Cuban Senators Link Grau With Abortive Dominican Invasion

By the Associated Press

HAVANA, Cuba, Oct. 1.—Two Cuban Senators accused President Grau San Martin last night of complicity in the organization of an expeditionary force which intended—before it was broken up by the Cuban Army and Navy—to overthrow the government of the Dominican Republic.

Senator Eduardo Chibas, an aspirant for the presidency in the June, 1948, elections, declared in a statement published in the newspaper Prensa Libre that the Cuban President "betrayed the cause of Dominican liberty after having offered hospitality and refuge to the political exiles of this sister republic."

Move for Dictatorship Charged.

"Grau, upon the pretext of liberating the Dominican Republic from the dictator (President Rafael) Trujillo, was conspiring to suspend the forthcoming elections and convert himself into the dictator of Cuba," Senator Chibas' statement said.

The Senator previously had been considered one of the President's staunchest defenders.

Senator Aurelio Alvarez, leader of the opposition, accused President Grau of having "armed gangs which sought to invade the Dominican Republic, in order to create an atmosphere of violence in Cuba and thus perpetuate himself in power."

The army announced Monday that it had crushed the ambitious expeditionary force—numbering about 1,500 men with four vessels and a considerable amount of arms, ammunition and explosives—which had been organized in Cuba in July to invade the Dominican Republic.

360 Detained in Prison.

Some 360 alleged members of the expedition were detained in the prison at Camp Columbia, army headquarters in Havana, and 781 other men described as members of the expedition were reported en route here by train from Antilla, where they surrendered aboard two of the expedition's ships yesterday.

Some of the expedition's leaders were reported to be among this latter group:

Col. Oscar Diaz, who made the army's announcement concerning the crushing of the expedition Monday, was quoted under banner headlines in the Cuban press as having said the expedition also had sought to stage "a revolutionary coup (in Cuba) to prevent the elections."

President Grau summoned reporters to the presidential palace yesterday and informed them that Col. Diaz had denied making this statement.
