REVOLUTION SET FOR TO-DAY IN SANTO DOMINGO. Friends of Don Juan Isidora Jiminez Plan to Oust President Figuereo and Put Him in Supreme Control. Maximo Gomez's Secretary Says It Will Be Necessary for a Humane Power to Interfere. Secretary of Interior Leuberes, Who Is in New York, Does Not Fear a Revolt Nor American Intervention.

CAPE HAYTIEN, July 31.—From a dispatch just received from a reliable source, it is learned that a revolution will be proclaimed to-morrow or the day after to-morrow in the Republic of Santo Domingo in favor of Don Juan Isidro Jiminez.

According to this dispatch the entire western portion of the republic has declared in favor of Jiminez; and he is the only candidate for the presidency throughout the rest of the country.

MINISTER LLUBERES PRAISES FIGUEREO.

Senor Pedro Lluberes, Minister of the Interior of Santo Domingo, arrived in this city Sunday night on the steamship Caracas from St. John, Porto Rico. He will rest here several days before leaving for Europe.

Senor Lluberes left Santo Domingo before the assassination of President Heureaux, and was shocked on learning the news.

In regard to the ferment caused by the death of the President and the revolution which has subsequently started, Senor Lluberes said to a Journal representative last night:

“The report of a revolution in my country, which you bring me, is news to me. I am in a position to say, however, that nothing serious will result from it. I can say, too, that General Wenseslau Figuereo, who succeeds General Hereaux as President, is strong enough to protect American interests.

“The dispatching of war ships by the United States to the scene of disturbance is a natural movement of a country in a case of this kind. There are many Americans in Santo Domingo who have large interests there, and the action of this country in sending war ships will be regarded by my countrymen as a precautionary measure.”

Senor Lluberes was very evasive when asked his opinion of a possibility of annexation of the Dominican Republic by the United States, and how his people would receive such a proposition. He would only say that he had never heard the subject discussed in his country, and that it would be a question to be settled only by the people.

“I do not believe the report that the real mission of the New Orleans and Machias is to force the annexation of Santo Domingo,” he said.

“The probability of General Maximo Gomez becoming President of Santo Domingo is something that I cannot discuss beyond saying that the only way he can become the chief executive at this time is by winning the office through revolution.

“Whether he intends this move I can't say. The term of President Figuereo will end in about a year, and although I think that he will give satisfaction to all parties the field will then be open for candidates for the place, including General Gomez.”
