REPUBLIC OF HAITI A MORTGAGED ESTATE Affairs in Island in Desperate Condition—Natives Threaten Massacre of Foreigners—Powers Urge a Protectorate.

"Haiti is not a republic. It is a mortgaged estate.

"Foreign bankers hold the reins of government and the President is at their mercy."

Such is the statement of a prominent American who has just returned from the Black Republic, where the demonstration against Syrians recently threatened to develop into a move against all foreigners.

Massacre Is Feared.

"A general massacre of whites is feared," the American continued. "The alarming statements of President Nord have frightened all foreigners. Discouraged and broken-hearted because of the deplorable condition of the republic's finances, the old man announced that he would defend the government against the foreigners, who have forced the rate of exchange up to 600. He even mentioned the massacre of 1804, when the blacks killed all the whites in Haiti."

Santo Domingo, the white republic which occupies the other half of the island of Santo Domingo, is in almost as bad a plight. General Jimenez, the revolutionary leader who has been the chief mover against President Morales, arrived in New York a few days ago. To Americans who were on the vessel with him he stated he expects to rest a while before resuming his activities. This probably means he is in the United States to get arms and ammunition.

American warships are kept in Haitian and Dominican ports constantly to prevent foreign interests from suffering at the hands of the turbulent citizens of the two republics. The great European powers are constantly intimating to the United States that the Dominican republic should be taken under the wing of this Government to prevent the interruptions of business which have ruined the commerce.

May Establish Protectorate.

Diplomatists are waiting with much interest to see what action the United States will take concerning the Dominican republic as soon as November election is over. It is freely predicted that revolutions in Santo Domingo will be ended for good and all by the establishment of a sort of protectorate by the United States.

United States Minister Dawson's latest reports from Santo Domingo indicate that there is little hope of long continued peace under present conditions. The United States cannot permit the European powers to regulate affairs in the republic because of the Monroe Doctrine, and consequently action on the part of this Government seems imperative.
