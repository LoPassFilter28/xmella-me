The West Virginia News · February 2, 19242 de febrero de 1924

Original in English.Original en inglés.

RED CROSS WANTS.

The American Red Cross appeals to local chapters and the Red Cross branches for assistance in relieving the great want and distress in the islands dependencies of the United States—the Virgin Islands, Porto Rico, Dominican Republic and Haiti, as well as the Canal Zone. After-war conditions have impoverished these insular people.

The Dominican Chapter is now teaching six classes in six different towns of the Republic in Home Hygiene and Care of the Sick, combined with District Health Nurses and sewing classes. There has been established nine clinics with an average of 200 calls monthly for each clinic. There are five hospitals, which are partly supported by the government. There is one Leper Colony in which there are 78 old men and women; one Poor Farm in which there are 90 old men and women; and five Orphan Asylums. The Red Cross has been asked to do everything in its power to help the Dominicans in their efforts to create a Red Cross and assist their own people. The quota alloted to your Chapter is as follows:

Six layettes, 6 rompers, 1 to 3 years, 6 boys suits, 3 to 12 years, 6 girls dresses with bloomers, 3 to 12 years, 6 garments for men and women.

The layettes consist of:

2 thin white dresses (kimo-no slip)

2 white muslin petticoats (Gertrude)

2 cotton, knit undershirts with sleeves

2 blankets, yard square outing flannel

Pieces of old linen

Box talcum powder

2 thin white bonnets

2 wash clothes

1 soft towel

Safety pins, large and small

6 diapers 20 by 20 inches finished

2 light weight flannel binders 6 by 18 inches

You will please make a bag of washable material with draw string which will exactly fit the layette when wrapped in a diaper and pinned securely with safety pins.

As the climate is tropical, all clothing is to be made of strong, washable cotton material, preferably of dark colors. Use any standard patterns which can be bought locally. Boys' and Girls' clothing may be renovated garments which the children have finished with and which mothers do not desire to put away for another season. Clothing for men and women should be outer garments renovated old suits or mechanics dungaree suits or soldiers fatigue uniforms for the men, and plain gowns or wrappers old or new, for women.

You have until March, 1924 to fill the above quota. All costs must be borne by the Chapter—purchases of material, packing on it. Advise this office of the number, type of garments, and date of shipment.

Packages should be sent via Parcel Post, maxium weight for one package is 22 pounds; maximum rate 12 cents per pound. Such shipments require only the ordinary parcel post custom declarations. Address your shipments as follows:

Major John M. Caton, 3rd.

Chairman, Dominican Chapter, American Red Cross

Santo Domingo City, Dominican Republic.

If you prefer not to accept the quota, will you send its equivalent by check to Division Headquarters, marking same "For purchases of clothing material for Virgin Islands". This Chapter has native women clever with the needle, capable and anxious to assist in the welfare of their own people if material can be supplied.

There is also great need for single and three quarter sheets and pillow cases. We cannot ask Chapters to supply these but we are asking each Chapter to send to the Manager of the Washington Division, one pair of sheets and one pair of pillow cases. In this way, with no tax on any Chapter, we will be able to supply the needs of our nurses.

Please call a meeting of your Executive and Production Committees and advise by early mail of your acceptance of the above quota.
