Tydings-Cheeseborough and Davies-Hutton Romances Involve White House Hopes and Diplomatic Change

Capital Buzzes With Rumors Following Engagement Announcements; Handsome Senator Popular Here

By GEORGE ABELL

The long-predicted engagement of Sen. Millard Tydings, dashing junior Maryland Senator, and lovely blond Eleanor Davies Cheeseborough, daughter of Mrs. Emlen Knight Davies, was officially announced last night.

Today the Capital is buzzing. If half the rumors heard are true, Millard is being groomed for the presidency in 1940.

The same story has his prospective father-in-law, Joseph E. Davies, who on Dec. 16 will marry in New York the wealthy Mrs. Marjorie Post Hutton, will become our next ambassador to France, replacing the rather weary Jesse Isidor Straus, who craves the comparative quiet of Macy's Department Store.

FORMER COMMISSIONER

Davies is a former chairman of the Federal Trade Commission.

In many long columns written about handsome Millard and his bride, nearly all the details of their personal lives and those of their various inlaws have been given. Only a few points have been omitted.

Such as—

Sen. Tydings, in addition to his political acumen, is an excellent painter of nudes and hunting scenes.

He's a widely known figure in artistic Georgetown studios, dressed in a purple bathrobe, paint brush in hand. Georgetown wits refer to the robe as "Millard's imperial purple toga."

LETS CAT OUT

The engagement was definitely surmised by Justice Harlan Stone some months ago when that gentleman took refuge beneath the porte-cochere of the Davies house on Massachusetts-av, during a sudden downpour and saw Millard emerge from the home, a tall lean suitor in immaculate morning coat.

Altho Eleanor Cheeseborough has not been seen here for several years, she appeared meteorologically in late June of this year (before obtaining a Reno divorce) and talked enthusiastically about taking a cottage at Rehoboth Beach, Del.

FOUNDED CLUB

Known as an ultra-smart personality in official Washington, Tydings enchanted exclusive circles by not only attending tea parties for a select group, but by starting a club of his own. He was one of the instigators and founders of the Democratic shooting club on an island in the Chesapeake.

There, fatigued and harassed solons of wealthy background retired to discuss politics around roaring fires on dim winter afternoons.

Both Tydings and the exquisite Eleanor are reported very much in love.

It is understood that they will retire temporarily to Oakington near Havre de Grace, an historic estate, recently purchased by the Senator.

Frequently Eleanor has visited the estate in recent weeks. One of the features of Oakington is its bridle path — both Tydings and Eleanor being horseback devotees.

In addition to Oakington, Tydings several years ago built a colonial house in Maryland, completely out of gray stone. This residence, it is believed, will be occupied by his father and mother.

SUDDEN MARRIAGES

While no date has been set for the wedding, predictions are it will take place in the next four weeks, at least, before the middle of January. Members of the Cheeseborough and Davies family have apparently adopted the habit of sudden matrimonial decisions.

Mrs. Cheeseborough's sister, the attractive Rachael Davies surprised Washington society thru a suddenly announced engagement to Aldace Walker, brother of Mrs. Evelyn Walker, who recently married "Chip" Roberts, assistant secretary of the Treasury.

RAMIFICATIONS

By Rachael's marriage to Aldace, Sen. Tydings becomes a sort of brother-in-law of the blond, ambitious Evelyn, whose rise in official circles has been spectacular.

Ramifications of the Tydings-Cheeseborough match are widely scattered. Millard's land purchases in northern Maryland have caused real estate values to rise tremendously in that sector. Already a number of Washington and Baltimore socialites have emulated the Senator by buying big farms and country estates in the vicinity of Havre de Grace.

BOOMS REAL ESTATE

Local real estate agents find that property nearby is eagerly sought after by their clients. For another thing, the engagement has caused unusual interest, not only in higher official and diplomatic circles, but in Central America and the West Indies. Cables of the impending event have been avidly read in such places as Santo Domingo, capital of the Dominican Republic.

In this tiny island, the name Joe Davies is as well-known as that of President-Dictator Rafael Trujillo. For many years, in addition to business and political connections, Mr. Davies acted as legal adviser to the Trujillo Government.

All social gossip connected with their American adviser is anxiously scanned.
