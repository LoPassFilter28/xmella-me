McDermott Named Envoy to El Salvador To Succeed Duke

President Eisenhower today nominated Michael J. McDermott, a veteran State Department career officer, to be Ambassador to the Republic of El Salvador.

Mr. McDermott, now special assistant to the Secretary for press relations, will succeed Angier Biddle Duke, who is retiring.

Gen. Eisenhower also asked the Senate to approve these appointments:

1. R. Douglas Stuart of Lake Forest, Ill., to be Ambassador to Canada, succeeding Stanley Woodward. Mr. Stuart, 67, is vice chairman of the board of Quaker Oats Co.

2. William T. Pheiffer, New York attorney, to be Ambassador to the Dominican Republic, succeeding Phelps Phelps, resigned. Mr. Pheiffer, a former member of Congress, is 62 and is also a former law partner of White House Secretary Thomas E. Stephens.

3. Joseph E. Talbot for reappointment to a six-year term on the United States Tariff Commission. Mr. Talbot, a former Republican member of Congress from Connecticut, was appointed to an unexpired term on the commission several weeks ago.

4. William V. Mitchell, Denver, assistant to the President of the Quick-Way Truck Shovel Corp., to be administrator of the Small Defense Plants Administration. He succeeds Yngvar Brynildssen.

Was "Dean" of Press Officers.

Mr. McDermott, 58, who is known affectionately by his newspaper friends around the world as "Mac" or "Mike," long has been the dean of United States Government press officers.

During his more than 32 years with the State Department, he has helped interpret to the public the policies of 11 Secretaries of State—Secretaries Lansing, Colby, Hughes, Kellogg, Stimson, Hull, Stettinius, Byrnes, Marshall, Acheson, and Dulles.

Started as Clerk

Born in Peabody, Mass., about 10 years after his parents immigrated from Ireland, he began working for the Government here in 1917 as a clerk in the War Department. After World War I service as a confidential clerk at the Supreme War Council in Paris, he joined the State Department in 1920.

He was made assistant chief of the department's old Current Information Division in 1924 and chief in 1927. Since then he has been the chief spokesman to newspaper reporters for one Secretary of State after another and their adviser on what they should say in their own news conferences.

Was Special Assistant.

Since a 1944 departmental re-organization, his title has been special assistant to the Secretary of State for press relations.

His name has become widely known to newspaper readers only since 1945 when Mr. McDermott began letting reporters quote him by name when he made announcements or answered questions. Previously, he had insisted on anonymity

For many years, Mr. McDermott has looked older than his age because of a thatch of snow-white hair. His hair became white since soon after, as a boy of 10, he saw a bolt of lightning shoot through his home.

Mr. McDermott intends to keep his home here at 1855 Upshur street N.W.
