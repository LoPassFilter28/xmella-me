Minister Sullivan Exonerated; Charged That He Used Office To Get Job For A Relative Secretary Bryan Issues Statement Showing That Connecticut Man Acted With Unusual Discretion And Judgment

Washington, Dec. 12—James M. Sullivan, American minister to the Dominican Republic, is completely exonerated of charges made against him. Secretary Bryan made a statement last night in which it is shown that Mr. Sullivan did not use his influence as minister to secure a job for a distant relative, Timothy J. Sullivan.

"In the opinion of the department," says Mr. Bryan's statement, "the criticism of Mr. Sullivan is entirely unjust and will not be given weight by those who take time to acquaint themselves with the facts.

"Minister Sullivan informs me, without invitation from him a distant relative by the name of Timothy J. Sullivan went to San Domingo shortly after the appointment of Minister Sullivan. This relative was for 27 years a roadmaster in the United States and when the bridge between Puerto Plata and Santiago was blown up the Dominican government, having no engineer available who could superintend the restoring of the trestle, asked the former roadmaster to undertake the work as a favor to the government, and he did so without compensation. On November 27 Minister Sullivan addressed to the minister of public works the following note:

"'It has come to my attention that one Timothy J. Sullivan has placed with the public works department a bid for the reconstruction of the bridge on the Puerto-Plata-Santiago railroad. I wish it distinctly understood that Mr. Sullivan by reason of any remote relation with myself has no claim on the Dominican government. Of course I have no right to prevent him from placing before you in fair competition with others an honest bid, but I desire to state to you that I have no sympathy with anyone who attempts to gain influence with the Dominican government through me.'

"Mr. Timothy Sullivan has had no other employment from the government and has no prospects of securing such employment. He is there only temporarily, expecting to leave within a few days."

James M. Sullivan is a Connecticut newspaperman and lawyer, well known throughout the state. In Bridgeport he is widely acquainted and has many friends. He is a man of pleasing personality, and one whom his friends believe little likely to become involved in any diplomatic error, or mistake growing out of a lack of judgment.
