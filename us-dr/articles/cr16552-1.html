The Journal prints an account of the flight: the president is said to be making his way by land to reach Monte Cristi, on the north coast and west of Puerto Plata, from which port it is not far distant. The account recalls that Señor Juan E. Sanchez, the former foreign minister of Santo Domingo, with the American special commissioners, Commander Albert C. Dillingham and former Minister Thomas Dawson, and Frederico Velasquez, minister of finance, on Jan. 20 last signed the agreement between the United States and Santo Domingo providing for the fiscal protectorate of the United States over Santo Domingo. Under the head 'Will Not Interfere', the paper prints a Washington item: for the present there will not be any interference by the United States in the difficulty which has arisen in San Domingo; the trouble is regarded by the State Department officials as entirely an internal one, and so long as outside interests are not menaced this attitude of non-intervention will be maintained; if, however, conditions should change materially and lawless acts should be committed against Americans and American interests and involving the collection of the Dominican customs by this government, or other acts of violence occur which in the opinion of the officials here would make it proper for this government to interfere, this step will be taken and measures adopted to quell the trouble. This decision was reached by the State Department officials during the day and was confirmed at a conference at the White House late this afternoon in which the President and Secretaries Root, Taft and Bonaparte participated.El Journal publica el relato de la fuga: el presidente, se dice, se está abriendo paso por tierra para llegar a Monte Cristi, en la costa norte y al oeste de Puerto Plata, puerto del que no dista mucho. El relato recuerda que el señor Juan E. Sánchez, exministro de Relaciones Exteriores de Santo Domingo, con los comisionados especiales estadounidenses, el comandante Albert C. Dillingham y el exministro Thomas Dawson, y Frederico Velasquez, ministro de Hacienda, firmó el 20 de enero pasado el acuerdo entre Estados Unidos y Santo Domingo que provee el protectorado fiscal de Estados Unidos sobre Santo Domingo. Bajo el título «Will Not Interfere», el periódico publica una nota de Washington: por el momento no habrá ninguna injerencia de Estados Unidos en la dificultad que ha surgido en Santo Domingo; el problema es considerado por los funcionarios del Departamento de Estado como enteramente interno, y mientras los intereses externos no se vean amenazados se mantendrá esta actitud de no intervención; si, sin embargo, las condiciones cambiaran materialmente y se cometieran actos ilegales contra los estadounidenses y los intereses estadounidenses y que involucraran la recaudación de las aduanas dominicanas por este gobierno, u otros actos de violencia que, en opinión de los funcionarios aquí, hiciera propio que este gobierno interviniera, se daría ese paso y se adoptarían medidas para sofocar el problema. Esta decisión fue tomada por los funcionarios del Departamento de Estado durante el día y fue confirmada en una conferencia en la Casa Blanca a última hora de esta tarde, en la que participaron el Presidente y los secretarios Root, Taft y Bonaparte.

PRES. MORALES Flees From Own Troops in San Domingo. Leaves Capitol Secretly and Latter Start in Pursuit. Thought to Have Headed for Puerto Plata---A Mixed Up Situation.

San Domingo, Republic of Santo Domingo, Monday, Dec. 25.—Following the announcement today that the president of the republic, Gen. Carlos E. Morales, had left the capital for an unknown destination, troops were sent in pursuit of the chief magistrate. What took place after that cannot be definitely determined, but it is known that a fight took place about 10 miles from here and it is supposed that Gen. Pimental, the rebel commander, was attacked by the government troops.

The whereabouts of the fugitive president is not known. It is generally supposed that he endeavored to gain the coast and embark on board a sloop with the object of reaching Puerto Plata, on the north coast of Santo Domingo, and there join Gov. Perez, who has been dismissed by the government from the post of governor of Puerto Plata, but who refuses to surrender his office. On the other hand there are those who believe that the president is endeavoring to cross Santo Domingo by land and reach Monte Christi, on the north coast and west of Puerto Plata, from which port it is not far distant.

Senor Juan E. Sanchez, the former foreign minister of Santo Domingo, with the American special commissioners, Commander Albert C. Dillingham and Former Minister Thomas C. Dawson and Frederico Velasquez, minister of finance, on Jan. 20 last, signed the agreement between the United States and Santo Domingo providing for the fiscal protectorate of the United States over Santo Domingo.
