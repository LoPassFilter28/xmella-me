CACERES DIES AS DID PREDECESSOR President of San Domingo Closes Career Begun by Assassination. IS THE VICTIM OF A PLOT Administration, Covering Four Years Recently Disturbed by Revolutionary Moves.

Washington, Nov. 21.—Ramon Caceres, president of Santo Domingo, who was murdered by malcontents Sunday, began his career in politics in 1899 by assassinating Hereux, who had executed his father. This made it possible for General Jimenez to attain the presidency. When, during the administration of President Morales, Jimenez, who had previously been deposed, led a rebellion, Caceres, as minister of war, put down the revolt. Later Morales, at odds with his party, was forced to flee the country and the cabinet made Caceres president. He had been in office four years when killed.

Under an arrangement made in 1905 the United States appointed a receiver general for the customs at Santo Domingo, thus taking steps to pay off the debts of that republic.

MUST CALL ON CONGRESS.

As the constitution of the republic of Santo Domingo makes no provision for a vice president, it will be necessary for the cabinet to call an extraordinary session of congress to provide for a special election.

In the interim the cabinet will discharge the duties of the president. According to those familiar with Santo Domingo affairs, it is possible for congress to convene, set a date for an election and have a new president in a little more than two weeks.

THINK PLOT CAUSE.

Lacking full information, it is presumed here that Caceres has fallen a victim to one of the characteristic Dominican plots. It is known that Caceres' administration, which has lasted four years, an almost unprecedented period in Dominican history, has very recently been disturbed by several revolutionary plots.

Last April Caceres became convinced that a formidable movement was under way for his overthrow. This was headed, he believed, by General Carlos F. Morales, who had directly preceded him in the presidency, and by Mauricio Jimenez, a former president.

PLANNED EXPEDITION.

General Zenon Toridio, a veteran of the Dominican army, had also cast in his lot with Morales and Jimenez. The three had arranged to launch an expedition from Porto Rico when they were suddenly arrested by the local authorities there on a charge of violation of the neutrality laws. They were acquitted and disappeared.

CRUISERS DISPATCHED.

Washington, Nov. 21.—To safeguard American interests and prevent anarchy in Santo Domingo as a result of the assassination of President Caceres, the cabinet today decided that the armored cruisers Washington and North Carolina be dispatched immediately to San Domingo city. They will take American Minister Russell back to San Domingo.
