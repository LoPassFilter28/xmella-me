Mr. & Mrs. Swihart Return From Six-Week Trip To South America

During the summer plans were made for a trip to South America. Through American Express Travel Service, we made flight reservations, also all hotel reservations. Armed with passports, permits, photographs, vaccination certificates and reinforced with a little silver and a few travelers cheques, we were at the airport in Miami October 2 ready to take the plane headed for Panama. We rode in Pan Am DC-7C Clipper with 65 passengers aboard. Left Miami 7 p. m. and arrived in Panama City 11:30. Next day we took a tour of city, (pop. 3/4 mill.) and the ruins of Old Panama, (founded 1519) and to the Canal Zone, where we watched a large ship from England entering the canal through the locks. Description and history of the canal make very interesting reading, but too lengthy. Will give a few points: Approximate distance from ocean to ocean is about 50 miles and runs almost north and south. As the level of the two oceans is not the same, it requires the raising and lowering of the vessels which require the huge gates or locks. An average of one boat per hour during every 24 hours passes through the lock, taking 8 hours to make the trip. Through the excavated part of the canal the boats are pulled by donkey engines each side of canal. As canal is a single file system, the boats pass each other in the lake between the oceans.

Next morning at 9 a. m., we took a plane bound for Lima, Peru with the first stop at Cali, Colombia, next was Quito, Ecuador, a few minutes south of the Equator. From then on we learned to look up, or north, to see the sun at noon, and also that the shade was on the south side of a building. Had a short stop at Trujillo and then arrived in Lima, Peru, (pop. 1,200,000). Took sightseeing tour of the city. Visited country club, bullfighting ring and a race track where races are held every Sunday, and is considered finest in S. A. University of Lima has claimed enrollment of 20,000 students. An all day tour to the mountain city of Metacan, 50 miles of beautiful mountain scenery and more or less primitive life of the natives. The Inca ruins was at one time a city of 30,000.

Next day took plane for Santiago, Chile, with a short stop at Cuso. Flying at 21,000 feet, we passed over beautiful Lake Ticaca, 13,000 feet above sea level, the highest lake in the world. Landed at LaPaz, Bolivia for refueling at highest commercial airport in the world, 13,558 feet. Arriving in Santiago (pop. 2 million) we took a sightseeing trip of the city. Took a station wagon trip covering 240 miles to Valparaiso through the beautiful Casablanca Valley and mile-long tunnel blasted through solidrock, requiring no reinforcing. Passed road work crews working by hand. Wages were 50-80c per hour. As it was springtime, farming was in progress. Saw quite a few ox teams at work. Such steep hillsides are farmed, it would be impossible to operate a tractor. Seeing wires strung from high in the hills to level spots, we inquired their purpose and learned that at harvest time grain is cut by hand and tied in bundles and then placed on the wire to travel down hill by gravity. Valparaiso is a very rugged-looking city as it is built on 17 small mountains and not too much level ground. Visited various points of interest; were impressed by the open-air market 4 blocks long. Most anything could be bought. A great casino, the Las Vegas of S. A. is there. Returned to Santiago and went shopping for copper goods as Chile is a great copper country. Were to leave at 4 p. m. but were delayed by engine trouble until 7 a. m. with Pan American paying our hotel for the extra night. Was not too reassuring to have engine trouble just before starting over the Andes mountains as they do not offer ideal landing spots in case of forced landing. But were glad we could make the trip in daylight. The sun was shining bright above the clouds and the mountains reminded one of giant fingers reaching up after you. Were flying at 19,000 feet; was wonderful to see the snow-covered peaks, the highest in the Western Hemisphere, with white clouds below. We managed to take quite a few pictures, both slides and movies.

Arriving at Buenos Aires airport, we learned we were thirty miles from the city. No traffic lights and a population of 6 million. Pairs of mounted policemen on corners, a few busloads of armed soldiers, some broken windows, a hole in a building where a bomb had exploded, taking the legs off a policeman. The guide explained that "just a few" had been killed the day before our arrival. Otherwise, everything was normal. On the second day continued sightseeing and boat ride in the waterways of the Parana Delta. Were impressed by the number of young people taking part in various sports in the many parks. Third day, a 38 mile trip to the city of LaPlata. Visited Paleontol Museum. Fourth day we were on our own for shopping and general pastime. We saw some of the creations of the Peron Regime. Some good, some not so good. As a class, the people of Argentina seem very well-dressed and have many fine homes. They are proud of their beautiful city. Built on level land, its streets are broad and straight, and many are shaded by carefully tended trees. Electric car lines connect all parts of the city. Government buildings, business places, banks and hotels are as fine as any in our country. At the wharves of Buenos Aires, you will see ships and ocean liners from Europe and the U. S. and freighters from all parts of the world. In the manufacturing center, there are flour mills, meat-packing plants, sugar refineries and tanneries.

A 40 minute plane ride to Montevideo, capital of Uruguay, level plains and beautiful farms. City had population of 1 million, 1/3 the entire population of Uruguay. Tour of city showed very fine residential district and 1800 bed hospital, the largest in S. A. Has a 100,000 seat stadium and miles of fine beaches. From Montevideo we headed for Rio, with a short stop at Sao Paulo, a city of over 3 million, considered to be the fastest growing city in the world and the strongest industrial city in S. A. and has one of the world's finest airports.

Arriving in Rio De Janeiro, we were taken to Copacabana Hotel on Copacabana Beach. Rio is the oldest city in the Western Hemisphere. On second day took 30 mile automobile trip through beautiful countrysides and banana plantations, to the city of Petropolis. It is the summer capitol of Brazil, high in the Organ mountain range. The country around Rio has an average of 70 inches rainfall, with a warm climate, and produces a wonderful growth of flowers and vegetation. Stopped for a look at what is considered to be the finest hotel in the world, Hotel Quitandiula. United Nations meeting was held here and it was a great casino until outlawed. Visited what was formerly Imperial Palace, but is now a museum. We were required to wear straw sandals to enter. Visited beautiful mountain home of Santos Demont. Next day more sightseeing and a trip on cable car 1200 feet up famous Sugar Loaf Mountain. Took a trip up mountain to Corcovado Peak. At 1500 feet a beautiful waterfall made one wonder where the water originates, as there is no snow above. At the peak stands a huge Statue of Christ, considered a highlight of South America, regarded to South America as Statue of Liberty to U.S.A. In P. M. watched military air show held on Copacabana Beach. After tragic death of Star photographer, show was called off. A very unusual situation for a city, Rio has docks almost in the heart of the city. Passenger ships unload within a few feet of city center.

Left Rio at 9:30 P. M. for Caracas. Short stop at Belem, arriving at Caracas Airport 7 a.m. Airport is 12 miles from city and a wonderful 4-lane paved highway costing some $80 million, through the mountains, makes it a trip to remember. We went to the Hotel Avila and in P.M. took usual sightseeing trip. The city has many fine homes and is making a strong fight to clear up its slum districts and is building many fine apartment homes. Oil is the main source of revenue. We found Caracas to be the highest priced city of any on the tour. Left Caracass 10 a.m. Short stop at the island of Curaco; at Ciuad Trujillo, Dominican Republic. Landed at Miami, having flown 11,650 miles, on a six weeks tour.
