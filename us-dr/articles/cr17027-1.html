The New York Herald · August 7, 18707 de agosto de 1870

Original in English.Original en inglés.

St. Domingo and Hayti.

The war cry has again been raised, and Hayti and St. Domingo are anxious to emulate the example of France and Prussia. For months past the relations between the two republics have been anything but friendly. Since Saget has been elected President of Hayti he has been in continual hot water. The admirers of Salnave did not become extinct with their chief, and they are now giving the President elect of Hayti all the trouble they possibly can. But if the Salnavists exercise a constant dread in the minds of the present rulers in Hayti, St. Domingo is not without its troubles also. Cabral is doing his utmost to embarrass Baez, the Dominican President. It is claimed now that President Saget will aid Cabral against Baez, because the latter favors the success of the Salnave party against Saget. This is precisely the situation of affairs in Dominica. A little reflection will convince any one that the only way to end these bloody dissensions which mark the history of this island would be by the annexation of either one or both of these republics to this country. We had an opportunity to acquire St. Domingo on what appeared to be not only fair terms, but on conditions more reasonable than we could expect; yet the opportunity was lost. With St. Domingo once under the flag of the United States these dissensions and disturbances which continually convulse the peoples of both Hayti and St. Domingo would be firmly ended, a rich and fertile tract of land would be added to the United States, and not only the Haytiens and Dominicans would be benefited, but the whole civilized world would rejoice at the change.
