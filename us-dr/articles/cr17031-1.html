Ex-Official Links Frank To Dominican Passport

A former official of the Dominican government testified here yesterday he had seen a Dominican dilomatic passport belonging to Washington attorney John Joseph Frank.

The testimony from Dr. German Emilio Ornes was heard in Federal court, where Mr. Frank is standing trial on charges of illegally acting as an agent of the Dominican Republic and Generalissimo Raphael Trujillo.

Dr. Ornes, former head of the Dominican Development Commission and now living in New York as a correspondent for a Puerto Rican newspaper, testified he first met Mr. Frank in May, 1954, at a hotel in Ciudad Trujillo, the Dominican capital.

He said later the same day he saw the passport, issued to Mr. Frank, on the desk of Enselmo Paulino, former Dominican secretary of state, now in exile.

The courtroom was thrown into laughter as Justice Department attorney Plato Cacheris questioned Dr. Ornes about the internal organization of the Dominican Republic.

"Unfortunately, in the Dominican Republic, there is some confusion between the national treasury and Generalissimo Trujillo's pockets," Dr. Ornes said.

Objection Overruled

Edward L. Carey, attorney for Mr. Frank, objected to this, but Judge James R. Kirkland overruled the objection.

"The court concedes a dictator may be in fact the national government, and the pocket of one may be the coffer of the other," the judge said.

But Judge Kirkland had to take back his "dictator" remark in an apology to the jury and Mr. Carey, explaining this hadn't been brought out by testimony.

In earlier testimony, Fred Woodcocke, an FBI agent, said Mr. Frank had told him in a 1956 interview that the attorney was representing two private firms located in Dominica.

But Dr. Ornes classified one of these firms as "a Trujillo corporation," implying it was linked with the Dominican government.

The former official's testimony also dwelt briefly on his acquaintance with Dr. Jesus de Galindez, a Columbia University professor and bitter critic of Generalissimo Trujillo who disappeared in March, 1956.

Met in New York

Dr. Ornes said he knew Dr. de Galindez when the professor was an official in the Dominican government and later in New York.

He said he met Dr. de Galindez in New York after breaking with the Trujillo regime himself.

Reciting another meeting with Mr. Frank, Dr. Ornes said he saw the attorney again in Kansas City in November, 1955. He said he had gone to a hotel there to announce his break to the Dominican strongman, who was then visiting Kansas City.

The man who ushered him to the generalissimo's hotel room was Mr. Frank, Dr. Ornes said. He said he again saw Mr. Frank in New York City in the summer of 1956 when the attorney was representing a Dominican firm in a court case.

Testimony in the trial, which goes into its third day today, has brought out very little about Gerald L. Murphy, a missing American who was a pilot for the Dominican government airline.

Agent Woodcocke testified Mr. Frank told him, in one of a series of interviews late last year and in January, that the attorney had met Mr. Murphy once in a bar in Ciudad Trujillo, but had no personal knowledge of the pilot's disappearance.

Some reports have said Mr. Murphy just before his disappearance had been talking about what happened to Dr. de Galindez. The Dominican government said Mr. Murphy was murdered by a Dominican pilot who later killed himself
