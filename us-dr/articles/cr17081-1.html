Russell of Pepsi-Cola Stresses Opportunity

By CLARENCE H. HUNTER

Star Staff Writer

Harvey C. Russell, recently elected vice president in charge of special markets for Pepsi-Cola Co., said here recently his new position is an example of economic opportunities increasingly being opened to Negroes by American industry.

"More and more each day," Mr. Russell said, "responsible jobs are being made available to qualified people."

Mr. Russell, 43, was elected unanimously as a vice president of Pepsi-Cola Co. on January 25. One of his boosters was movie star Joan Crawford, one of the 14 directors. The Associated Press reported Mr. Russell's annual salary to be in the neighborhood of $20,000.

Develops Markets

As vice president in charge of special markets, Mr. Russell is responsible for developing marketing programs and sales approaches in predominantly Negro communities as well as those with a large percentage of Spanish-speaking persons.

There are 16 persons in Mr. Russell's department who work in the company's regional division offices. Included on his staff are three Washington people. Julian Nicholas is assigned to the company's public relations department and John Rector works for Pepsi's company-owned-plants division at the world headquarters in New York. Ernest Humbles is special markets representative for the Washington region.

A tour of duty with the Coast Guard during World War II convinced Mr. Russell he could select a career other than in sociology or psychology.

Employment with Pepsi-Cola began in 1950, when he became a field representative. He advanced to assistant to the sales vice president in 1953; to projects manager in 1955, and then to director of special markets activity in 1957. He has visited about 350 of the company's 550 plants in the 12 years.

Mr. Russell has successfully completed numerous key tasks in the domestic and international fields for Pepsi. He recently conducted a comprehensive marketing survey for Pepsi-Cola International, an assignment which took him to Nigeria, Ghana, Sierra Leone, Liberia and Senegal. He represented the company president at the opening of a new bottling plant in Eastern Nigeria. Locally, Mr. Russell helped with the introduction of the 16-ounce bottle.

Louisville Native

A native of Louisville, Ky., and a graduate of the Coast Guard Academy at New London, Conn., Mr. Russell served in that branch of the military from 1942 to 1946. He was an officer aboard the Sea Cloud, a 317-foot yacht once owned by Ambassador Joseph E. Davies and later by Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo, jr., of the Dominican Republic. The yacht has been confiscated by the republic.

Before joining Pepsi-Cola, Mr. Russell worked for a New York advertising agency where he got his first experience in setting up soft drink franchises for the Joe Louis Punch Co. At another time, he was national sales manager for a cosmetics manufacturer.

Mr. Russell is one of a family of five children who together have earned 14 college degrees. His younger brother, George Russell, 38, is general manager of a new Pepsi-Cola bottling plant at Onitsha, Nigeria.
