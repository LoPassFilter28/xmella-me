'Lunatics' in Dominican Invasion Caused Failure, Leader Says

By the Associated Press

HAVANA, Cuba, Oct. 4.—Gen. Juan Rodriguez Garcia, who organized the abortive expedition against the Dominican Republic, said today it failed because it was formed by "99¾ per cent of lunatics against ¼ per cent of sane people."

The 60-year-old Rodriguez said failure could also be attributed to the preparations taking too long. He related his views in an interview at the military hospital, where he is being held prisoner with his son, Horacio, 28, who accompanied him in the expedition.

He denied that the expedition had at any time thought of turning against the Cuban government.

The commander of the so-called "American Liberation Army" said he had invested all his money in the attempt to overthrow the regime of Dominican President Rafael Trujillo by creating an expeditionary force on Cuban soil to attack the Dominican Republic.

"Naturally," he added, "many Cuban friends and those of other nations also contributed money for the expedition's expenses, but it is not the truth that any government had officially financed the movement." He said the attitude of some high Cuban authorities toward his expedition could be described at best as "tolerant."

The Cuban Supreme Court is deliberating what action to take against members of the 1,097-man expedition. Magistrate Evelio Tabio said today only top leaders would be held and that all but 176 were already set free.

Accepts Whole Responsibility.

Rodriguez said that a few days before the expedition's failure he had left Confites Key aboard a Cuban navy launch for Havana with officials who "came to the island to talk and find out what was going on." He said these talks were with "high authorities" whose names he refused to divulge.

He said he was concerned that "high Cuban authorities who helped us because they sympathized with our cause be made to suffer. I wish that all responsibility should be left to fall upon my shoulders."

The expeditionary force was frustrated in its attempt to start a Dominican revolution by Cuban authorities who seized the ships Aurora and Maximo Gomez as they sailed for the Dominican Republic to launch the attack.

Gen. Rodriguez blamed Orlando Masferrer, a Cuban with the rank of lieutenant colonel in the expedition, with responsibility for letting the Aurora fall into the hands of the Cuban navy.

"I had already given orders to depart for the Dominican Republic with the ships Aurora and Maximo Gomez," he said. "I was leading the way on the Aurora with 400 men and enough arms and ammunition to equip another 1,000 men, but then we received a call from the Maximo Gomez in which Masferrer informed me he was in a tough spot.

"I thought his ship went aground and ordered the Aurora to turn around and go to his help. Suddenly we found ourselves surrounded by Cuban warships."

Gen. Rodriguez said that if Masferrer considered himself lost "the thing for him to do was to let me go ahead."

3 Americans Await Action.

Meanwhile, three Americans who joined the expeditionary forces aiming at the overthrow of President Trujillo said they signed up three months ago because "we had friends in it and because we knew the country was under an oppressive dictatorship."

They were Hollis Smith, 26, Palisades Park, N. J.; Rupert E. Waddel, 27, and Thomas Sawyer, 26, both of New York City. They made the statement in an interview while awaiting action of the Cuban Supreme Court after having been taken prisoners when the expeditionary forces surrendered.

Mr. Waddel and Mr. Sawyer were with the United States Army in the war. Asked how they fared as expeditionaries they said "it reminded us of the Army." Mr. Smith said he was employed as an exposives expert with private firms before joining the expedition.

Mr. Sawyer had worked at an automobile plant. Mr. Waddel said he was preparing to enter the University of Virginia.

Earlier, Magistrate Tabio said Mr. Smith and Mr. Waddel were listed as officers with the rank of captain in the expedition and would not be released at once. He said Mr. Sawyer had enrolled as a soldier and would be set free.
