Evening Star · August 19, 193419 de agosto de 1934

Original in English.Original en inglés.

Ambassador of Germany Leaves City on Vacation Herr Hans Luther Takes Cottage at Falmouth. Minister of Finland Sails for States. Other Diplomatic Notes.

The Ambassador of Germany, Herr Hans Luther, accompanied by the third secretary of the embassy, Herr Gerrit von Haeften, left yesterday for Falmouth, Mass., to join the Ambassador's daughters, Fraulein Gertruad Luther and Fraulein Eva Marie Luther and their aunt, Frau Koerner, in the cottage which the Ambassador has leased for the remainder of the season.

Herr von Haeften, whose marriage to Fraulein Gertruad Luther will take place in the Autumn, will return to Washington the first of next week and the Ambassador will remain in the North for some time.

The Minister of Finland, Mr. L. Astrom, sailed yesterday from his home in Finland where he has been since early June on vacation, and is expected to arrive in this country Monday, August 27. He will come at once to Washington.

The Minister of Venezuela and Senora de Arcaya and their children will sail Tuesday, August 28, for this country after spending several months in Europe and in their home in Venezuela.

The Minister of the Dominican Republic, Senor Don Roberto Despradel, will return the latter part of the week from a brief visit in Santo Domingo where he went by air last week.

The Minister of Bolivia and Senora de Finot are spending a short time in New York and are expected to return to their apartment in the Mayflower the first of this week.

The Minister of China and Mme. Sze are expected to sail for this country the latter part of this week after spending two months in England.

The charge d'affaires of Brazil, Senhor Cyro de Freitas-Valle, was host at luncheon Monday at Wardman Park Hotel where with his son he is making his home. Senhora de Freitas-Valle will join them later in the year.

The charge d'affaires of Belgium, Prince Eugene de Ligne, is expected to return tomorrow from New York where he went Friday with Mme. May and Mlle. Francoise May, wife and daughter of the late Ambassador of Belgium, M. Paul May, to see them sail yesterday aboard the Ile de France for their home.

The charge d'affaires of Venezuela, Senor Don Luis Churjon, will return tomorrow from Atlantic City where he is spending the week end.

The charge d'affaires of Rumania, Mr. Radu Florescu, has gone to Bethany Beach to remain over Sunday.

The charge d'affaires of Sweden, Baron Beck-Friis, is spending the week end in Bluemont, Va.

The charge d'affaires of the Dominican Republic, Senor Don Emilio Garcia Godoy, is at the Chastleton until the arrival of family in September. Senor Garcia Godoy recently came to Washington and took up his duties as first secretary of the legation and Senora de Garcia Godoy with their two children, Aura Garcia Godoy, a miss of 15, and young Hector Garcia Godoy, 13, will join him here early next month.

The Minister counselor of the Spanish Embassy, Senor Don Luis M. de Yrujo, who with Senora de Yrujo and their children has been in Mexico City since the Mid-Summer, will return to Washington tomorrow. Senora de Yrujo and her children went to Mexico City immediately after the closing of school in the early Summer and Senor de Yrujo joined them there for a vacation.

The military attache of the Mexican Embassy, Brig. Gen. Juan F. Azcarate, is expected to come to Washington the first of the week from Rehoboth Beach, where with Senora de Azcarate and their three children, he is spending the Summer in their cottage.

The military attache of the French Embassy, Lieut. Col. Emmanuel Lombard, who has been in his home in France for some time, sailed Wednesday aboard the Lafayette for this country and is expected to arrive in New York Thursday. Mme. Lombard and their little family remained in this country and is again in their home at 3006 Thirty-second street.

The naval attache of the Italian Embassy and Signora Casardi returned to their apartment in the Mayflower yesterday from an extended trip to the West and to the Pacific Coast. Signora Casardi will sail in September to visit in her Italian home where Capt. Casardi will join her in the early Winter.

The naval attache of the Japanese Embassy and Mme. Kobayashi have issued invitations for a reception tomorrow afternoon at the Mayflower. Capt. and Mme. Kobayashi will be hosts at dinner Thursday evening, entertaining at the Mayflower.

The first secretary of the Japanese Embassy, Mr. Takemi Miura, left Washington yesterday with Prince and Princess Kaya and will remain with them until they sail next week from San Francisco for their Japanese home. Mr. Miura will return at once to Washington, arriving here immediately after Labor day.

The first secretary of the Spanish embassy, Senor Don Luis de Olivares, is spending the week end on Long Island, where he went by motor the middle of the week. Senor de Olivares will motor this week to Newport where he will make a brief visit en route for a series of visits on the New England coast. He will return the middle of next week.

The new assistant military attache of the Mexican embassy and Senora de Vazquez Zarco, who with their children were at the Roosevelt, have taken the house at 3426 Seventeenth street where they recently moved.

The secretary of the Netherlands legation, Jonkheer H. M. van der Wyck has sailed for his home in Holland where he was called by the serious illness of his father, who died shortly after his son's arrival. Jonkheer van der Wyck spent his vacation early in the Summer in the Netherlands and returned to his post in Washington about a month ago.

The commercial attache of the Danish legation, M. Harald Osterberg, has joined Mme. Osterberg and their two children, in their cottage at Fisher's Island, N. Y., for a short stay and will be accompanied by his family on his return this week.

The first secretary of the Canadian legation, Mr. Merchant Mahoney, is expected to return to his post here early in September. Mr. Mahoney and his children are spending their vacation in their Canadian home.

The second secretary of the Canadian legation, Mr. E. D'Arcy McGreer, will sail aboard the Bremen the end of next week to return to this country and is expected to arrive about September 6. Mr. McGreer has been in Switzerland during his vacation and will come at once to Washington after his arrival in New York.
