Young Trujillo Quits In Dominican Crisis Rusk Warns Against New Dictatorship Effort Seen To Thwart 2 Uncles

The United States sees signs of a return to iron-fisted dictatorship in the Dominican Republic and is studying steps to prevent it.

Even the possibility of intervention by American Marines is not ruled out.

The resignation of Rafael Trujillo, jr., as chief of the Dominican army and his departure from the country were regarded in the Government here last night as supporting United States fears of a plot to establish a new dictatorship in the Dominican Republic.

Secretary of State Rusk sounded the warning note in an unusual statement issued late yesterday. While confirming reports that "leading figures" of the old Trujillo dictatorship had returned to the island republic, Mr. Rusk added:

"It appears that they may be planning an attempt to reassert dictatorial domination of the political and economic life of that country, threatening the recent gains of the Dominican government and people toward democratization."

The "leading figures" were identified as Hector and Arismendi Trujillo, brothers of the assassinated "Benefactor," Rafael Trujillo. Hector was president in the old regime and Arismendi held the military rank of general.

Return From Exile

Their nephew (and the late benefactor's eldest son), "Ramfis," was exercising control of the country. But the Dominican Republic announced last night that young Trujillo had resigned as chief of the Dominican army last Tuesday, the day after his uncles Hector and Arismendi returned to Ciudad Trujillo.

The Department said there was evidence that the uncles hoped to take over from "Ramfis," but denied knowledge of any collusion between the nephew and his uncles.

In Secretary Rusk's statement, the future course of American action was left wide open. He said:

"In view of the possibility of political disintegration and the dangerous situation which could ensue, the Government of the United States is considering the further measures that unpredictable events might warrant."

Troops Not Excluded

Department officials would not exclude the possibility that troops might be sent if the Dominican government requested them. However, they did not commit this country to that course of action, either.

Mr. Rusk also noted that the United States had asked the Organization of American States to postpone "further consideration of a proposal on withdrawing the suspension of trade with the Dominican Republic in certain products."

This action was taken Thursday, only two days after the United States had asked the OAS to consider easing the anti-Trujillo embargo.

Diplomatic relations between the United States and the Dominican Republic were severed in August, 1960, in a hemispheric anti-Trujillo move. Consular relations are maintained, however.

At the time of the diplomatic break the United States committed itself not to deal with the Dominicans in war materials. In January, trade restrictions were imposed, banning sale of petroleum products, trucks and truck parts to that country.

Effort Seen To Thwart 2 Uncles

CIUDAD TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic, Nov. 18 (AP).—The government announced tonight that Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo, jr., has resigned as head of the nation's armed forces and left the country.

Departure of young Trujillo from the political scene is expected to creat a political vacuum potentially as dangerous as the one left by the assassination of his dictator father last May 30. Rafael, jr., was generally regarded as the force that helped keep the military from splitting into factions and scrambling for power after the old generalissimo's death.

There was no indication of who would replace him as armed forces chief.

The government statement said Gen. Trujillo resigned on Tuesday and left the country yesterday.

Yacht Anchored Offshore

But it was learned that as late as this morning the 32-year-old Trujillo was still at a beachside chalet at nearby Boca Chica. His luxury yacht, Angelita, which he normally uses on long trips, was missing from its usual dock. Sources close to the Trujillo family said the ship was riding at anchor offshore just beyond territorial limits.

Highest sources said the young armed forces boss had been pushed into his position by a government crisis stemming from the return of two leading generals in the Trujillo family.

The two, Gens. Hector and Jose Arizmendi Trujillo, brothers of the late dictator, returned Wednesday from a brief period of exile.

It was felt here that young Trujillo was using the announcement of his resignation and departure as a last device to force his two uncles to leave the country again. Their presence is widely regarded as detrimental to the nation's attempt to end the old dictatorial regime and introduce democracy.

Warning From Rusk

The government statement said Trujillo, jr., had resigned because of "personal reasons." It gave no further details but promised more information when there is "time available to make it." This was not explained.

The statement came a few hours after the United States consulate had distributed a warning issued by Secretary of State Dean Rusk in Washington that the return of the two uncles may be the prelude to an attempt to seize power and set up a new dictatorship in the Caribbean island nation.

Informants said the young armed forces chief had tried in vain to persuade his uncles to leave again.

Opposition groups have demanded the departure of all the military members of the Trujillo government as a condition for co-operation with the government headed by President Joaquin Balaguer.

Mr. Balaguer himself announced the departure of the two uncles last month and it was widely hailed as a major concession to opposition demands. But Mr. Balaguer had insisted that young Trujillo would remain.
