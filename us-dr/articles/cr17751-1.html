U. S. Asks Dominicans To Explain Ship Halting

By the Associated Press

The State Department said today it is taking up with the Dominican Republic the halting of a United States merchant ship. It is the third recent incident prompting complaints to the Caribbean republic, object of a rebel invasion force.

The vessel, the Florida State, en route from Ponce, Puerto Rico, to Port Everglades, Fla., where it was due today, was halted last Monday.

The State Department said it was 13 miles at sea from Dominican territory, or 10 miles outside the area the United States recognizes as a nation's territorial waters.

The Dominican Republic earlier had been asked to explain (1) Why a United States Air Force transport plane was forced down and (2) Why the American Ambassador was tricked into shaking hands with a secret agent of Dictator Rafael Trujillo.

The State Department said the Caribbean country has not replied to either complaint.

Plane Fired On

The department said the Air Force transport was forced to land at Ciudad Trujillo Tuesday by a Dominican fighter plane, which fired a burst of machinegun bullets. The transport was en route from Puerto Rico to Cuba.

The plane was quickly allowed to take off and continue on its way, but the department said it had told the United States Ambassador to express serious concern and get a complete explanation.

The incident of the secret agent is more complicated. The State Department said it placed the Ambassador "in a position which was subject to misinterpretation," and told this story:

United States Ambassador Joseph F. Farland, an Eisenhower political appointee from Morgantown, W. Va., was called to the Dominican foreign office with other members of the diplomatic corps.

There the diplomats were introduced to Lt. Col. Juan de Dios Ventura Simo and they shook hands. Photographs were taken and widely published.

Loud Protests

Anti-dictator forces throughout the Caribbean area protested at the American Ambassador's seeming approval of Col. Ventura Simo.

Col. Ventura Simo was said to have falsely defected from Gen. Trujillo's forces and flown to Puerto Rico. He returned to the Dominican Republic with rebel forces dedicated to overthrowing Gen. Trujillo, and was "captured." He later was promoted from major to lieutenant colonel.

The implication was that Col. Ventura Simo had infiltrated the rebel forces and then turned them in to Gen. Trujillo.

PILOT REPORTS AIR INCIDENT

PATRICK AIR FORCE BASE, Fla., June 25 (AP).—The pilot of a United States Air Force plane says the Dominican Republic plane that forced him to land Tuesday fired a warning machinegun burst and came dangerously close.

"He had hot guns and we weren't able to resist him with an unarmed C-47," Maj. George A. Williams told a news conference yesterday.

The C-47 transport with seven men aboard was forced to land in Ciudad Trujillo after being escorted for an hour and a half by the single-seat F-51 Dominican fighter.

The transport, based here, was en route from San Juan, Puerto Rico, to Guantanamo Bay, Cuba, when the F-51 appeared.

"At no time during the flight," Maj. Williams said, "did we venture closer than 5 miles to Dominican Republic territory."

Ordered to Land

He said the Dominican pilot "signaled us with his hands to follow him to a landing, then lowered and raised his wheels, which is an aircraft signal to land.

"When we didn't comply right away, he fired a short burst from his 50-caliber machine gun. He fired straight ahead and the shot did not come close to our plane.

"But he came dangerously close to let us know he meant business."

Maj. Williams said a second Dominican plane joined the first briefly, then both flew away when they were about 70 miles from Ciudad Trujillo. The C-47 landed there because it was short of fuel.

Maj. Williams said Dominican air force officers at Ciudad Trujillo were unable to explain the incident.
