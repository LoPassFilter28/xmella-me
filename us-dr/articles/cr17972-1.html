Raul Castro Flies to Chile For Parley

SANTIAGO, Chile, Aug. 17 (AP).—Fidel Castro's brother Raul flies into Santiago today as the foreign ministers of Latin America and the United States argue out final details of a compromise plan to calm things down in the Caribbean.

The younger Castro's impact on the foreign ministers' talks—now in their sixth day—was likely to be light. It was not even certain he would appear before the conference. However, it was understood in Havana that he was bringing documents purporting to link the Dominican Republic's dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, with the anti-Castro conspiracy the Cuban regime says it uncovered last week.

Haiti Makes Protest

Haiti protested to the conference today against the invasion of that Negro republic by a small band of revolutionaries.

The Haitian delegation said the invaders, who terrorized a village after landing Thursday, are believed to have come from Cuba. Haitian troops are hunting them.

This was the first case of violence formally brought to the attention of the conference, called to discuss Carribbean tension.

Haiti's foreign minister, Louis Mars, delivered the protest to Foreign Minister German Vergara Donoso of Chile, chairman of the conference.

The Haitian note, however, may be referred to the Organization of American States instead of bieng taken up formally by the ministers. Their meetnig is scheduled to wind up tomorrow.

Aggression Charged

Mars declared Haiti is "the victim of aggression whose manifest objective is to alter public order and menace the stability of a constitutional government."

His complaint noted that about 30 men with long beards and green unifroms, who were possibly Cuban revolutionaries, had landed on Haiti's south coast. He said that, for the moment, Hati is not accusing Cuba or any other government as the nationality of the leaders has not been proved.

The foreign ministers have hammered out agreement on a set of resolutions to be presented to the conference's general committee. It was expected they would be signed at a final conference session tomorrow.

Study Under OAS

The key resolution would give the already existing Inter-American Peace Committee of the Organization of American States temporary powers to study "methods and procedures to prevent activities originating abroad designed to overthrow constituted governments."

The aim is to prevent such things as the invasion of Panama last April by about 100 men, mostly Cubans, recent invasions of Nicaragua from Costa Rica and Honduras, the Dominican campaign against Premier Castro and his against Gen. Trujillo.

The resolution balances this fire prevention agency urged by the United States with provisions to meeet demands by Cuba and Venezuela for emphasis on strict observance of human rights and representative democracy and economic help for underdeveloped nations.

The resolution includes a "declaration of Santiago" which restates the principle of nonintervention in each other's internal affairs; an order for a legal study defining intervention; an order for preparation of a draft treaty which would give the force of law to declarations on human rights; a resolution urging speed in putting into effect the inter-American economic program advocated by Brazilian President Juscelino Kubitschek, and a request for a study of the possibility of reducing military costs.

U. S. Heads Committee

The United States is chairman of the peace committee at present. Other members are Venezuela, Uruguay, El Salvador and Mexico. The committee's findings will be reported to the regular inter-American foreign ministers' conference scheduled to be held in Quito, Ecuador, next year.

Raul Castro said he was going to Santiago to convey the good will of the Cuban people. But during a stopover in Caracas last night he described the conference as "farcical" and said his brother is not interested in it. He also described the OAS as "useless."

"Nobody in it realizes or will deal with the real problem of the Caribbean and in all Latin America, which is the economic problem," he declared.

He also charged the OAS "frequently is used by certain people for their own needs." He asserted that Gen. Trujillo wanted the conference held to coincide "with the plot to invade Cuba."

Maj. Castro, who is commander in chief of Cuba's armed forces, was accompanied by his wife and 40 others.
