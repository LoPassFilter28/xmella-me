FBI Man Says Frank Offered Data on Trujillo

Washington Attorney John Joseph Frank, accused of acting illegally as an agent of the Dominican Republic, volunteered in 1956 to supply information about Dominican activities to the Federal Bureau of Investigation, a District court jury was told today.

FBI Agent Fred Woodcocke also told the jury during the second day of Mr. Frank's trial here that the attorney said in a 1956 interview that his first contact with Dominican Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo came as a result of a tip from a State Department official in 1954 that the Dominican strong man was getting ready to make a trip to Europe.

Mr. Woodcocke said he was told by Mr. Frank that Robert Cartwright, then connected with the State Department's Office of Security, called he attorney about the Trujillo trip. The agent said Mr. Frank's information from Mr. Cartwright was that Trujillo wanted someone who "knew about communism and could arrange for security precautions in Europe."

Edward L. Carey identified Mr. Cartwright's present job as consul general at the United States Embassy in Mexico City.

"Confidential Basis"

Mr. Woodcocke said he was told by Mr. Frank that the attorney had "occasion to meet Dominican officials" through contacts in the republic and "advised us he would give us information on a strictly confidential basis" about Dominican affairs.

But the agent said he knew of no information "to my knowledge" that Mr. Frank ever supplied to the FBI.

Earlier, District Court Judge James R. Kirkland ruled that reports of an FBI agent be turned over for examination by defense attorneys in the case.

The judge made the ruling under the terms of the Jencks law after Mr. Carey asked to see reports made on interviews with Mr. Frank by FBI Agent Fred Woodcocke in September and December of 1956 and last January.

The judge ruled, however, that only those parts of the reports can be released which relate directly to the interviews.

To Make Deletions

Two reports have already been turned over, and several others will be given to Mr. Carey for use in cross examination of the agent after certain deletions have been made by Justice Department Attorney William G. Hundley.

Mr. Frank was indicted last March by a grand jury here investigating the disappearance of Jesus Maria De Galindez, a Columbia University professor, and Gerald L. Murphy, an American pilot.

The Government has alleged that Mr. Frank carried on an investigation of Mr. De Galendez' activities in New York in opposition to Generalissimo Trujillo and worked with Mr. Murphy in arranging to get a rented airplane sent to the Dominican Republic.

The FBI agent whose reports will be examined by Mr. Carey has already testified that Mr. Frank has denied any knowledge of Mr. De Galendez's disappearance other than what appeared in press reports. Mr. Woodcocke also has testified the Washington attorney claimed he met Murphy only once—in a bar in Ciudad Trujillo, the Dominican capital.
