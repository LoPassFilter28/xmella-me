New Fare Promises More Latin American Travel

By DAVID GOLLAN

A recently published airline excursion fare good until March 31, 1958, promises to increase South American travel figures to a new high. The new rate, which came into effect on June 1, slashes almost $200 from the ordinary tourist round-trip fare.

To qualify for the excursion ticket, you must travel as far South as Buenos Aires with at least two stopovers each on the outward and inbound legs of the trip. The full itinerary must be completed within 30 days.

There are many and varied routings open to passengers originating in Washington. For the fare to one point, Buenos Aires, you are entitled to stop over in many cities and countries. Travel will be on tourist class flights using pressurized equipment with the usual 44 pound baggage allowance.

Braniff Via Miami

Braniff Airways can fly you from the Nation's Capital to Buenos Aires via Miami, Panama, Guayaquil, Lima, Peru; La Praz, Bolivia, and Asuncion, Paraguay. The round-trip fare, including Federal taxes on the Washington/Miami portion, is $647.34. Homeward bound you can visit Montevideo, Sao Paulo and Rio de Janeiro before winging across the lofty Andes en route to Panama and the States.

Braniff do not offer service between Buenos Aires and Rio, but the ticket would cover your transportation on one of several lines flying this route.

Trip to Cuzco

Proving popular with American tourists is an unforgettable side trip from Lima to Cuzco, one-time capital of the Inca Empire. The round-trip fare is $40.80 and you fly by Faucett Airlines over some of the most rugged country anywhere in the world. This section is flown by DC-4 Skymaster aircraft, which are not pressurized.

(Unpressurized cabins make the passenger aware of thin air at high altitudes and any abrupt changes in altitude.)

Pan American Grace Airways (Panagra) is scheduling seven weekly tourist class departures from Miami to Buenos Aires via the West Coast of the Continent. Stopovers on their El Pacifico flights can be made at Panama, Cali, Colombia; Quito, Ecuador; Lima, La Paz, Antofagasta and Santiago, Chile.

Switch to Pan Am

Switching to Pan American for the homeward stretch from the Argentine, you can touch down at Montevideo, Rio, and Port of Spain, Trinidad, before reaching Florida. This fare, including day-coach flights between here and Miami, is $675.14. Using night-coach schedules on the domestic portion saves you an additional $10.78.

Varig Brazilian Airlines, which maintains a K street Washington office, also participates in the new fare. Flying aircoach from Washington to New York on any line, you may connect with Varig at Idlewild.

Stopovers can then be made at Ciudad, Trujillo, Dominican Republic, Belem on the East Coast of South America, Rio, Montevideo and Buenos Aires. Including tax on the Washington, New York section, your fare will amount to $640.62. Another routing using Varig as the principal carrier would take you via Miami.

Island Connection

Flying day coach by National or Eastern to Miami, you connect at the Florida gateway with Pan American or CDA Dominican Airlines for the hop to Ciudad Trujillo. At this city you then board an incoming Varig plane from New York for South America. Travel via Miami would be $647.34 Washington back to Washington.

With filet mignon priced at 45 cents in top Buenos Aires restaurants and summer just around the corner, the Latin Continent eagerly anticipates its greatest-ever invasion of North American tourists.
