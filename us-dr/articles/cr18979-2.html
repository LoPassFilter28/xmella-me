THE SAN DOMINGO TREATY.

Efforts Being Made by European Powers to Have it Abrogated.

The Governments of Great Britain, France, Germany, and Italy are endeavoring to coerce the Republic of San Domingo into abrogating its commercial treaty with the United States. Will they succeed? is a question which is at the present time worrying State Department officials, and is one they cannot as yet answer. The Republic of San Domingo is likely to suffer, whether they abrogate the treaty with this country or not, as they are now between two fires. President Henreaux is placed in a very embarrassing position, and one from which he would gladly extricate himself; but thus far he has not succeeded.

The story of the persecution of the “Black Republic” by the European powers mentioned—for persecution the State Department officials call it—is an interesting one, and dates back one year, when negotiations looking to the ratification of the reciprocity treaty between that country and the United States were concluded. The European powers mentioned above followed these negotiations with a jealous eye, as they were the only countries outside of the United States which had any considerable commerce with the island, and it was very manifest that the adoption of such a favorable treaty would give our country a practical monopoly of San Domingo's import trade. These Nations at first protested individually, and subsequently united in a joint protest, and argued that under their respective treaties with San Domingo each was to be placed on a similar footing to that enjoyed by any other Nation with whom the Republic might form a treaty.

To this the Dominican Government replied that the only exception was in favor of the United States, and this was due to the fact that over one-half of their trade was with this country, and that their refusal to give the United States these advantages would shut off their market with us for coffee, sugar, and hides, their principal exportations. These European Governments still continued their protestations, and San Domingo's President referred the whole matter to our Government, and he furnished Secretary of State Foster with copies of the correspondence between himself and the protesting Governments, and asked Gen. Harrison's Administration if it could not assist him to escape from the situation in which he was placed.

The reply of the Government in substance was that we could not agree that the European nations in question should be placed on the same footing with us, nor would we permit any abrogation of the treaty, except that it be followed by a reimposition of the duties on San Domingo's exportations. This reply of our Government was laid before the protesting European Governments, which had the effect of silencing for a time all these nations but Germany, which nation was still obdurate, and insisted that they had been compelled to make concessions to us in return for a market in this country for their beet sugar, and that they were now impaled on the other horn of the dilemma, by the discrimination in our favor by San Domingo. President Henreaux not acceding to Germany's request, the German foreign office determined to retaliate upon San Domingo by imposing a practically prohibitory tariff upon their tobacco. This threat is the more effective, because Germany is the principal consumer of the tobacco raised on that island, and it added new embarrassment to President Henreaux's position, as the two Provinces in which tobacco is grown showed signs of discontent and mutterings of revolution were heard. There the matter stands at present.

After all, this tempest in a teapot over the concessions granted by the Dominican Government to the United States seems to be rather an absurd affair. The entire export and import trade of San Domingo does not exceed $4,500,000 annually. Of this amount, prior to our present treaty, $3,000,000 was expended with the United States alone, leaving about $1,500,000 with the rest of the world. Since the treaty went into effect our exports to San Domingo have increased something like $250,000, which has been principally at the expense of Great Britain and the continental countries.
