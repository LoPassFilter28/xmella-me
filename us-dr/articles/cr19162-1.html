The Revolution in St. Domingo

A vessel arrived at New York from Turk's Island brings some interesting details of the progress of the revolution in St. Domingo. The advices from Port-au-Platte are to the 17th ultimo. The revolution is said to be drawing rapidly to a close. With the exception of the city of St. Domingo, the whole of the Republic has declared in favor of the new Government. The revolutionists have an army of 8,000 men encamped before the walls of the city of St. Domingo, and despatches received on the 17th from the generals in command state that they were in communication with parties inside the walls, and that the inhabitants were momentarily expected to declare in their favor. Guns were heard firing, and it was supposed they were fighting among themselves. President Baez had retired to the fortress. Soulouque is said to be assembling a large force on the northern frontiers. The revolution thus far' has been accomplished without the shedding of blood and without arrests.

In a proclamation by the people of Cibao, dated at Santiago on the 8th of July, the causes which have led to this movement among the people are set forth. After stating that the previous Governments which have ruled the people have invaded personal liberty, imprisoned and arbitrarily judged the citizens, destroyed the liberty of the press, deprived the nation of its freedom, and spread terror among the people, the proclamation proceeds to state that the present administration of Baez has done more. It has been engaged in issuing additional paper money when the national treasury was deluged with gold and silver; and, furthermore, a large sum had been directly usurped from the savings of the people. The people deplore the want of good roads and the want of an organized defence against the empire of Hayti. All that contributes to the public welfare, the proclamation states, has been neglected by the Government, which, marching in the path of despotism, has submerged the nation in ignorance and deprived it of educational establishments.

Another proclamation was issued by the Dominicans on the 16th of August, asserting that Baez had combined with Soulouque to invade the republic, and had joined with him in the plan of dividing between them the spoils of the country.
