Gen. Trujillo to Be Feted by Dominican Minister

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

Oh, ye of little faith! Yes, you were disappointed when the reception in honor of the Dominican Republic's Gen. Rafael Trujillo was cancelled, weren't you? But it's all right now. All those who had accepted the invitation to the Pan American reception the Dominican Minister and Senora de Pastoriza planned for July 8, will get in on the party to be given Thursday afternoon at the Legation.

In lieu of those terrifyingly formal invitations which summoned several hundred guests to that fete at the Pan American Union, the Minister and his wife have sent out just normal big white invites. It's to be an informal cocktail party and just across the top of the card is written by hand, "In honor of Gen. Trujillo." That's all. No R. S. V. P. about it. No decorations and medals. Just a chance to say hello to the Strong Man of the Dominican Republic.

Now that is a very smart move. Washington is too far sunk in mid-summer lethargy to haul out the old white ties and tails again. Some people say that's one reason the reception of two weeks ago was cancelled—that, and of course, the fact that Secretary Swanson died. But there just weren't enough people in town to make a big showing in the huge rooms of the Pan American Union. And there weren't enough people who were willing to deck themselves in formal winter attire on a hot evening.

So it's all working out for the best. The General will have a chance to talk to the people he has known and everybody will have a good time. The Minister, Andres Pastoriza, who has been in New York giving his chief a whirl, will be back the early part of next week to see that things run smoothly.

After his return visit to the Capital, Gen. Trujillo will sail for Europe to join Senora de Trujillo in Paris, and to make the acquaintance of his small daughter born in the French capital some weeks ago. The General doesn't do things by halves. When he left his island republic for the first time early in July, he didn't content himself with merely a jaunt to Washington. No indeed. He bought one of the biggest yachts afloat, "Junky" Fleischman's Camargo, renamed it Ramfis for his young son, and sailed over to Miami.

Since then the yacht has been in New York and the General has been doing his traveling around those parts aboard her. But for his first trip to Europe—whew, what a thrill to be on your very first voyage across the ocean!—Trujillo has chosen the Normandie. But the Ramfis will follow close behind to be on hand for any European cruising the owner wants to do.

Paris, among other places, is on the schedule. To date no mention has been made of a visit to Germany and a chat with Der Fuehrer about that famous Samana Bay business. Last spring, when it was rumored that Gen. Trujillo was en route to this country, it was simultaneously reported that he was en route to Germany! Well, he has been to Washington, met our President, sowed whatever seeds of business he had to sow. Maybe he is really a bargainer and wants to put his proposition to Mr. Hitler! Anyhow, there won't be any politics or business at the party Thursday afternoon.
