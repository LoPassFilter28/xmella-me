Revolutionaries Claim Capture of 3 Cities on Dominican North Coast

By the Associated Press

HAVANA, Cuba, June 21.—Revolutionaries invading the Dominican Republic to overthrow the government of President Rafael L. Trujillo claimed last night they had captured three cities on the north coast.

A radio station identifying itself as "The Voice of the Revolution" said San Pedro Macoris and Puerto Plata—third and fourth cities in size in the republic—had fallen and also claimed to have occupied Monte Christi, near the border of Haiti.

Reports from Ciudad Trujillo, on the south coast, said that capital city of the republic was calm and there were no indications of revolutionary activity. Dispatches said transport, telephone and telegraph communications were normal and that all provincial capitals were quiet, too.

General Strike Urged.

The Dominican Ministry of War yesterday said invaders, landing on the north coast from two seaplanes, had been killed and the situation was under control.

The revolutionary broadcasts heard here urged Dominican workers to start a general strike in support of the insurgents and a feminine voice call on Dominican women to help.

The radio broadcasting on a frequency of 6,000 kilocycles, seemed to be putting out a powerful signal.

It signed off about midnight but said it would continue more broadcasts today.

Rebels Declared Advancing.

Meanwhile, headquarters of a group calling itself the Caribbean Democratic Action, said here that revolutionary forces led by Gen. Miguel Angel Ramirez were advancing southward toward Ciudad Trujillo from San Pedro Macoris. Juan Bosch, a Dominican exile, said the troops were only 70 miles from the capital.

The CDA is an organization of Dominican exiles and Cuban sympathizers. Its general secretary, Alejandro Fidel Valdes, a Cuban, called for volunteers to go to the republic.

In September, 1947, the Cuban government crushed an expeditionary force being recruited to storm the Dominican Republic.

The Spanish-Speaking Dominican Republic occupies roughly two-thirds of the island of Hispaniola (Santo Domingo) or 19,332 square miles. The French-speaking Republic of Haiti makes up the remainder of the island.

There are about 2,183,109 people in the Dominican Republic gaining their living mostly from sugar and coffee growing and cattle raising.

The country was occupied by United States Marines from 1916 to 1925 and until 1947 the United States dollar was the official currency. It was replaced then by the gold peso whose value is the same.

Rafael L. Trujillo Molina seized control of the country in a revolution in 1930 and has since ruled with a iron hand.

In 1936 he changed the name of the capital city from Santo Domingo to Ciudad Trujillo.

There is only one legal political party—the Dominican—controlled by Trujillo whose official title is "Benefactor of the Fatherland."
