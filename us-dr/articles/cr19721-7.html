A few words may be said of

THE POPULATION

subsisting on the shores of the great bay of Samana. The primitive people, who are said by Columbus and others to have been very numerous, were the Ciguayens, and were a fierce and warlike people. The population now consists of the descendants of the Canarians or Islenos, the French refugees from Hayti, creoles from the same country and the Spaniards of St. Domingo. In 1824 another element was added in the arrival of a few free colored people from the States of Pennsylvania and Maryland. About 800 of these people and their children are still living, the majority at Samana. These people rank among the most intelligent and industrious on the island. In 1851 the population of the town of Samana was about 1,800 souls, of which nearly 300 were colored Americans. The chief commerce of Samana is with Turk’s Island, shipping provisions to that barren spot, and receiving in return quantities of salt.

The small town of Santa Barbara is situated on the acclivities bordering a small bight on the north side of the bay, about six miles northwest of Cay Levantado. It was founded in 1756 by Don Francisco Rubroy Peñarando, then Governor of St. Domingo.
