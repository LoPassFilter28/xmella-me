Evening Star · May 14, 193514 de mayo de 1935

Original in English.Original en inglés.

STROKE IS FATAL TO GEN. HARRY LEE

A fatal heart attack last evening ended the career of Maj. Gen. Harry Lee, commandant of the Marine Corps post at Quantico, Va., following a brief illness.

He was 63 years old and during his 37 years in the service had attained distinction in the World War, when he commanded the 6th Regiment of Marines overseas and later as military governor of Santo Domingo.

Friends here recalled today that Gen. Lee as a young man once served as gate keeper at the old Sixth Street station for the Pennsylvania Railroad. His father was one of the chiefs of division in the Adjutant General's Office at the War Department. He was born here June 4, 1872.

At Marine Corps Headquarters it was said plans are being made for his burial tomorrow in Arlington National Cemetery with full military honors. Tentative arrangements call for services at 9 a.m. at the Quantico post, with Lieut. August F. Hohl of the Navy Chaplain Corps, officiating. At 11:30 a.m. the body is due to reach Fort Myer, Va. Further details of the last rites will be announced later, as will the honorary pallbearers.

Stricken Saturday.

Gen. Lee suffered a severe heart attack Saturday morning and this resulted in his death about 8:45 o'clock last evening. Capt. W. L. Mann, U. S. N. M. C., post surgeon at Quantico, remained at the stricken general's bedside for some 60 hours without rest, in an effort to give him every medical attention.

Flags were at half staff at Quantico today.

Maj. Gen. John H. Russell, commandant of the Marine Corps, said in a statement at the Navy Department:

"Gen. Lee's death leaves me keenly aware of what a great loss the Marine Corps has sustained in his passing. To me personally, the loss is deeply touching, for not only were we close friends, but brother officers who served for many years together in the Marine Corps."

Surviving the Quantico general are his widow, three daughters, Evelyn, Mercedes and Laura; two sons, Harry, jr., and Ernest, and a sister, Mrs. George Watson of this city.

Cited for Services.

Taking over command of the Quantico post on March 1, 1933, Gen. Lee thus climaxed an illustrious career which he began as a second lieutenant during the Spanish-American War. He was second in command of the famous 6th Regiment of Marines at Chateau Thierry during the World War, and later he commanded the regiment in the Aisne-Marne offensive, the Marbache sector, the St. Mihiel offensive, the Meuse-Argonne offensive and in the Allied armies' march to the Rhine.

Gen. Lee received 15 decorations, citations and medals for his service in France. Included in these were the Distinguished Service Medal from both the War and Navy Departments, the French Legion of Honor, three times the Croix de Guerre and four citations for his conduct with the 2d Division.

He first saw service in Cuba and was promoted through the various grades until he became a permanent major general on May 29 last. He served in the Philippine Islands, in China aboard several vessels of the Navy, in Panama, in Nicaragua, in Haiti, Santo Domingo.

As a lieutenant colonel on August 1, 1917, Gen. Lee joined the 6th Regiment of Marines and went overseas with it, returning to the United States in August, 1919. After the war he commanded the 1st Regiment of Marines at Philadelphia, served for a time at Quantico and then went to the Dominican Republic to command the 2d Brigade of Marines there. In December, 1922, he was appointed military governor of Santo Domingo. Later he was commanding general of the Marine barracks at Parris Island, S. C., and from that duty he came to Quantico.
