Washington Is Worried About San Domingo. With Four Separate Revolutions Raging Simultaneously United States Wonders How Peace Is to Be Restored There.

[ASSOCIATED PRESS DISPATCH]

WASHINGTON, July 8—Reckless firing by Dominican rebels sent several shots against the hull of the American gunboat Machias in Puerta Plata harbor late last night and drew upon themselves a warning volley from the Machias' three-pounders and automatic rifles. The shooting stopped at once.

A few days ago the gunboat used her main battery four-inch guns to silence President Bordas' batteries, which were bombarding the rebel garrison holding the city in violation of the agreement that the lives of foreigners, and other non-combatants shall not be endangered by artillery fire. After the firing last night the revolutionists who bombarded the Machias gave assurances of their desire to avoid endangering the safety of Americans.

WASHINGTON, July 8.—With four separate revolutions raging simultaneously and the American minister to that country returning home after several months of fruitless efforts to restore peace, the little republic of San Domingo is causing grave worry on the part of the Washington government which is morally bound to maintain order there under the semi-protectorate created by treaty.

Official opinion here is slowly crystallizing into the conviction that only through powerful outside influence, probably that of the United States, can these chaotic conditions be terminated and a stable government set up. The revolutionists appear to be unable to overcome the Bordas government, which is supported by monthly proportions of customs receipts handed over by the American collectors, but Bordas has not been able to diminish the number of his enemies after a six months campaign. Mr. Vick, the American collector of customs, has resigned and now Minister Sullivan is returning to the United States, leaving Secretary White in charge of the American legation, without any definite assurance that he will ever resume his official duties.

The state department is still reluctant to intervene forcibly in San Domingo at this time, however, and it is expected that an effort will be made to send some strong man to the republic who may secure the confidence of all elements and bring them together. Meanwhile the navy is maintaining a considerable fleet in Dominican waters, including the South Carolina and Machias at Puerto Plata, the Marietta at Sanchez and the Castine at Azua. Other vessels are on the Haytien end of the island in case of need.

Today's reports showed no improvement in conditions on the island. For many months past, President Bordas has been obliged to maintain himself in office by a series of extra-official decrees and partial elections and has been leading in person his troops against rebellious generals in the northern part of the republic without being able to overcome them. Meanwhile revolutions have broken out in other sections, at Azua, where the government navy found it necessary to destroy the custom house, along the line of railroad between Sanchez and Samana Bay at Monte Christi and at La Romana in the southeast corner of the island. In this last neighborhood there are some extensive American-owned plantations, the agents of which appeared at the state department looking for protection of their property from unorganized bands of revolutionists.
