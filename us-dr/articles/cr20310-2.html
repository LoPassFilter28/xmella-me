U.S. and Santo Domingo Renew Diplomatic Ties

By the Associated Press

The United States and the Dominican Republic resumed diplomatic relations today.

The step was the latest in a series of international moves to return the island nation to a normal situation within the inter-American system as a result of internal political changes.

The United States and other American countries broke off formal diplomatic relations after a decision by Western Hemisphere foreign ministers in August, 1960, to impose sanctions as a result of an assassination attempt on President Romulo Betancourt of Venezuela.

A year ago the sanctions were stiffened. They continued in force for months after the assassination of the late Dominican dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo. His death, however, opened a period of political reform in the republic which led to the present improvement in its international relations.

Restoration of relations occurred when a United States representative presented his credentials to the foreign office in Santo Domingo at the same hour a Dominican representative in Washington presented credentials to the State Department.

"These two acts constituted the resumption of diplomatic relations between the two countries," the State Department then announced at noon.

The United States representative is John Calvin Hill, jr., who has been consul general at Santo Domingo. He now holds the position of diplomat temporarily in charge of United States affairs in Santo Domingo pending the appointment of an ambassador.

The Dominican government designated Dr. Marco A. de Pena as its temporary diplomatic representative in Washington.

The resumption of relations, the State Department said, "follows the decision of the Council of the Organization of American States on January 4, 1962, to discontinue the (sanction) measures . . . which called for, among other things, the breaking of diplomatic relations of all the member states of the Organization of American States with the Dominican Republic."
