Mansfield Presses OAS For Quarantine of Cuba

By the Associated Press

Senate Democratic Leader Mansfield called today for consideration of a hemispheric quarantine of Cuba by the United States and other members of the Organization of American States.

The Montanan, a member of the Foreign Relations Committee, said it is time the OAS took some active leadership toward ousting communism from Cuba instead of merely "passing resolutions and making declarations."

Noting that it was just six months ago that President Kennedy imposed a quarantine to force Soviet offensive missiles out of Cuba, Senator Mansfield said in an interview:

"I hope that the unanimity of the OAS, as expressed so solidly and emphatically six months ago, could now transform that organization into a policy making body which would consider the possibility of a hemisphere quarantine against Cuba."

The Democratic leader said there was precedent for this action in the 1960 economic sanctions imposed against the Dominican Republic when Dictator Rafael Trujillo was in power there.

President Kennedy has ruled out military action under present circumstances. Senator Mansfield's proposal would be designed to tighten the economic screws on Cuba without the necessity of using armed forces.

Meanwhile, Attorney General Robert F. Kennedy stepped into the simmering debate over Cuban exiles.

For one thing, Mr. Kennedy denied that his brother, President Kennedy, had promised Jose Miro Cardona that he would back a second invasion of Cuba.

In quitting Thursday night as president of the Cuban Revolutionary Council, Mr. Miro Cardona issued a statement alleging the President had made such a promise and that he then reneged.

The President has denied the charge. Backing him up, the Attorney General said in a radio-television interview (ABC—Issues and Answers):

"I happened to have been present during the conversation and of course nothing like that ever happened."

The Attorney General urged, also, unification of Cuban refugees into a single organization that could speak with one voice and have "some relationship" with the United States and other countries. Such a step, he said, would be the most important one exiles could take in efforts to drive communism from Cuba.

In a separate interview, Senator Sparkman, Democrat of Alabama, said the Kennedy administration appears to be moving "steadily forward and in an orderly way" on a course that eventually will topple Fidel Castro.

But Senator Mundt, Republican of South Dakota, said there has been a "sorry series of retreats" by the Democratic administration in the six months since the October quarantine move.

Senator Hickenlooper of Iowa, chairman of the Senate Republican Policy Committee, couldn't find much progress in efforts to oust Prime Minister Castro. But Senator Hartke, Democrat of Indiana, said the solution is to obtain the right kind of hemispheric co-operation.

Senator Thurmond, Democrat of South Carolina, a member of the Armed Services Committee, counseled strong action. He said the United States should demand removal of Soviet troops from Cuba and break off diplomatic relations with the Kremlin if the demand were not complied with.

In this case, he proposed an air and naval blockade of the island. He said direct military action, in concert with other OAS members, should be taken as a last resort.
