DECLARATIONS MADE BY FORMER PRESIDENT OF MEXICO EMILIO PORTES GIL

I HAVE just returned from Santo Domingo where I had been invited by the University and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to become a member of a study Committee to which would be submitted a questionnaire regarding international politics.

The Committee was composed of Professor Marcel Roussin, Director of the Inter-American Institute of the University of Ottawa, Dr. Enrique Corominas, a well-known jurist and former president of the Peace Committee of the Organization of American States, Dr. Geoffrey Hornsey, Dean of the Faculty of Law of Leeds University, Great Britain and Attorney Genaro V. Vasquez, former Minister of the Supreme Court and former Secretary of Labor for Mexico. The Committee completed its task in ten days, having consulted every Statute, Treaty, Juridical Antecedent and Jurisprudence connected with the question.

After carrying out a careful analysis we came to the conclusions which I now have pleasure in handing to you. Any discussion or study in which five lawyers are taking part, is very difficult, but the matter was so clear and concise that right from the first moment, we were in complete agreement on the respective points, that is, two points, without expressing any sort of opinion whatever as to the internal regime of the Dominican Republic, a matter we ignored completely, respecting the doctrine of that great Mexican jurist, Don Genaro Estrada which states that no country has a right to pass judgment on the government of another country since each and every one is entitled to the government of its choice. The report we produced was limited exclusively to Treaties between Nations and the Statutes of the O. A. S.

Diplomats are sometimes indiscreet and in the Dominican Republic, as in the United States, there is the belief that the United States State Department led the maneuver against the Dominican Government, the belief being based on the following conjecture: "I will use the power of the O. A. S. to overthrow Trujillo and, once that precedent has been established, we will use the same power to overthrow Prime Minister Fidel Castro, of Cuba." That means to say, with such a precedent, the organization of the O. A. S. is converting itself into a super state composed of 21 Republics and, if it is dangerous for one country to subvert the institutional order of another, it will undoubtedly be very much more dangerous to use the power of a super state to violate the sovereignty of weaker nations.

What would the United States say if tomorrow, or the day after, a request was presented to the O. A. S. to investigate racial discrimination and the lynching of negroes and, what is still more unjust, the discrimination shown against Mexicans, Puerto Ricans, Cubans and other immigrants and tourists whose skin is dark? Would the United States allow it? Would the O. A. S. super state have the valor and nerve to make such a decision in this connection? I must state that I understand that the President of the O. A. S. Peace Committee who led the maneuver against the Dominican Republic, is a United States delegate who has now resigned. I would also like to cite the declaration made by Don Manuel Tello, the highly erudite Mexican Minister of Foreign Relations: "The Mexican Government on examing the request presented by you, considers that it has before it two grave responsibilities: the first, not to abandon the principle of non-intervention and, the second, not to establish a precedent which might subsequently be invoked to the detriment of other countries in the American Continent." This declaration was made by Foreign Minister Tello on June 5 last, in reply to a petition presented by the Latin American Student Front asking for the breaking off of our diplomatic relations with the Dominican Republic. It would almost appear as if Don Manuel Tello could foresee what was going to happen in connection with the O. A. S. agreement, an agreement that has established a very regrettable precedent by violating the sovereignty of a weaker nation. Furthermore, I am of the opinion that the United States totally lacks justification for what it is doing. To "overthrow Trujillo so as to later overthrow Fidel Castro" is an illusion. Trujillo may fall or he may not, but it will be extremely difficult for Fidel Castro to fall. This man has brought about a deep social revolution which will be extremely difficult to undo in the future, and he has taught Latin America to lose its fear of United States leaders, and I mean, the fear of United States leaders and not of the North American people who are great, well-intentioned and only wishing for peace and coexistence with our Spanish American countries. A peace without human exploitation and without the exaggerated ideas of the great imperialist companies of suffocating, or trying to suffocate the economic freedom of our countries, countries that are ignominiously called under-developed, by extracting raw materials at ridiculous prices and then dumping in order to suffocate free competition. Such actions are in accordance with what former Secretary of State, John Foster Dulles said, when he declared: "The United States are not interested in the friendship of other countries, but they are interested in their investments," through which doctrine the United States has not only lost the sympathy of our poor countries which they unpityingly call under-developed, but also the sympathies of their European allies and of the millions of Asiatics and Africans the United States press call Communists simply because they want to get away from the slavery and colonialism under which they have lived for so many hundreds of years.

To sum up, the Study Committee of which I formed part went to the Dominican Republic to play the part of a Peace Committee and not to serve a regime which we have no right to classify."

Attorney Portes Gil then handed the newspapermen the report issued by the Committee of Jurists to the Dominican Ministry of Foreign Affairs and to the University of Santo Domingo.
