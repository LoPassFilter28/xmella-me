This long feature charges that President Morales used peace compacts arranged with American help, one by Commander Dillingham of the Detroit at Monte Cristi and another in the south aboard the Bancroft, to get the rebels to lay down their arms, conceding anything in the agreements and then keeping none of his promises. It describes the shooting of surrendered rebel chiefs, fighting at La Vega, Moca and Esperanza in which the Minister of War was killed and government troops routed, and Morales's attempt, backed by the Bancroft's investigation, to retake Monte Cristi after claiming its people had broken their compact.Este extenso reportaje acusa al presidente Morales de usar acuerdos de paz concertados con ayuda americana, uno por el comandante Dillingham, del Detroit, en Monte Cristi y otro en el sur a bordo del Bancroft, para lograr que los rebeldes depusieran las armas, concediendo cualquier cosa en los acuerdos para luego no cumplir ninguna de sus promesas. Describe el fusilamiento de jefes rebeldes rendidos, combates en La Vega, Moca y Esperanza en los que murió el ministro de Guerra y las tropas del gobierno fueron derrotadas, y el intento de Morales, respaldado por la investigación del Bancroft, de retomar Monte Cristi alegando que su población había roto su acuerdo.

The Sun · October 16, 190416 de octubre de 1904

Original in English.Original en inglés.

Morales has now given out that Luis Pelletier and Candelaria de las Rosas, both Generals commanding the revolutionary forces in the south, with whom he celebrated a treaty about the time of his inauguration, have started another revolution, and he has sent one of the gunboats to Azua with soldiers to suppress it. The facts, as near as they can be learned here, are that neither of these men has started a revolution, but Morales has anticipated them in an outbreak which has been brewing ever since he demonstrated clearly that he had no intention of keeping any of his agreements. He demonstrated this by the shooting of José Gonzales, Pedro Moto and several other former revolutionary chiefs who surrendered according to the terms of the various treaties guaranteeing them their lives and liberty.

José Gonzales was a chief under Perico Lasala and contrary to the advice of the old rebel he accepted Morales's promise of amnesty and went in and surrendered. A few days after his surrender he was arrested and under escort of two negro soldiers, sent to Santiago on the pretence that his presence was required at that place in connection with some court proceedings. The negro soldiers when a few miles out of town, on the lone country road, shot their prisoner in the back, and leaving him dead in the road returned with the information that he had tried to escape and they had shot him.

As such executions as this have been of common occurrence ever since Morales has held the reins of government, it can scarcely be expected that the people would be content with his rule. Ciefs who have seen their friends and former comrades shot in cold blood have sought refuge in the districts of Monte Cristi, Azua and the interior mountains. Perico Lasala has left his mountain retreat to confer with the Governor of Monte Christi, as also has Candelaria de la Rosas journeyed over the mountains of the interior for the same purpose, and it is known that these men have held a conference at Monte Cristi.

The result of that conference is not known, but Morales, anticipating them, has declared some of them in rebellion, particularly Rosas and Pelletier, and Pelletier, to avoid arrest, has fled to the mountains. Ramon Carceres, the leader of the Horacista party, who placed Morales in power, has resigned from the Cabinet, and although he declares that it is simply because he desires to retire from politics, his resignation is looked upon as significant.

There is no question that the revolution will be on just as soon as the old Jimenez faction can unite on a leader. The majority of the inhabitants throughout the country are strong adherents of Jimenez, but the leaders are satisfied that Jimenez is not the man to conduct a successful revolution. He has no ability as a warrior or ruler. If he had had the ability of a fighting man, the Horacistas would never have chased him out of the Presidential chair, for he was popular throughout the country, it being asserted that he was the one ruler which Santo Domingo has ever known to maintain a government without executing his political enemies.
