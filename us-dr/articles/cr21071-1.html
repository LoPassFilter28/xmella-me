Waited Thirty-Odd Years for Country to Regain Freedom

President of Santo Domingo at Least Sees His Dreams Coming True.

By BOB DORMAN,

NEA Service Writer.

SANTO DOMINGO, S. D., Nov. 29.—We dream—and the dreams vanish with the dawn. Only to a lucky few is it given to see their dreams come true—to have their castles in fact as well as fancy.

Horatio Vasquez, 63-year-old president of Santo Domingo, is one of the few lucky ones.

For 34 years he dreamed—built castles in the air.

His dream—a free and independent Santo Domingo; his castle—a Dominican government "of the people; by the people, and for the people."

Peace at Last Comes.

Now the time of realization seems to be at hand. Santo Domingo is pacified—the country is at peace. The armed forces of the United States which have held sovereignty over the republic for so long, have left.

The reins of government have been placed in the hands of Vasquez by the people in order that he may rule for them.

What manner of man is he whose task it is not only to give honest government to a land that has known none, but to restore that friendly relationship between the United States and Santo Domingo which is so necessary to the economic welfare of both?

A Dignified Man.

Kindly eyes look out at you through old-fashioned glasses.

White hair, a white mustache and white imperial, lend dignity to a full face, that is seamed with lines of endeavor, of care and worry.

Despite his age, he carries himself with the sturdy erectness of youth.

"I am a poor man," he told me. "I was a merchant in Puerto Plata, back in 1886, a partner in one of the most prosperous establishments there.

"I continued it for five years and the prospects for success were bright. But that was back in the days of Ulysses Heureaux, who, for 18 years, kept Santo Domingo under a dictatorship, and whose name was a synonym for murder, rape, robbery and assassination; whose government took away from the people every vestige of the rights held by them under our constitution.

"It was unbearable.

Took No Part in Murder.

"I am a man of peace, but I took up arms and swore never to rest until Santo Domingo was free; until she possessed an honest and free government.

"I had no part in the final murder of Heureaux; it was a just retribution for the many murders that he had committed but two wrongs do not make a right.

"Since then I have been waging continual warfare on those whose aim has been government by oppression and riches by graft.

"And I believe that now most of the undesirables have been eliminated; those that remain we can control.

"The prospects for Santo Domingo are bright; our first work is the reformation of our constitution and then we will devote ourselves to the economic rehabilitation of the country, continue the building of roads and railroads; the establishment of irrigation systems where necessary; increase of the present educational facilities; a general improvement of the living conditions of the working class; and the re-establishment of the former cordial relations between the two sister republics—the United States and Santo Domingo."
