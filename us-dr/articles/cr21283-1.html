Haiti Charges Troops Bashed Babies to Death

By LOUIS J. HEATH

Copyright, 1937, by United Press

An estimated 10,000 Haitians, terrified by alleged ruthless attacks by Dominican rural police and soldiery which cost the lives of "thousands" of their fellow countrymen in the Dominican Republic, have fled to their homeland, it was reported here today.

A report from an American resident in northern Haiti, dated Dec. 18, said more than 5000 Haitians had crossed the border and the flow of refugees was continuing.

Meanwhile Elie Lescot, Haitian minister to the United States, in an official communique issued here, fixed the number of Haitian victims of the alleged mass killings at 12,168.

The communique charged that mass killings had taken place in more than 65 Dominican localities almost simultaneously and at a given signal. The document alleges that victims were slain without distinction as to age or sex, that pregnant women were shot and that children were stabbed and their heads beaten against walls.

Lescot's estimate of the number of dead was the highest thus far. President Stenio Vincent of Haiti recently placed the figure at 8000. The communique was issued at the order of the Haitian government.

CITES PRESIDENT'S SPEECH

The American's report said stories of wounded refugees, substantiated by Father Robert, a priest of Ouanaminthe, convinced the American that "several thousand Haitians were killed."

The American reported that he went to Ouanaminthe Oct. 7 to investigate reports that 212 Haitians had crossed the border on the previous day at that point and that he found a large crowd of refugees awaiting word from relatives from whom they had become separated in flight. The refugees, the writer said, reported that President Rafael Leonidas Trujillo Molena made a speech at a ball at Dajabon on Oct. 3 and that the attacks on the Haitians began immediately thereafter.

HURLED INTO SEA

"I saw the Haitian consul, who closed his office in Dajabon on Oct. 7 on the grounds that the attitude of the Dominican authorities was so threatening he felt his life was in danger," the report continued. "He had seen four murdered Haitians on Oct. 5 in a house in Dajabon but had not been permitted to make any investigation — or even to bury the dead. The civil authorities told him that it was a matter for the local military commander to handle, and when he appealed to the commander he was advised that the Sanitary Service would take care of the matter and that he could not guarantee the life of any Haitian."

The report described the arrival of a group of refugees who had been hiding at Monte Christi.

"These people stated that on Sunday night a group of 50 Haitians had been marched to the wharf at Monte Christi by a number of Guardia (Dominican rural police) and thrown into the sea and drowned," the writer reported.

WHOLESALE ARRESTS

"On the following day wholesale arrests began of all Haitians unable to hide themselves or flee during the night."

The Haitian government, it was reported, provided trucks and a small motor vessel to aid the flight of their nationals. Fourteen Haitians were brought to one border hospital, all suffering from knife or machete wounds.

"In most cases they insisted that they were the only survivors of their groups and had escaped by feigning death after being wounded," the report went on.

A report from J. M. Jan, French Bishop of Cape Haitian, quoted President Trujillo as having said substantially that he had investigated complaints of Dominicans against "depredations of Haitians in their midst" and said, "I can settle that."

FED TO SHARKS

" 'We have begun to settle the question; already 300 Haitians are dead at Banica and the settlement must continue,'" the bishop quotes Trujillo as saying.

The prelate records that he questioned a Frenchman refugee who escaped because of his nationality and who reported that Haitians were herded into Monte Christi, thrown into the sea as food for sharks, "led into the woods near the city to be massacred and told of nameless savageries which the pen cannot describe."
