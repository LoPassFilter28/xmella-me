Milford Chronicle · January 16, 195316 de enero de 1953

Original in English.Original en inglés.

HE LOCKED HIS STABLE

Generalissimo Rafael L. Trujillo, former president of the Dominican Republic and presently Ambassador-at-large and delegate to the United Nations, was a guest of honor at a dinner the other night at Tregaron, the fabulous Washington home of former Ambassador and Mrs. Joseph E. Davies—where the Generalissimo was described by Chief Justice Fred M. Vinson as a latter day George Washington.

Trujillo, the Chief Justice told the dinner group, which included General Omar Bradley, Senator Estes Kefauver and Justice Stanley Reed, is one of the great personalities of our time, and the man who lighted the torch of civilization for his country.

Which reminds us, that besides giving his country a stable government and a sound economy, the Generalissimo may also have provided an example of enlightenment to the United States. We have off our desk a translation of Dominican "Law No. 1443" signed by Trujillo on June 14, 1947, outlawing the communist party as representing "doctrines and platforms that are incompatible with the civil, republican, democratic and representative nature of the Government of the Republic."

But in this country of ours, the number one target of the Kremlin, there is today no Federal ban on the Communist Party, which is openly and avowedly devoted to the destruction of our Constitution. Neighbor Trujillo reminds us that we are stretching the intent of this document and the mantle of freedom in sheltering a criminal ideology.
