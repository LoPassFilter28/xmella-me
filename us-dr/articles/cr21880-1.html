The Evening Star reported that Cuban magistrate Evelio Tabio ordered the release of nearly 1,200 participants in the aborted anti-Trujillo invasion expedition, holding only its purported leaders in custody. A captured diary published by the newspaper Hoy detailed training at Holguin and miserable conditions on Cayo Confites, where troops endured internal infighting, accidental drownings, and failed logistics aboard vessels including the Aurora, Berta, and Angelita. Un diario de bitácora ocupado y divulgado por el diario Hoy detalló los adiestramientos castrenses en Holguín y las precarias penalidades sufridas en Cayo Confites, donde los reclutas padecieron motines internos, ahogamientos accidentales y severos descalabros de abasto a bordo de los barcos Aurora, Berta y Angelita. El fracaso del complot culminó con rendiciones multitudinarias en Cayo Guinchos y la bahía de Antilla.

Cuba Cabinet Member Quits After Link to Dominican Invasion

By the Associated Press

HAVANA, Cuba, Oct. 2.—Education Minister Jose Manuel Aleman, whose name has been mentioned in connection with a recently smashed expedition organized in Cuba to invade the Dominican Republic, quit his job last night under fire.

The Cuban Senate had voted "no confidence" in Mr. Aleman on the ground that arms were found at his home and that he allegedly had on his payroll persons not connected with his ministry.

Mr. Aleman resigned at once. President Ramon Grau San Martin immediately appointed him a minister without portfolio and elevated Undersecretary Carlos F. Arazosa to the Education Ministry. Mr. Aleman had held the education post since May, 1946.

Col. Oscar Diaz of the Cuban Army charged at a news conference Monday that Mr. Aleman was implicated in the organization of the expedition plotted to overthrow the Dominican Republic government of President Rafael Trujillo.

The diary of one of the troopers in the 1,500-man expedition, published yesterday in the newspaper Hoy, said he had been given rifle practice for 13 days at the military and civic institute at Holguin, in Eastern Cuba. The institute is under control of the Cuban Education Ministry.

Senators, in voting nonconfidence in Mr. Aleman, said that arms, including depth bombs, were seized by Cuban soldiers September 20 at the ranch home which Mr. Aleman rents. Other Cuban officials have linked this arms seizure to the Dominican expedition.

In the meantime, Magistrate Evelio Tabio of the supreme tribunal of the Criminal Court announced that only the purported leaders of the expeditionary force would be kept in custody after today. Nearly 1,200 others now being held will be released.

About 725 members of the expedition who surrendered Tuesday at the port of Antilla arrived here last night by train to join 360 others who were picked up earlier from Guinchos Cay, a sandspit off the Cuban coast.

Mr. Tabio did not disclose the names of those he considered to be the leaders, nor their number.

Hoy withheld the name of the trooper who wrote the diary. The diary's account:

The volunteer enlisted July 20 for the campaign against President Rafael Trujillo, was trained at Holguin and then transferred with other revolutionaries from a coastal point to Confites Key. The diary said the men were moved aboard the ships Aurora and Berta and a Dominican schooner registered as the Angelita, which the expeditionary force captured near Confites Keys.

Two volunteers drowned while being disembarked. One was a Cuban, the other a Venezuelan. One man was killed and some others were wounded in fighting among themselves.

The troops became discontented over delays in action against the Dominican Republic. On August 16, which was D-day, a ship expected to arrive with war material and food did not make port. The diary said the troops showed distrust afterwards upon learning there had been fighting among members of their high command. Food and water also were short.
