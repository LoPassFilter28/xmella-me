Original in English.Original en inglés.

U. S. RULE BRINGS ORDER TO HAYTI, BUT MAY EXPOSE IT TO GERMAN ATTACK The Island's Two Republics Now Under American Protection

Port-au-Prince, capital of the Republic of Hayti, looking toward the sea. Back of the city rise the mountains. It has more than 100,000 inhabitants and is the chief port of the foreign commerce of Hayti. The houses are mostly of wood. Some of the Government buildings are of an ambitious order. The region round about is exceedingly rich, but remains undeveloped owing to misgovernment and frequent revolutions.

Old cathedral, Port-au-Prince, one of the sights tourists seek out because in it images and pictures of the Virgin and the saints are colored brown or black to gratify the sentiment of the people.

Port-au-Prince has some broad, well paved streets, as this picture shows, and it has also some luxurious homes. But the city has suffered so much from fire and war that the greater part of it presents a ramshackle or half ruined appearance.

The Columbus Tower, Santo Domingo, seen from the city. The gateway in the foreground is of much later construction. The soldiers show the Spanish strain which prevails in the Republic of Santo Domingo, whereas in the Republic of Hayti the African type is dominant.

The Columbus Tower at the entrance to the port of Santo Domingo, capital of the Dominican Republic, seen from the water. The city is the oldest city and the tower the oldest structure of the kind in America.

The market at Port-au-Prince is large and well planned. Business is carried on exclusively by women, who are picturesque and shrewd traders. Many of them come long distances from the country carrying the fruits and vegetables they sell.

[Editor's note: the paper writes of the east as part of Hayti.
