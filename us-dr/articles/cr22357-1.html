DOMINICAN REBELS REPORTED FIRED ON BY AMERICAN SHIP Private Message to San Juan, Porto Rico, Says Marines Were Landed But Later Withdrawn. ANNEXATION PARTY GROWING. Business Men of Much-Disturbed Republic Seek Safety of Life and Property. NO QUIBBLING OVER DETAILS. If United States Should Interfere It Would Find Conditions Vastly Different From Anything in Cuba.

San Juan, Porto Rico, Feb. 17.—A private message has been received here from San Domingo, dated February 17, saying that an American warship bombarded the insurgents at Pajarito, near San Domingo, and then landed marines. The men, however, subsequently re-embarked.

EAGER FOR ANNEXATION.

SPECIAL CORRESPONDENCE OF THE NEW YORK HERALD AND THE REPUBLIC.

San Juan, Porto Rico, Feb. 10, 1904.—A strenuous effort is now being made by the most prominent merchants of Porto Plata to bring about the annexation of Santo Domingo to the United States, with a view to extricating the country from its chaotic condition.

Annexation to the United States has long been the cherished dream of the Dominican commercial element. Not much thought is given to the particular form of government the United States would establish, it being immaterial to the business men whether it be annexation or a protectorate, so long as a firm and stable government be established, insuring peace and guaranteeing life and property.

Should the American Government be ultimately induced to interfere in Dominican affairs, it will be confronted with a situation that it has at no time had to deal with. Cuba, Porto Rico and the Philippines were ruled by an organized government when the United States took charge of them, and it has since been the task of the United States to adapt that administration to the American methods. No such thing will be found in Santo Domingo, as not even when in the enjoyment of undisturbed peace can that country boast of a form of government that could be styled organized or modern.

TO AID AMERICANS.

Washington, Feb. 18.—The landing of marines at Pajarito, near Santo Domingo, by an American warship, no doubt, was authorized by the commanding officer as a result of some menace to American interests in that section of the country. The cruiser Columbia is now near Santo Domingo, and the marines landed, presumably, were from that vessel. Her commanding officer is Captain J. M. Miller.
