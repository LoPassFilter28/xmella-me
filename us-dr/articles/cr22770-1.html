Evening Star · January 20, 195620 de enero de 1956

Original in English.Original en inglés.

'Job Well Done'

The Star is to be commended for its January 14 editorial, entitled "Job Well Done," wherein it recognized the importance of the inter-American regional system and praised its latest achievement — the signing of conciliatory accords between Costa Rica and Nicaragua. Very seldom is space devoted and publicity given—especially on the positive side—to both the procedures and efforts of the 21-member Organization of American States for the peaceful settlement of disputes. The recent triumph by the five-member Inter-American Peace Committee of the OAS in resolving the dispute between Costa Rica and Nicaragua isn't the first and only example of success, but only the latest. In 1950, the year of its creation, the committee expedited the solution of a dispute involving the charges of President Trujillo of the Dominican Republic against Haiti and Cuba which threatened to disrupt Caribbean tranquility. During the crisis in May and June of 1954, involving Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua, the peace committee's efforts were frustrated by the Communist-infiltrated regime of President Arbenz denying it entry to Guatemala in order to conduct an investigation prerequisite to recommending remedial action. By the time a subcommittee of information was dispatched — Guatemala finally guaranteeing entry and safe transit—hostilities were ended and the leader of the insurgent forces, Col. Armas, was negotiating with the junta left in control when President Arbenz fled.

It is gratifying to know, as the recent settlement between Costa Rica and Nicaragua exemplifies, that not only do the necessary treaties exist for peaceful settlement of all disputes in the Americas—inter-American Treaty of Reciprocal Assistance (Rio pact), American Treaty on Pacific Settlement (pact of Bogota), and Charter of the Organization of American States (OAS)—but there is a consensus desiring their implementation. These past successes within the purview of a regional organization set a precedent to serve as a criteria for the disposition of future inter-American disputes, whether originating from internal or external causes. Also, the successful operation of the OAS vindicates Article 52 of the United Nations Charter providing for regional arrangements and attempts at settlement before referral to the U. N. for action.

Larman C. Wilson.
