Airliner Hijacked, Lands in Cuba; Eastern Pilot Shifts Course At Gunpoint; U. S. Jet Fighter Goes With Plane To 3-Mile Limit

MIAMI, Fla., July 24 (AP). An Eastern Air Lines prop-jet Electra was hijacked today and the pilot flew it to Cuba at gunpoint. A United States fighter accompanied the captive airliner to a point three miles from Cuba.

The plane, Flight 202, took off from Miami International Airport at 10:05 a.m. (EST), bound for Tampa, New Orleans and Dallas with 33 passengers and five crewmen. It landed at Jose Marti Airport in Havana at 11:29 a.m.

In his last radio report, Capt. W. E. Buchanan said: "I am proceeding to Havana at pistol point."

This call was picked up by Aeronautical Radio, Inc., in Key West, one station of a nationwide radio network supported by the airlines.

Goes Off Course

First indication that something was amiss, however, came when the $3.5 million plane went sharply off course on the radarscope.

A fighter from the Homestead Air Force Base near Miami tailed the airliner. There were reports that the fighter tried unsuccessfully to turn the big plane back.

The Electra carried 21 paying passengers, 12 pass-carrying EAL employes, and the crew members.

An airline spokesman said the passenger list had been turned over to the border patrol and the United States Immigration Service.

The report on the fighter plane came from the North American Air Defense Command at Colorado Springs, Colo. NORAD headquarters is the nerve center for aerial defense of the United States and Canada.

U. S. Asks Release

The United States Government promptly asked the Castro regime to release today's hijacked plane.

In Washington, Lincoln White, State Department press officer, said the United States has "requested the Swiss to request the immediate release of the plane, its crew and its passengers."

The request was made through the Swiss Embassy in Havana, which represents American interests in Cuba in the absence of United States-Cuban diplomatic ties.

Mr. White confirmed that an American Air Force plane had followed the airliner to the three-mile, territorial-water limit off the Cuban shore. He said this was done because the airliner "got off its regular course, and we keep an eye on things like that."

Eastern said the other members of the crew were: Pilot J. Yandell, Flight Engineer P. A. Knudsen, Flight Attendant Joan Jones and Robert Reese. All crew members are from Miami.

Other Hijackings

In the last two years, nine airplanes have been hijacked between Florida and Cuba. Seven were Cuban planes which were forced to land in Florida. Previously, one other American plane was forced to fly to Cuba.

On May 1 a man armed with a steak knife and a pistol hijacked a two-engine National Airlines plane over Florida and forced the pilot to fly to Havana.

The plane was bound from Miami to Key West, but just after it had taken off from an intermediate point, it was seized by a bandit who called himself "El Pirata Cofresi," the name of an old-time buccaneer. He told the captive audience he had been offered $100,000 blood money by the late Rafael Trujillo, Dominican Republic strongman, to assassinate Prime Minister Fidel Castro of Cuba.
