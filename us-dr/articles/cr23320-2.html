Received Diplomats in His Undershirt Englishman Tells of Personal Habits of Minister Sullivan.

New York, Jan. 15.—Roger L. Farnham of the National City Bank, which handled a $1,500,000 loan to the Dominican Republic, was the first witness today before Senator-elect Phelan of California at the continuation of the inquiry to determine the fitness of James M. Sullivan, American minister to the republic.

The National City Bank, it has been testified, was represented in negotiating the loan by Santiago Michelena, a Santo Domingo banker, whose institution was depository of the customs funds of the republic until they were transferred, at the instance of Sullivan, it is alleged, to the Banco Nacional.

Bryan Against Michelena.

Michelena was not in favor with Secretary of State Bryan, it was testified, because Mr. Bryan "was obsessed with the idea that Michelena represented the money trust."

Farnham began his testimony by saying that the National City bank had no interest in Santo Domingo in any way, other than in lending $1,500,000 to the government two years ago. This loan by arrangement with the United States state and war departments, was being paid off at the rate of $30,000 a month out of the government customs receipts.

No Relations With Sullivan.

Farnham said he knew only by hearsay of the reputation of the Banco Nacional and the Jarvis interests controlling it, and that he had no relations with Sullivan. He said that Alexander Baird, representing English capitalists, in the promotion of the Samana railroad in the republic, had approached the National City bank, seeking financial aid, but that the bank had declined to interest itself in the road. The only relations the bank had with Michelena, he said, was to ask him to present the bank proposition to the government.

Farnham denied that there were any plans contemplated by the bank to exploit the island in any way.

Knox Approved Loan.

It was Sec. of State Knox who approved the loan, the witness said. Knox cabled the bank proposal to the then American minister, who presented it to the Dominican government. A copy went to Michelena. The minister and Michelena reported that the government favored their proposition in preference to propositions of other bankers.

"There has been talk of the existence of a money trust in New York," said Commissioner Phelan. "What do you know about that?"

Question Not Answered.

"The National City bank does not know that a money trust exists," remarked Charles H. Strong, the commissioner's attorney. The witness smiled and the question was not answered.

Farnham said he had met Samuel N. Jarvis and that Jarvis had proposed that his interests and those of the National City bank be pooled in negotiating the loan.

The witness added he had declined to consider the proposition.

Met Walker With Vick.

Farnham said he had met Walker W. Vick, instigator of the present investigation, shortly after Vick was appointed receiver general. Vick had not in any way made representations to him either for or against the Banco Nacional or any other institution, he said. He had sought an interview with Vick, he continued, in connection with the question of the deposition of the customs receipts out of which the bank's notes were being paid.

"We wanted to know where the funds were going," said the witness.

Sullivan Requested Proposition.

Charles A. Butlin, an Englishman, former head of the wireless service established by the Dominican government, said that Minister Sullivan had come to him with a proposition for the extension of the wireless system of the island. At the request of Sullivan the witness had made a report on the subject, which he sent, however, to his superior in the government.

Butlin said he did not know of his own knowledge that the Banco Nacional had sought a wireless concession, but believed it to be true. He said Sullivan has inspired his removal from office. This Butlin attributed to "The fact that I was an antagonist to any private concessions being granted by the government."

Received in Undershirt.

Asked about Sullivan's personal habits, Butlin said that he was told that the minister on one occasion received the Italian minister and the British charge d'affaires in his undershirt. It was an official call, he said.

"I can testify," interrupted Vick, "that he received them in a disgraceful attire."
