Gen. Trujillo Coming Here; Maybe He'll Answer Question

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

Well, since the President allowed as how it would be a nice thing if the nation celebrated Columbus Day, which is this very day, you can imagine how many people are going to be absent from work just on the theory that is what they thought Mr. Roosevelt meant.

Now I never felt very near Mr. Columbus despite a childhood of learning about his antecedents and his activities, most of which you just skim over in the history books. But when I went to Santo Domingo last spring and looked right at Christopher's bones—a handful of dust in a nice ancient little chest, all surrounded by a marble and bronze monument—I felt that, after all, there was a man.

After prowling around the ruins of Christopher's brother's palacio and wandering thru that ancient little cathedral where the bones are resting, I felt a bit more friendly toward the fellow who discovered this side of the world and made possible this peaceful place we own right now. Then I met Gen. Trujillo, the man who is putting the Dominican Republic on the map. Why, until he began cleaning up the country and dredging harbors and talking about Samana Bay as a pretty fine American submarine base, I hardly knew where the country was.

AMAZING REPORT

Then in the very midst of Columbus Day celebrating somebody stands up in the British House of Commons and asks what about the report that Dominican ships are being used for the refueling of German U-boats in the Caribbean! Wow! That hurts, neutral as we are supposed to be. What with the General sitting comfortably out in the Mayo clinic and preparing to storm Washington the end of the week and to give a good rousing party before he heads for home.

Maybe while he's been away—it's several months now—some of the mice have been playing. The report has been denied, but somehow the denial still doesn't make us feel any more comfortable. Because we were counting a good deal on that famous Columbus Memorial, to be dedicated in 1942—the 450th anniversary of the landing in Santo Domingo of Christopher himself—for the making of a big package of good will.

However, the original plans for that 1942 celebration have fallen thru. There was to have been a massed display of the great naval powers of the world in Samana Bay. Commanders-in-chief were going to run their own flag ships into the new harbor at Trujillo City. Even Benito Mussolini—remember Columbus, despite Queen Isabella and his Spanish affiliation, was Italian by birth—had promised to be on hand.

Maybe the Dominican ships were guiltless, but you know the old adage about giving a dog a bad name?
