Evening Star · May 14, 192214 de mayo de 1922

Original in English.Original en inglés.

Details of the charmingly arranged military wedding of Lieut. Joseph Howard Fellows, United States Marine Corps, son of Mrs. C. Viola Fellows of Washington, to Miss Lillian Alice Watkins, daughter of Dr. and Mrs. Victor E. Watkins, at Santo Domingo City, Dominican Republic, April 18, have reached Washington, where Lieut. Fellows is quite well known.

The ceremony, which took place at La Romana, the beautiful estate of the bride's parents, was witnessed by Rear Admiral Samuel S. Robison, U. S. N., Military Governor of the Dominican Republic, and Mrs. Robison, Brig. Gen. Harry Lee, U. S. M. C., commanding the 2nd Brigade, U. S. Marines, and on whose staff the bridegroom is serving; Mr. William Russell, American minister to the republic; Mr. E. T. Maxwell, Mr. E. L. Klock, Mr. J. W. Shehan, American consul, and Mrs. Shehan, Commander Ralph M. Warfield, C. E. C., U. S. N.; Commander and Mrs. Reeves, Mrs. Howard A. Fellows, Mr. and Mrs. J. L. Manning, Lieut. and Mrs. Brown, Lieut. Morton Day, Lieut. Commander and Mrs. Conger and Miss Conger.

The splendid grounds of La Romana served as a fitting scene for the wedding ceremony, which took place just at sunset in a setting of palms and with the Caribbean sea rolling in against the stone wall surrounding the garden. The approach of the bride and her father was heralded by a cornetist, who played the opening strains of Mendelssohn's wedding march, the tones being taken up by a pianist, and the "Venetian Love Song" was played softly all during the ceremony.

The bride, who is a lovely brunette, wore a splendid imported robe of ivory duchess satin with a court train and silver incrusted girdle, a headdress of the same holding the flowing veil of tulle. Her bouquet was of orange blossoms and gardenias. Mrs. E. L. Klock served as matron of honor for the bride and wore an exquisite gown of wistaria taffeta and cream chantilly lace and carried a bouquet of orchids. Miss Heloise Sorelle and Miss Florence Gorton were bridesmaids and wore gowns and hats of lavender taffeta and carried colonial bouquets of cream roses and lavender orchids. The flower girl, Marsha Morgan, and Master Buddy Nadler were dressed in white satin and carried between them a basket of gardenia de amour.

The best man was Lieut. William S. Bunkley and the ushers were Capt. Charles P. Gilchrist, First Lieut. Edward A. Fellowes, Lieut. Christian F. Schilt, all of the Marine Corps, and Mr. Joseph Wheeler, late of the Navy, who formed an arch of swords under which the bride and bridegroom passed. The bridegroom and his attendants were all in uniform, which, with the sidearms worn by the man guests, gave quite a military air to the event.

Mrs. Watkins, mother of the bride, wore a gown of heavily embroidered white crepe de chine and Spanish lace, with a picture hat and Mrs. Fellows, mother of the bridegroom, wore a gown of Neapolitan lace with orchid girdle and a picture hat of the two shades. Mrs. Fellows has just returned to Washington from attending the wedding.

Lieut. and Mrs. Fellows will make their home in Santo Domingo City, where he is stationed with Gen. Lee.
