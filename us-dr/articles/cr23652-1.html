Dominicans Set to Vote Thursday Despite Row

SANTO DOMINGO, Dec. 18 (AP).—The way appeared clear today for the Dominican Republic's first dictator-free election in 38 years on Thursday.

A Roman Catholic priest retracted his charge that one of the major parties was communistic—a charge that threatened to disrupt the voting.

Informants said the Dominican Revolutionary Party, target of the accusation, would drop its threat to boycott the election.

The Rev. Laudico Garcia, a Spanish Jesuit who stirred the row, made his retraction in a 5-hour television-radio debate last night with Juan Bosch, presidential candidate of the powerful left-of-center Dominican Revolutionary Party.

Mr. Bosch told newsmen afterward: "We were satisfied with both the explanation of Father Laudico Garcia and the favorable reaction of the people to our posture."

Party leaders met today with a majority reportedly in favor of withdrawing their demand for a month's postponement of the election.

Emilio de los Santos, president of the national electoral board, declared last night that so far as the board was concerned the elections will not be put off.

He said there is no way for the Revolutionary Party to withdraw from the ballot at this late date.

The government has not yet acted on the postponement petition, but President Rafael Bonnelly scheduled a nationwide address tonight calling on Dominicans to exercise their right to vote. This was accepted as a sign that the ruling council planned to reject the petition.
