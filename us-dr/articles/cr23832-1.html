GLYNN DENIES HE WAS IMPLICATED

Albany, N. Y., Jan. 20.—"The statement of M. E. Davis in the investigation of James M. Sullivan, American minister to the Dominican Republic, that I am interested in Santo Domingo matters is an absolute falsehood," said former Governor Martin H. Glynn, in a statement tonight. "I never heard of Mr. Davis before."

Davis asserted Beer had told Call that arrangements had been made for the Walsh-Call Company to obtain 20 per cent profit on the contract.

G. O. Baker, a construction engineer, who said he had a contract to build a power plant in Puerto Plata, charged Minister Sullivan with taking no steps to protect his property when the city, in possession of revolutionists, was threatened with bombardment by a Dominican gunboat. He said he formally protested to Minister Sullivan and that Sullivan replied:

"Not only will the United States government not prevent the bombardment, but will encourage it."

A letter written by Sullivan to this effect was put into evidence. A copy of it, he said, had been sent to Secretary Bryan.

Baker declared Sullivan generally was held responsible for the revolution and charged with precipitating it through his activity with the lease of a railroad to the government, the terms of which the revolutionists objected to. He said Sullivan had once threatened to get him arrested by the Dominicans and had said to him, "Don't forget that you're not on American soil."

As evidence of conditions in Santo Domingo, Baker said that on one occasion when all Americans "had taken to the cellar," it was reported that De Sidario Arias, revolutionist leader, had recommended that "it would be a good thing to shoot off three or four good Americans" in order to bring about American intervention.

Commissioner Phelan also is trying to procure the attendance of Jack Rose, one of the witnesses at the trial of Police Lieutenant Becker for the murder of Herman Rosenthal. Sullivan acted as Rose's counsel in the case and was associated with him, according to testimony in the present investigation, in the promotion of prize fights in Waterbury, Conn.
