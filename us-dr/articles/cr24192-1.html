2 Links to Dominicans Told at Frank Trial

By PHIL THOMAS

Government prosecutors added two more stands today to a web of evidence seeking to show Washington attorney John Joseph Frank acted illegally as an agent for the Dominican Republic.

The testimony was brought out in testimony of Myles J. Ambrose, a Treasury Department official, and Albert C. Fox, an employe of the Central Intelligence Agency, in Mr. Frank's District Court trial here.

Under questioning by William Hundley, Justice Department attorney, Mr. Ambrose said he met Mr. Frank in New York in January, 1956. He said Mr. Frank told him he was representing a newspaper controlled by the Dominican government.

Mr. Ambrose was then acting as an administrative assistant United States attorney in New York. He said Mr. Frank wanted an investigation made of charges that Dr. German Ornes, a former Dominican official, had absconded with $14,000 of the newspaper's money.

Case Dismissed

The case later was dismissed, Mr. Ambrose said, on grounds the United States attorney had no authority to act.

Another link between Mr. Frank and the Dominican government was related in today's testimony by Mr. Fox, who said the Washington attorney had told him in a letter in the summer of 1955 that he (Frank) was "doing legal work with the Dominican Embassy in Washington, D. C."

Mr. Fox said the correspondence was in connection with the rental of his home near here, and Mr. Frank was acting as the agent while Mr. Fox was overseas.

Dr. Ornes, ending a day and a half of testimony, told the trial jury that "there is no freedom of the press in the Dominican Republic."

Left Country

He said he left the country after he found out he could not control the editorial policy of his newspaper, El Caribe. He said he took the $14,000 to New York ostensibly to buy newsprint for the paper, but later cabled the newspaper, which he bought in 1954, to transfer the money to a personal account.

He denied a statement by Edward L. Carey, Mr. Frank's attorney, that he (Dr. Ornes) stole the money. He said that as the sole owner of the paper he was entitled to it.

Displaying a fiery temperament under the questioning yesterday, Dr. Ornes stuck by his earlier testimony that he broke with Trujillo because the Dominican strong man had once had his brother imprisoned.

But Mr. Carey read a letter Dr. Ornes wrote to Trujillo in 1946, in which the former official praised the generalissimo as "one of the greatest figures" in Dominican history.

"Did you ever tell Trujillo you disagreed with him?" Mr. Carey asked.

"Nobody in his right mind would say that to Trujillo," Dr. Ornes said. "You have to approve of the regime, or you don't live long."

Dr. Ornes said he had to take over as editor of a Trujillo-backed newspaper in 1949 "or else."

Calling Trujillo a dictator, Dr. Ornes said he had to leave his three children (by his first wife) in the Dominican Republic as "hostages" when he broke with the generalissimo in 1955 and came to the United States.

Whereabouts Unknown

"Everyone who leaves the country has to leave behind someone who is dear to him," he said, explaining the hostages "pay" for anyone who "does something inimical" to the Dominican government after leaving.

He said he does not know where his children are because the Trujillo government won't let him contact the youngsters.

In earlier testimony, Dr. Ornes has testified he met Mr. Frank on several occasions: Once in Ciudad Trujillo, once in Kansas City when the generalissimo visited the country and once in New York.
