New Quake Hits Area Devastated On Sunday By Tremors and Tide Dominican Republic Shaken as Rescue Parties Speed to Valley Stricken by Earlier Shock Which Reportedly Killed Two and Wiped Out Two Towns

Ciudad Trujillo, Dominican Republic, Aug. 5—(AP)—New earth tremors shook this Caribbean republic today in the wake of Sunday's severe earthquake and tidal wave which left a toll of at least two dead and spread devastation in at least 11 towns.

(A Pan American airways pilot who flew from Ciudad Trujillo said today at Miami that a fresh quake struck the island republic at 9.20 a. m. (EDT) and violently shook the control tower at the city's airport. He said he had been told that two towns on the north coast of the Dominican Republic had been completely wiped out by Sunday's quake and tidal wave.)

The government rushed aid and rescue parties to the stricken areas which lie in the valley south of the Monte Christi range. Efforts to reach the towns by telephone and telegraph failed, and there was no available information on how many were injured. Radio communications were interrupted by the tremors and the stricken communities were completely cut off from the outside world for many hours.

(The earthquake apparently was centered in the deepest point in the Atlantic ocean, approximately 50 miles off the northeast coast of the Dominican Republic, and was so intense it knocked out a seismograph at St Louis, Mo., more than 2000 miles away.

(At New York the Fordham university observatory said the shock was the most severe recorded there since the observatory was founded in 1910.)

Reports from the hardest hit areas were sparse, but the trickle of information indicated that heavy damage was done to homes and public buildings.

Santiago Hard Hit

Santiago, with a population of 50,000, the largest city in the affected area, was one of the hardest hit. Reports reaching here said many buildings were toppled or damaged and one person was killed.

Another was killed at San Francisco de Macoris, where almost 35 homes were destroyed and many other homes and buildings were badly damaged.

Unconfirmed reports from Molina said the sea lashed out far beyond the normal low tide point, and then rushed back, carrying a number of launches and other craft to shore. No lives were lost.

In this oldest capital in the Western Hemisphere, now celebrating the 450th anniversary of its founding by the brother of Christopher Columbus, the earthquake caused only slight damage to old churches and to the docks. No casualties were reported.

(Slight tremors were felt in Cuba, and the Virgin islands reported a mild shock. At San Juan, Puerto Rico, there was slight damage from tide fluctuations, and the weather bureau there said a major shock was felt at Trinidad.)

Extensive damage was reported from the high waves borne by the quake, but no definite reports were available nor was there any estimate as yet on the number of persons injured.
