Send Book on Contest Rules to Architects

More than 1,100 architects in 56 countries are receiving copies of the book containing the conditions of the architectural competition for the Columbus Memorial lighthouse, which will be erected on the coast of the Dominican republic.

The project to honor the memory of the discoverer by the erection of a lighthouse in the Dominican republic has been agitated for many years, the campaign finally culminating in the adoption of a resolution at the Fifth Pan-American conference at Santiago, Chile, in 1923, recommending that the lighthouse be erected through the cooperation of the governments and people of America, and governing board of the Pan-American union has taken the measures which have resulted in the present architectural competition.

The requirements are that the memorial shall stand on a mound, or terrace or terraces, almost 40 feet high, that the structure shall contain a chapel large enough to receive the Columbus monument now in the cathedral at Santo Domingo; that a museum be provided, that it shall support at least one great lantern. It is further suggested that the total height of the monument should not exceed 600 feet; and that it be constructed on a properly protected structural steel frame, of reinforced concrete.
