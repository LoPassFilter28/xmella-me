Affairs in San Domingo.

OUR SAN DOMINGO CORRESPONDENCE.

SAN DOMINGO, April 27, 1857.

Collision of American and Dominican Vessels—Arrest of the American Captain—His Sentence and Abandonment of his Schooner—A Call on the United States Cabinet.

We have an excellent case on account of the interference of this government with us. They abuse the Yankees here with impunity, and this is an excellent chance for redress.

It appears that some seven weeks since the American schooner Charles Hills, Captain Smith, bound from Mayaguez for this port, during the night came in collision with the Dominican packet schooner and stove her badly. The American schooner came in here, and when ready for sea was refused a pass and the captain summoned to appear before a court. He was sentenced to pay the sum of $3,300 for “wilfully running into and damaging the Dominican packet.”

Now, every one knows that the captain was right, being on the starboard tack and on a wind, whereas the other had his larboard tack aboard and from the wind; therefore he should have kept off and not crossed the bows of the American. This any Marine Court would allow, but here he was condemned without a trial, and not being able to pay was sentenced to a year's imprisonment. He (Captain Smith,) has abandoned his vessel to the Dominican government, and looks at home for redress. It is one of the most urgent cases ever heard of, and a complete burglary and robbery on their part. They even now laugh at our threats. Only to think of it. A petty government like this. It is too bad. I am in hopes for a time to come to correct all.

We have received the Gaceta Oficial of Santo Domingo to May 4.

The American schooner Charles Hill, Captain Albert Smith, had come in collision with the Dominican packet schooner named the Nineteenth of March; and the Charles Hill having run in to San Domingo was detained, and the captain sentenced to pay $3,100 for wilfully running into and damaging the Dominican packet. We find the following documents, in reference to the case, published under the head of “official:”—

THE UNITED STATES COMMERCIAL AGENT TO THE MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS.

COMMERCIAL AGENCY OF THE UNITED STATES, CITY OF SAN DOMINGO, March 13, 1857.

SIR—You will find herewith the protest of Mr. Albert Smith, Captain of the American schooner Charles Hill, against the Dominican authorities, and which is to lead the way to proceedings for illegal and unjust detention. I am, sir, &c. JONATHAN ELLIOTT.

To the Minister of Foreign Affairs of the Dominican Republic.

THE DOMINICA MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS TO THE AMERICAN COMMERCIAL AGENT.

Mr. AGENT—My government has been ignorant of your return and entering on the discharge of your functions. Entrusted ad interim to Mr. Jacob Pereyra on account of your not having notified my government thereof in an official manner. Nevertheless, on receiving your note of the 18th, accompanied by the protest of Capt. Smyth, I have brought it to the notice of the government, for the proceedings to which it may give rise.

It is proper for me to say that if the Captain had only availed himself of the means of conciliation to which he was invited, it is more than probable that that question would have had a solution as speedy as it would have been peaceable, but being obstinate in his refusal, it became necessary to submit the matter to the common tribunals, to be decided by them in conformity with the laws.

I also believe that it would have been more effectual for him to show before the tribunal, which had charge of the matter, the illegal proceedings and injustices of which the captain complains, that being impartially examined they might be redressed or declared null. I am, &c. FELIX M. DELMONTE.

SAN DOMINGO, March 16, 1857.

THE UNITED STATES COMMERCIAL AGENT TO THE DOMINICAN MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS.

SENOR MINISTER:—The tribunal of the first instance, of this district, has granted judgment against Capt. Albert Smith of the American schooner Charles Hill, for injury done to the Dominican packet 19th of March, in a collision, amounting, with cost, to some $3,160, and from which decision the Captain will not appeal.

I desire now to know, and beg of you to inform me, whether the American schooner Charles Hill and its crew will be detained, or its voyage prevented in this port, to the injury and loss of its owners, until such time as the Captain shall have complied with the judgment, or until the same shall have been exacted? JONATHAN ELLIOTT.

To the MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS.

THE DOMINICAN MINISTER OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS TO THE AMERICAN COMMERCIAL AGENT.

SAN DOMINGO, March 27, 1857.

SIR—I have the honor to acknowledge the receipt of your official note of this day, in which, after assuring me that Capt. Smith will not avail himself of the resource of appeal which the laws permit him to do, you ask to be informed whether his voyage, or that of his crew, will be prevented before the execution of the sentence of the tribunal of first instance.

I assure you that the government has no other action over matters submitted to the tribunals than the interest of having the laws fulfilled, or distributing justice impartially, without meddling in any manner in decrees or their execution. Therefore, the tribunal alone is able to answer the question submitted by you, in determining whether it will accept bail. I am, &c. FELIX M. DELMONTE.

To the COMMERCIAL AGENT OF THE UNITED STATES.

THE COMMERCIAL AGENT DEMANDS RESTITUTION, ETC.

COMMERCIAL AGENCY OF THE UNITED STATES, April 17, 1857.

SIR:—It was brought to my notice yesterday that Mr. Albert Smith, captain of the American schooner Chas. Hill, of Portsmouth, was to be condemned on the 18th to two years imprisonment in the public prison of this city, against which sentence and proceedings I solemnly protest as unjust.

The accusation against the Captain for having wilfully injured the Dominican packet “19th of March” is irrational.

The schooner Charles Hill has been anchored here six weeks, and as it has not been copper fastened, it is so much injured that it will require a large outlay to repair it.

Consequently, as commercial agent of the United States, I ask in the name and on the belief of the owners or shippers of the said vessel the sum of $5,000 from the government of the Dominican republic, to be paid or secured before the 22d inst., and also the freedom of the vessel and its crew to depart hence on that date.

The undersigned regrets extremely the occurrence of this thing, and his being forced to this disagreeable duty, and takes this occasion to express to the honorable Minister, &c., JONATHAN ELLIOTT.

THE MINISTER'S REPLY.

Mr. AGENT—My government has seen with extreme regret your note of 17th inst., substantially affirming that the proceeding against Captain Albert Smith, who through recklessness, negligence, or some other unfavorable cause, ran into and injured the Dominican packet, is unreasonable, and that the detention of the vessel is, in your judgment, equal to a loss of $500.

Neither the fact itself nor the consequent detention of the vessel can be unreasonable without acknowledging a theory fatal to all nations. 1st. That wrongs committed at sea do not constitute crime nor carry responsibility for the losses occasioned; and 2d, That the republic has no jurisdiction to take cognizance of these acts.

In reference to the first it does not even admit of discussion, and the second contains the first, because in denying jurisdiction to the republic, it must be denied to every government, and therefore there being no jurisdiction anywhere in the world to decide these controversies would establish impunity.

If the act had taken place on the coasts of the republic, in one of her ports both vessels rode for the first time since the event, and the aggrieved party presented its formal complaint, it is evident that the jurisdiction is in this republic and in no other, and that the government in having submitted the question to the tribunals has done wrong to no one.

To disregard, as Captain Smith has disregarded, the right of appeal—to resort to the extraordinary course of international resources—and that against the government which invited him several times to the frankest settlement, is to reveal the desire of recurring to force, and not to the means which universal reason has accepted and sanctioned. Such a fatal precedent being permitted, it would be impossible for the civil power to decide questions between two foreigners.

If the act in question had occurred between an American and an English vessel, the American Captain denying the jurisdiction, and the English captain demanding justice, how could he evade the international demand? In no way, because the one would be described as abuse of power or the other a denial of justice.

After these observations you will admit that your demand is not founded in reason. The government regrets that your pretensions are not acceptable, and trusts to the enlightenment and rectitude of the government of the United States for the approval of its moderate and just proceedings in the present question.

SAN DOMINGO, April 21, 1857.

The commercial agent of the United States, in another letter of April 23, notified the Minister that at noon that day the schooner Charles Hill would be abandoned to the authorities.
