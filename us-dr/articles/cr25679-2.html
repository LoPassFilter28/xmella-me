COLBY DEFENDS HAITI STATUS

Declares United States Has Done Only What It Had to for Sake of Haiti People

Washington, Sept. 20—Announcing that the "benevolent purpose" of this country's occupation of the island of Haiti was nearing attainment, Secretary Colby declared to-night that the United States hopes to withdraw and leave the island's administration to the "unaided efforts of the Haitian people" when that purpose has been fully achieved. The relations of the United States with Haiti, he added, should not be "misrepresented even under the temptations that a political campaign presents to irresponsible utterances and conscious unveracity.'

While not replying directly to recent charges by Senator Harding that the administration was conducting "unconstitutional warfare" against Haiti and San Domingo, Secretary Colby recited at length the history of American occupation of those countries and defended it as being guided by "no thought except to help" their peoples. Senator Harding's attack on the administration's record in Haiti was made in connection with a criticism of recent statements in regard to the island by Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Will Withhold Haitien Salaries

Earlier in the day it was learned that the state department had informed G. Baran, Haitian minister of foreign affairs, that the salary of the president and other high officials of the Haitian government, which had been withheld since last June, would be withheld until the native government ceased to interpose obstacles to the carrying out of certain provisions of the treaty entered into at the time of this country's occupation of the island.

The American government informed the Haitian minister of foreign affairs that it could expect no relief from this section until the Haitian government adopted a less antagonistic attitude toward the American authorities.

Protest against American intervention in San Domingo was made in a simultaneous statement to-night by Julio M. Cestero, representing the Dominican junta in the United States. Referring to the recent declarations by Senator Harding and Secretary of Navy Daniels, Cestero declared the citizens of the Dominican republic "have never sought the present American intervention" and have never ceased to ask for "restoration of their freedom and sovereignty."

Secretary Colby's Statement

In his statement to-night, Secretary Colby said:—

"The position of the United States with relation to Haiti should not be misunderstood and it certainly should not be misrepresented even under the temptations that a political campaign presents to irresponsible utterance and conscious unveracity."

"This country is engaged in a task which has only a benevolent purpose. It is a task which was undertaken for the benefit of the Haitian people and with their acquiescence the task was defined in a treaty entered into by the two countries. The work which the United States undertook to do is nearing completion and upon its completion this government hopes to withdraw and leave the administration of the island to the unaided efforts of the Haitian people."

What Aid Has Done

"The United States has confined its energies entirely to the announced purposes of its occupation, and as a result thereof complete tranquillity exists throughout the republic. The Haitian revenues are greatly improved and much progress has been made in road construction, port works, establishment of telegraphs, etc."

"These results have been brought about by Americans appointed under the provisions of the treaty, and from every quarter in Haiti with the exception of a few centers of selfish and interested agitation, credit and gratitude are freely manifested and expressed to this government."

"The course of this country has been moderate and dictated only by the desire to meet its duties and not in any way to go beyond them. Had less been done, this country would have been guilty of a dereliction of duty and might have been exposed to sound criticism."

About the National City Bank

"As to assertions that have appeared in the press recently to the effect that the National City bank of New York has received improper support from this government in the matter of its Haitian branch, the following are the facts:—"

"No assistance or support has been given the National City bank in Haiti other than that which would be extended to any first-class American bank in any foreign country."

"Far from encouraging the National City bank in obtaining monopolistic privileges in connection with its interests in Haiti, the department has, in so far as it has had an opportunity to exert an influence, used it toward preventing the National City bank from enjoying monopolistic privileges."

"The National City bank does not control, directly or indirectly, the customs collectors, nor is it the financial arbiter of Haiti. It exercises no control of loans, exchange rates or commercial transactions, as has been charged."

Daniels Also Explains

Washington, Sept. 18—Senator Warren G. Harding's charge that the democratic administration had been guilty of misrule in the conduct of affairs in Haiti is answered by two members of the president's cabinet to-day, Bainbridge Colby, secretary of state, and Josephus Daniels, secretary of the navy.

The statements made by Senator Harding were characterized by Secretary Colby as "cheap and disgusting" and in support of the position taken by this administration Mr Colby harked back to his former mentor, Theodore Roosevelt.

"The United States is carrying out in Haiti and San Domingo," he said, "a practice of the Monroe doctrine that was advocated by President Roosevelt, a practice that, if the United States did not engage in other nations would, for the protection of their nationals and of their nationals' interests. America went into these countries upon invitation and will come out when the need for the presence of American forces ceases."

Secretary Daniels announced that he would soon be ready to make public two reports on conditions in Haiti, one prepared by Brig-Gen George Barnett and the other by Maj-Gen John Le June.

"Gen Barnett reported," said Secretary Daniels, "that what the marines had done in Haiti made him prouder than ever of the corps, that they had repelled the attacks of bandits with firmness, but with no semblance of resentment, had preserved order under trying conditions and were actuated by a spirit of helpfulness and kindliness to those people and that the improvements effected by the marines had been most gratifying. His report, now in preparation, will detail the few acts that called for discipline and punishment. My instructions to Gen Barnett and his instructions to the officers on duty in Haiti were to regard their duty as trustees and to be considerate, tactful, kind and helpful."

"From the beginning of the call for duty by the marines in Haiti and San Domingo every officer charged with duty there has been charged with the duty of carrying out directions with every consideration for the feelings, interest, prosperity and happiness of the people, who had so long been oppressed and dominated by bands of bandits, who had made the government there a farce. The United States undertook its task there in pursuance of treaty obligations and the preservation of order by the marines is directed toward the end of securing stability and safety to the inhabitants."
