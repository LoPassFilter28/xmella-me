MUCH COMMENT ON SANTO DOMINGO. This Country Is Expected to Interfere There. ENGLISH PRESS EXPECTS IT Island Cannot Be Permitted to Relapse Into Barbarism. The United States the Natural Protector, and No European Country Would Object, Save Possibly France, Even If Absorption Followed—The Revolution Expected to Start at Any Moment, and It Is Believed Jiminez Has Backing of Gomez and Other Prominent Cuban Officers, Also Some Americans

LONDON, Aug. 5.—The occurrences in Santo Domingo have naturally led to comment upon the probable action of the United States. It is generally recognized that something must be done to prevent the island from slipping back to African barbarism, and the trend of comment points to the United States as the natural pacifier of the country, and indicates that absorption will follow as a consequence.

Rumors come from France of objections from that quarter, but while the French have considerable financial and commercial interests in the former dependency, they will hardly go to the length of actively opposing any action America may see fit to take toward establishing a protectorate over both Santo Domingo and Hayti.

Powell Cables for a Warship.

WASHINGTON, Aug. 5.—Hayti is in a state of ferment, perhaps, for sympathy with the neighboring state of Santo Domingo. Minister Powell has cabled the state department that the presence of a United States warship may be required if the present threatening condition continues, to insure the safety of the United States legation and consulates.

JIMINEZ FOMENTS REVOLT.

Leader of the Dominican Insurgents Predicts the Downfall of the Government.

NEW YORK, Aug. 5.—A special to the Herald from Havana says:

Gen. Jiminez today displayed a cipher cablegram, which he received from an agent in Santo Domingo, which reads as follows:

"Revolution commences today in Dajabon and La Vega. Send definite answer when you will arrive. Must be as early as possible.

"FRANCE."

Jiminez has replied:

"Be there by last of month with party. Delay so as to avoid bloodshed. Think present government will retire before then."

Jiminez declared that he had 30,000 stands of arms in Cuba, ready to be used, and a party of Americans in the United States is preparing to sail at once. When asked if he did not think the United States would stop his expedition, he smiled and said:

"Has the United States enough vessels to guard the entire coast of Cuba and the Isle of Pines that can be spared? Never fear we will have no trouble in sailing, and we will sail soon."

Jiminez is said to be backed by Gomez and other prominent Cuban army officers.

Officers Cannot Find Jiminez.

HAVANA, Aug. 5.—Gen. Jiminez, accompanied by six Cubans and four natives of Santo Domingo, is believed to have left here this afternoon for the Dominican republic. The authorities are making a close investigation in order to discover which direction the party went.
