SANTO DOMINGO HAS THREE DAY ELECTIONS Always Somebody Killed and Contests Generally Is a Death Dealing Affair—In the Recent Election The Governor Led a Mob—He's Gone Now. The Presidential Election There is to be Held Next Month and It is Always the Livliest—The Coming Contest is to be a Very Warm One so They Say.

ATLANTA, Oct. 5.—Georgia’s recent election, while it was full of pep and excitement, was a very tame affair when compared to a recent election in the Dominican Republic, according to Captain W. H. Pritchett, of the U. S. Marine Corps now stationed at Santiago, D. R., and who has written a very interesting letter to a friend in Atlanta.

Captain Pritchett is a native Georgian, a graduate of the Georgia School of Technology in Atlanta, and formerly resided in this city.

They take three days in the Dominican Republic to hold an election, instead of taking only one as we do in the United States, and “from an observer’s standpoint,” writes Captain Pritchett, “the main reason is in order to give plenty of time for fights and shooting.”

The last election in the Republic was for delegates to the constitutional convention.

“Political parties in Santo Domingo take their names from the names of the leaders of the party,” writes Captain Pritchett. “At present the two parties are the Aristas and the Horacistas. The constitutional convention is empowered to make certain proposed changes in the present constitution of the Republic. The Aristas are opposed to the changes and the Horacistas are in favor of changes.

“During the entire three days and nights of the recent election there was firing going on in some part of Santiago. Several brawls occurred in the crowded plaza in the heart of the city. One of these was led by the recently appointed governor of the Santiago province. This particular brawl was promptly quelled by a lieutenant of the Native Guard, who with several members of the guard charged into the mob, seized the governor and disarmed him. The governor escaped in the melee and has been in hiding since. The lieutenant of the Guard, having failed to locate the governor, promptly proclaimed himself governor and up to the present writing is getting away with it.

“Five men and two women were killed in Santiago during the election. The Aristas were overwhelmingly defeated. In several precincts at the recent election no votes were cast by the Arista party, although there were a number of that party in those precincts. The reason they gave for not voting was that they were out of ammunition and did not dare go to the polls without being fully armed.

“The presidential election will be held in November. Present indications are that Vazquez will be elected. He will undoubtedly make an excellent chief executive, if the country can be rid of a few trouble makers and professional revolutionists.

“The present president, Frederico Henriquez Carvajal, was elected president ad interim by the national assembly on July 26, following the resignation of President Jiminez, which was forced by Desiderio Arias, his secretary of war.
