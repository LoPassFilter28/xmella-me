Col. Roland Plotted With Dominican Leaders To Revolt

WASHINGTON, March 9. (ANP)—A vicious plot to overthrow the Haitian government was revealed here last week by Joseph D. Charles, ambassador from Haiti to the U. S.

This complex scheme involved Col. Astrel Roland, former Haitian diplomat and military leader, his sweetheart, a Johanna Verbracken of Belgium, and several officials of the Dominican Republic including President Trujillo.

According to the ambassador, the government of Haiti considers the support given Roland by the Dominican government is an act of hostility toward Haiti. He said his nation also considers the broadcasts of Col. Roland from the Dominican Republic as subversive.

News of the various Roland plots became public recently when Roland attacked the Haitian government from the radio station of the Dominican Republican president's brother, Col. Arismendi Trujillo, from the R. D. He promised to liberate the Haitian people.

Latest developments in the situation have been a protest against the R. D. by Haiti before the Council of the Organization of American States and the capture of Lady Verbracken by the D. R.

Roland's campaign against the Haitian administration began in 1947. As blocks to his success in Haiti he named the president of Haiti, the chief of staff, Gen. Lavaud; the colonel of the presidential guard, Col. Magloire, and the director of the military academy, Col. Levelt.

Communications written by Roland and captured by the Haitian government reveal that he worked with Lady Verbracken, the president of the Dominican Republic, referred to as Desire in his letters; the Dominican minister of interior, Aluvarez Paulino, referred to as P. in his letters, and other Dominican officials.

The most important person to the success of Roland's plans evidently was the Belgian lady, with whom Roland was reported to be in love. Originally, she went to Haiti as an thnologist with aim of destroying those named as Roland's enemies. In July and August of 1948, she failed in this mission and left Haiti for Brazil.

She returned to Haiti in December, but this time was trailed by the police. She sought information on the nation's military strength and setup, and promised that Col. Roland would return to Haiti soon. She left Haiti December 29 and met Roland two days later in Quito, R. D.

Two weeks later she met with Roland, Paulino, and another person in New York at the Hotel Martinique. There they discussed an invasion of Haiti. During this time Roland was a Haitian official, councillor of legation in Quito, D. R. His job was dropped by Haiti January 19.

Not long after the loss of his job Roland began his propaganda broadcasts against Haiti.

The Haitian ambassador to the U. S. revealed that his government had a number of letters written by Roland showing his connection with plots to overthrow the government.

What may develop now that Haiti has filed protest against its sister nation can only be speculated.
