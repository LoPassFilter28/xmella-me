Barbara Hutton Weds Rubirosa But Keeps Control of Estate

By the Associated Press

NEW YORK, Dec. 31.—Heiress Barbara Hutton and international playboy Porfirio Rubirosa have exchanged marriage vows, but the bride apparently intends to retain firm possession of her 5-and-10-cent store millions.

Miss Hutton and the Dominican Republic diplomat wed yesterday in a 10-minute civil ceremony, making it the fifth marriage for Miss Hutton and the fourth for Mr. Rubirosa.

The rites capped several days of speculation that the wedding might be called off.

The slim, pale, 41-year-old bride was all smiles as the wedding rites began, but toward the end she became visibly nervous. Mr. Rubirosa, 45, tall, handsome and composed, put a comforting arm around her.

The marriage was performed by Dr. Joaquin Salazar, Dominican consul general, in his Park Avenue apartment.

The bride, a black taffeta dress contrasting with her blond hair, was attended by her 17-year-old son, Lance Reventlow, her only child.

Mr. Rubirosa's best man was Maj. Gen. Rafael Trujillo Martinez, head of the Dominican Air Force and one-time brother-in-law of the bridegroom.

Mr. Rubirosa previously was married to Flor Trujillo, daughter of the Dominican Republic's first family; French actress Danielle Darrieux, and Doris Duke, heiress to a tobacco fortune.

Miss Hutton, Woolworth dime-store heiress, formerly was married to the late Prince Alexis Mdivani; Count Kurt von Haugwitz-Reventlow; actor Cary Grant, and Prince Igor Troubetzkoy.

Became Dominican Citizen.

Miss Hutton became a citizen of the Dominican Republic on Tuesday in order to go through the ceremony under Dominican law and invoking Mr. Rubirosa's diplomatic status as Dominican minister plenipotentiary to France.

A New York City official said, however, that without a New York license such a marriage ceremony was open to legal question so far as State records were concerned.

At a hectic press conference in her Hotel Pierre suite prior to the wedding, Miss Hutton answered "certainly" to a query if her considerable fortune would remain in her own hands.

As for Mr. Rubirosa, he said: "What property she has is hers, and what property I have is mine."

Miss Hutton told the press in a dramatic pre-marriage news conference:

"I loved him from the moment I met him. I've known him a long time."

Bride Appears Frail.

She appeared frail and tired as she entered the room on the arm of her son Lance, 17. She walked slowly to a sofa and said:

"Lance, hold my hand. I'm frightened of these people. Don't leave me. Don't leave me." Then Mr. Rubirosa entered from another door and she said:

"Rubi, darling, here we are."

Reporters asked Mr. Rubirosa when he proposed to her.

Before he could reply, she said:

"I'll tell. He doesn't remember. It was in July at Deauville. I don't remember what he said. He didn't exactly propose. He just told me he loved me."

Reporters asked if she were happy. A sad look appeared on her wan face and she said:

"No one will give anyone credit for loving me because I am myself."

Regrets "Silly" Things.

Leland Rosenberg, a friend of Mr. Rubirosa, said at this point, "it's time to go," but Miss Hutton continued on:

"It makes me sad to think of all the silly things I've done. Of course, I didn't think they were silly at the time. I don't like to look in the mirror. I don't want to look at my face."

Asked if she thought the marriage would last, she said:

"I wouldn't try if I didn't know it will."

To a question as to where the couple would spend their honeymoon, Mr. Rubirosa said they would stay in New York a few days, then probably go to Florida for a few months.

After that, he said, they would go to Paris and live in his house "on the left bank."

Statements by actress Zsa Zsa Gabor that Mr. Rubirosa asked her to marry him within the past week brought this retort from Mr. Rubirosa: "It's a publicity stunt."

Asked to comment on Miss Gabor's assertions, Miss Hutton replied:

"I'm terribly sorry, I don't know her. I happen to be a lady. I'm terribly sorry."

She sighed and added, "I've been crucified these past few days."

As Miss Hutton rose to go back to her bedroom, she swayed and appeared about to fall until reporters caught her.

She reappeared a few moments later, wearing a royal purple velvet coat over the black dress. She wore a large black taffeta hat with an underside of royal purple, matching the dress. She also wore diamond and pearl earrings.

Would Like Baby.

After the ceremony, there was a private reception in Miss Hutton's suite at the Pierre Hotel, attended by only 25 persons.

Earl Wilson, New York Post columnist, reported the bride talked about babies, and said:

"Oh, I would like to have another baby . . . a child for my wonderful husband . . . it would be such a great thrill . . . but I am probably too old . . . I'm 41."

Mr. Wilson reported some one mentioned to the bride that women of 41 have had babies, and she said enthusiastically, "I'll be hoping."

At the Dominican Republic's information center today, a spokesman said:

"Rubirosa has enough income from the Dominican government to support them in the manner to which they are accustomed. Dominican diplomats are the highest paid of any in the world."
