REBELS CONTROL REPUBLIC President of Santo Domingo Forced to Quit As Executive REVOLT BLOODLESS Period of Depression Is Blamed for Trouble in Island Nation

SANTO DOMINGO, Feb. 25. (U.P.) — The Dominican Republic is in a state of revolution, rebel troops have captured the fortress of Monte Cristi, with the support of the national army, and President Horacio Vasquez has resigned, according to the latest published reports here.

Following the capture of the fortress on the northern coast, President Vasquez took refuge in the Santo Domingo fortress, while his wife sought protection at the American legation. Both returned to the presidential palace last night.

Reports of the uprising were published by the newspaper Listin Diario, which is authority for the statement regarding the resignation of Vasquez.

The revolution has grown out of two situations—dissatisfaction over the reported intention of President Vasquez to seek another term, and an economic collapse due to a bad sugar and coffee year. Planters and their employees have been hard hit in the past six months.

Thus far the success of the rebels has been complete, and not a drop of blood has been shed, it is reported. The national army, joining with the revolutionists, has nowhere encountered resistance.

According to the Diario, Vasquez, in presenting his resignation to the congress, stipulated as well that he would go into retirement and decline to be a candidate for reelection. A provisional president, it was said, would be named to take over the government during the elections.

President Vasquez already had served one term, the last year of which has been marked by an economic depression.

He is said to have been planning his escape across the border to Haiti.

PART AU PRINCE, Haiti, Feb. 25. (U.P.) — An insurrection of serious proportions has upset the administration of President Horacio Vasquez of the Dominican Republic, and is sweeping through the country, according to meager information reaching here through strict censorship today.

Telegrams indicated that the rebels have seized the fort and police station at Santiago de Los Caballeros, the principal town in the tobacco district of the Republic, and that the Dominican town, Dajabon, near the Haitian border is in the hands of rebels.

At Dajabon the municipal police have been disarmed and administration sympathizers rendered helpless.

Firing has been reported near the Dominican border by American travelers and reports indicated that several Dominicans had been wounded.
