INTERESTING FROM ST. DOMINGO. The Gold, Silver and Coal Mines to be Worked by the French and English—Official Concession by the Government, &c.

The schooner Independence, Captain Thompson, which arrived at this port from Dominica, brought us later advice from that place. By her arrival we learn that Baez's paper money was taken up by the government at $2,000 for $1. When first issued, the money, if it can be so called, was at the rate of $800 to the doubloon of $16. The paper at Curacoa will not be included in this redemption. It is expected that a great deal of excitement and confusion would result from this circumstance, as some of the holders will be relieved, while others will have suffered seriously.

OUR ST. DOMINGO CORRESPONDENCE.

St. Domingo, April 30, 1859.

I send you a Gazette, in which you will find a contract whereby this government have given all they possess to the French. The French government is to pay three million francs in the middle of July. As the negroes will not work, and the climate is too fatal for whites to labor, it must be their intention to bring apprentices from Africa, or coolies. Probably it is a speculation to let our capitalists in. It is sure to loose money if they mean to work here.

You remember what a row they raised here when the United States attempted to rent a piece of land in Samana for a naval and coal depot. What would be said if we thought of the coal mines of Samana.

In January last a messenger was sent by this government to Washington to ask the United States to make a treaty. We have not yet heard the result of his mission, She rejected twice to have a treaty with us; but now it suits the interests of the French. The Danish government have made a claim on this for $140,000; and as our people refuse to settle it, it is probable that we shall be blockaded in the course of a few months.

The country is in a truly distressing situation; the crops have failed, and the government have millions of paper money to redeem without one hard dollar to do it with. In short, this island never will prosper until negroes can be made to work it; it is useless for whites to attempt to labor here.

The following is the official decree giving to a French and English company the right of working the mines in St. Domingo:—

Pedro Santana, Liberator of the Country, General-in-Chief of the Armies, and President of the Republic: Considering everything to be of national interest which tends to encourage any enterprise that has the object of developing foreign industry to the benefit of the products of the territory of the Republic and for enriching it;

In view of the solicitude evinced by Mr. Ambroise Benillot, Gentleman of the Legion of Honour, and chief administrator of Messageries Imperiales, the Viscount de Kerveguen, Deputy of the Legislative Body of France, Counsellor General of War, Member of the General Council of Manufactures, and Mr. Ferdinand Barrot, Senator of the Empire, former Minister of France, Vice President of the Municipal Council of Paris, and of the General Council of the Department of the Seine, Grand Cross of the Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus, Commander of the Order of the Legion of Honor, etc., and Baron de [illegible], Attorney of Petitions in the Council of State, Officer of the Imperial Order of the Legion of Honor, etc., who, according to a letter bearing date the September, 1858, directed to his Excellency the Minister of the State, declare themselves to be disposed to form between them, and in union with other capitalists, a French and English company for the exploration of mines, property of the State, &c., &c.;

And the contract celebrated on the 21st inst., between Don Jose Miguel Lavastida, Secretary of State, entrusted with the Budget of War and Marine and of Foreign Affairs, and Thomas Bobadilla, ex-Senator of the republic, former Minister of Justice, on the part of the Dominican government, and Mr. Louis Pierre Edward Augustus Perault, officer of the Legion of Honor, now in this city and operating in the name of and for these gentlemen;

By virtue of the 19th section of the 35th article of the constitution, and the general faculties with which I am invested as President, I have made to the gentlemen referred to the following concessions, so that the conditions which appear in the contract referred to may be executed in the express terms and conditions:—

Article 1.—The concession will last during forty years, purely and consecutively, which will commence counting from this date, and gives the right of exploring the mines belonging to the State which are in the vicinity of the peninsula of Samana.

2.—The concession for twenty years for exploration of guano, wherever it is found in the possessions of the Republic.

3.—The concession to dispose of the exploration of parts of the State which the government may think fit to dispose of.

—A concession for forty years for the exploration of mines of silver, gold, copper, mercury and other metals which are found in the territories of the State, as well as the indications of gold; the explorations not even begun, or which may have been abandoned. The Sahona is excepted from all concessions, its property having been previously disposed of by the nation.

—The gentlemen referred to promise on their part:—

—To transmit the concessions expressed above to English and French capitalists, to obtain a definitive answer from them.

—To have one or more representatives in the cities of St. Domingo and Santiago de los Cabelleros.

—To justify a capital sufficiently large to undertake the exploration of the concessions mentioned.

—To pay to the Dominican government a maximum duty of ten per cent over the rough products of the exploration of the mines conceded, and which were explored, with an understanding with the Dominican government for the purchase of the said materials.

—To pay a duty of ten per cent on the value of each quintal (cwt) of guano collected.

—To pay a tax, to be agreed on, for the rent of the land which the Dominican government thinks it right to give up for exploration, according to the extent and importance of the land.

—To open a road of direct communication from Samana to Puerto Plata, under the model and according to the system of the imperial roads of France, as ways of communication. This enterprise will begin as soon as possible, and will proceed without interruption.

—To remain responsible with the Dominican government in all the obligations which are imposed, in case the said gentlemen should cede all or any part of the concessions granted to them.

—To advance one million francs to the Dominican government, to be paid only and entirely with the products which might arise to them by the explorations above mentioned, the liquidation of which will annually be made by those respectively concerned. This sum will be delivered in Paris at the order of the Dominican government, and within four months from date, and if the same shall be not complied with, the present concessions will remain null of act, and without any value or effect.

The gentlemen referred to are obliged to use all the diligence possible to organise and begin their works within three years from this date, and if at the close of this term the works should not be commenced, the present concessions will be purely and simply null, and the Dominican government will not be bound to reimburse the million of francs to which the previous article refers, on any consideration.

—The Dominican government, in order to give an impulse to this enterprise, will permit the introduction, free from all duties, of the machines and instruments which might be necessary in the explorations, and the products which may have to be exported will also be free from duties of exportation; but the ships which come on account of the company to the regular ports of the republic will be obliged to pay their port charges, and other duties affect them, according to the laws.

—Every appeal or difficulty which is presented in the execution and fulfilment of this contract, will be regulated and decided in the Dominican republic by arbitrators named by the parties, who, in case of disagreement, will name a third, reserving the right of appeal to the competent tribunal.

It was thus adjusted and agreed by its due execution. Done, in double form and in good faith, in the city of St. Domingo, capital of the republic, on the 12th of October, 1858, and 16th of the country.

Given in the National Palace of St. Domingo, the 15th of October, 1858.

SANTANA.

PERDOMO, Secretary of State, of the Treasury and Commerce.
