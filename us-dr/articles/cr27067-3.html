News from Hayti. ANOTHER REVOLUTION DREADED—THE LEGISLATURE WILL NOT VOTE THE LIFE EXPULSION OF SOULOUQUE—GEFFRARD ABOUT TO IMITATE OLIVER CROMWELL AND NAPOLEON—THE BRIG AFRICAN—SALE OF THE FILIBUSTER ARMS TO THE GOVERNMENT, ETC.

OUR PORT AU PRINCE CORRESPONDENCE.

Port au Prince, May 15, 1859.

Great Political Agitation—Soulouque's Banishment for Life Asked for by Government—The Legislature Refuses to Vote it—Geffrard about to Drive Them Out—Case of the Brig African, &c.

Although you receive news from here that the country is as quiet as a lamb, allow me to tell you that people who write such things ought not to write at all. It is want of time, else I would give you all the particulars going on here.

Allow me to tell you that the President, having proposed to the Chambers the expulsion for life of Soulouque and his family, as well as for that of Gen. Delva, the Chambers refuse to do so. Deputations are coming in from all quarters to have this proposition retaken, and the President will not do it. Right he is; let him stick to it. The Senate will not give up, and so, allow me to tell you, that on the 17th of this month the President will drive out of the houses Senators and Representatives, and that he himself, in my humble opinion, will be sustained by every honest citizen.

What may arrive after this is not to be foreseen.

Haytiens call their last revolution a glorious one—"les poltrons"—so many niggers picked out of dirt, made barons, dukes, and Lord knows what, were poltroons enough not to fire one single shot in defence of Soulouque. And now they sing, "glorious revolution." Let them know now that if they do not fight for their liberty they will be shot. How can a man like the ex-French Consul, Mr. R. (in one of your papers of the month of February), defend Soulouque? Is it not proved now that in the prison on the Cape Mole there were discovered 1,500 corpses? What for? How were those eighteen prisoners in Aux Cayes condemned—à la Soulouque? Bay li, touez li, f.....

The brig African is in a very bad position. Never mind; I say that if the Spaniards take or sink the vessel they are in the right. Certificates, opposition and everything is done to save the captain and vessel, but, but—she cleared from New York for Port au Prince direct, and had no business whatever on the coast of Cuba. She tried to put the passengers on shore, but did not succeed. Powder, guns and other ammunition of war are prohibited in this country, and if there is a captain that knows this it is certainly Captain Button, of the brig African, who has been trading for the last eight years here. Besides, Capt. Button ought to know—and he knows perfectly well—that he is not very well liked by the government here. I hope that everything will be settled quietly.

PORT AU PRINCE, May 16, 1859.

Profitable Sale of the Filibuster Arms to Government.

The brig African, Capt. Button, arrived in this port on the 13th ult., with passengers, arms and ammunition, which, after having been duly certified to, were legally permitted to enter this harbor. Various and contradictory rumors have been in circulation ever since the arrival of said brig, many asserting that their original destination was the Island of Cuba; but as the arms have been already disposed of to the Haytien government at a handsome advance, and a number of the passengers having already established themselves in this country with a view of seeking a livelihood under the auspices of the enlightened government which now guides our destinies, it is but fair to infer that the above mentioned rumors, surmises and reports are as groundless as the shallow brains who conceived them.
