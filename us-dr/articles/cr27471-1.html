Letters Reveal Link Of Peron, Trujillo

By FRANCIS P. DOUGLAS

New evidence of the conspiratorial trail of Juan Peron and indicating that he is working in cahoots with Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, dictator of the Dominican Republic, has been made public here.

Letters of the deposed Argentine dictator were presented at the opening meeting yesterday of the Inter-American Press Association's Committee on Freedom of the Press. The committee is meeting at the Mayflower Hotel.

Today the committee will, among other subjects, take up press freedom in Cuba. It is understood that an effort will be made on the part of President Batista of Cuba to obtain modification of a report denouncing the Batista government as undemocratic because it does not permit freedom of the press.

The Peron letters presented yesterday, in photostat, were written from Caracas, Venezuela, where Peron has sought asylum. One, to Dr. Geraldo Rocha at Rio de Janeiro, enclosed confidential directives for the Peron "commandos" in exile about measures to regain power in Argentina. The letter also said:

"My main interest is to defend Generalissimo Trujillo, who is now working with us toward the same goal and from whom I have received effective help in my personal affairs. I wish to return the favors he has done me."

Dubois Named as Target

Peron also asked that a campaign be focused against "Jules Dubois and the mob he leads." Mr. Dubois, Latin American correspondent of the Chicago Tribune, is chairman of the committee, referred to as the "mob."

A second letter was signed "PECINCO" — otherwise PE5. This was said to be a cloak-and-dagger code designation for the displaced dictator. It was addressed to Peron's last minister of education, Armando Mendez San Martin, at Rio.

The letter said a pamphlet was being sent containing three editorials from the Dominican Republic press attacking the IAPA and referring to Mr. Dubois as "the Black Beast." The writer said he had told the Generalissimo that the former minister would get in touch with the Dominican ambassador at Rio.

The letter said that the help of labor organizations and students' bodies should be recruited. All should be done in the greatest secrecy, it was stated.

Mr. Dubois' reaction was, "For us this is a great honor, coming as it does from the man who stole La Prensa (famous Buenos Aires newspaper) and who shut down more than 100 papers. He has not been able to forget the Inter-American Press Association."

Report Heard

The meeting also heard a report on efforts, so far unsuccessful, to obtain a revival of the newspaper La Razon, of La Paz, Bolivia, shut down in 1952. Similarly unsuccessful efforts have been made in behalf of Demetrio Canelas, whose paper, Los Tiempos, of Cochamba, Bolivia, was sacked and who was banished and is unable to return to his country.

Mr. Dubois reported on a visit last May to Bogota, Colombia, where four secret agents called on him led by "Agent 1,333." An order to quit the country was later rescinded. He also said the Dominican Republic issued a decree in April banning him from that country and later issued a statement calling him an "undesirable adventurer."

Present at the meeting yesterday were Guillermo Martinez Marquez of La Pais, Havana, president of the association; John T. O'Rourke of the Washington Daily News, first vice president; and these committee members: Raoul Alfonso Gonse, El Mundo, Havana, committee secretary; Julio de Mesquita Neto, O Estado de Sao Paulo, Brazil; Pedro G. Beltran, La Prensa, Lima, Peru; Augustin Edwards, jr., El Mercurio, Santiago, Chile; Juan S. Valmaggia, La Nacion, Buenos Aires; Pedro Joaquin Chamorro, La Prensa, Managua, Nicaragua; Max Chauvet, Le Nouvelliste, Port-au-Prince, Haiti, and Rodolfo Junco de la Vega, El Norte, Monterrey, Mexico.

Hailed as Hero

Mr. Beltran, Peru's ambassador here in 1944-45, was hailed as a "hero of freedom of the press." In February, 1956, police raided La Prensa, of which he is publisher. Thirty of his staff were jailed and he, likewise, was arrested.

The association's directors will meet next Monday and Tuesday. The general assembly, with about 300 members present, will open a three-day meeting Wednesday. Secretary of State Dulles is scheduled to make an address on the final day.
