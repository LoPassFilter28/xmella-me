Members of Congress Decorated By the Dominican Republic

Three members of Congress, an American businessman, and a Catholic priest were awarded the Dominican Republic's highest decoration yesterday.

The ceremony took place at the embassy preceeding a large 5-to-7 reception commemorating the 113th anniversary of the national independence of the Dominican Republic.

Dominican Ambassador Salazar presented the "Order of Merit of Duarte, Sanchez and Mella" to House Majority Leader John W. McCormack and Representatives Katharine St. George and Donald L. Jackson. Mr. Jackson was unable to be present yesterday, as he had just departed for the Middle East.

Also honored were Col. Carroll Cone, vice president of Pan-American World Airways and Father Joseph Thorning of Frederick, Md. An award also was received by the widow of Thurmond Chatham, the representative from North Carolina who had been designated to receive the decoration before his death.

AMBASSADOR SALAZAR stressed that these awards were given for important contributions to mankind in general as well as for outstanding services to the Dominican Republic.

He lauded Mr. McCormack as one of the "staunchest and most outspoken enemies of that atheistic communism which would destroy the cherished institutions of the Dominican Republic as well as of the United States."

Honoring Mrs. St. George, the Ambassador explained, "affords the people of my country an opportunity to render deserving tribute to the women of the United States."

The Ambassador said his country's "relentless" fight against communism is why the Dominican Republic is so often "a target of crafty attacks, even of a military nature, by elements who would further the Kremlin's plans for world conquest."

REPORTERS afterwards asked the Ambassador to comment on the recent story in Life Magazine which claimed it had turned up information that the missing Dr. Jesus Maria De Galindez, bitter foe of the Dominican Republic's strongman Rafael Trujillo, was kidnaped in New York last March and flown to the Dominican Republic.

The magazine also contended that the pilot of the plane was a young American Gerald Murphy, who later disappeared in the republic. The story implies that both Dr. Galindez (a professor at Columbia University) and Murphy met with foul play at the hands of the Dominicans.

"It's a tremendous libel," said Ambassador Salazar of the Life article.

Mrs. St. George was asked how she felt about receiving the award in view of the furor created over the Galindez disappearance.

"I feel this order has been given to me for what I've done to better the welfare of women and children and my contributions in general. The award has nothing to do with this particular episode."

And Father Thorning said, "A man or a nation is innocent until proven guilty by a judge or jury of his peers."

Mr. McCormack, when replying formally to Ambassador Salazar's speech referred to the old adage—"a friend in need is a friend indeed"—saying that the real test of friendship comes when a trying incident arises.

"The friendship between the United States and Dominican Republic has been tested and found true," he added, citing the co-operation of the two countries during World War II and now against communism.

After the ceremony witnessed by close friends and relatives, the Ambassador and Mme. Salazar received several hundred guests to celebrate their independence day.

ANGELITA TRUJILLO, the 17-year-old daughter of the generalissimo, attracted more attention than all the other guests put together.

A striking brunette in ruby red satin, her wavy hair worn youthfully long and loose, Miss Trujillo has recently come to Washington to attend school here. With her was Miss Elsa Alvarez, daughter of the Dominican Minister of Finance, who is a classmate of hers.

Mrs. John Foster Dulles, wife of the Secretary of State, headed the list of United States dignitaries attending. And of course there was the usual large turnout from the South American diplomatic missions.

Chilean OAS Ambassador Sepulveda said the new Chilean Ambassador to the White House, Mariano Puga, will arrive in Washington on Sunday, accompanied by his 22-year-old daughter, Josefina.

Panama's handsome Ambassador Arias and his wife were among the guests, as were the new Ambassador of Paraguay and Senora de Chaves; Senora de Urrutia, wife of the Colombian Ambassador; the Philippines Ambassador and Mrs. Romulo, the Mexican Ambassador and Senora de Tello, former Ambassador and Mr. John F. Simmons, Italy's Ambassador Brosio, Canada's Ambassador and Mrs. Heeney, Argentina's Ambassador to the OAS and Senora de Garcia, the Secretary General of the OAS, Jose Mora.

Others were Representatives Eugene Keogh and Albert Morano, Mr. and Mrs. Leslie Biffle, Brazil's Ambassador Peixoto, Ambassador Berckemeyer of Peru, Miss Marianna Moran and her fiance Michel J. Parsons, Mrs. Ray Henle, Mr. Jack Neal, and Mr. and Mrs. Harold Mott.
