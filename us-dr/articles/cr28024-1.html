Santo Domingo Voting Periled

SANTO DOMINGO, Dominican Republic, Dec. 17 (AP).—A political party's quarrel with the Roman Catholic church threatened to delay the Dominican Republic's first free elections in 37 years.

The left-of-center Dominican Revolutionary Party withdrew last night from the elections scheduled for Thursday. It demanded a postponement of one month in the balloting because a Spanish Jesuit priest had charged that the party's presidential candidate is a Communist.

The candidate, Juan Bosch, 53, fought the late Dictator Rafael L. Trujillo from exile for 25 years. He and Dr. Viriato Fiallo, 67, of the rightist National Civic Union, are considered the two leading candidates in a field of seven.

The governing state council had no immediate word on the party's demand, but it did warn that interference in the elections by "foreign elements" would not be tolerated. A council spokesman said this could be interpreted as applying to foreign clergy.

The church said that the statement attributed to the Spanish Jesuit did not reflect its "true neutral position." Archbishop Octavio Beras of Santo Domingo and three other Dominican bishops denied the party's charge that the church was trying to persuade peasants not to vote for Bosch.

Catholicism, the state religion of the Dominican Republic, is professed by most of the country's 3 million people.

Although the governing council was reported in no mood to delay the election, it also was said to be worried over the effect the party's decision might have on smaller parties.
