Evening Star · December 20, 190920 de diciembre de 1909

Original in English.Original en inglés.

DIPLOMATS NAMED Important Appointments Go to Senate Today. BACON SENT TO FRANCE District Man Will Represent Us at Persian Capital. HITT MINISTER TO PANAMA Well Known Here, Where His Father Was Representative From Illinois for Years.

President Taft sent his selections for a number of important diplomatic posts to the Senate for confirmation today.

The important nominations are:

Robert Bacon of New York, to be ambassador to France.

Richard C. Kerens of Missouri, to be ambassador to Austria-Hungary.

Henry Lane Wilson of Washington, to be ambassador to Mexico.

Charles Page Bryan of Illinois, to be minister to Belgium.

William James Calhoun of Illinois, to be minister to China.

Henry P. Fletcher of Pennsylvania, to be minister to Chile.

Henry T. Gage of California, to be minister to Portugal.

R. S. Reynolds Hitt of Illinois, to be minister to Panama.

John B. Jackson of New Jersey, to be minister to Cuba.

Fenton R. McCreery of Michigan, to be minister to Honduras.

Edwin V. Morgan of New York, to be minister to Paraguay.

Charles W. Russell of the District of Columbia, to be minister to Persia.

Lauritz S. Swenson of Minnesota, to be minister to Switzerland.

Horace G. Knowles, to be resident minister and consul general to the Dominican Republic.

Other nominations in the Department of State are:

Secretaries to Embassies.

To be secretaries of embassies—Philip M. Brown at Mexico; Henry L. Janes of Wisconsin, at Constantinople, Turkey; Irwin B. Laughlin of Pennsylvania, at Berlin, Germany; Hoffman Philip of New York, at Rio de Janeiro, Brazil; Montgomery Schuyler, jr., of New York, at Tokio, Japan; George Post Wheeler of Washington, at St. Petersburg, Russia.

To be agent and consul general of the United States at Cairo, Egypt—Peter Augustus Jay of Rhode Island.

To be second secretary of the embassy at Rio de Janeiro, Brazil—Marshall Langhorne of Virginia.

To be secretaries of legations—Lewis Einstein of New York, at Peking, China; Paxton Hibbon of Indiana, to the Netherlands and Luxemburg; Charles Dunning White of New Jersey, at Christiana, Norway; Balkam Scheyer of Pennsylvania, at Bogota, Colombia.

Baker Not Nominated.

Ten United States attorneys are nominated in the list sent to the Senate from the White House, but there is no nomination made for the United States attorneyship for the District, despite the fact that the term of Mr. Baker has expired.

Gustavus B. Timanus is nominated for postmaster in Laurel, Md., and Alfred W. Thompson for Ridgely, Md. There are a couple score of other postmaster nominations.

Two appointments of ministers are of particular interest to Washington—the appointment of Charles W. Russell of the District of Columbia, as minister to Persia, and the appointment of R. S. Reynolds Hitt as minister to Panama.

Russell Goes to Persia.

Charles W. Russell is now assistant attorney general of the United States. He has for the past twenty years been connected with the Department of Justice. Mr. Russell was Mr. Knox's assistant when Mr. Knox was Attorney General at the time of the examination of the title of the Panama canal.

R. S. Reynolds Hitt, who is promoted to be minister to Panama, is appointed from Illinois, but he lived for many years in Washington, where his father was a member of the House. His mother, Mrs. Hitt, and his brother, W. F. Hitt, are living here now, and Mrs. Hitt has just built a handsome house on Dupont Circle. Mr. Hitt is a graduate of the Yale and of the Harvard law schools. He was appointed third secretary of embassy at Paris in 1901, second secretary at Berlin in 1902, secretary at Rome in 1905 and secretary at Berlin in 1908.

Bacon Harvard Man.

Robert Bacon of New York, named as ambassador to France, is a graduate of Harvard University. He was formerly a member of the firm of J. P. Morgan & Co. He was appointed assistant secretary of state September 5, 1905, and was Secretary of State from January 27 to March 6, 1909.

William James Calhoun of Illinois, appointed minister to China, is a lawyer who has had considerable diplomatic experience in Cuba and Venezuela, and served also for two years as a member of the interstate commerce commission.

Richard C. Kerens, named as ambassador to Austria-Hungary, is a native of Ireland, where he was born in 1842, came to the United States in infancy. He served in the Union army from 1861 to 1865, and after the war went to Arkansas and still later to San Diego, Cal., where he was a railway contractor. He removed to St. Louis in 1876, and has since resided there.

Henry Lane Wilson, transferred from the mission to Belgium to be ambassador to Mexico, was first appointed in the diplomatic service in June, 1897, as minister to Chile. He was made minister to Belgium March 8, 1905. Mr. Wilson was born in Indiana in 1857. At the time of his appointment in the diplomatic service he had been a resident of Spokane, Wash., for fifteen years.

Bryan's Former Appointments.

Charles Page Bryan of Illinois, transferred from Portugal to Belgium, is fifty-three years of age. His diplomatic appointments have been as minister to China, November 10, 1897; minister to Brazil, January 19, 1898; minister to Switzerland, September 26, 1902, and minister to Portugal, January 7, 1902.

Lauritz S. Swanson of Minneapolis, Minn., who is appointed minister to Switzerland, was born in 1865, of Norwegian parents. He was engaged in educational work for a number of years and has been a regent of the State University of Minnesota. He was United States minister to Demark from 1897 to 1905.

Henry P. Fletcher of Chambersburg, Pa., who is appointed minister to Chile, is thirty-six years of age and is a lawyer by profession. He served in the army in Cuba in 1898 and was afterward for two years in the Philippines. He was appointed second secretary of legation at Havana in 1902, second secretary at Peking in 1903, secretary at Lisbon in 1905 and secretary at Peking April 26, 1907.

Edwin V. Morgan, transferred from Cuba to Paraguay and Uruguay, was born in Aurora, N. Y., in 1865. He is a Harvard graduate and has been minister to Cuba.

Formerly in Navy.

John B. Jackson, transferred from the mission to Persia to the Cuban mission, is a native of Newark, N. J. He is a graduate of the United States Naval Academy. He resigned from the navy in 1886 and was admitted to the New York bar in 1889.

Henry T. Gage of California, who is appointed minister to Portugal, is a resident of Sacramento, and has taken a prominent part in the upbuilding of his state. He was Governor of California from 1899 to 1903.

Fenton R. McCreery of Flint, Mich., transferred from the Dominican Republic to Honduras, was born in Flint in 1866. He served as minister resident and consul general at Santo Domingo in 1907.

Horace G. Knowles of Delaware, transferred from Nicaragua to Santo Domingo, was born in Delaware in 1863. He is a lawyer by profession. He was consul at Bordeaux from 1889 to 1893 and was appointed minister to Roumania and Servia in 1907 and minister to Nicaragua in January, 1909.
