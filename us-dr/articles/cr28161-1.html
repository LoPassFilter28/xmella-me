U. N. Expected to Drop Cuba Invasion Charge

UNITED NATIONS, N. Y., Nov. 23 (AP).—United Nations delegates expect the Security Council to quietly drop the Cuban charge that the United States threatens to invade the Dominican Republic.

The council scheduled another session tomorrow to hear the views of the Dominican government in full. It was expected after that to consider the case closed despite efforts by Cuba and the Soviet Union to blow it up into a major issue.

Cuban Ambassador Mario Garcia-Inchaustegui called on the council to condemn the United States as an aggressor and order it to withdraw the ships and planes it stationed off the Dominican capital when two brothers of the late dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, threatened a coup.

Sponsor Lacking

Informants said, however, that no council member—not even Russia—was willing to sponsor such a demand. Cuba is not a member of the council.

The Cuban complaint was first debated yesterday at a stormy council session interrupted briefly by an outburst from the gallery by two Dominicans who were rushed from the chamber by U. N. guards.

United States Ambassador Adlai E. Stevenson asserted Cuba's charges were without foundation and that there was evidence of a "sinister alliance" between Fidel Castro's forces and the Trujillo family which had held sway in the Dominican Republic for 31 years.

Mr. Stevenson denied Cuban charges that United States ships were in the territorial waters of the Dominican Republic and that United States planes had flown over Dominican territory.

He said the warships and planes were staying outside Dominican territorial limits and that they had been sent to the Caribbean area with the full knowledge of President Joaquin Balaguer's government.

Distortion Charged

Mr. Stevenson accused Cuba of distorting the situation to make anti-American propaganda and to conceal from the Cuban people efforts of a friendly government to aid the Dominicans "to take a great step forward" toward democracy.

Mr. Garcia-Inchaustegui accused the United States of sending warships into Dominican waters so that "later they can send these same warships against Cuba." The Cuban delegate called Mr. Balaguer a puppet of United States imperialism.

Dominican delegate Carlos Sanchez Y Sanchez retorted that Mr. Balaguer was receiving instructions from no one. He asserted the Cuban was engaging in "insulting diplomacy."

"I represent the Dominican people and I can prove it," Mr. Sanchez said.

At this point a Dominican in the gallery shouted in Spanish, "It's a lie!" He and a companion were hustled out.

"The Dominican Republic does not feel itself threatened," Mr. Sanchez said later in the debate. "The Dominican Republic has not been invaded. We would have known what to do had this been the case."
