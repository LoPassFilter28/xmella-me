WILL RUN BLOCKADE. Cherokee Owners Determined to Enter Domingo Ports.

NEW YORK, Nov. 17.—Notwithstanding an announcement by Manuel de T. Galvan, Dominican consul at this port, that the Clyde line steamer Cherokee will be sunk by Dominican gunboats if she attempts to enter the blockaded ports of Samana, Sanchez, Monte Christo, Macoris and Puerta Plata, the agents of the Clyde line insist that the vessel will take a cargo to these ports.

Evidently the dispatches did not frighten the officers, for one of them, F. S. Cameron, when seen said:

"Who says we cannot land at these Santo Domingo ports? The Santo Domingo government? Well, you may say that the Clyde line is attending to its own business and that a part of that business is to touch at Dominican ports, and that is what we are going to do."

Signor Galvan, consul general for Santo Domingo, who is a godson of General Jiminez, the head of the rebels, said:

"Well, the gunboats will not parley with her this time. If the Independencia does not sink her El Presidente certainly will."

The offices of the consul general were besieged with anxious shippers who wanted to know what the government was going to do about it. One of them informed Senor Galvan that the Clyde line had informed him that it would carry his goods to Puerto Plata and that two United States gunboats were to convoy the Cherokee. The consul general snapped his fingers.

"My instructions are that the cargo of any vessel entering rebel ports will be confiscated and the crew treated as pirates. Now, you can take your chances if you wish, but I am telling you what will happen."

The Cherokee is the ship that ran the Dominican blockade some days ago.
