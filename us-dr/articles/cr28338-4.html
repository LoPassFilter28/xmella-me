THE SHOT THAT BACKFIRED

No 8 of a series

El Caribe

EDITORA DEL CARIBE, C. por A.

J. A. OSORIO LIZARAZO, Director

CIUDAD TRUJILLO, DOMINICAN REPUBLIC

THE SHOT THAT BACKFIRED

By GUILLERMO F. CRUZADO

IN ITS internal aspect, the sovereignty of a nation, that is to say, the idea of a single and overall power for each and every State, is the authority of the State in regard to its subjects. Externally, this sovereignty is reflected in the mutual respect one State has for another.

When the sovereignty of a State is mocked at, either by those who oppose it, or through the breaking off of diplomatic relations without just cause, as set forth in Article 7 of the Treaty of Mutual Assistance, the case must be considered to be one of aggression against a Right, since by virtue of its ethical nature, it is a question of political science and should represent a just and equitative understanding of the corresponding rights of every other state. There is a natural law that regulates every human relationship and identifies the ethical norms to which every government is subject in order to achieve the respect and prosperity of a conglomerate of nations, such as the States that share the destinies of Latin America.

Sovereignty is a power that governs subjects and citizens, an omnipotent power that must be respected by every state. And yet, we have been attacked by Venezuela and Cuba without the governments of either of these two countries being penalized for violating the sovereignty of a people by financing and sending invasion forces against the Dominican Republic.

Nevertheless, everything that has been written and established appears not to favor the equality of rights, of assistance to and respect among all Latin American peoples. It is obvious that, for some reason or another, there is a tendency to back only those to whom one feels obligated, or whose political ideology is that of the majority of the member states of the O.A.S.

In the struggle that has arisen between Cuba and the United States, everyone has volunteered their good offices to avoid the worsening of the situation and to help improve relations between these two member states of the O.A.S. Why did they not do the same thing in our case? Why have they allowed intervention in the domestic affairs of our country to continue? Why do they accept secret and false charges and anonymous accusations?

Since we very soon realized that we could count only on our own efforts, our own valor, our own Armed Forces and on the Dominican people, a people always prepared to defend the sovereignty of their fatherland, we found ourselves forced to spend millions of dollars on arms with which to prepare the defense of our country. Prior to Betancourt's and Fidel's aggressions, these millions were spent on works of progress and on public welfare.

Things would be different if causes and effects were always dealt with equanimity and justice and the rights of a free nation, one that has its laws and its Constitution, were caused to be respected without anyone having a lawful right to impose upon norms and codes for our government, norms and codes which follow the criterion and ideology of countries vastly different to our own in race, customs, criteria, feelings and religion. If such were the case, there would be better understanding, better coexistence and greater assistance in economic problems and friendly relations between the American nations.

However, everything seems to point to the fact that certain countries are interested in opposing Trujillo in order to see whether, by so doing, Fidel's fury against the United States can be placated. Injustice and abuse have their own champions who lay down the line of conduct to be followed by the O.A.S. and the latter, in turn, can harm, with or without reason, those whom it wills, or those whom it is ordered to so harm.

If injustice is to continue to be applied against friendly nations in such a treacherous manner, how dim is the future of Latin America. Communism will reign throughout the whole of the Continent. Small nations will finally end up by tiring of being overrun and abused and will endeavour to alter their course towards horizons that promise more as regards security and peace.

The Saavedras Lamas Treaty was signed at Rio de Janeiro by the twenty-one American Republics that attended the Conference. It is a Treaty of non-aggression and conciliation: any of those American Republics, in signing the 1928 Treaty of Paris, or the 1933 Treaty of Non-Aggression and Conciliation, or both, or of other instruments for peace, will consult the governments of the other American Republics and, in such cases, these, working together, will endeavour to find formulas for peaceful co-operation. Yes, but they have forgotten this fact and have proceeded willfully and in accordance with certain interests, favoring only those whom, through special circumstances, they feel bound to favor.

Resolution VIII of the Inter-American Conference on problems of war and peace, held at Mexico City, recommended the signing of treaties in order to take care of any threat, or act of aggression, against any American nation.

The undertaking is one of mutual assistance between, and common defense of, American Republics. These pledges were not fulfilled when invasion forces from Venezuela and Cuba attempted with their landings at Maimon, Estero Hondo and Constanza, to destroy Dominican peace and tranquility.

Each and every one of the remaining Republics remained deaf and blind. Not one of the 19 signatories either protested, co-operated, or lent any effective assistance, nor did they even trouble themselves to ascertain the truth and sanction the aggressors. For this reason, the threats of aggression have continued and so have provocations, and they will still continue since it is obvious that Venezuela counts on the backing achieved, no doubt, at the price of these provocations and threats against the government and people of the Dominican Republic.

To these enemies of Dominican peace and progress, we say: we have the Government we desire and the one that is best suited to our requirements; no one and no country has the right to impose their will and their sympathies on us since we know what we are doing and accept full responsibility for our future.

Trujillo is our sole leader since it was he that saved the Republic and made it great, converting a forgotten nation into one that is envied because of its progress and civic greatness, and for the peace it enjoys despite the intrigues, snares and aggressions to which it has been subjected by those who forget we know how anxious they are to impose upon us a government of their own political ideology, one that would be easy for them to manage and exploit.

We were attacked, but Article 3 was ignored. The attack on the Dominican Republic was not considered an attack against all the American states. No one did anything about the armed attack we suffered. We were forced to defend ourselves alone. No one faced up to the situation or tried to find a means of avoiding further attacks. We have been waiting expectantly, our attention fixed solely on our national defense, well aware that Betancourt and Fidel would endeavor to find the means to attack us and overthrow our government.

And what a great mistake these countries have made in thinking that by sacrificing us, they could placate Castro. The shot has backfired. Fidel has Russia to defend him. Now, Russia is offering aid to all small American nations should there be any attempt at invasion of their respective territories.

Every day, the situation becomes more complicated and if the causes are not dealt with without bias, and an end is not put to the continual abuse, the hurricane will follow on its course, destroying Pan-American unity and American solidarity and, without any shadow of doubt, ultimately leading to a cruel and devastating war.

THE DOMINICAN REPUBLIC PRESS SOCIETY

Ciudad Trujillo, Dominican Republic
