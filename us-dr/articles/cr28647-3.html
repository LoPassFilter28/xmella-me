New-York Dispatch · April 20, 186120 de abril de 1861

Original in English.Original en inglés.

The Spanish war steamer Pizarro—with some small craft as tenders—has been hovering along the coast, and making night trips between Porto Rico and this island, for three weeks, and the Dominicans now fear she has been secretly landing men and supplies in secure, unnoticed corners.

Meanwhile, the whole population was disarmed before anybody suspected the cause. In the towns of Neyba, Azua and San Christoval, single officers went to collect all the arms, "in order to replace them," it was said, "with a better class," and under various pretences, the most popular and patriotic leaders of the people, were inveigled within the walls of Santo Domingo, where they are detained as prisoners. When all was ready, the President, the three members of his cabinet, and the half dozen high officers, who had managed the affair, raised at six in the morning of the eighteenth, the Spanish flag over the forts, and proclaimed Isabella the Second, Queen of the Dominican territory. The notification was received in silent consternation by the people. The Spaniards raised a feeble shout of "long live the Queen," but scarcely a single Dominican, outside the paid officials, responded, although double pay, and lavish promises for the future, were distributed over night among the soldiers of the garrison and the police. There will be a desperate resistance in the interior, but the Dominicans say that "Spain will send twenty thousand of her war-hounds to tear the life out of the Dominican Republic, and devour the last morsel of its body."

E. D.
