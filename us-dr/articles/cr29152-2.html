Authorities Investigate Here Conspiracies Against Two Caribbean Governments

Arsenal and Plane Captured From

Group of Dominicans From North

Ex-Employees of Sheriff's Office Among

Those Killed in Haiti's Abortive Coup

By United Press International

Miami authorities were investigating yesterday two international conspiracies in which U. S. citizens took part in subversive attempts against the governments of Haiti and the Domminican Republic.

Sheriff Thomas Kelly revealed here that one of his ex-Deputy Sheriffs, Arthur T. Payne, 34, was one of those killed in the bloody uprising Tuesday against the Haitian government. Another ex-Deputy has disappeared, and four more took part in the conspiracy, but they dropped out at the last minute, added Kelly.

At the same time, 16 persons were arrested when, it seemed, they attempted to take off for the Dominican Republic in a plane loaded with arms, ammuntions, and equipment, sufficient to equip, according to customs agents, a force of 150 men.

Kelly stated that Payne, who he called “a soldier of fortune”, was

the principal recruiter of Deputy Sheriffs for the expedition to the Haitian Republic.

According to Kelly, two of the Deputy Sheriffs who refused at the last minute said that they had been offered $2,000 each to accompany the conspirators to Haiti, although they added that later the offer dropped to only $500.

The four Deputy Sheriffs, who refused to go along were identified as Jesse Goodwin, 34; Michael O'Brian, 38; Nelson G. Cloud, 34; and Kenneth Nebauer, 37. Cloud and Neubauer resigned on Monday and the other two on Tuesday.

Customs agent Charles Wyatt stated that the arrested men, mostly Domminican citizens who have been living in New York, told him that they had planned to go to the headquarters of the rebellion against Batista's regime in Cuba.

But he added that customs officials had confiscated a large amount of propaganda against Trujillo, which indicates that the expedition was really headed for an attack against Trujillo.

The plane, a C-46 Cargo plane, was loaded with hand grenades, boxes of 50 calibre machine gun shells, rifles, pistols, Tommy guns, knives, machetes, bayonets and two boxes of pistols.

"It is difficult to lay hands on them, said Wyatt, "these rebels travel from place to place, and from district to district. Some Cuban rebels were recently trained in the Domminican Republic".

The arrested men were identified as: José Antonio Batista, 22, New York; Herbert Manuel Walter, 43, New York; Frank Guzmán, 40, Jacksonville, the three U. S. citizens; and Pedro Pablo Fernández, 24, Chicago; Carlos Luis Monzano, 31, Stanford; Miguel Meléndez, 39, Chicago, all Domminican citizens Daniel Batista, 25; Manuel Perozo, 39; Juan Batista Alomonte, 29; José Mason, 33; Rafael Tomás Perello, 30; Simeón Guillén Guzmán, 56; Luis Alvarez, 29; Juan Alamanzar Díaz 25; Ramón Asensio Valverde, 42; and José Manuel Batista, 50, all residents in New York, but Domminican citizens.
