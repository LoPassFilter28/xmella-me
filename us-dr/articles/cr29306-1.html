War in Santo Domingo

Insurrection Growing and Success Certain, Says General Berroa.

New York, June 30.—General Q. Berroa, one of the fighting enemies of President Ramon Caceres of Santo Domingo, has just arrived in New York from Turks Island, whence he was sent into exile after a brief stay in prison. General Berroa said last night that the insurrection now going on in the Dominican Republic was daily growing in strength and would result in the overthrow of the Caceres dynasty.

The present movement is a continuation of the uprising started by General Berroa last November, in Macoris against President Morales, and is now directed against President Caceres. General Berroa is a lawyer, and is here on professional business only, he explained. He was formerly governor of Macoris.

He said that next month there is to be an important conference in this city between the leaders of the party opposed to the existing government in Santo Domingo, which will include General Juan I. Jimenez and General Carlos Morales, both ex-presidents of the republic, who have sunk their differences and will again work together.

Others who will take part in this conference are General Eugenio Deschamps, ex-vice-president, and Dr. Francisco Sanchez, former minister of foreign affairs.

General Jimenez is still in Ponce, Porto Rico, according to General Berroa, and it was General Mauricio Jimenez who took command of the insurgents, and not the ex-president, as was announced from Washington on Sunday. General Morales is in St. Thomas.

General Berroa said: The existing conditions in Santo Domingo are terrible. Men are being killed every day for political reasons and the jails are filled with enemies of the government. Every constitutional right is denied to my compatriots. People are also starving to death. The right of suffrage is a dead letter with the present government.

The revolution now in progress is a Christian uprising against barbarism. The movement is increasing in strength every day, and has spread from Monte Cristi, where it is strongest, to the states of Azua, Barahona, Macoris and Santo Domingo.

It is our purpose, if we are victorious, as I am certain we shall be, to establish land taxes, modify the tariff and remove the pernicious export duties, which retard the progress of our industries. We also want the right of habeas corups, which is unknown in our country. As matters are, the government puts in jail and exiles citizens without any process of law.

The revolution was started without the consent or advice of General Jimenez. General Morales and General Jimenez have come to an understanding. The government has suspended payment of the army and civil employes and seeks to pay preferred creditors, which cannot be done according to the modus vivendi.

Of course we recognize the Santo Domingo treaty, and we are anxious for an even better one.

It is probably not generally known in this country, but I know that Ederico Velasquez, minister of finance, who is now in this city, has come here to get, if possible, half of the $1,300,000 on deposit for the payment of Santo Domingo's foreign creditors, that the money might be used for the expenses of the war. If the United States could guarantee us free elections the revolution would end, but as we are without help we were compelled to protest with arms.

Even General Horacio Vasquez, formerly president of the republic, who was the delegate to the Cibao for President Caceres, has deserted him.
