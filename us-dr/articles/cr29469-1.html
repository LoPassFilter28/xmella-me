Trujillo Near Flight Recently, U. S. Hears

By EARL H. VOSS

Star Staff Writer

The Dominican Republic's Dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, has been on the verge of flight several times in the last two months, according to information reaching officials here.

His plan, it is said, was to take off in the manner of Gen. Batista in Cuba on New Year's Day, 1959.

Gen. Trujillo is reported to feel that he is getting nowhere in the Dominican Republic against mounting opposition and is running the risk of being forcibly liquidated. But he is said to figure that he could leave now with enough resources to live comfortably in exile.

Fear Worse Problem

There would be no tears shed in the State Department over Gen. Trujillo's departure. But the vacuum that would be left in the Dominican Republic might present a worse problem than the present one, officials here feel.

The Fidel Castro regime in Cuba might try to move some of its Dominican associates into high Dominican government positions, it is feared.

The United States Government is not pushing Trujillo to leave, officials here say, partly because they do not see any alternative to head a government in the Dominican Republic. Dangers of conflict, civil as well as international, would be great in the resulting vacuum.

Other Latin American nations, however—prominent among them Venezuela, whose President has charged the Trujillo regime paid assassins to try to kill him—are eager to see Gen. Trujillo leave, no matter what the consequences.

First Problem of OAS

The Organization of American States met here Monday to consider Peru's call for a Latin American foreign ministers' meeting. Most of them reportedly see Gen. Trujillo as the first problem to be dealt with in Latin America, not the United States' problems with the Castro regime in Cuba.

The Latin American Foreign Ministers' Conference probably will be held in early August, officials here said. Washington is prominent among the sites under consideration.

Gen. Trujillo was elected President of the Dominican Republic in 1930. Although he permits no freedom of press or speech or political opposition, his government supports the free world.

Under his regime, modern housing has been built for workers, schools have been erected and the economy, which formerly had depended entire-cumulated record of at least diversified.
