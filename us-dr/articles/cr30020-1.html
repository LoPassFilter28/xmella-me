Evening Journal · August 3, 19153 de agosto de 1915

Original in English.Original en inglés.

The year 1914 was one of trial and tribulation for the Dominican Republic—revolutions, bankruptcy, disease, and that original "deserving Democrat," James M. Sullivan, United States Minister to Santo Domingo, being some of the bitterness which the Black Republic was obliged to swallow. Notwithstanding all that, they managed to increase their exports to the United States by 60 per cent., thanks to the Democratic tariff law. The largest increase was in sugar, which jumped from $1,611,161 to $3,367,470 in value, the reduction in duty going into the pockets of the American and Federal Sugar Refining Companies. In all, the island sent $8,844,165 worth of goods to our markets for the calendar year 1914, and took $4,452,347 from us. Secretary Redfield will not be justified in figuring the Dominican Republic in on that billion dollar trade balance which he credits to Democratic policies.
