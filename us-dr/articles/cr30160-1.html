The Birmingham Age-Herald · February 11, 190411 de febrero de 1904

Original in English.Original en inglés.

In Santo Domingo.

The murder of a machinist on the gunboat Yankee in a Dominican port, by one of the contending factions, will no doubt be followed by a demand for reparation. It is difficult to see, however, how reparation can be secured when no one can say which faction is de facto, or which de jure. The administration has recognized to some extent the Morales crowd, but Jiminez has still, it is said, a following.

The island is about 450 miles long, Hayti holding the western one-third, and the Dominicans the balance. It seems a mass of mountains from the sea, but the island contains many beautiful valleys. The population of the Dominican republic is about 600,000. In Hayti the French tongue is spoken, but the Dominicans speak Spanish. As a rule the Dominicans are a mixture of Spaniards, Indians and negroes in about equal proportions. The republic was established in 1843, and it has been the scene of many revolutions and insurrections since that time. Our minister, Mr. Powell, considers the present situation chaotic and an invitation to this country to intervene and restore order.

As we will not permit any other country to become peacemaker and order restorer, the responsibility seems to rest upon us to do something in Santo Domingo, and doubtless the murder of the machinist will be all the excuse we need for a degree of intervention that will restore order, or at least enough authority to grant us reparation for the firing on an American gunboat, and the killing on it of an American citizen.
