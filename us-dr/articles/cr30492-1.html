Gregory Simms Named Deputy Peace Corps Representative

WASHINGTON, D. C. — Gregory Simms, a 34 year old internationally known concert baritone singer, former boxer, wrestler and former high school football star, has been named Deputy Peace Corps Representative to serve in the Dominican Republic.

In his newly assigned duties, Simms will assist in the overall supervision of the Caribbean Republic's Peace Corps project. Twenty-one Peace Corps Volunteers have already completed their U. S. training in the Peace Corps Training Camp, at Arecibo, Puerto Rico. They are now in the final phase of training in the Dominican Republic. Simms will work with Andre' Hernandez, of New Mexico, who has been selected for the Peace Corps Representative post.

A second group of Volunteers will depart for Santo Domingo in the near future. More than 200 are expected to be on duty in the little tropical island by the end of the year, where three million people live.

The overall project consists of help for secondary and normal education, community development, forestry and reconservation, co-operative organizing, aided-self help school construction, housing and well drilling.

This native mid-westerner from the Jay-Hawk State who will soon depart with the second suppleemntal Peace Corps group is not a newcomer to community work and community development. He has served as director at New York City's Housing Authority-operated Marlboro Houses Community Center. There he directed, developed and supervised community recreational and educational programs, administered the budget and fund raising activities.

AT HARLEM YMCA

The youthful U. S. Air Force veteran was at Harlem's 135th Street Branch YMCA for 2 years as a group worker. Later he was a consultant with the National Conference of Christians and Jews Brotherhood Workshop.

In 1958, he served as summer dance coordinator — organizing and supervising dances throughout greater New York — under the auspices of the Mayor's Committee for Living Music.

While working in New York City, Simms along with Attorney Newton Greenburg led protests that eliminated color and race from birth certificates.

Simms has performed for the United States Information Agency in Spain, Switzerland, Belgium, Portugal, France and Germany. His accompanist in many of his concerts was his wife, Mrs. Patricia Roth Simms, of Allentown, Pennsylvania. Mrs. Simms is a concert pianist in her own right. During other concerts, Simms accompanist was Easley Blackwood, son of the noted Bridge authority.

The mid-western vocalist received his bachelor of music degree at Kansas University, in Lawrence, Kansas. Subsequent studies in music literature and languages were followed at Sorbonne University, Vienna's Academy of Music and the University for Foreigners, in Perugia, Italy. He was a John Hay Whitney and Jean Tennyson Fellow.

In 1956-7, Simms was lead baritone with the Sienna Opera Festival; the featured soloist at the University of Kansas Music Week; and dubbed "Mid-West Young American Artist for 1957." Later, he served as soloist for the Little Orchestra Society in New York City.

Mrs. Simms was accompanist for the American Concert Choir, for five years. She has frequently accompanied Metropolitan Opera Star Leontyne Price, and was accompanist at the Westminister Recording Company.

Simms is the son of Master Luther Simms and Mrs. Bessie Simms of Newton, Kansas. Mrs. Simms is the daughter of Mrs. Irene Roth of Franklin Square, L. I. The Simms and Gabriela, their 21-month daughter, will live in Santo Domingo, the island's capital.
