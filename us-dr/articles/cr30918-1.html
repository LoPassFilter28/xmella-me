Venezuelan Violence Rises, Betancourt Blames Castro

CARACAS, Venezuela, Oct. 8 (AP).—President Romulo Betancourt blamed Fidel Castro last night for the increase in anti-government violence in Venezuela that has cost at least 20 lives in the last week.

In a nationwide broadcast, Mr. Betancourt appealed for public support of the police and military campaign to crush the terrorists. He said:

"Cuba is living in hours of desperation. In spite of millions of dollars spent daily by the Soviet Union on its Havana vassal, there is hunger in Cuba."

Mr. Betancourt said Communists and fellow travelers are waging the campaign of terrorism to wreck Venezuela's national elections scheduled for December 1 but he said control of the government is even more firmly in his hands than when he assumed office four years ago.

Promising impartial elections, Mr. Betancourt said, "I will deliver power in March, 1964, to a president freely chosen by Venezuelans."

Terror Continues

There was no abatement in the terrorism that has promoted the government to move paratroopers and other military units into Caracas. Three more persons died in street battles last night.

The violence intensified last week when Mr. Betancourt ordered the arrest of 23 Communist and extreme leftist members of Congress and a roundup of other Red leaders after a terrorist attack on an excursion train.

Accusing the pro-Castro lawmakers of directing the terrorism, Mr. Betancourt said the constitution permitted removal of their parliamentary immunity because they were seeking to overthrow the Constitution.

Other Developments

Mr. Betancourt said the Congressmen were being held for military trials. He said they had been caught with plans for knocking out the Caracas power and telephone service and for a mass jail break so convicts could terrorize the city.

Elsewhere in Latin America:

The new military regime in Honduras set up machine guns in the central plaza of Tegucigalpa, the capital, after it had received reports of plans for anti-government demonstrations by students. A junta headed by Col. Oswaldo Lopez Arrelano ousted the elected government of President Ramo Villeda Morales last Thursday.

In the Dominican Republic, where the elected government was also deposed in a coup, the military government ordered a state of siege after university students battled police in Santo Domingo, the capital. The students attempted to march on the presidential palace to protest the ouster of President Juan D. Bosch. Police fired machine guns, hurled tear gas, and the students retaliated with stones. About 50 students were arrested.

In Brazil, a dispute between workers and guards at a big steel mill resulted in the death of six workmen when police fired on them. The outburst occurred at Acesitas, 200 miles north of Rio de Janeiro. President Joao Goulart, meanwhile, withdrew his request to Congress for a 30-day state of siege to keep order. He said extreme measures were no longer necessary, but most observers believed he backed down because Congress was going to deny him most of the emergency powers he asked for.

Gen. Andrew O'Meara, commander of the United States Southern Command with headquarters at the Panama Canal, said he had canceled a scheduled visit to the United States military mission in Brazil next week because of the political situation there.
