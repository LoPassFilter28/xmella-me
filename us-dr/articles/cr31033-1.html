TEXT OF THE INTERVIEW GIVEN BY THE ITALIAN CONSUL DR. FOLCO ZUGARO, ON FRIDAY, MAY 19, 1953 OVER RADIO STATION WHK

(Testo dell'intervista concessa dal Console d'Italia Dr. Folco Zugaro, Venerdi' 19 Giugno, dalla Stazione Radio WHK)

Le risposte date dal Console vugaro, non richiedono commento di sorta trattandosi di risposte che rispecchiano la nuda e cruda situazione degli argomenti trattati. Vorremmo che i nostri lettori ponderassero attentamente il theto dell'intervista e la facses-nella necessita' di dare spiegazioni concernenti la reale situazione italiana.

La Direzione dell'Araldo e' grata al Console Dr. Folco vugaro per aver messo disposizione dell'Editore la copia integrale dell'intervista stessa.

1ST QUESTION: The tornadoes which hit Cleveland kept the attention of the people away from the Italian election. Do you think that the De Gasperi Government, having slipped off the elections with a slim majority, would be able to insure a stable Government?

ANSWER: If we look into some figures about the last elections, we see that the center parties, though having lost some ground, still hold the support of the strongest part of the Italian public opinion. We can also assume that the only Government possible in our country has to be formed by grouping the center small parties around the Christian Democratic Party. I nfact, the Christian Democrats hold 116 seats in the Senate and 261 seats in the Chamber of Deputie, all alone. Together with their electoral allies, this party holds the majority in both chambers, i.e. 125 seats on a total of 237 in the Senate and 303 on a total of 590 in the Chamber of Deputies. In a very recent statement, Premier De Gasperi affirmed his firm determination to continue his middle course policy of sincere cooperation with the Western Powers, particularly with the United States. I think that this promise will be undoubtedly carried out, because this policy answers to a requet largely felt among the Italian people. Therefore, most of the small parties will feel that it is their duty to give support to the new Government o be formed by Premier De Gasperi. I hink that there is no other solution, looking at the Italian situation with a realistic mind, if we want a firm and stable Government to be established as the results of the great national and international prestige which surrounds Mr. De Gasperi.

2ND QUESTION: What parties were competing in the Elections?

ANSWER: The three major political groups in the last Italian elections were: the center, the extreme right and the extreme left. Each of these three groups was split in several parties, but not all of them were running in coalition. The largest party of the center group was, of course, the Christian Democratic Party (D. C.) and his allies were the Liberal Party (P. L. I.), the Republican Democratic Party (P.R.I.) and the Social Democratic Party (P.S.D.I.).

The extreme right included two parties: the M. S. I., that is to say the New Fascist Party and the Monarchist Party (P. M. I.)

The extreme left included two major parties, the Left Wing Socialist Party (P. S. I.) and the Communist Party.

3RD QUESTION: Do you think that the Monarchist Party thus challenging De Gasperi's attitude, would ask for entering the Government?

ANSWER: Premier De Gasperi himself stated very recently, that he was not going to change his policy by including the Monarchist Party in the Government, and I envisage that we will have a center Government without the Monarchits. This does not mean that, in some matters of great national importance, he minor parties would not give their full support to the Government, hus strengthening the Government itself.

4TH QUESTION: Are you inclined to think that Italy is going to face a situation similar to the one existing in France?

ANSWER: I do no think so, due to the great difference existing between the French and the Italian political scene. First of all, in France the democratic majority fs split among several parties of almost equal strength, which causes present instability of the Government. In Italy, the majority is reached through grouping of small parties around the Christian Democratic Party, which holds the support of some 11 million voters. This makes the difference to our advantage.

5TH QUESTION: Will th eGovernment be compelled to resign if one of its proposals has been rejected by the Parliament?

ANSWER: No, the Government will not have to resign, unless the so-called "question of confidence" is put up along with the proposal involved.

6TH QUESTION: Which are the basic differences between the old Fascist Party and the M. S. I.?

ANSWER: It is very hard to answer this question, because the former party was a Government party, while the newM. S. I. holds, so far, the support of a small part of the Italian public opinion. If I may quote the statements made by the leaders of the party, the main difference is this: The new Facist party claims to accept democratic constitution of Italy and that they will make they will make no attempt to bring ....FI:Doereef:a no attempt to bring back a dictatorship of Italy as we had before the war. Moreover, the old Fascists Party was basically in favour of the Monarchy, whereas the M. S. I. is fundamentally Republican.

7TH QUESTION: It has been said that the financial aid supplied to Italy by this country has been improperly used and that, as a whole, we have waisted our money. What can you say about this?

ANSWER: The statements which have appeared in the American press during the last few months to the effect that the aid so generously given to Italy by the U.S.A., since the war, has been completely waisted, are in myopinion, really and truly unfair, and much too far from the real situation.

Italy is now in better condition (40% better) than she ever was before at any period of her history. Our industrial output is much higher than it was before the war; our railway and general transportation sytems, whic hwere disrupted after the war, are now in the same shape as before the war. In agriculture, we have accomplished in five years more than in the twenty years preceding the war period, by obtaining reclamation of farm areas larger than it ever was done by the previous Government.

8TH QUESTION: Do the Italian people know all about the aid received from this country?

ANSWER: Of course they do. I was well aware of this myself before coming to this country, but not as a result of my being in the diplomatic service. When traveling through Southern Italy, just before leaving for the United States, I recall very well that I would come across, at a distance of ten or twenty miles, roads, bridges, and farm-houses carrying the incription: "This public work has been made possible with American aid" or "The construction of this public work is being made through American Aid". Everybody in Italy is acquainted with the American assistance and, of course, everybody is grateful to this country for it.

9TH QUESTION: Ie that true that, according to some statements made, Italy will not be able o exist as a nation if she does not find a solution to the problem of emigration?

ANSWER: The problem of over-population is one of the most difficult to solve for our Government. In spite of all efforts, such as the improved living standards and the increased output. We still have some two million unemployed persons. This figure has not changed since the end of the war, and this may prove that all we can do is to prevent the increase of unemployment which is caused by the increase of our population, but that we are unable to decrease the unemployment figures. As a matter of fact, this situation existed in all periods of our history. Since 1921, that is after the first World War, the emigration to this country and to South America became subject to restrictions brought about by laws and by other different unfavorable conditions.

Nothing better than the following data may explain the real situation. Here in the United States the density of population is 19.2 persons per square kilometer; in Australia, 1.5; in Brazil, 5.9; in the U.S.S.R, 9; in France, 73; in Spain 55; in Italy 156.

Since the end of World War II, we have tried to improve our emigration to South America (Argentina, Brazil, Venezuela), to Australia, and we have achieved some success, but the number of persons who have migrated is too small, in comparion to our needs to improve our unemployment situation.

This could be done with the help from this country through many different and not sensational measures, some of which have already been suggested to the American Government. I refer to the propoed bill allowing an extra quota of some 100,000 Italians to come into this country within the next two years. I can quote the offer made by the Dominican Republic's representative to the U. N., General Trujillo, to settle in said country 100,000 Italian immigrants an dalso the bill, as it has been discussed, letting Italy and the other less favored countries to dispose of their unused quotas.

10TH QUESTION: How did the "McCarran Act" affect this problem?

ANSWER: The McCarran Act has not endangered very much the Italian quota, insofar as number is concerned. What caused a great disappointment to our country was the fact that we were hoping that the United States would have given us a hand in finding a way to solve this problem. But the quota situation is just as bad now as it was before.

11TH QUESTION: We have heard muc habout the Italian land reform. Could you tell me what has been done in this field?

ANSWER: As you know, the land reform, which has been restricted, so far, to the depressed areas in Italy, (Calabria, Sicily, Apulia, Sardinia, Campania, some parts of Latium and Tuscany and some areas by the Po river) has been carried out by expropriating and dividing among the poorest peasants about four million acres of land, and giving them loans to buy cattle, implements, and to build farm houses, etc.

There is no doubt that the former owners who have been expropriated and paid by the Government have resented the loss of their lands, and have switched, during the last elections, from the center to the right wing parties, which they believe to be more conservative.

In my opinion, it is untimely to state how large a part of the poorest peasants can be gained to he cause of democracy hrough the improvement of their living condition caused by the land reform: It takes time for a poor peasant to become a farmer and you may note that the largest part of the land has been distributed only in '51 and in '52. I think that the good results will show after some years, but I sincerely think—as I know by personal experience—how our peasant is coming from a little town just in the heart of the area which has been affected by the land reform and that in five years from now the government policy in the agricultural field, if it can be pursued, will not only bring a complete change in the living condition of these people but also the most important political result in the political situation of our country.
