Trujillo Foes Wage Radio Nerve War

Enemies of the Dominican dictator, Generalissimo Rafael Trujillo, have opened a war of nerves against him.

Led by Dominican exiles, who seek to unseat the veteran ruler and give his people freedom to choose a new government, barrages of words are being beamed at the Caribbean nation from three directions.

From Cuba, various radio transmitters bombard Generalissimo Trujillo's country with threats and propaganda, some of it billed as originating from rebel radios within the Dominican Republic.

Propaganda Rampart

Other propaganda volleys are fired from Caracas and from Puerto Rico—all aimed at rallying sufficient support to drive the long-ruling generalissimo from his seat.

Listeners to these programs find some of them amazing examples of propaganda warfare. Some depend on rumors or half truths for their effectiveness. Some make their own reports of what is going on inside and outside the Dominican Republic.

False Attack Beamed

One such program pretended to carry one side of a communication between enemies of Trujillo outside and those within his country.

It began with the urgent appeal "Atencion! Atencion!", then repeated a series of messages directed to "rebel forces fighting Trujillo in the Dominican Republic."

One message repeated a report from Puerto Rico that new invasion boats had been sighted along the Dominican North Coast.

Then came a period of well-staged "atmospherics" followed by the announcer's frantic calls to the "rebels" ending with the repeated phrase "Invaders have arrived! Invaders have arrived! Over! Over!" Then came another period of atmospherics from the same sound track.

"Bond" Sales Aid

Such transmissions go on hour after hour. At times Havana stations pick up and repeat what an anti-Trujillo station in Caracas or Puerto Rico has transmitted.

Main efforts of Dominican exiles abroad at this time appears to be concentrated on this "softening up" period. The sale of "bonds" by the Dominican government in exile and frequent benefits of various kinds here and in Venezuela help finance the radio transmissions.

The effect of this campaign on Generalissimo Trujillo is uncertain. It is probable he has been shaken much more severely by the defection of two trusted diplomats—his ambassador to Ecuador and one of his representatives before the United Nations—than by the theatrical and at times frantic propaganda programs of his enemies.
