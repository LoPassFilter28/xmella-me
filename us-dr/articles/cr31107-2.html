The Killing of Heureaux. A Statement from Revolutionists Here. Late President Charged with Government by Terrorizing—A Plea for Jiminez, Who Used to Live Here

Señor Adrianno Grullon, of the firm of Huttinger & Vivig, No. 101 Pearl-st., the representative in the United States of the Santo Domingo revolutionists and formerly the business partner of Señor J. Y. Jiminez, the leader of the insurrection, last night made public the following statement of the reasons for the recent assassination of President Heureaux:

"The assassination of President Heureaux was not an act of revenge for the murder of Caceres's father, but the signal for the beginning of the uprising against the existing government there. Caceres is the cousin of General Horacio Vasquez, and was with him at the time of the shooting. General Vasquez is a member of one of the best families at Moca and was for some years a partner in the house of Cosme Batlle, a firm well known there and former agents of the Clyde Steamship Company. Both gentlemen were well known and bore excellent characters.

"Neither Mr. Caceres nor General Vasquez can be considered a common assassin, as their motives were not personal, but patriotic. They attacked the President in broad daylight in an open space, and not from an ambush of any kind, nor even in the dark, although their little party was outnumbered two to one by the President and his bodyguard, the report to the contrary notwithstanding.

TERRORIZING CHARGED.

"For fourteen years Heureaux has held power by a system of terrorizing; citizens were sent to prison without the slightest process of law and more than three hundred were clandestinely shot, without any trial whatever, or without even being given a reason for their incarceration. Even youth was no safeguard, as was shown by the execution of Erricson, seventeen years old, and for no reason other than that the boy carried a letter addressed by General Luperon. He arrived at Puerta Plata one day and was shot to death at 2 p. m. the same day.

"General Marchena, a widely known and much loved man, was put into prison in Santo Domingo City and kept there for one year 'incomunicado.' One day, without any preliminaries, the Minister of the Interior, who arrived in New-York last Saturday, took him from the prison and placed him on board a warship which sailed to Azua, where, with several of his friends, General Marchena was shot, and this without legal proceeding of any kind. There has been no legal election of any kind since 1889, and then, in order to secure his retention in power, Heureaux had to count more votes from interior provinces than there were actual inhabitants, including men, women and children.

"As the result of this opposition party, whose candidate was Moya, appealed to arms, but after a long struggle was defeated. Since that time few reputable citizens have taken part in the elections, knowing it to be a mere farce. The constitution of Santo Domingo, in common with those of most South and Central American countries, provides that no one shall hold office for more than one term in succession, but this the late President overruled. There is no such thing as a free press in that country; no paper dare publish anything unless favorable to the existing regime. Even here in New-York the Dominicans who are residents dare not give information regarding the politics of their country, fearing that their relatives and interests there would suffer.

"During the war between the United States and Spain Heureaux showed his sympathy for Spain in many acts and put every difficulty possible in the way of Americans and Cubans. His efforts on the side of Spain were so marked and of such assistance to that country that they resulted in the Queen of Spain recognizing them to the extent of a decoration which she sent to him.

"He used every effort to discredit the insurrectionists by proclaiming that Jiminez intended to sell the country to the Yankees, and if this were done and they fell into the hands of the United States they would be treated like slaves. This naturally gained some credence among the ignorant people of the island, the number of which has steadily increased in view of Heureaux's policy to discourage education of every sort. He deliberately closed the only high school in the island and drove away Eugenio Hostos, a renowned educator and the founder of the high school.

JIMINEZ KNOWN HERE.

"Mr. Jiminez was a merchant doing business in this city for many years, and his character as an honorable man is well known. He is neither a soldier nor a politician; his one desire is the good of his country, and, recognizing that there is no permanent good to be expected from military government, it is his wish and purpose to establish a civil one.

"He desires the most friendly relations with the United States, and there is not the slightest fear that the insurrectionists will interfere in any way with the property of foreigners on the island. Notwithstanding the many revolutions which have taken place in Santo Domingo since it attained its independence, in no instance has either life been taken or property molested of any foreigners, with the single exception of the action of Heureaux, who opened the vaults of the French bank because of a personal quarrel with one of its directors. Women and children are safe at all times, as the Dominicans never stoop to war with the helpless, nor do they ever sack or pillage, but fight for what they believe to be right in the open, as men and soldiers should do.

"Mr. Jiminez and General Gomez are warm personal friends, in fact, both reside in the same house in Havana. As Heureaux was a usurper, the Vice-President is equally so, but bloodshed and insurrection would cease at once if the latter would announce that a legal, public election would take place on August 16, which is the independence day of the country.

"The entire country is aroused to the necessity of a change to a government where life is secure, and it recognizes that the former Vice-President and the present pretending President is but one of the accomplices of Heureaux, and as such has no confidence in him.

"There is but one man who at this time represents the people and their best interests, and that is Jiminez, and if an opportunity is given to the people they will prove it by their ballots. But if none is afforded in that way then force will be used."
