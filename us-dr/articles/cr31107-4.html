The Tribune prints part of a letter written in defense of General Gomez: in it, he strove to obtain independence for the island for thirty years, and his efforts have been little recognized; the sweat on the horse is hardly dry when the rage of those who did not dare to take the field by his side falls upon him; insults will not affect him more than if they were directed against the Czar of Russia by a person with whom he had no connecting links; what, however, is irritating and painful, and has no logical explanation, is the desire to injure him, to despise the Dominican, to insult the stranger who believes he has done much for Cuba — they would insult the old Dominican Republic, which has brilliantly served the Cuban cause more than any Latin people, with money, love and blood; the Dominicans are called savages because their President was killed by the angered hands of vengeance — why then not call other nations savages on account of assassinations committed in them? In this connection the writer cites the instances of the killing of President Garfield of the United States, Señor Canovas del Castillo, the Spanish Premier, and President Carnot, of the French Republic; and, continuing, the writer says that some Cubans think they can get at him by means of this unfortunate occurrence, accusing him of aspiring to fill the place made vacant by the act of the assassin, forgetting that he aspires still higher; and the history of his life proves that he prefers liberating those writing things against him — if Cuba cannot see a position to offer him after all his efforts, how could he see one in Santo Domingo?El Tribune publica parte de una carta escrita en defensa del general Gómez: en ella, él se esforzó por obtener la independencia de la isla por treinta años, y sus esfuerzos han sido poco reconocidos; el sudor en el caballo apenas está seco cuando la rabia de aquellos que no se atrevieron a tomar el campo a su lado cae sobre él; los insultos no lo afectarán más que si fueran dirigidos contra el Czar de Rusia por una persona con quien él no tuviera lazos de conexión; lo que, sin embargo, es irritante y doloroso, y no tiene explicación lógica, es el deseo de herirlo, de despreciar al dominicano, de insultar al extraño que cree que ha hecho mucho por Cuba — insultarían a la vieja República Dominicana, que ha servido brillantemente la causa cubana más que ningún pueblo latino, con dinero, amor y sangre; los dominicanos son llamados salvajes porque su Presidente fue muerto por las manos enojadas de la venganza — ¿por qué entonces no llamar salvajes a otras naciones por razón de asesinatos cometidos en ellas? En esta conexión el escritor cita los casos de la muerte del presidente Garfield de los Estados Unidos, el señor Cánovas del Castillo, el premier español, y el presidente Carnot, de la República Francesa; y, continuando, el escritor dice que algunos cubanos piensan que pueden llegar a él por medio de esta ocurrencia desafortunada, acusándolo de aspirar a llenar el lugar vacante por el acto del asesino, olvidando que él aspira aún más alto; y la historia de su vida prueba que él prefiere libertar a aquellos que escriben cosas contra él — si Cuba no puede ver una posición que ofrecerle después de todos sus esfuerzos, ¿cómo podría él ver una en Santo Domingo?

Havana, Aug. 1.—General Gomez's secretary has written a letter to "La Lucha," saying:La Habana, 1 de agosto.—El secretario del general Gómez ha escrito una carta a «La Lucha», diciendo:

Gomez is now passing through a period of disagreeable transition. He has endeavored to obtain independence for the island for thirty years, and his efforts have been little recognized. The sweat on the horse is hardly dry when the rage of those who did not dare to take the field by his side falls upon him. Insults will not affect him more than if they were directed against the Czar of Russia by a person with whom he had no connecting links. What, however, is irritating and painful, and has no logical explanation, is the desire to injure him, to despise the Dominican, to insult the stranger who believes he has done much for Cuba. They would insult the old Dominican Republic which has brilliantly served the Cuban cause more than any Latin people with money, love and blood. The Dominicans are called savages because their President was killed by the angered hands of vengeance; why then not call other nations savages on account of assassinations committed in them?Gómez atraviesa ahora un período de desagradable transición.
