Hoover's message was read at the Capitol soon after a dispatch to the State Department announced the clash at Aux Cayes.

In the House it was immediately referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.

Chairman Stephen G. Porter of the House Foreign Relations Committee said he would prepare a resolution appropriating the $50,000 which Hoover requested for a commission to investigate the Haitian situation.

The House is not organized, however, and Porter is without a committee. This situation can not be remedied before Wednesday and so the resolution can not be taken up until next Friday.

President's Message

President Hoover's message said:

"In my message to Congress of the third insant I indicated my concern as to the future of our policies in Haiti. I stated that we have there about 700 Marines, and that we are confronted with a difficult problem, the solution of which is still obscure. I further stated that if Congress approves I shall dispatch a commission to Haiti to review and study the matter in an endeavor to arrive at some more definite polisy than at present.

"Our representatives in Haiti have shown great ability and devotion, and have accomplished signal results in improvement of the material condition of that people. Yet our experience has revealed more clearly than was seen at first the difficulties of the problem, and the entire situation should be reviewed in the light of this experience.

Emphasize Need

"Since the dispatch of my message disturbances in Haiti emphasize the importance of such an investigation and determination of national policies in the immediate futuer.

"The students at the Agricultural School at Damien went on a strike on Oct. 31st as a protest against a new policy of the Haitian government. The Haitian government had heretofore allotted $10,000 per annum to this school for scholarships, but this year it withheld $2000 of the appropriation in order to make it possible for needy students to perform practical school work on the grounds.

"Sympathetic strikes were subsequently declared in the medical and law schools. President Borno appointed a committee of Haitians to inquire into the matter and it seemed probable at the time that recommendations presented by this committee and accepted by the authorities would adjust the difficulty. Unfortunately, advantage was taken of the situation by various agencies to foment disturbances against the Haitian administration, and on Dec. 3 the American High Commissioner reported that the strike movement had spread thruout the country and that it was feared that the Haitian employes of the departments under American treaty officials might become involved.

Abandoned Work

"On Dec. 4 customs house employes at Port Au Prince abandoned their work in a disorderly manner and crowds have gathered in Port Au Prince. At the same time there were reported demonstrations by crowds at Cape Haitian in sympathy with the disturbance in Port Au Prince. The American High Commissioner reported that on the morning of Dec. 4 it was feared that disorderly conditions would arise at Aux Cayes and similar disturbances were possible at other places.

"The High Commissioner has asked that additional Marines be in readiness to make sure that if the situation becomes serious American lives will be protected, and the force he has suggested has been ordered dispatched for that purpose.

"I feel that it is most desirable that the commission mentioned in my message of Dec. 3 be constituted and sent to Haiti without delay and I, therefore, request the Congress to authorize the immediate sending of such a commission and to appropriate for this purpose $50,000. It is my intention to include one or two members from each House of Congress on this commission."

273 Marines, 4 Officers

Now at Hampton Roads

A company of 273 Marines and four officers was to arrive at Hampton Roads, Va., early today from the Marine base at Quantico, to await further orders from the President.

The order for movement of troops last night came after Brig. Gen. John H. Russell, American high commissioner, had informed Secretary Stimson of the grave situation at Aux Cayes, a coffee port, where 25 Americans reside.

3000 White Foreigners

There are about 3000 white foreigners in the republic which shares an island with the Dominican republic. Marines landed in Haiti July 26, 1915, two hours after President Sam was dragged from the French legation by a mob and literally torn to pieces. The funeral of 200 political prisoners who had been massacred had just ended when Sam was lynched.

Agreed to Aid Government

Under a treaty ratified in 1926 the U. S. agreed to assist Haiti establish orderly government. An American commissioner was appointed and an American financial adviser. Arthur C. Millspaugh, financial advisor, resigned last spring after an argument with High Commissioner Russel. The State Department has refused to reveal the source of the disagreement.

Martial law, technically in effect since Marines landed in 1915, was invoked and, altho Russel feared spread of the disturbance, he reported the invocation had an excellent effect.
