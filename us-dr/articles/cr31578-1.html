By the Way—

—Beth Blaine

CUIDAD TRUJILLO, Dominican Republic: The first secretary of the United States Legation and Mrs. Edward Lawton have a charming and informal way of entertaining here (in addition to the delightful small dinners they give). They charter a small launch, gather a few congenial friends, and take them up the beautiful Yabacao River to see the lovely interior country and have a picnic supper.

The party usually starts at 4:30, in time to see the sunset and then home by the light of the moon. There is no twilight in the tropics—only a magnificent swiftly dying sun, whose flaming glory is gone almost in a matter of moments—then dusk and the moon and the stars. For this reason, it's important to start on time, or the darkness overtakes one before scarcely any of the picturesque river country has been seen. Twice we were asked to go on such excursions, and both times, just before we were about to start, it poured!

Now the tropical rains, like their sunsets, are short-lived, and after half an hour or at most an hour—the sky is clear and blue again, the gardens fresh and green and more fragrant than ever—but the delay of even an hour on the boat trip made all the difference, so both times we had to give up the idea of the river and have our picnic at the Lawtons' house. It turned out to be great fun, just the same, with a huge thermos of chilled cocktails, and a bulging picnic basket of all sorts of good things to eat.

The Lawtons temporarily are occupying the house of the second secretary of the legation, Mr. McEnelly, who is on leave in the States with his mother. They will remain there until the house they have just leased nearby is freshly painted and tiled and ready for them. All the houses here, by the way, have tiled floors—which are much cooler and more practical for this climate and most attractive.

The only wooden floor we've seen here is the beautiful and ornate one in the formal drawing room of our Legation. Also no one here uses curtains, for the very obvious reason that Venetian blinds or a sort of shutter (that is very like our American Venetian blinds, only it's permanently attached to the window) are far cooler and more in keeping with the decor of the houses.

But to go back to the Lawtons. They came to the Dominican Republic from Cuba recently. It was Bob Joyce who took Ted Lawton's place at the United States Embassy in Havana—in fact, the Joyces' arrival overlapped the Lawtons' departure one day and night—long enough to have a group of the Lawtons' friends give them a farewell party that was also a "welcome to Cuba" party for Jane and Bob Joyce.

They (the Lawtons) knew Washington well and have many friends there. They asked especially about Mr. and Mrs. Hugh Cumming, jr., and Mr. and Mrs. Lewis Clark—also told us they saw the Ed Reeds when they went to Cuba on vacation this past winter. (The Reeds themselves were stationed in Havana some years ago.) They (again the Lawtons) were in Geneva when the late Prentiss Gilbert and his attractive wife, Charlotte, were there and told us that Charlotte has recently gone back to Washington to live, which will be a delight to her many friends.

They also knew the Ralph Millers well, since all were stationed in Havana together. The Millers are, of course, still in Havana and are apparently so delighted with Virginia after several summers on leave at Hot Springs that they've bought an old farm just outside of Charlottesville and are doing it over.

* * * * *

This will be our last column on the Dominican Republic, as we leave tonight on the S. S. Coamo, sailing from Cuidad Trujillo. We will be back in Washington just about the time this goes to press.

Despite the fact that every one writes that Washington is now in the full bloom of her spring beauty, and that we will be thrilled to be "home" again—it is with a very definite regret that we leave Santo Domingo and all its quaint loveliness and its charming people, its balmy climate and its gay, romantic charm.

It is not hard to understand, once having been here, why that pioneer of travelers, Columbus, requested that his remains rest here and why the Dominican Republic is always referred to as "the land Columbus loved best."
