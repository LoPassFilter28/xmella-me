News - And - Views Trujillo vs. Stalin. Invitation to San Domingo. A Project That Failed. More Protests.

I see where Generalissimo Rafael Leonidas Trujillo of the Dominican Republic, in an interview with press correspondents upon his arrival in New York to attend the United Nations General Assembly, said a) he would bring the issue of anti-Semitism behind the Iron Curtain before the forthcoming session of the Security Council and b) that he was prepared to throw open the doors of the Dominican Republic to the "non-Communist Jewish refugees from behind the Iron Curtain."

General Trujillo is a colorful figure. For years he has been the source of all power in his native land. He has managed to do for his country what Washington did not succeed in doing for his, namely, to give, during his own lifetime, his name to the capital of his nation. Moreover, you cannot make a step in the Dominican capital without running smack into one of the many statues erected in honor of the Generalissimo. In addition, the university and the biggest hotel in the country bear his name. When I was in Trujillo City, some ten years ago, the Generalissimo was the undisputed ruler of the country; now it's his brother who is exercising supreme authority.

If Israel, which has been directly maligned by the Soviet Government along with the Zionist movement, will agree that Trujillo is the man to raise the issue of red anti-Semitism before the United Nations no one, I'm sure, no one will object. For red anti-Semitism is a matter of international concern; it should be probed into and exposed, and, if possible, stopped, or punished, or both. Surely the United Nations is the place for the conscience of mankind to speak out against such a monstrous wrong as the persecution of Jews in the countries behind the Iron Curtain.

Whether the Dominican Republic is the place for the Jewish refugees from the countries behind the Iron Curtain, if and when they manage to escape from their persecutors, is a matter open to doubt. It is questionable, whether with all the difficulties attending their absorption, the Jewish refugees from the Soviet Union and the satellite countries would not be better off in Israel than in the Dominican Republic. A number of years ago, following a fine gesture by Generalissimo Trujillo in inviting Jews to come to his country, a substantial number of Jewish refugees came to San Domingo prepared to take their place in a large Jewish settlement scheme. The colony of Sosua was heralded as a project holding out great promise to the Jews all over the world in search of a home. However, the promise has not been fulfilled. For reasons that have to do with the climate as well as with the cultural and economic conditions in the country, the great project has not succeeded.

I doubt whether wholesale settlement of Jews in the Dominican Republic can be carried out under the present conditions. The fact is, our chief worry these days is not so much the finding of a place of refuge for the Jews living in the countries behind the Iron Curtain as is the formulation of a feasible plan for getting them out. For Stalin is not only determined to embitter the lives of the Jews who have the misfortune to live under his rule, but is disinclined to let the Jews go. Until the Soviet Union and its satellites permit the Jews to leave the country, the talk about finding a domicile for them is largely academic. And so, for that matter, may the protests against Soviet anti-Semitism go unheeded, though none of us is free from doing what we can to arouse the conscience of the world against the anti-Jewish propaganda now going on behind the Iron Curtain.
