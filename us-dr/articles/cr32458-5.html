An official Dominican statement defends the independence of the nation's courts, asserting that magistrates render sovereign decisions without outside reproach or interference. The text cites principles proclaimed by Generalissimo Trujillo during an address to judicial magistrates in Santiago in 1933, warning that yielding to outside interests leads to defection, prevarication, or abuse of power. The passage also highlights Trujillo's address on February [illegible], 1959, at the inauguration of the Palace of Justice, where he and President Hector Bienvenido Trujillo Molina were awarded medals of the Order of Judicial Merit, reiterating his commitment to an independent judicial order in the service of the public.Una declaración oficial dominicana defiende la independencia de los tribunales del país, afirmando que los magistrados dictan decisiones soberanas sin reproches ni interferencias externas. Communist Infiltration Has Been Stopped In The Dominican Republic

The Dominican Republic Attorney General's Report to his Nation's Congress Answers the Charges of Misinformed Individuals and Accounts for both the Actual Numbers Involved and the Careful Judicial Procedure Followed in the Recent Sentencing (and in many Cases, the Reduction of Sentences) of Those Convicted of Terrorist Crimes Against the State and the Destruction of Public and Private Property. He also names those Found Innocent, and consequently, Released by the Dominican Courts.

Honorable Members of the Dominican Congress:

I am appearing before this legislative body, pursuant to the provisions of article 38, paragraph 18 of the Constitution of the Dominican Republic, after receiving authorization from the Executive Power, in order to reply, in my capacity of Secretary of State for Justice (Attorney General), to the questions put to me following the unqualified allegations contained in the letter sent by some American citizens, over the signature of Mr. Norman Thomas, misled in their good faith to the U. S. Ambassador at the Organization of American States, under date of March 1 of this year in order to "denounce" the existence, in the Dominican Republic, of an alleged situation of unrest and political uneasiness which endangers the peace of the Hemisphere.

I categorically refute this allegation and am in a position to affirm that the statements made in said denunciation, namely that there are over 1,500 prisoners in the jails of the State, as a consequence of the persecutions for terrorist deeds which were discovered by the Military Intelligence Service, do not correspond to facts. In reality the total number of the accused amounts to 222 persons, 121 of whom have been tried, while 101 await trial; the respective causes will become known in the course of this week. Of those tried so far, 11 were released and the others had been condemned to sentences varying between 15, 10, 5, and 2 years.

The denunciations referred to are undoubtedly intended to render plausible the unfounded reports published in a section of the American press by some left-wing journalists who were here recently.

The American journalists who visit our country should compare the decent treatment they receive in our country with the methods employed against them by the Police of Fidel Castro in Cuba, who arrests them, molests and humiliates them, as can be illustrated by the example of what has just happened, in Havana, to three staff members of the Associated Press who, in the exercise of their journalistic functions, endeavored to collect data in connection with the explosion of the French freight boat at one of the piers of said country.

The Court has performed its duty by applying the laws of the Republic calling for punishment of acts of anarchy and terrorism perpetrated by various Dominicans whose goal it was to carry through assaults against the security of the State, to destroy public and private property, and to massacre thousands of innocent people, using for this purpose numerous bombs of different types and makes, as the ones which you see here, forming part of a quantity that exceeds 500 units, said arms were in possession of the evildoers who were thrown in prison and submitted to legal action; they admitted their crimes and then signed their confessions before Dr. Gustavo A. Latour Batlle, Public Prosecutor of said trials, which confessions they later on ratified in connection with the cases which were heard in the penal Chambers of the Lower Court of the National District, in the presence of the public and of members of their own families who attended the trials.

It is pointed out, Honorable members of Congress, that the Military Intelligence Service which so efficiently cooperates in the discovery of all acts that tend to disturb the public peace in the country, had for some time suspected the subversive activities of the conspirators, enemies of the public order which the country has enjoyed since 1930, thanks to the outstanding vigilance of the Illustrious Republican, Generalissimo Rafael Leonidas Trujillo Molina, Benefactor of the Country and Father of the New Country, and which efforts are, in an exemplary manner, backed by Generalissimo Hector Bienvenido Trujillo Molina, Honorable President of the Republic, both Illustrious excellencies who take interest in the tranquility of the Dominican family and the survival of the State. Nevertheless said Military Intelligence Service abstained from any action before securing irrefutable proof of the communist and terrorist plot, such as the bombs and infernal machines which you can see here, many of which have been built with foreign money emanating from international communism, which these accused persons who have been sentenced had received, as well as the others who will be sentenced in due course by the Courts of the Republic.

As these followers of Moscow now appear before us wrapped in the mantle of piety and of a fake humanitarian sentimentalism, trying to hide their cruelties, it is useful to remind them, in an interrogative manner of the following:

Was it not an act of anarchism alone that caused the fire in the building of the Director of the Budget, with great losses for the Dominican State?

Was the fire in the Public Works Building in this city not caused by an act of terrorism alone?

Was the fire in the "Camara de Cuentas" building not an act of anarchism?

Was it not absolute lack of any humanitarian feeling which induced the communists to place a bomb in the Model Market of this city, killing children and innocent people?

Was not the attitude of some foreign communist leaders, living in this country, and abusing their investitures, the hospitality and the assistance which this Government had offered them, and who, with the most brazen shamelessness were teaching young teenagers the method of manufacturing these infernal machines intended to spread terror and desolation in the Dominican home—were these not criminal and despicable acts of anarchism against the elemental human feelings and the principles of Christianity?

Was not the fire in the sumptuous Palace of Justice, in the hall assigned for the deliberations of the Magistrates of the Supreme Court of Justice, and whose repair caused considerable expenses to the National Treasury an act of anarchism and communism?

Was it not terrorism and absolute lack of elemental patriotic feelings that caused some malefactors to plan the total destruction, by fire, of the Atlantic city of Puerto Plata, vandalism which started with fire in the offices of the Dominican Social Security Building of that city and in other premises where numerous incendiary bombs were placed, which bombs were at once picked up by the Military Intelligence Service?

What other measures could the Government authority take but seize the evildoers, confiscate the infernal machines and the materials made on same and submit the malefactors to the action of the justice of our Courts?

We must thank God for having inspired the representatives of the Military Intelligence Service to seize in time the evildoers who disregarded the most elemental rules of Christian ethics, without a ray of light having revealed the darkness of their plans, as they were determined to spread chaos, terrorism, and anarchy throughout the country, bringing about death and destruction.

I am in a position to prove to you, based on the documents extracted in the file which I am herewith submitting to you for more ample information, that in this reprehensible conspiration the Dominican Justice has been able, as always, to live up to the letter of the law and to apply it with absolute liberty of action.

Bearing in mind the gravity of the plot organized by the aforementioned wrongdoers to subvert the order and the institutions of the country, the judges of the penal chambers of the Lower Court of this judicial district, in charge of the proceedings, condemned those accused to thirty years of hard labor each, among other punishments pursuant to the provisions of the Penal Code and under application of the same wise security laws which you have prepared.

These condemnatory judgements of thirty years of hard labor to each of the accused, seem too severe to those individuals not having a sufficient knowledge of the law in order to judge the gravity of the cases and to place them within the context of the penal laws which sanction them. All of the accused have, during the course of frequent illegal meetings, organized a series of secret operations, criminal in character, against the external and internal security of the country.

The members of a plot of this kind, pursuant to the governing statutes of Article 90 of the Penal Code, are considered co-authors of the plot and equally guilty in the eyes of the law, even though their individual actions may not involve the perpetration or planning of a crime. According to an eminent French criminal lawyer, the participation in a plot is, "like a choral mass in which everyone contributes individually in order to perform and perfect the work." In the case under discussion, any slight participation in an act of this kind is sufficient to constitute a definite action. The infraction is so subtle that the unaccepted proposal is punishable, in crimes against the external security of the country as well as against its internal security. This infers that not to provide any information constitutes a crime, because the failure to denounce such an act is, in a certain sense, comparable to the approval of treason. Thus it is punishable to keep silent about the plot's existence.

As Secretary of Justice, and as a citizen unswervingly dedicated to the political policies of the Illustrious Leader of the Dominican people since 1930, and who has not hesitated and will never hesitate to assume vigorously all responsibilities that may be necessary in the defense of the postulates of the aforementioned political policies and of this era, I have been particularly sensitive to the profound social disturbance experienced by the Dominican people.

This disturbance was caused by the joint and cooperative action of those elements divorced from the concept of patriotism and lacking the noble sentiments of gratitude and loyalty which should be found in human beings, virtues which Goethe characterized as being essential to mankind and on a higher plane than culture. Because of the social and political conditions under which the majority of the accused have lived, they have unfortunately forgotten that they are the product, either direct or indirect, of Generalissimo Trujillo's benevolence, which when not bestowed upon them personally, was bestowed upon their parents and relatives, to whom the Generalissimo liberally gave honors and favors in the form of very high public offices.

After the judges of the Lower Court sentenced said malefactors to 30 years of hard labor, and they later expressed interest in making use of the right to appeal which our Constitution offers them, they were granted the most ample facilities to file said appeals, as can be seen from the record which I am herewith submitting for the purpose of fully clarifying this matter.

When the Court of Appeal of this Department received said appeals it pronounced a sentence under date of February 29, 1960 based on the appeal filed against the sentence of the Second Penal Chamber of the Lower Court of the National District, dated February 2, 1960, by virtue of which it CHANGED THE INTERPRETATION GIVEN TO THE FACTS by the Judge of the Lower Court, and acting by contrary order, it told the condemned appellants DR. RAFAEL FRANCISCO BONNELLY BATLLE, DR. JOSE ANTONIO FERNANDEZ CAMINERO and their accomplices, those complete names are shown in the sentence referred to: "You are accused of having plotted with foreigners to excite the citizens, in considerable number, to arm themselves against the legally constituted authorities, to use vehicles, explosives and implements, with the intentional aim of disturbing the public peace and the order of the State, by virtue of our Penal Code and articles 1 and 2 of the law No. 483 dated April 5, 1933, compiled and prepared by the National Congress of the Republic." Said accused and appellants against the sentence which condemned them to 30 years of hard labor, were sentenced to FIFTEEN (15) years of hard labor by making use of the principle of non-cumulation of penalties, and based on clause 2 of art. 463 of the Penal Code.

By the same decision of the Court of Appeal the accused, RAFAEL EMILIO GONZALEZ PIMENTAL, HUGO CESAR QUEZADA ALMANZAR y LISANDRO ANTONIO MACARRULLA were pronounced guilty of the crime of forming single groups of three persons, in order to excite the citizens to arm themselves against the legally constituted authorities to commit hostile acts against the Government, and to disturb the public peace and the order of the State. These three appellants were condemned to TEN (10) years of hard labor, making use of the principle of non-cumulation of penalties, based on clause 2 of art. 463 of the Penal Code.

And with regard to JOSE MIGUEL LEYBA PEREZ, ADOLFO ALEJANDRO FRANCO BRITO and their accomplices, they were pronounced guilty of the crime provided for in articles 87 and 90 of the Penal Code, and condemned to FIVE (5) years of imprisonment.

With regard to the appeals filed by the accused whose names figure in the sentences of the First Penal Chamber of the Lower Court of the National District, dated February 8, 1960, said Court of Appeal, by its sentence of March 3, 1960 which likewise is contained in the records, changed the interpretation given the facts by the Judges of the Lower Court, which led them to impose a 30 years sentence to hard labor against the appellants in question, modifying the sentence against which an appeal was made, and acting by its own authority it pronounced the accused ROLANDO ANTISTENES ALVAREZ ABREU (a) EL HURON, DR. ALFREDO PARRA BEATO and accomplices, GUILTY OF CRIMES against the legally constituted authorities by holding meetings and using explosive implements with the deliberate aim of disturbing the public peace and the order of the State, making use of the principle of non-cumulation of penalties, it condemned them to TEN (10) years hard labor. The same decision of the Court of Appeals found the accused JOSE ANTONIO HURTADO PAULINO (a) DANTON, VINICIO RAFAEL GRISANTI ISIDOR and their accomplices, guilty of the crime of exciting the citizens to arm against the legally constituted authorities, in order to disturb the public peace; and making use of the principle of non-cumulation of sentences and based on art. 463 of the Penal Code, it condemned them to FIVE (5) years of hard labor.

Furthermore, said second degree sentence found the accused CESAR LEOPOLDO DE LOS SANTOS ALMONTE, RAFAEL OCTAVIO SILVERIO MESSON (a) PELLITO and accomplices, guilty of the crime provided for in articles 87 and 90 of the Penal Code, because of their having plotted to arm themselves against the legally constituted authority, and condemned them to TWO (2) years of imprisonment.

And finally, said sentence cleared the accused: RAFAEL ANTONIO BAEZ PEREZ, (a) CUCHUCHO PINDARO BOBEA PRANDY, DIEGO CASANOVAS GARRIDO, JOSE CADET MENA, ARCADIO ENCARNACION PALMER, FRANCISCO ANTONIO SALDANA MEDRANO, (a) TONO LIC RAFAEL ALBUQUERQUE ZAYAS BAZAN, (a) CHICHI, CARLOS SULLY, BONNELLY VALVERDE, RAMON CACERES TRONCOSO, LUIS BELTRAN PEREZ ESPINOZO and their accomplices

Only a deep humanitarian interest could have led the Judges of the Court of Appeal to revoke the sentences appealed, and to grant a considerable reduction of the penalties imposed on the accused by the Judges of the lower court, clearing an important group of the accused.

The judicial decisions prove how much the inherent principle of liberty and independence of the judicial in its sacred decisions are respected in the Dominican Republic, in the present era of Trujillo. What an immense difference there is between the democratic life of the Dominican people in this era and the harrowing agony which the Cuba of Fidel Castro and the Venezuela of Betancourt are suffering, countries where justice is trampeled by the communist dictatorship that imposes its will in said sister republics, and where the courts continuously pronounce death sentences against the citizens of the nations in question, who had the civic courage of voicing their opposition against these pernicious regimes which, ignoring all Christian principles and the rights of men, daily stain the execution walls with innocent blood. Yet, the Court of Appeal of this Department, basing itself on the principles of justice, in this shiny and illuminating era of the Dominican people, so free and generous, has considerably reduced the 30 year sentences imposed by the Judges of the Lower Court on many accused and appellants, and at the same time cleared another important group sentenced to equal penalties, without anybody having reproached the attitude of the magistrates or interfered in their sovereign decisions. This constitutes a fine example of the respect and the atmosphere of security in which justice functions, whose guiding principles were proclaimed by Generalissimo Trujillo, for the moral redemption of the Dominican people, when he stated in referring to the magistrates of the judicial branch assembled in Santiago in the course of an important meeting in 1933, that "Nothing and nobody may influence your dignity and responsibility; seeing that any service rendered to outside interests or passions, will lead you to the censurable sin of defection, prevarication, or abuse of power, which, in my opinion, are the most brutal forms of social treason", likewise compare the statement made by the Illustrious Leader of the Destinies of the Country in his memorable speech on February 26, 1959, on the occasion of the inauguration of the splendid Palace of Justice, when he, together with Generalissimo Hector Bienvenido Trujillo Molina, Hon. President of the Republic, were awarded the medals of the Order of Judicial Merit, and in making his reference to justice he stated: "The moral interest I have always had in the judicial branch of our government system, in my desire that it may achieve the highest possible perfection, the true expression of a supreme order of justice in the service of the public."

The reason I have made free to refer to these judicial decisions which are so different and independent from each other is because they offer a true example of genuine democracy that fortifies the principles of the Dominican justice, which is based on the free judgment of the magistrates, even in matters of such a serious and dangerous nature as are all the facts and circumstances relative to national safety and "which are so severely punished in all other civilized nations", as the recent decision of the Spanish Cabinet has shown, which, on the 4th of this month, approved the death sentence pronounced against one of the terrorists accused before the Madrid Courts for simple complicity in some dynamite attacks which took place in that city last month. The same sources stated that the Cabinet which met on the 5th, in the evening, under the chairmanship of Generalissimo Francisco Franco, confirmed to life-imprisonment the sentence which was pronounced against the other accused party.

The parties accused in this country, who were about to commit the same attacks as those carried through by the Spanish terrorists, namely to dynamite public and private property, setting fire to them, assassinate numerous innocent people with their infernal machines, derived the benefit of the non-existence of the death penalty in this country, as the latter is considered to be contrary to the humanitarian and Christian principles of the Dominican people. Contrary to what happens in other civilized countries where the laws permit that prisoners condemned to death be executed, in our country the longest sentence is 30 years of hard labor, sentence which had first been imposed on the parties accused When they filed their appeals to the Court of Appeal the latter, chiefly out of humanitarian reasons, it is assumed, considerably modified the sentences which were imposed, reducing the penalties of a considerable group of the accused, and condemning another group to up to 2 years in prison, whilst, at the same time, clearing an important number of appellants

These decisions of our Courts should prove the humanitarian growth of the latter to the ingenious citizens of the U. S. A., who by invoking violations of imaginary "human rights". try to justify an absurd protest before the Organization of American States, because of the safety measures taken by the Dominican Government, in face of the activities of a gang of malefactors, engaged in subverting our Republican institutions, in order to be able to introduce communism into the country

The U. S. citizens who signed this protest are palpable victims of the violent and unwarranted anti-Dominican propaganda machine operating outside the Dominican Republic, which maliciously disregards our socio-political evolution, trying to ignore the great transformations which have been carried through both in the moral, cultural, educational, religious, and humanitarian fields on behalf of the Dominican people, by its authentic guide, Generalissimo Trujillo, Benefactor of the Country and Father of the New Motherland.

Mario Abreu Penzo,

Secretary of State for Justice

(Dominican Republic Attorney General)

Ciudad, Trujillo, D. R.
