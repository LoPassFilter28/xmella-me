2 Nations Renew Probe Of De Galindez Mystery

By the Associated Press

The United States and the Dominican Republic are working together to try to solve a bizarre 6-year-old mystery that erupted into charges of international kidnaping and assassination.

The center of the cloak-and-dagger intrigue is Dr. Jesus de Galindez, who stepped through a New York subway into oblivion on a March night in 1956 after writing a book to expose the brutalities of Gen. Rafael Trujillo's iron-fisted dictatorship in the Dominican Republic.

Foes of Gen. Trujillo contended the dictator had reached into the heart of Manhattan to eliminate an enemy. There were stories that the professor, who was teaching international law at Columbia University, was drugged and flown to the Caribbean country by an American pilot who also disappeared.

Charges Heatedly Denied

Gen. Trujillo heatedly denied the charges and there were hearings and investigations. But no light was shed on Dr. de Galindez' disappearance, nor was he ever reported seen alive again.

While Gen. Trujillo was alive United States investigators looking into the case got no co-operation from Dominican Republic authorities.

Gen. Trujillo was assassinated in May, 1961, and his regime of terror ended soon afterward. The new government is anxious to solve the De Galindez case to help "overcome the bad name" the country got under Gen. Trujillo, a State Department source said last night.

For several months, he said, United States and Dominican Republic investigators have been checking through long-locked files and questioning members of Gen. Trujillo's intelligence police now held in prison.

Linked to the professor's disappearance was an American free-lance pilot, Gerald Murphy.

Pilot Vanishes

He vanised in the Dominican Republic December 3, 1956, after getting a job as a co-pilot on the Dominican Airlines.

The Dominican government said Mr. Murphy got into a fight with another pilot on the airline, was knocked into a shark-infested bay, and that the pilot, Octavio de la Maza, hanged himself in remorse in his prison cell.

But Trujillo foes alleged that Mr. Murphy piloted a plane carrying Dr. de Galindez to face the general's wrath and was later killed to silence him.
