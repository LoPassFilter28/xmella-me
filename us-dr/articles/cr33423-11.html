Original in English.Original en inglés.

THE ST. DOMINGO LOAN.

Particulars of the Contract—How It is to be Met—Views of Parties Concerning It.

A flysheet containing an exposition and a condemnation of the loan recently entered into by the government of St. Domingo has been extensively circulated in St. Domingo city and other towns of the republic. A liberal translation of this document follows:—

The English papers contain an account of the negotiation of a loan between this republic and British bankers of the nominal value of £757,700 sterling, on the following basis:—

Seventy pounds per cent discount.

Six per cent annual interest for the capital.

Four per cent for the liquidation of the loan.

The whole is to be paid in twenty-five years. The guarantees upon which this loan is raised are these:—The hypothecation of the entries of certain custom houses and the exportation dues of the guano of Alta Vela, the coal mines and mahogany of Samana and the other mines of the republic. The condition is that the loan shall be applied to the construction of railroads and the improvement of the highways (carreteras) generally. It is intended in this document to point out the impracticability of this negotiation.

The nominal capital of £757,700, calculating the maximum at 480 cents to the pound sterling, amounts to $3,636,960

Of this sum there must be deducted—For discount...... $2,545,872

For interest, first year, six per cent...... 218,217

For sinking fund, four per cent...... 145,478

For commission, four per cent...... 145,478

For freight, insurance, charges, &c...... —

$3,055,046

Difference...... $581,913

Out of the sum of $3,636,960 for which the republic negotiates it receives only $581,913, without including charges for freight, printing bonds, &c.

For this sum of $581,913, representing a nominal capital of $3,636,960, the republic pledges itself to pay annually during a period of twenty-five years the sum of $363,696, in this manner:—

Six per cent interest...... $218,217

Four per cent sinking fund...... 145,478

Total...... $363,696

This sum accumulates during twenty-five years to the enormous amount of $9,402,400.

It is evident from this that for $3,636,960 loaned to the republic it is required to pay in twenty-five years $9,402,400, representing a difference of $5,765,440, which, with the premium of $2,545,872, makes the sum of $8,311,112, the ultimate result to the borrower.

Or, take another view:—For the sum of $581,913, which the republic will receive (without including the charges already mentioned), we shall become liable for $9,402,400, which will make a difference of $8,820,486. This difference is the dreadful sacrifice imposed on the country.

Now, how shall this sum, costing $9,402,400, be invested so as to yield an interest, at least, equal to the annual exigencies? Can we pay with this the domestic debt of the republic, which amounts to more than a million? No. Even if we employ this fund as proposed in the prospectus of the loan in the construction of railroads and a highway from St. Domingo city to the province of Cibao, it will require more than double the amount indicated. Will this serve to develop an industry which does not exist? Will it tend to increase agricultural pursuits which are as yet purely imaginary? We say the least part of it will be consumed in the improvement of the South; the largest portion in paying the Jesurun debt; and the rest—Oh, the rest! The admission of such a loan is only possible to a government that has treason as its object.

Who will defend the monstrous negotiation we have examined—for some, no doubt, a benediction from heaven; to the country the greatest calamity? This loan will necessitate the excessive increase of the existing import duties and the creation of new ones. It will be the last addition to the burden of the landed proprietors. It will make it difficult to live, for we shall have to pay for the very air we breathe. Even in ordinary times the entries are not sufficient to meet the exigencies of the estimates. How, then, can we raise the important sum of $363,696 every year without scandalously altering the present import rates and creating fresh duties? To raise this sum the poor man will have to pay more for his bread, for his clothing; in short, living will become more costly to all, how much more it is impossible now to say. Does not every thoughtful man see that this loan will become an insupportable burden on the public at large; that it will prove, in fact, a halter to choke us? What advantage are we to reap in exchange? None. We have demonstrated mathematically that it will result in fatal injury, and that not in the most remote distance is any benefit visible.
