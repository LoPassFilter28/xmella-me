Philatelic News

In celebration of the ground breaking ceremonies for the 1938 Exposition at the Yerba Buena Shoals in San Francisco Bay, the San Francisco Junior Chamber of Commerce is preparing an appropriate cachet to mark the beginning of one of the major projects of California. The site, when filled, will contain approximately 400 acres and will be used by the San Francisco Exposition Company for the 1938 Exposition. After the Exposition, the site will be turned over to the city of San Francisco for use as a metropolitan airport. Aeronautical Engineers have stated that this site will provide one of the best airports in the United States, if not the world.

Plans for the extension of the transpacific air mail service into China will probably be announced by the Post Office department shortly. Negotiations are now under way between officials of the Pan American Airways and representatives of the postal service. It has been announced that Pan American Airways has been granted a concession at Macao, the Portuguese colony near Hongkong and Canton, China, where a Giant Clipper ship could be landed, but, it is pointed out, difficulties might arise over the transfer of air mail to China, where Macao used as a landing place. Therefore, it is hoped that the Chinese government can be persuaded to afford land facilities for Pan American Airways, either at Hongkong or Canton, preferably at the latter place.

It is understood that the postage rate for air mail letters to China when the new service is established will be $1.10 for each letter weighing not more than half an ounce. As there is at present a one-dollar stamp and a ten-cent stamp, no special postage stamp will have to be authorized for the service into China, and none is contemplated by the department. Regular postage, it is pointed out, is used on letters sent by air mail into Central and South American points.

It is not expected that a decision respecting the design that will be used on the Texas centennial postage stamp will be reached until after the first of next year. Several working models of the proposed stamp have been made, but none seems to meet with general approval. As a matter of fact, President Roosevelt himself will pass upon the design for the Texas stamp in co-operation with Vice-President Garner and State and Exposition officials. The Exposition is slated to open on March 15, 1936.

Two extra adhesives have been released by the post office department of the Dominican Republic as part of the recently noted series commemorating the final settlement of the Santo-Domingo-Haiti frontier dispute. The two new values are the 5c., red, blue, and red-violet.

A set of three stamps has been issued by Germany for the forthcoming Winter Olympic games to be held at Garmisch-Partenkirchen from February 6 to 10. The values and descriptions are as follows: plut 4 pfg., green, skater; 12 plus 6, carmine, skiing; 25 plus 15 pfg., blue, bobsled racing team of four. There are also two special cards, namely the 6 plus 4 pfg. and the 15 plus 10 pfgs., both showing winter blue, bobsled racing team of four. There are also two special cards, namely the 6 plus 4 pfg., and the 15 plus 10 pfg., both showing winter scenery in the Alps.

Many collectors appear to have classified the recent "Vor Het National Luchvaartfonds", stamp, 6 plus 4c., as an air mail. The Dutch post office declares that this is an error. It is a regular postage due issue, though the surtax is for the benefit of the Aviation Relief Fund. The stamp was valid only from October 16 to November 30. All air mails bear the word "Luchtpost."

Three new air mails, for domestic use only, are reported to be in preparation by Spain. The values are 15c., 30c., and 50c. There is talk also of a Velasquez commemorative.
