PLANS FOR PEACE IN SANTO DOMINGO First Steps Taken to Ameliorate Conditions in Island by Commissioners. AMNESTY FOR THE REBELS

The first step in the efforts of the special American commissioners to Santo Domingo to bring about an amelioration of the menacing situation in that republic was announced yesterday in dispatches received at the State Department. The features of the plan agreed upon thus far are:

The declaration of President Victoria, promulgated on October 21, that he will retire on July 1, 1914, the date when the term of office of the late President Caceres would have expired. By Dominican law, in case of the death of a President in office, his successor serves not the unexpired portion of his predecessor's term, but begins a new term of six years. Elections for Victoria's successor will be held in May, 1914.

Alfredo Victoria, nephew of the President, his Minister of War, and generally regarded as the "casus belli" in the Dominican Republic because of the use he has made of his extraordinary influence with his uncle, the President, is to retire from the Cabinet at once.

Minister of Finance Cordova will also withdraw in the near future.

Amnesty is offered all rebels, political prisoners are to be released at once, and all political exiles are to be permitted to return to the Dominican Republic as soon as they please.

It is understood that all of these changes were brought about through the negotiations of W. T. S. Doyle, of the Department of State, and Brig. Gen. Frank McIntyre, of the Bureau of Insular Affairs, who are special commissioners instructed by the United States to do what they can to remedy conditions in the Dominican Republic. The purpose is to allay, if possible, the rebellion now in progress against the Dominican government by a policy of conciliation, and especially by assurances that the time will not be long before all opponents of the present administration will have opportunity to make themselves felt in the forthcoming election.

Reason for Change.

The withdrawal of Gen. Victoria from the situation was arranged as a guarantee to the Dominican people that henceforth they may expect no more of the harsh and exasperating measures for which the Minister of War was held responsible. Victoria is to be succeeded in the Cabinet by Ellas Brache, a young lawyer, said to have the support of all factions.

The retirement of the Minister of Finance is said to be desirable in order that a new order of things may be instituted in the financial affairs of the republic. There have been serious charges of the diversion of government funds from their proper purposes, assumption of unwarranted financial obligations, the nonpayment of debts, and downright graft and dishonesty. A change in this department will, it is hoped, effect a beneficial change.

The other measures are for the purpose of conciliating the rebels and giving assurance that all parties and individuals will have a fair chance to participate in the affairs of the republic. Whether the rebels now operating in the north will be contented with this programme and give up their struggle against the government is not known here. There is much hope that the programme announced yesterday will have the desired effect. It is believed that even if the leaders do not accept the programme that the people will and withhold from the revolution their further support.

Meantime, much consideration is being given to the question of the opening of the custom-houses on the frontier, closed because of rebel control of that territory, and also to the question of providing some influence which shall act as a restraint upon extravagance and worse in the administration of the government finances. This problem of financial supervision has not been solved. It is understood that the Dominicans will not accept an out-and-out supervision of their expenditures by the United States.

Receiver General W. C. Pulliam, the head of the Dominican custom service, reached Washington yesterday and had extended conferences with Acting Secretary of State Wilson. Mr. Pulliam's advice was desired particularly in regard to the problem of providing some means of keeping the Dominicans from reckless and wrongful use of their revenues.

The Prairie, with 750 marines on board, has arrived at Puerto Plata, where it was reported a rebel attack on the city was expected at any moment. On the way to Puerto Plata the Prairie looked in at Samana, where the city was reported to have been bombarded by rebels.
