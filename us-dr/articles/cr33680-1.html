IN THE U. S. MARINES One of Uncle Sams “Devil Dogs” Writes Home From the Dominican Republic And Tells About the Earthquakes and Niggers of This Tropical Isle of the Sea

Santo Domingo, S. R. October 24, 1918.

Dear Uncle:

Your letter of October 1st, received and enjoyed. Glad everyone is well and sincerely hope the Spanish Influenza will not get spread through West Virginia. It must be in a very bad stage as there are so many dying with it.

I wish I could have been with you all at the Brown Reunion, to have met my relations and helped eat up the good "Chow" as they always have abundance of it. I heard from home and they all enjoyed the day very much and said you were still the man of the day on the "eats". but if I had been there I bet I would have run you an awful race for this Marine training certainly gives one an awful appetite. I would enjoy seeing the Service flag and am certainly proud that one of the stars represents myself.

I can see the old town in my mind and know there is nothing prettier unless it is a tropical sun set, for we certainly have beautiful ones, especially just after a rain.

This is the rainy season here so Camp life is not so pleasant as the sun is shining one minute and its raining the next minute.

I would like to see the West Virginia Hills in their coat of gold and many colors and this very evening I wish I had my feet planted on one of the hills, as I was at a picture theatre watching the French war pictures and directly the old building began to rock from an earthquake; the lights went out and the people started out on the run. I thought I was going to have to battle my way out for a while but finally they quieted down. There was a big crowd there too, as it was for the benefit of the French Red Cross. It did no damage here but I expect some place will suffer. We felt the one about three weeks ago that did so much damage at Porto Rico, Cuba, but it did no damage on this island, but it makes one have an odd feeling. However I guess we will get used to them as the older boys say.

Things are in pretty good shape here now as the niggers have gone to work making up their sugar and helping on the plantations, but the Americans or the Dominicans wont hire one of them if they know he belonged to the bandit gang.

We are training now in throwing hand grenades and just finished studying signals, bayonet work and machine gun. So if this war is not over by spring and we get to France the Germans will think the "Devil Dogs" as they call the Marines, are turned loose on them for sure, I am anxious for the war to end, as soon as possible but I would like to help the boys collect from Germany the damage she has caused the world and especially for Preston County. We know you all at home are doing everything you can to help get this conflict over with and I have no other idea but what the 4th Liberty Loan will go over the six billion mark by a large amount. Last week we had a parade over the city for the benefit of the Liberty Loan. We heard the Admiral, the American Minister, and our General all make very nice speeches in the interest of it. Then the next day we all subscribed for bonds to our limit, you know a soldier cant buy any big amount but every little bit helps, so we bought our bit and I understand our Company beat the other Companies at this Post for amount in dollars. I have some coins for you and will try to get another relic for you as a souvenir. I had already put the coins away for you as I know you have quite a collection of them. I received two papers from you and enjoyed them very much. I was quite surprised when I found my letter to you published in one of them.

The Government is certainly giving the boys from 18 to 20 years a fine chance to receive a military education. It will do them good, make stronger and better citizens out of them. Guess they wont be put through the mill like we went though, that is in so short a time.

I hope Germany is ready for peace, but we must not let her pull anything over our eyes and then in a few years have to fight her again.

Let me hear from you as often as you have time. Here's hoping your next letter tells me Col. Watson and the entire Democrat ticket is elected. Remember me to Aunt Stella and the boys and Hazel.

As ever,

Charles Slidell Brown.

Censored by S. R. Pitten,

Second Lieutenant,

U. S. Marine Corps.
