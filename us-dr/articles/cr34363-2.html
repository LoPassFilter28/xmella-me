Haiti Threat To Asylum Arouses Latin America

..WASHINGTON (NANA) — Latin America's governments are especially aroused against Haiti's President Francois Duvalier because he is threatening one of the Latin politician's principal means of survival — the right of diplomatic asylum.

When Duvalier's militia pursued political refugees into the Dominican Republic's Port Au Prince embassy in late April, they violated a tradition that has enabled opposition leaders to escape dictatorial bloodbaths in Latin America for over a century. Technically, a country's embassy is its national territory, hence the Dominican charge of "invasion" after the Haitians burst into their embassy, and the threat of retaliatory war.

Disputes over diplomatic asylum periodically shake the hemisphere. In the early 1950's Colombia and Peru came almost to war after the Peruvian anti-communist leftist, Victor Raul Haya de la Torre, took refuge in the Columbia embassy in Lima.

Odria Bought Up Property

Haya de la Torre sought asylum in January, 1949, to escape a blood purge by a ruling military clique headed by Gen. Manuel Odria. When Odria's police prepared to storm the embassy, Colombia's ambassador Carlos Echevarri Cortes announced he would defend the grounds with gunfire and plunge Colombia and Peru into war.

Odria then bought the property surrounding the Colombian embassy and installed 600 soldiers to guard it with machine guns and floodlights. Colombia twice took the case to the United Nations. The U.N. referred it to the International Court at the Hague which turned it back to the disputants.

The International League for the Rights of Man (ILRM), with the Inter-American Association for Democracy and Freedom (IADF), waged a hemisphere-wide drive to free Haya. He was even named U.N. observer for the International League in hope of getting him out of Peru, but the stratagem failed.

Haya had been living in the embassy over five years when the dispute threatened to sink the hemispheric Foreign Ministers' meeting at Caracas in March, 1954. The U.S. brought pressure, and he was allowed to go into exile soon after.

Haya de la Torre and Odria were rivals for Peru's presidency in the 1962 campaigns and will oppose each other again in elections promised for 1963.

World Record

..The fact that common criminals may not use diplomatic asylum often leads to disputes. Two Buenos Aires police officials, brothers, Carlos and Luis Amadeo Cardosa, took refuge in the Paraguayan embassy in Buenos Aires as Dictator Juan Peron's regime collapsed in September 1955. The succeeding governments have indicted them for torturing political prisoners and denied them safe-conducts abroad. The brothers have lived in the Paraguayan embassy for 7½ years, the world record for political asylum.

In both Cuba and the Dominican republic, men, women and children have sought diplomatic asylum en masse from dictatorship. During 1962, Cuban exiles estimate that over 500 people jammed Havana chanceries and diplomatic residences. With over 200 to house and feed, Brazil's ambassador finally moved to a local hotel to live.

Cuban exile sources and the International Rescue Organization both estimated that about half this number are still awaiting safe-conducts.

In the death throes of the Trujillo dictatorship, especially in 1960, over 400 refugees crowded into asylum in Santo Domingo. At one time the Venezuelan embassy had nearly 300 people, including one family of 13.
