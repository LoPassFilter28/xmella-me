Mr. Knowles, who said he had accompanied the official investigating party to Santo Domingo and had remained there after the departure of the Senate committee two weeks ago at the request of Senator Medill McCormick, chairman of the committee charged that following "threats" by Rear Admiral Samuel S. Robinson the Military Governor, he had been compelled to leave the city under cover of darkness. His departure he said, followed the receipt of a letter from Francisco J. Peyando former Dominican Minister to the United States, which said: "For reasons I stated to you Friday, I consider it best for you not to remain here an hour beyond the first chance you have to leave."

"If the treatment accorded me, an American citizen, whom the military authorities must know would complain of it upon his return to the United States, is an example of the methods of the American military government in Santo Domingo, what would they stop at in the case of natives?" said Mr. Knowles.

"I had remained behind to take the testimony of some fifty other witnesses as Senator McCormick had requested. On the day when the Senate committee was to return to the United States I went to the dock with a list of witnesses to deliver to Senator McCormick. I was informed that he had boarded the Argonne, the ship on which the official party, myself among them, went to Santo Domingo.

"I delivered personally the communication to Senator McCormick to Mr. Leonard, stenographer of the commission, just as he was boarding a launch to take him to the Argonne. Desiring to supplement the written communication by a verbal communication to Senator McCormick, I leaned forward for the purpose of giving such a message to Mr. Leonard. In a brusque and very uncivil manner Captain Day, delegated to represent the United States Navy on the trip of the Senatorial Commission, pushed up against me and used words to the effect that I could not communicate with Mr. Leonard. I told him that the message was most important and that it was in compliance with a request by Senator McCormick, and I asked Captain Day if he would not ask Mr. Leonard to come to the gang-plank.

"At that point an officer of high rank, whose name I did not know and whose uniform I did not recognize, rushed up, ordered me away from the gang-plank of the launch gave in the most austere manner orders to cast off, and for the launch to proceed to the Argonne. Immediately he had done that he addressed me in as rude a manner as it could be possible for an uncultured man to do, stating that he had heard and seen enough of me, and ordered me off the dock. That he did without my having said one single word to him, or even at that point knowing who he was, or having acted either by manner or speech in any way that was in the slightest degree improper. In fact, I had said absolutely nothing to him. He then told me that he was the Military Governor, and used words to the effect of a threat to place me under arrest, and again told me that he was the Military Governor and that he had heard and seen enough of me and took a menacing attitude toward me, which had every appearance of using violence had I not discreetly walked away."

Mr. Knowles said that the news of the incident spread rapidly about the city, where he had many friends made when he was United States Minister to Santo Domingo ten years ago, under President Taft. In order to prevent a demonstration of any kind, he said, he visited all the newspapers and asked them to refrain from mentioning the occurrence. Within an hour, Mr. Knowles asserted, Admiral Robinson sent the Provost Marshal, demanding his immediate appearance before the Admiral. When he at first ignored the demand, he said, Admiral Robinson threatened force. On his appearance at the Admiral's office, said Mr. Knowles, he asked to have his counsel, Dr. Peynado, present, but this was denied. It was at this interview that Mr. Knowles was told by Admiral Robinson "that he wanted me to know that the American military authorities considered me in the most unfriendly way and that I would receive absolutely no consideration at their hands."

"Because of certain threats made by the Admiral," Mr. Knowles continued, "I was virtually a prisoner in my hotel and was advised not to appear on the streets. I had planned to leave Santo Domingo on December 23, on the Algonquin, but as a result of the letter received from Dr. Peynado, who was Minister of State of Santo Domingo, under the deposed government, I arranged to sail on the Guantanamo, so as to make my escape at an early hour before daybreak."
