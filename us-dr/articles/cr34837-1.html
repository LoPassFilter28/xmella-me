PLAN TAKING COLUMBUS REMAINS TO EXPOSITION Placing on View at San Francisco of Discoverer's Bones Is Contemplated.

That the casket containing the bones now resting in San Domingo which are claimed to be those of Christopher Columbus be placed in the Dominican building at the San Francisco exposition, where people from all over the United States may see it, was the suggestion made today by men interested in the authenticity of the claim made by the Dominican people.

Assistant Secretary of State John E. Osborne and Walker W. Vick, the newly appointed collector of customs at San Domingo, who recently returned from that place, bringing with them the story that Columbus' remains are in Santo Domingo instead of at Seville, Spain, are much interested in the suggestion that they be taken to San Francisco.

John Barrett, director general of the Pan-American Union, expressed pleasure today over the interest taken in the location of the remains of Columbus, and said that it is a subject of great significance. He said that officials of the Dominican republic are very anxious to establish the authenticity of their claim.

"The Pan-American Union has always taken a deep interest in the subject, and we have a number of publications dealing with the claims of different places that the remains of Columbus rest there," said Mr. Barrett. "It is a question which has never been finally settled to the satisfaction of every one."

Exposition Site Selected.

The Dominican minister to the United States is now at Santo Domingo, but before going there he selected a site for the Dominican building at the San Francisco exposition, and the little republic expects to make a good showing at the exposition. Officials of the republic are now considering the suggestion that the remains be taken through the Panama canal, and then placed in their building at the exposition.

An attempt was made a number of years ago by representatives of the Dominican republic to have their claims investigated by a committee from different American countries, but the claim has never been passed upon by any competent body.

Spaniards scout the idea that the remains of Columbus are not in Seville, where a beautiful tomb has been erected. The remains were supposed to have been taken to Seville from Havana, following the Spanish-American war. The claim of the Dominicans that the remains taken to Seville were really those of Diego, the son of Columbus, is supported by a number of authorities, however.
