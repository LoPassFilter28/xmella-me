Curb Urged On Private Detectives

Commissioner Walter N. Tobriner will seek a regulation requiring private detectives to be licensed before they can operate in the District.

Mr. Tobriner, in the wake of a "bugging" incident uncovered at the Mayflower Hotel, said last night over Radio Station WWDC that he favors "strict licensing requirements for private detectives, possibly bonding them," to assure that they are of good moral character.

He said later he will ask Corporation Counsel Chester Gray, chief of the city's legal staff, to see what steps officials must take to obtain the licensing power.

"The likelihood of private detectives invading privacy . . . is very great and should be curbed," Mr. Tobriner said.

Would Apply to Outsiders

He added that the proposed license would apply to any detective from outside the Washington area working within the city limits.

An electronic device was discovered last week in the Mayflower Hotel rooms of a San Francisco lawyer, Malcolm T. Dungan, who had come here to argue a case before the Federal Power Commission.

Mr. Dungan sought, and received, a court order against further use of such devices by various known and unknown defendants.

As a result of the incident, Federal Communications Commission technicians are testing the "bugging" device to determine whether there was a violation of FCC regulations. The tiny transmitter, about the size of a pack of cigarettes, was found taped under the lawyer's coffee table in the hotel. The FCC said it had a good power output and a range of a good many feet.

Among these named in the case were two private detectives, Oliver W. Angelone, a licensed private detective with offices at 1830 Jefferson place N.W., and John J. Frank, who authorities said is the same man who was fined in 1959 on a charge of failing to register as an agent for the Dominican Republic and the late dictator, Rafael Trujillo.

Agencies Now Licensed

Under present District law, licenses are required only of private detective agencies, not individual detectives working in the agencies.

Mr. Tobriner also reported that he and other District officials soon will interview 19 candidates to replace retiring Fire Chief Millard H. Sutton. All the candidates have been screened by a reviewing board.
