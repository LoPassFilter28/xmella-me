CLOSELY INTO TOUCH. Was Porto Rico With Santo Domingo Revolution. HUNT CABLED FACTS. JIMINEZ, AT MAYAGUEZ, KEPT TAB ON THINGS. Dominican Exile, Recently From Cuba, Makes a Statement of Interest.

Special Correspondence of The Evening Star.

SAN JUAN, P. R., November 10, 1903.

Porto Rico came pretty closely into touch with the Santo Domingo revolution last week; so closely, in fact, that Gov. Hunt deemed it wise to promptly cable the facts to Secretary of State Hay.

For a month ex-President Juan Isidro Jiminez has been at Mayaguez, P. R., keeping in communication with Dominican exiles in Cuba. On October 28 the Spanish steamer Maria Herrera brought twenty-seven of these exiles to Mayaguez to join Gen. Jiminez.

In the party were Gen. Ramon Casceres, who killed President Ulises Heureaux of Santo Domingo June 26, 1899; Senor Luna, secretary of Gen. Casceres; Gen. Limardo, ex-governor of Puerto Plata; Arturo Guzman, ex-fiscal of Puerto Plata, and others of prominence in former Dominican revolutions.

On November 4 Gen. Jiminez and his party proceeded to San Juan and boarded the French steamer St. Simon as passengers for Cape Haytien. At about the time they went on the steamer Gov. Hunt received a telegram from Gen. Eugenio Kunhardt, sub-governor of the province of Puerto Plata, S. D., asking Gov. Hunt, as the representative of a friendly power, to stop the proposed embarkation, and stating that the Dominican consul general in San Juan would call on the governor.

No Violation of International Law.

The consul general did not call, but Gov. Hunt, as a matter of precaution, consulted with United States District Attorney Pettingill and United States Marshal Wilson, and those officials visited the office of the agents of the French line of steamers and satisfied themselves that no violation of international law or courtesies had occurred. The St. Simon was in midstream, and Judge Pettingill and Marshal Wilson did not board her. The manifests showed no contraband goods.

At about the time the revolutionists sailed from San Juan, Gen. Kunhardt sailed from Mayaguez for Puerto Plata.

On being interviewed upon the subject of the revolution in Santo Domingo one of the Dominican exiles, who arrived from Cuba on October 28 on the steamer Maria Herrera, made the following statement:

Makes a Statement.

He said that no sooner had the exiles in Cuba received notification from the governor of Puerto Plata, Gen. and ex-Priest Don Carlos Morales, the result of their plans, they decided to co-operate with a general uprising along the frontier of Haiti, taking in the towns of Monte Cristy, Dajabon and Guayubin, and extending to the towns of Santiago de los Caballeros and La Vega.

Then they prepared to take the Cuban steamer Maria Herrera with a view of landing in Puerto Plata, which was held by Gen. Carlos Morales, and so join the revolutionary movement. Unfortunately the Dominican consul in Cuba became aware of their movements and applied to the Cuban authorities to have them prevented from embarking, but he was informed by them that as there was no evidence that these men were taking arms and war stores, and as they were leaving the country as peaceable citizens, they could not be interfered with.

The Dominican consul then telegraphed to the president of the Dominican republic at the city of Santo Domingo informing him of their having embarked for some port of the republic, presumably Puerto Plata.

Took Passage for San Juan.

The Dominican exiles, being perfectly aware of the uncertainty of such uprisings, took passage for San Juan, Porto Rico, intending to disembark in Puerto Plata if all were well, but if not to continue to Porto Rico and join ex-President Jiminez.

Upon arriving off the port of Puerto Plata a Dominican warship was sighted, but no attention was paid to her, and they continued their course; they were, however, brought to by a shot fired across the bow of the Maria Herrera and the captain was ordered not to enter the port.
