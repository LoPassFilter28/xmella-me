Trujillo's Son Denied U. S. Army Diploma

Young Rafael Trujillo, jr., isn't going to graduate from a United States Army staff school, but he has a good military future ahead of him nevertheless.

The free-spending son of Dominican Dictator Rafael Trujillo has been scratched from the graduating list at the Army's Command and General Staff College at Fort Leavenworth, Kans., it was announced yesterday.

But in the Dominican Republic it was announced that a new job has been created for him—chairman of the joint chiefs of staff.

Parties Criticized

The 29-year-old lieutenant general made headlines recently when, convalescing from a sinus operation in California, he bought expensive foreign cars for movie actresses Kim Novak and Zsa Zsa Gabor. He also gave Miss Gabor a coat.

He was on leave at the time from the Army college, where he had been studying since last summer. His pursuit, lavish gifts to women friends and other displays of luxury stirred criticism in Congress. He angrily denied any of the money came from United States foreign aid funds.

Representative Brownson, Republican of Indiana, said yesterday the Army had advised him Lt. Gen. Trujillo "did not successfully complete" the Army course. He said the general will be given only a certificate of attendance rather than a certificate of graduation.

To Head New Office

Almost simultaneously came the word the Dominican Republic was setting up a new combined chiefs of staff office and Gen. Trujillo would head it. He was already commander of the Dominican air force, although he cannot fly a plane.

The advancement continues the young general's rapid rise in his own country. He was a major at 3 and a colonel at 6. He entered the Dominican army cadet school at 16 and completed the course in a year.
