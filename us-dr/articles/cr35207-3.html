THE SAN DOMINGO SITUATION.

J. H. Balch, of Greenwich, Conn., who is an electrical engineer, has just returned from San Domingo, which has been having a sort of miniature revolution. Mr. Balch is engineer for the San Domingo Improvement Company, and will return to San Domingo in about ten days. He spends most of his time when in San Domingo at Porto Plata, the principal seaport on the northern coast. He left there on August 21 last, and in talking of the situation on that island he said: "Things move kind of lively down there at times. On August 16 the Governor of the town of San Francisco de Macoris, a town sixty miles from Santiago, in the interior of San Domingo, surrendered the town to the insurgent chieftain, General Ramon Caceres, the assassin who killed President Heureaux, and who has never been captured. Caceres had about three hundred men with him, and the Governor of San Francisco de Macoris had a garrison of only fifty infantrymen, armed with Remington rifles. The Government offered no opposition, and so there was no fighting, and no bloodshed. As soon as Caceres got control he telegraphed all the other Governors of important cities in San Domingo, all of whom were appointed by the President, that he was in supreme control at San Francisco de Macoris, and that he held himself subject to their commands, which was a fine way of defying them to come and take him."
