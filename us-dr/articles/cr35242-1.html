Miss De Oca Best In Spanish Songs

NEWS OF MUSIC

By DAY THORPE

Teresa Montes de Oca, soprano; Alberto Sciarretti, piano. At Pan American Union. Program: Le Violette, Scarlatti; Se tu m'ami, Pergolesi; Vittoria, mio core, Carissimi; Voi che sapete, Mozart; Beau soir, Debussy; L'Ombre des arbres, Debussy; Romance, Debussy; Fantoches, Debussy; Ah, je veux vivre, Gounod; When I have Sung My Songs, Ernest Charles; Let My Song Fill Your Heart, Charles; Colored Stars, Bainbridge Crist; Cantares, Turina; El Pano moruno, Falla; Jota, Falla; Seguidilla, Falla; Con amores, Obradors; Dos cantares, Obradors.

Teresa Montes de Oca, heard last night at the Pan American Union, is a young soprano from Ciudad Trujillo in the Dominican Republic. Her first concert appearances were in New York in 1949, and since that time she has appeared in opera with the New York Opera Guild and the New Jersey State Opera Company.

Miss de Oca has a warm, expressive and pleasant voice. Last night she was at her best in the Spanish songs of Manuel de Falla, which she sang with abandon and passion. Her group of Debussy was also good, but the precision of French impressionism is perhaps somewhat less suited to her artistic talents. The coloratura of the Gounod aria from "Romeo et Juliette" is also not her forte.

It is a customary gesture for foreign singers in recital at the Pan American Union to include one or more American songs on their programs. I feel sure that Miss de Oca, in singing three songs of Ernest Charles and Bainbridge Crist, was intending a compliment and was not trying to ridicule the United States. However, the impossibly sentimental ballads of these Americans, which interrupted an otherwise interesting program, are, it is fair to say, not typical of good American song-writing even at its most commonplace level.
