Haiti Rushes Troop Reinforcements Into Northeast War Zone Exiles Claim Capture Of 150 Of Duvalier's Police

By MATTHEW T. KENNY

(United Press International)

SANTO DOMINGO, Dominican Republic—(UPI)—Haiti rushed troop reinforcements to Cap Haitien in the northeastern war zone Wednesday as exiles here claimed capture of 150 armed members of President Francois Duvalier's feared secret police.

Army regulars, estimated at 150 strong, took off from Port au Prince's military airport in three troop carriers early Wednesday armed with light and heavy machineguns and field equipment.

Cap Haitien was reported encircled by the exile invasion force which was said to have crossed the Dominican border last Monday in a drive against the Duvalier regime.

The capture of the secret police was reported here by Paul Verna, a spokesman for the National Democratic Union which sponsored the Monday invasion. He said they had been taken at Derac, one of the three towns in Haiti just across the Dominican border which the exiles claim to have captured.

Verna reported the men had been jailed but he had no information as to how they had been taken. He admitted he had received no direct word from the invasion force in more than 24 hours.

Haiti, meanwhile, protested a second time to the Organization of American States against Dominican aggression, and demanded that the OAS take immediate action to halt it.

BELIEVED MORE CONCERNED

The new Haitian protest hinted at growing concern over the military thrust in the northeast. In its original protest to the OAS, considered in Washington Tuesday, Haiti asked no action on its complaint.

The feeling was growing in Port au Prince that perhaps the invasion, originally described by the Duvalier regime as of minor importance, was being taken more seriously now.

Rumors circulated in Port au Prince of a new exile landing at Grande Saline, on Haiti's west coast. They may have been based on exile claims to that effect made in New York Tuesday.

Verna said he knew nothing about the New York reports that a 45-man exile force including 12 Cubans had attacked Grande Saline and left a token 15-man occupation force there.

However, Verna added "there are groups of Haitians all over the Carribbean area ready to begin similar invasions."

In Mexico City, informed diplomatic sources said a potential army of several thousand men may be formed among Haitian refugees on the Dominican side of the border between the two countries.

URGES U. S. SUPPORT

Verna called on the United States "and all other democratic nations in the Western Hemisphere" to support the exile invasion force.

His plea was similar to that voiced in Washington by Roger Rigaud, a former Haitian cabinet minister whose brother is with the invasion force.

Rigaud also claimed to be "in constant contact" with the invasion force commanded by retired Gen. Leon Cantave, a former Haitian army chief of staff and one-time Port au Prince police chief.
