A President Murdered.

Our recent acquisitions in the West Indies make us feel a livelier interest in all the islands of that group. The capture by our forces of Porto Rico, and our present supremacy in Cuba, make us realize that we are immediate neighbors of Hayti, Jamaica, and adjacent isles. For this reason the tragedy which a week ago Wednesday deprived San Domingo of her ruler, leads us to sympathize with that republic. President Heureaux was a mulatto, and had held the office of President for a number of terms. He was a man of much ability, and had administered the affairs of the country with fair success. Most of the republics of South America are liable to rebellions. In fact not a few answer to the description once given of Russia; they are despotisms tempered by assassination.

The Dominican republic, indeed, like Hayti, is subject to insurrections. A President will not be scrupulous therefore about the use of force. His subjects must have a fear of him to deter them from plots, and if he would retain his power he must oft use what in an established republic like ours would be called rigor. Present reports affirm that the assassin of President Heureaux was the son of an offender whom he had put to death over a dozen years ago. The young man's name was Caceros, and he came to our country shortly after his father's death. He attended one of our higher literary institutions, but seems to have brooded over his father's death, and fostered revenge. He shot his victim as he was preparing to mount his horse, and the whole land is convulsed. The population is about six hundred thousand, composed of Spanish half-breeds, mulattos and blacks. Vice President Figuereo assumes control of public affairs, but how long he may have peaceable supremacy is uncertain. It is asserted that our old friend Gen. Gomez is designing to leave Cuba to see if he can obtain authority in San Domingo.

The Dominican republic and Hayti occupy different parts of the island formerly called by the latter name. Hayti has control of the western part, while San Domingo claims the eastern section. It contains not far from twenty thousand miles of territory, and is of great fertility. Its area is about equal to the combined area of New Hampshire and Vermont. It used to produce a great deal of coffee, though of an inferior quality. It furnishes mahogany of a superior grade, which has a reputation of being the finest in the world. It of course abounds in tropical fruits, and the good order which the late energetic President maintained contributed to the public prosperity. We hope that the late murder may not set the land aflame with civil war.
