Davies Eager to Assume Post as Envoy to Russia

One of America's Richest Women Will Act as Hostess in U. S. Embassy in Russia as Result of Roosevelt Selection

By EVELYN PEYTON GORDON

The appointment yesterday of Joseph E. Davies as United States Ambassador to the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics confirms a prediction made in this column two months ago.

Mr. and Mrs. Davies—she is the former Marjorie Post Close Hutton—came to Washington just before the President started on his South American trip, and plan to remain in their Shoreham apartment until after Thanksgiving.

Apparently Mr. Davies will sail for Europe in the near future to assume his new duties for he said yesterday that he expected to be en route to Moscow before Mr. Roosevelt returns next month. The post has been vacant since the appointment of the former Ambassador, William C. Bullitt, to Paris, and the new Ambassador is eager to start his work.

NEVER BEEN IN RUSSIA

While photographers made flash after flash of the handsome new envoy yesterday, Mr. Davies answered questions fired at him by a group of correspondents. A well-dressed man with regular features and piercing dark eyes, he cautiously avoided being drawn into comment over the Soviet experiment. Mr. Davies has never been to Russia but said he "had followed the experiment with deep interest."

Seated in his handsome law offices in the Shoreham building, Mr. Davies looked at the fine oil paintings, and autographed pictures of such notables as Warren G. Harding, the late Sen. James Couzens, and Secretary of State Cordell Hull.

A framed banquet menu dating back to the 1932 campaign carries the following inscription:

"To old Jo from his sidekick, Franklin Roosevelt."

Washington is proud of Mr. Davies' success, for having lived in the Capital for 20 years, he is regarded almost as a native son. His marriage last December to the breakfast-food heiress caused great excitement in social circles, and was followed within a few weeks by the marriage of Mr. Davies' eldest daughter, Mrs. Eleanor Davies Cheeseborough, to Sen. Millard Tydings of Maryland.

HAS ANOTHER DAUGHTER

A younger daughter, Mrs. Aldace Walker, of New York, is at present visiting President Trujillo of Santo Domingo, with her husband, a son of Mr. and Mrs. Harold Walker of Washington and the Eastern Shore of Maryland. Mr. Davies has been counsel for several years for the government of the Dominican Republic.

One of the world's richest women will be hostess of the United States Embassy in Communist Russia, and she will add charm and dignity to the position. The former Ambassador was not married and the embassy in Moscow will welcome so delightful a chatelaine.

In New York, Mrs. Davies lives in a 66-room triplex apartment that nearly equals in feudal magnificence the huge mansion that is leased as the embassy in Moscow. The residence is called the Spaso and belonged to one of the Commissars. Made of white stone, it is a two-storied structure with a high dome, and resembles a small edition of the Capitol.

Mr. Davies is the second envoy from this country to Soviet Russia, and his problems will be numerous and varied. Almost every Russian-speaking American diplomat applied for a place on the staff of Mr. Bullitt when he opened the embassy several years ago, and since, for the past 12 years, the State Department has been training young men in the Russian language schools in Paris and in Riga, Latvia, where Russian is spoken by all the citizens, the posts were easily filled.

LAVISH HOSTESS

Mrs. Davies will probably miss her great host of friends whom she entertains at her estates on Long Island and at Palm Beach, and the gay groups she invites on cruises aboard her yacht, the Hussar, one of the largest privately owned ships in the world.

But for those interested in watching great social changes, no place is more fascinating than Moscow, tho for those who depend entirely upon social activities for amusement, no city dulls more rapidly.

Ambassador and Mrs. Davies will find at least one former acquaintance when they arrive in Moscow, Signor Augusto Rosso, former Italian Ambassador to Washington, who has recently arrived in the Soviet capital for duty.
