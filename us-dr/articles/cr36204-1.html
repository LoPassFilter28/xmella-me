Venezuela's New Regime Seeks Recognition of the Americas

By United Press

CARACAS, Oct. 23—Venezuela's new revolutionary government bid for diplomatic recognition by the rest of the world today.

Carlos Morales, Foreign Minister of the military junta, told a press conference that diplomatic representatives of all American nations in Caracas had been informed of the constitution of the new government.

Representatives of the American republics conferred at the American Embassy yesterday on the situation and will meet again tomorrow at the home of the Papal Nuncio.

HINTS SPAIN BREAK

Morales said he expected recognition because of the popularity of the revolution abroad. He said the junta would comply with all international commitments entered into by previous Venezuelan governments.

He suggested that the junta might break relations with Spain and the Dominican Republic on grounds they were Fascist-controlled.

CHURCH BACKS IT

The government already had won internal support from the Catholic Church and the Venezuelan Chamber of Commerce. A spokesman said the government was preparing for election of delegates to a convention to draw up a new constitution.

The government issued a decree prohibiting any member of the present interim junta from being a candidate for president in the April election. The junta also froze the bank accounts of all persons having more than $30,000 on deposit.

The government announced that it intends to try all officials who were believed to have enriched themselves in the last 50 years, including deposed President Isaias Medina.
