MEXICO’S POSITION IN THE CONFLICT WITH CUBA

No. 6 of a series

El Caribe

EDITORA DEL CARIBE, C. por A.

CIUDAD TRUJILO, DOMINICAN REPUBLIC

The following editorial is translated from a recent issue of El Caribe, the leading newspaper in the Dominican Republic. The members of the Press Society of the Dominican Republic feel that it is a message of importance to the people of the United States. As a public service, they have arranged for its translation and publication.

(Editorial from "El Caribe," Ciudad Trujillo, July 8, 1960)

By JESUS MARIA TRONCOSO

RELATIONS between the United States and Cuba have been reduced to a state of conflict as a result of a series of highly complicated factors. Being ignorant of the reality of Latin American policies, the State Department gave its blessing to the exaltation of Castro, basing its action on the erroneous supposition that one of its main functions was to get rid of Batista's dictatorial government, without giving a thought to what might come after.

At the instigation of deluded or over-hasty advisers, among which must be included Vice President Nixon, this important government department believed that if it helped to destroy the dictatorship in question, every American nation would immediately swear allegiance to the United States and would forget the sad story of the old exactions and imperialist expansionism to which they had been subjected.

When Castro came to power, he launched himself against the United States in a systematic and tenacious campaign, using every kind of insult and accusation possible. Once again, the United States had been wrong in thinking that every country would oppose Castro and crush him with their criticism. Their surmise was just the reverse to what has actually happened, since Castro's campaign has won more converts and sympathizers than was to be expected. This has been demonstrated on a number of occasions but never so forcibly as the way in which Mexico has just declared herself.

In effect, an official spokesman for the government party, Emilio Sanchez Piedra, called together a group of members of parliament in order to explain to them the government's policy as regards the situation which has just arisen between the two countries that have declared the most truculent and sensational economic war against each other as a prelude to more positive and dangerous actions. At the meeting in question, the spokesman declared that in the event of the conflict attaining greater proportions, Mexico would back Cuba, and accused the United States of having closed the door to friendship and understanding. The applause and other expressions of overwhelming approval received by the speaker clearly indicated that this statement is in line with the government's position which, through another spokesman, had already been declared to be-of a totally leftish nature.

The Mexican Government's decision has caused considerable surprise among those of us who are anxiously watching this convulsed and maddened world of our America. But still greater confusion and anxiety must have been produced in United States government circles and more particularly among the State Department experts. There can have been very few of them who could have thought that Mexico would suddenly declare herself a belligerant and ready to back, albeit morally, the Cuban revolution.

On account of its traditions, the constant activities of its politicians and its sense of patriotism, it would have better become Mexico to be a mediator in the tremendous dispute which is daily becoming more acute. Its geographical position, its interests, its political background, everything contributes to assign to it that function rather than one of a combatant, on the side of Cuba.

The present circumstances and the need for clearing up the tremendous confusion that is corroding the morals of Latin America makes it imperative that President Lopes Mateos corroborate or amend the statement made by his spokesman since the matter is much too important to be dealt with by intermediaries and since other nations require an inspiration and an example to help them to define correctly their own position vis-a-vis the common destiny. And Mexico, one of the Continent's most important countries, could well be the guiding light of the present moment.

The decision of the Republic of Juarez, openly and clearly confirmed by the Head of the State, might represent the opportunity for a definition of the general trend of thought and feeling in regard to the problem that has arisen between Cuba and the United States.

Similarly, it is up to the State Department to convince itself that it is in the process of losing another friend and, if it does not convert this friend into an enemy, at least, it cannot count inconditionally on his allegiance and affection. The possibilities are that the friend in question is closer at the moment to becoming an enemy. The continuous errors, mistakes, lack of sincerity and even double-dealing with which the State Department has conducted its business, are producing an isolationist atmosphere around it. If Mexico confirms her position, she will take with her a great part of America and the gulf will widen enormously. And Cuba, in the long run, could very easily become a symbol of independence and freedom for certain countries that have been oppressed, badly treated or unjustly dealt with.

THE DOMINICAN REPUBLIC PRESS SOCIETY

Ciudad Trujillo, Dominican Republic
