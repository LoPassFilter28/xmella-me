COMPENSATION BY SAN DOMINGO. ITS CONGRESS VOTES A CREDIT TO THE IMPROVEMENT COMPANY.

San Domingo, Jan. 12.—Congress has voted a credit for the Government to compensate the San Domingo Improvement Company, of New-York. Control of the customs will continue with the Government, which will pay salaries monthly. A commission composed of the Dutch and Belgian Consuls, the American and French Vice-Consuls and one Dominican, will receive the customs duties. General satisfaction is felt throughout the country. The President has been congratulated on the action taken. All is quiet.

TO PROTECT AMERICAN INTERESTS.

Washington, Jan. 12.—The Government probably will take cognizance of the troubles in San Domingo and will take such action as is necessary for the protection of American interests there. Thus far, however, the State Department has not heard from Consul-General Maxwell on the reports that the Dominican Government has resumed the direction of its own fiscal affairs, thus terminating the system by which an American syndicate collected the duties and paid the debts of the Government. This plan of private management was adopted some years ago, and it is understood that some Belgian bond owners raised an objection to it, this objection terminating in the present course of the authorities. The State Department will, however, await information from Consul-General Maxwell before taking action on the matter. Messrs. Holls, Abbott and Ward, of New-York, representing the San Domingo Improvement Company, have held long conferences with Acting Secretary Hill and Solicitor Penfield, of the State Department, concerning the reported repudiation of the company's grant by San Domingo. It is understood that a line of action is being formulated and a cable message of inquiry has been sent to the American representative in San Domingo.
