Spanish Diplomats Shun Son of Ex-King Alfonso Fear for Jobs if Republicans Should Learn of Their Entertaining Royalty; Visitors Flocking to Capital

By GEORGE ABELL

Visitors are arriving in Washington so thick and fast that one needs a good constitution, an Olympic marathon record and two typewriters to keep pace.

President Rafael Trujillo of the Dominican Republic is said to be coming here early in December. The legation knows nothing official, but rumors persist.

Pale, pleasant Don Juan de Bourbon, son of former King Alfonso of Spain, and his attractive bride are being feted at a number of rather small parties. The Ambassador of France and Mme. de Laboulaye went to the 1925 F Street Club to bow to him, together with several Italian and Belgian diplomats. Chief Justice Charles Evans Hughes diplomatically combed his white whiskers for the occasion.

Naturally, the Spanish Embassy isn't letting on that it even knows of Don Juan's existence. Most members of the staff would like to greet him, but they are scared for their republican jobs and salaries. Elegant, soft-spoken Bill Phillips, Undersecretary of State, was among those who met the Prince.

* * *

I GIVE YOU SIR MATTHEW!

Great Britain temporarily has taken the spotlight, so far as visitors are concerned. Lanky Vice Admiral Sir Matthew Best, commander-in-chief of the American and West Indies station, and Lady Best are being regally entertained.

In fact, the Capital is overrun with British officers, all saluting on street corners, rubbing elbows in private bars and treading on one another's feet at dances and receptions. The Italian Embassy in self-defense may be forced to import Signor Vittorio Buratti, a Roman senator who has just arrived in New York, as a counter-propagandist.

They do say, also, that Dr. Hantaro Nagaoka, of the Japanese House of Peers and the Japanese Imperial Academy, may also hop down from Manhattan—to show that the Nipponese Empire is still on the map. Only fly in the ointment being that Japanese Ambassador Hirosi Saito is out of town.

* * *

CHEWING GUM REMEDY

Incidentally, Mme. Hirosi Saito, small, dark wife of the Ambassador from Tokio, took an airplane ride for the first time in her life yesterday. She flew with her husband to Chicago.

"The trip," smiled Saito, landing in Cleveland, "is entirely social."

Mme. Saito smiled wanly. She had felt slightly ill en route, she admitted. A newspaper photographer stepped forward with a piece of chewing gum. She chewed it, felt better immediately, and posed for pictures.

"I think this must be, what you say, the life, is it not?" she inquired brightly.

* * *

HAMBRO AND KERN

Next week, the Hon. Carl Joachim Hambro, president of the Norwegian Parliament, hops into town. He'll be here only long enough to have dinner as the guest of Mr. and Mrs. William McClellan Ritter, then will hop back to New York.

Another visitor of note was the socialite mayor of Indianapolis, John Kern, who paid a visit on President Roosevelt. A Harvard man and former judge, Kern used to live in Virginia when his father was U. S. Senator. In 1906 the elder Kern was a candidate for the Vice Presidency.
