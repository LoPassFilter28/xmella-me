More Arms for Foes of Castro Seized by Miami Detectives

MIAMI, Fla., May 23 (AP).—Another batch of military equipment apparently destined for use in the uneasy Caribbean was seized by Miami detectives today.

An automobile loaded with 5,000 rounds of ammunition, machetes, and field tents was grabbed and its driver, Aroldo Fernandez Lieges, 23, was jailed. Unconfirmed reports said the raiding officers narrowly missed another car loaded with arms.

First reports said three men and a woman had been rounded up in today's raid, but police said only Fernandez Lieges was held.

The arrest followed Friday's roundup of 13 persons, including the Dominican Republic's consul general, in an alleged plot to arm foes of Cuban Premier Fidel Castro.

Timetable Upset

The Miami Herald quoted unidentified Cuban sources in Miami as saying the Friday seizure of the 13 persons and a C-47 transport plane loaded with arms and ammunition may have upset the timetable of a Cuban counter-revolution.

These sources said an attack against Premier Castro had been set for the first part of June, but now would be delayed.

The arms taken by customs agents, however, amounted to only a small fraction of the military equipment already put into the hands of the expeditionary force in Santo Domingo, these sources added.

In Havana, Cuba's defense minister said the Miami raids proved that followers of Fulgencio Batista, ousted dictator of Cuba, are preparing for a counterattack from the Dominican Republic, where Batista lives in exile.

"Every day we are better prepared to defend our revolution at whatever moment we are attacked," said the defense minister, Augusto Martinez Sanchez.

13 Are Charged

The Dominican Republic's consul general at Miami and 12 other persons, including a woman pilot, were charged after Friday's roundup with plotting to arm the enemies of Premier Castro.

Augusto Ferrando, the consul general; Jose M. Paulino, 41, Dominican vice consul, and Leonard Trento, 35-year-old Newark (N. J.) businessman, were ordered held on $25,000 bonds each. Paulino was held as a material witness.

All three were charged with conspiring to bribe Federal officers and with conspiring to export war materials illegally.

Also charged with conspiring to ship arms were Virginia L. Bland, 38, of Miami, attractive co-pilot of the plane, and two Tampa pilots, Samuel Eugene Poole, jr., 34, and John J. Embry, 38.

They denied any knowledge of the plane's cargo, which included 200,000 rounds of ammunition, 17 machine guns, 38 Garand rifles and parts for 20 carbines, or of its destination.

Chief Customs Agent Joseph Fortier said the arms were to be flown to the Dominican Republic for delivery to an anti-Castro expeditionary force.

Others arrested as participants in the alleged plot were: Joseph Liquori, 29-year-old Miami policeman; William Quitman Brookley, 30, University of Miami engineering student; Dominick E. Bartone, 45, Cleveland (Ohio) aircraft broker, and four Cuban nationals: Joseph Andrew Viera Valdes, 41; Elizio Carrado Tirana, 52; Pedro Santana, 39, and Oscar Pages, 35. Their bonds ranged from $5,000 to $500, excepting Liquori, whose bond was set at $10,000.

Liquori, who previously had served as bodyguard for Rafael Trujillo, jr., son of the Dominican Republic's chief of state, had been stationed at the Dominican Republic consulate in Miami at the request of officials there who said they feared an attack by Castro partisans.

United States Attorney James L. Guilmartin said Ferrando gave $1,400 to customs agents William B. Lankford and Wallace D. Shanley not to molest the arms shipment. They turned the case over to superiors.

All 13 denied knowledge of the alleged plot and posted bonds. Arraignment was set for June 8.

The plane was owned by Akros Dynamics, Inc., and was one of 11 Globemasters bought from the Air Materiel Command for $1.8 million. Alvin A. Maiman of Cleveland, Ohio, who owns 20 per cent of Akros said Bartone had leased the plane.
