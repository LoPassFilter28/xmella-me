MAY TAKE A HAND IN THE GAME THAT IS GOING ON IN SANTO DOMINGO. SUPPORTERS OF WOS-Y-GIL THINK THE UNITED STATES WILL HAVE TO INTERFERE. Steamer That Had Touched at Dominican and Haytien Ports Arrived at San Juan and Reported Conditions in the Republics—Revolution and Dissatisfaction Stalk Abroad—Attempts at Assassination Made.

San Juan, Dec. 20.—The Cuban steamer Julia, from Santo Domingo and Haiti, arrived here to-day, having on board Senor Espaillat, former Minister of War of Santo Domingo; Commander Peres of the Dominican army; Capt. Geirra, Col. Espaillat and other supporters of former President Wos-y-Gil.

The Julia also brought Juan Medine, a Porto Rican, who has been appointed by the provisional government of Santo Domingo as consul general to Porto Rico in succession to Senor Morales. The latter, however, refuses to surrender his office, claiming that the provisional government has not been recognized by the United States. He has turned over the effects of the consular office to Senor Galvan, a son of the former Dominican Minister of Finance. Senor Medine will appeal to Gov. Hunt to-morrow for official recognition.

The supporters of Gen. Wos-y-Gil held a long conference to-day. The members of the party arriving on the Julia express the belief that the intervention of the United States into the affairs of San Domingo will be found necessary in the near future. They declare a crisis is rapidly approaching and that the provisional government finds it impossible to negotiate loans.

The Julia brought news that former President Jiminez is now at Monte Cristi; that former Vice President Deschamps is in control of Puerto Plata, and that Gens. Morales and Caceres are fortifying the city of San Domingo.

It is said that an attempt was recently made to kill Gen. Jiminez, and that Gen. Caceres lives in imminent danger of assassination.

According to reports brought from Hayti, a revolution is feared there which may result in the assassination or at least the incarceration of several ministers and delegates.

The chief of the French bank at Port Au Prince has been imprisoned.

Te attitude of the people of Hayti toward President Nord is decidedly threatening, as they allege that he has been guilty of embezzling public funs.

It was stated in July last that there was a serious conflict between President Nord and the Chamber, the outcome of an alleged discovery of extensive frauds in the issue of Haytien government securities, the amount involved being $200,000.
