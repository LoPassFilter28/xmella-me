ADJUST DOMINICAN DEBT. The Island Republic Would Issue Bonds in Settlement. United States Government to Be Asked to Administer Customs as Guarantee for Loans.

New York, Sept. 20.—Frederico Velasquez, minister of finance and commerce of Santo Domingo, who has been in the United States for two months as special commissioner, seeking to adjust the financial affairs of his country, left New York on Wednesday for Santo Domingo by the steamer Cherokee. Before leaving Senor Velasquez made the following statement:

"The Dominican republic is desirous of adjusting its public indebtedness upon a basis which shall be fair and reasonable to the creditors and within its actual and prospective resources. To accomplish this it will ask the United States government to administer its customs and to apply the revenue therefrom to the service of a loan, the proceeds of which are to be devoted to adjusting the valid and established obligations of the republic upon a reasonable basis. In other words, instead of asking the United States both to supervise the adjustment of the debt and to administer the customs, it will itself seek to arrive at a fair adjustment with its creditors and thereafter ask the United States to administer its customs for the benefit alike of the creditors and the republic.

"Messrs. Kuhn, Loeb & Co., of New York city, have engaged to purchase upon favorable terms to the republic an issue of $20,000,000 of 5 per cent. sinking fund gold bonds, subject to the conditions and to be devoted to the purposes above stated.

"The Morton Trust company of New York city has also engaged to act as financial agents of the republic, both as depositary under the plan of creditor settlement and thereafter for the service of the loan. The plan of settlement to be offered to the creditors is now being prepared, and will be completed and issued at an early date."
