OAS Again Scrutinizes Haiti-Dominican Crisis

SANTO DOMINGO, Dominican Republic, May 14 (AP).—A peace mission of the Organization of American States arrived last night for another look at the Haitian-Dominican crisis, eased further by the Dominican Republic's announcement that it is withdrawing its troops from the Haitian border.

Part of the OAS fact-finding team was to fly on today to Port au Prince, where opponents of President Francois Duvalier have vowed to assassinate him by tomorrow.

The Dominican government's announcement gave no reason for the troop pullback.

The main consideration apparently was Haiti's gradual release of Duvalier opponents who had taken refuge in Latin American embassies in Port au Prince. Dominican President Juan Bosch rushed troops to the border two weeks ago after Haitian militiamen invaded the Dominican Embassy in search of refugees there.

Army Leaders Reluctant

The Dominican army hierarchy also was known to be reluctant to launch an attack against Haiti. There also has been some criticism here of Mr. Bosch because he did not consult the Dominican Congress before putting the army on a war footing.

Associated Press Correspondent William L. Ryan reported from Port au Prince that the consensus there was that Dr. Duvalier would survive to start a second presidential term despite opposition vows to destroy him by tomorrow.

Dr. Duvalier's first six-year term expires tomorrow. Although the Haitian constitution bars a President's re-election, he is to be inaugurated May 22 for a second six-year term to which he declared himself elected after a rigged election two years ago.

Mr. Ryan said fear of an uprising was evidenced by a steady exodus of Haitians from Port au Prince into the countryside. The movement reversed a normal trend of poverty-stricken country people seeking a better living in the city.

Opposition Leader Clement Barbot, once Dr. Duvalier's trusted deputy president, apparently was still heading an underground band somewhere in the country. Interior Minister Luc Francois said reports that Mr. Barbot had gone into asylum in the Argentine Embassy were not true.

Mr. Barbot, a master of terrorist tactics, has sworn to assassinate Dr. Duvalier by tomorrow. Circulars were distributed to Port au Prince several weeks ago threatening that a revolution would start tomorrow.

Most Latin-American members of the OAS hold the view that, repugnant as the Duvalier dictatorship is to them, they cannot interfere in Haiti's internal affairs.

American warships remained on emergency stations off Port au Prince to evacuate Americans from Haiti should it become necessary. The State Department last week moved out several hundred dependents of United States officials and military officers, but there has been no exodus of the estimated 1,600 other Americans living in Haiti.
