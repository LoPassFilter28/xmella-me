Society Again Gets Breath With Departure of Royalty And Summer Season Here Arrival of Gen. Rafael Trujillo, However, Will Revive Interests; Harriet Dawley Makes Debut

By MARGARET HART,

Society Editor.

Washington society is settling down to normalcy again after weeks marked with excitement and brilliant festivities for royal visitors. And, may it be said that the National's Capital liked royalty and for many years to come stories will be retold of the pleasant informality of these persons of royal birth. To those who had never met royalty before, the natural and wholesome manner in the royal visitors' conversations and the sincere interest displayed in all with whom they came in contact, was a revelation.

The visits of their Britannic Majesties, King George VI and Queen Elizabeth; the Crown Prince Frederik and Crown Princess Ingrid of Denmark and Iceland and the Crown Prince Olav and Crown Princess Martha of Norway all added indelibly to the pages of history of society in the Capital of our country, as well as furthering the bonds between this Nation and the kingdoms across the sea.

Queen and Princesses

Leave Fashion Impressions.

Fashion played no minor party in the happy impressions left behind by the visiting Queen and the two Princesses. On each and every occasion these royal ladies wore costumes that delighted the eyes of not only the fashioned-minded woman but, as well, the men who know not one color or material from another. Little is the doubt that the trend of style will be greatly swayed by the costumes and accessories selected by the lovely visitors for their stay in this country. Memory recalls the dainty parasols carried by the gracious Queen Elizabeth during her daytime appearances; the beautiful fan of ostrich feathers which complemented the striking ensemble worn by Princess Martha at one of the dinners in her honor as well as the fetching veiling effect which formed the crown of one of the Princess Ingrid's becoming picture hats.

Another Foreign Visitor

Is Due in Capital.

It never is quite safe to state that the curtain has been dropped on all formal entertaining in this town. For just when the social calendar appears absolutely blank something is just bound to happen. Members of society who are still lingering in the city will find the latter part of the week not quiet socially, but gay with festive parties for distinguished visitors.

The former President of the Dominican Republic, Gen. Rafael L. Trujillo, and now commander in chief of his country's army, will arrive in Washington Thursday. While he will spend much of his time visiting service posts around the Capital, several interesting social events will center around his stay here. Gen. Trujillo will have luncheon at the Senate Friday, in the afternoon will visit the Navy Yard and the Marine Barracks and that night will be the honor guest at dinner of Maj. Gen. Commandant of the Marine Corps Thomas Holcomb. The following day the visitor will visit Fort Meade, have luncheon at the Army Navy Club and in the evening will be feted at a reception at the Pan-American Union, with the Minister of his republic and Senora de Pastoriza as hosts.

Sunday, July 9, the army chief will be the guest in whose honor the Haitian Minister, Mr. Elie Lescot, will give a luncheon and the following day Gen. Trujillo will visit Quantico, where the commandant, Gen. James C. Breckinridge, will entertain him at luncheon after a review of the troops. The last event on the program for the visitor, who will leave the Capital Tuesday, will be the dinner Monday night given for him by the Chief of Staff, U. S. A., Maj. Gen. George C. Marshall. Also included on Gen. Trujillo's schedule will be a visit to Mount Vernon, Arlington National Cemetery and Federal housing projects in and near the Capital.

Another visitor who will arrive on the scene this week is Gen. Goes Monteiro, commander in chief of the Brazilian Army. Gen. Goes Monteiro is no stranger to Washington and during his last visit he was much feted. His wife, Senhora de Goes Monteiro, arrived in the city yesterday from New York, where she was met upon her arrival by the Brazilian Ambassador and Senhora de Martins. There undoubtedly will be parties for her as well as her husband. Included among the festivities planned for the Brazilian general is a dinner Friday night, with the Undersecretary of State, Mr. Sumner Welles as host, and a luncheon the following day given by the Assistant Secretary of War, Mr. Louis Johnson.

Miss Harriet Webster Dawley

Made Debut Yesterday.

The ranks of all circles of society in Washington are considerably depleted over this week end, with many planning to remain away until after the holiday. Many deferred their departure until late yesterday in order to attend the debut of Miss Harriet Webster Dawley, daughter of Mrs. M. Webster Dawley.
