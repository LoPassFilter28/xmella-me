New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints the close of a dispatch on the revolutionEl Tribune publica el cierre de un despacho sobre la revolución

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

The Kingston item says that, owing to the efforts of the Government of Santo Domingo to suppress news and information about the recent assassination of President Heureaux and the reported revolution in Santo Domingo, the facts in the case are difficult to obtain; but a dispatch received by a messenger who has passed through Hayti indicates that the assassination of President Heureaux was not on the programme, the purpose of the revolutionists being to capture Moca, with the President, thus decapitating the Dominican Government at the first blow, the insurgents not possessing the means of prosecuting a protracted conflict; the premature departure of President Heureaux threatened to frustrate this scheme, whereupon an enthusiast, who was watching the President's movements, committed the act; a subsequent attack on Moca being repulsed, the insurgents withdrew into the fastnesses between Moca and Porto Plata, hoping to secure sufficient accessions of men and arms to attack Porto Plata and prosecute the movement, in spite of its initial failure.

La nota de Kingston dice que, debido a los esfuerzos del Gobierno de Santo Domingo por suprimir las noticias y la información sobre el reciente asesinato del presidente Heureaux y la reportada revolución en Santo Domingo, los hechos del caso son difíciles de obtener; pero un despacho recibido por un mensajero que ha pasado por Haití indica que el asesinato del presidente Heureaux no estaba en el programa, siendo el propósito de los revolucionarios capturar Moca, con el Presidente, decapitando así al Gobierno dominicano al primer golpe, al no poseer los insurrectos los medios para sostener un conflicto prolongado; la partida prematura del presidente Heureaux amenazó con frustrar este plan, ante lo cual un entusiasta, que vigilaba los movimientos del presidente, cometió el acto; rechazado un ataque posterior contra Moca, los insurrectos se retiraron a las asperezas entre Moca y Puerto Plata, con la esperanza de conseguir suficientes refuerzos de hombres y armas para atacar Puerto Plata y proseguir el movimiento, a pesar de su fracaso inicial.

The Black Hills Union

The Union prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of July 28 with the details of the murderEl Union publica un despacho de Cabo Haitiano del 28 de julio con los detalles del asesinato

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

Details of the murder received here are to the effect that the president was assassinated as he was about to leave on horseback for Santiago de las Caballeros; the assassin, Ramon Caceros, surrounded by several accomplices, approached the president as he was talking with some friends and fired twice with a revolver; the first bullet struck the president in the left side and penetrated the heart, causing instant death; the second bullet killed an old man who was standing near the president; the assassin and his accomplices then fled to avoid the shots fired at them by the president's friends; it is not known whether any of them was wounded; the body of the president was taken to the palace of the governor of Moca; President Heureaux's death has caused a panic; it is said that Juan Isidoro Jiminez, who took part in the attempted insurrection of last year, is a candidate for the presidency; Jiminez landed from the Fanita, but was driven off with the loss of most of his band of ten men; the dispatch is headed 'Revenge on a President.'

Los detalles del asesinato recibidos aquí indican que el presidente fue asesinado cuando estaba a punto de partir a caballo hacia Santiago de los Caballeros; el asesino, Ramón Caceros, rodeado de varios cómplices, se acercó al presidente mientras conversaba con unos amigos y disparó dos veces con un revólver; la primera bala alcanzó al presidente en el costado izquierdo y le penetró el corazón, causándole la muerte instantánea; la segunda bala mató a un anciano que estaba cerca del presidente; el asesino y sus cómplices huyeron entonces para evitar los disparos que les hicieron los amigos del presidente; no se sabe si alguno de ellos resultó herido; el cuerpo del presidente fue llevado al palacio del gobernador de Moca; la muerte del presidente Heureaux ha causado pánico; se dice que Juan Isidoro Jiménez, que tomó parte en el intento de insurrección del año pasado, es candidato a la presidencia; Jiménez desembarcó del Fanita, pero fue rechazado con la pérdida de la mayoría de su banda de diez hombres; el despacho lleva por título «Revenge on a President».

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

Caceres is a cousin of General Horacio Vasquez and was with him at the time of the shooting. General Vasquez is a member of one of the best families of Moca and was for some years a partner in the house of Cosmo Batile, a firm well known there, and former agents of the Clyde Steamship company. Neither Mr. Caceres nor General Vasquez can be considered common assassins, as their motives were patriotic. They attacked the president in broad daylight and in an open space and not from an ambush of any kind nor even in the dark, although their little party was outnumbered 2 to 1 by the president and his bodyguard. For 14 years Heureaux has held power by a system of tyrannism. Persons were sent to prison without the slightest pretense of law and more than 300 were clandestinely shot.'

Cáceres es primo del general Horacio Vásquez y estaba con él en el momento del tiroteo. El general Vásquez es miembro de una de las mejores familias de Moca y fue durante algunos años socio de la casa de Cosmo Batile, firma muy conocida allí, y antiguos agentes de la Clyde Steamship Company. Ni el señor Cáceres ni el general Vásquez pueden ser considerados asesinos comunes, pues sus motivos fueron patrióticos. Atacaron al presidente a plena luz del día y en un espacio abierto, y no desde emboscada alguna ni siquiera en la oscuridad, aunque su pequeño grupo era superado 2 a 1 por el presidente y su guardia. Durante 14 años Heureaux ha mantenido el poder mediante un sistema de tiranía. Dakota Farmers' Leader

The Leader prints a sketch of the late president, Gen. HeureauxEl Leader publica una semblanza del difunto presidente, el general Heureaux

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

Gen. Heureaux was born in Porto Plata in 1846 and served in the war against Spain from 1868 to 1874; later he was put at the head of the advanced Dominicans, who sought to improve their country by inducing foreign capital to locate on the island; as he advanced, all the wealthy inhabitants joined his forces, and when he arrived at the gates of the historic city of Santo Domingo he was chosen president of the republic and served his term of four years; at the expiration of every four years an electoral college assembles in the city to vote for a president for the following term, but owing to Gen. Heureaux's popularity and ability he has always been unanimously re-elected, and was serving his fifth term.

El general Heureaux nació en Puerto Plata en 1846 y sirvió en la guerra contra España de 1868 a 1874; después fue puesto al frente de los dominicanos avanzados, que buscaban mejorar su país atrayendo capital extranjero a la isla; a medida que avanzaba, todos los habitantes acaudalados se unieron a sus fuerzas, y cuando llegó a las puertas de la histórica ciudad de Santo Domingo fue elegido presidente de la república y sirvió su período de cuatro años; al expirar cada cuatro años, un colegio electoral se reúne en la ciudad para votar por un presidente para el período siguiente, pero debido a la popularidad y la capacidad del general Heureaux siempre ha sido reelegido por unanimidad, y servía su quinto período.

Pawtucket Gazette and Chronicle

The Gazette prints an editorial on Santo Domingo: the countryLa Gazette publica un editorial sobre Santo Domingo: el país

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

Present reports affirm that the assassin of President Heureaux was the son of an offender whom he had put to death over a dozen years ago; the young man's name was Caceros, and he came to our country shortly after his father's death, attended one of our higher literary institutions, but seems to have brooded over his father's death and fostered revenge; he shot his victim as he was preparing to mount his horse, and the whole land is convulsed. The population is about six hundred thousand; Vice President Figuereo assumes control of public affairs, but how long he may have peaceable supremacy is uncertain; and it is asserted that Gen. Gomez is designing to leave Cuba to see if he can obtain authority in San Domingo. The Dominican republic and Hayti, the editorial says, occupy different parts of the island formerly called by the latter name: Hayti has control of the western part, while San Domingo claims the eastern section; the republic contains not far from twenty thousand square miles of territory, of great fertility, its area about equal to the combined area of New Hampshire and Vermont; it used to produce a great deal of coffee, though of an inferior quality, and it furnishes mahogany.

Los informes actuales afirman que el asesino del presidente Heureaux era hijo de un delincuente a quien él había hecho ejecutar hacía más de una docena de años; el joven se llamaba Caceros y vino a nuestro país poco después de la muerte de su padre, asistió a una de nuestras instituciones literarias superiores, pero parece haber rumiado la muerte de su padre y fomentado la venganza; disparó contra su víctima cuando se preparaba a montar su caballo, y todo el país está convulsionado. La población es de unos seiscientos mil habitantes; el vicepresidente Figuereo asume el control de los asuntos públicos, pero es incierto cuánto tiempo podrá mantener la supremacía en paz; y se afirma que el general Gómez se propone salir de Cuba para ver si puede obtener autoridad en San Domingo. La república dominicana y Haití, dice el editorial, ocupan partes diferentes de la isla llamada antiguamente por este último nombre: Haití controla la parte occidental, mientras que San Domingo reclama la sección oriental; la república contiene cerca de veinte mil millas cuadradas de territorio, de gran fertilidad, con una superficie aproximadamente igual a la superficie combinada de New Hampshire y Vermont; antes producía mucho café, aunque de calidad inferior, y suministra caoba.

The trouble between the Government of San Domingo and its creditors has reached an acute stage; the creditors, who are almost entirely Americans, have begun to clamor for an American protectorate, which would necessarily be the first step in the direction of annexation; political plots are already shaping themselves; the adherents of Gen. August 5, 18995 de agosto de 1899

I had no connection with Ramon Caceres, who did the shooting. I do not believe that the United States will interfere with my government in San Domingo, which will be a government peaceful and helpful to all, for the United States did not interfere with the government of Heureaux, who robbed and murdered for fourteen years.' General Jiminez admits that he has often been to the United States incognito, but says he has not had interviews with official persons; he considers that there is no need of promoting expeditions to land in San Domingo, as there are already a thousand men waiting for him, a force which he deems quite sufficient for his purpose; referring to the future relations of San Domingo with the United States, he expressed a desire to be on friendly terms with Washington, and said he believed he could count upon American sympathy; Gomez he referred to as 'esteemed by all classes of Dominicans, but not sought for as President'; and the people of San Domingo, he declares, are impetuous, and are urging him to come to them at once, but for a time he prefers delay, as 'each day is worth a battle won.' The funeral item, from San Domingo, August 4, says solemn funeral services in memory of the late President Heureaux were held in the Cathedral to-day, and the diplomatic and consular bodies were present.

No tuve ninguna relación con Ramón Cáceres, que hizo los disparos. El general Jiminez admite que ha estado a menudo en los Estados Unidos de incógnito, pero dice que no ha tenido entrevistas con personas oficiales; considera que no hay necesidad de promover expediciones para desembarcar en San Domingo, ya que ya hay mil hombres esperándolo, una fuerza que juzga bastante suficiente para su propósito; al referirse a las relaciones futuras de San Domingo con los Estados Unidos, expresó su deseo de mantener relaciones amistosas con Washington y dijo que creía poder contar con la simpatía americana; de Gómez se refirió como 'estimado por todas las clases de dominicanos, pero no buscado como presidente'; y el pueblo de San Domingo, declara, es impetuoso y lo insta a ir junto a ellos de inmediato, pero por un tiempo prefiere la demora, ya que 'cada día vale una batalla ganada'. La nota fúnebre, desde San Domingo, el 4 de agosto, dice que hoy se celebraron en la Catedral solemnes servicios fúnebres en memoria del difunto presidente Heureaux, y que estuvieron presentes los cuerpos diplomático y consular.
