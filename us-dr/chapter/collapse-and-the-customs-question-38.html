Evening Star

The Star prints an item headed 'Minister Powell's Report' and a Norfolk dispatch of October 30El Star publica una nota titulada «Minister Powell's Report» y un despacho de Norfolk del 30 de octubre

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

A cablegram received at the State Department to-day from United States Minister Powell at San Domingo reports that conditions in the republic are in a state of great disturbance; the State Department is making vigorous efforts to communicate with Mr. Powell by cable, but with unsatisfactory results, owing to the fact that the insurgents appear to be in possession of the land telegraph lines connecting the capital, San Domingo, with the cable station on the east coast; incidentally this has also interrupted communication with Venezuela. To guard against any further cable lapses the State Department is sending, through the Navy Department, written instructions to Minister Powell for his guidance during the revolution, and these will go forward on the Baltimore, which sails to-day from Hampton Roads directly for San Domingo. The Norfolk dispatch says the cruiser Baltimore sailed at high tide this afternoon for Puerto Plata, San Domingo, where she goes to protect American interests at that port.

Un cablegrama recibido hoy en el Departamento de Estado del ministro estadounidense Powell en Santo Domingo informa que las condiciones en la república están en un estado de gran agitación; el Departamento de Estado hace esfuerzos vigorosos para comunicarse con el señor Powell por cable, pero con resultados insatisfactorios, debido a que los insurgentes parecen estar en posesión de las líneas telegráficas terrestres que conectan la capital, Santo Domingo, con la estación de cable en la costa este; incidentalmente esto también ha interrumpido la comunicación con Venezuela. Para precaverse contra nuevas interrupciones del cable, el Departamento de Estado envía, por medio del Departamento de la Marina, instrucciones escritas al ministro Powell para su orientación durante la revolución, y estas irán en el Baltimore, que zarpa hoy de Hampton Roads directamente hacia Santo Domingo. El despacho de Norfolk dice que el crucero Baltimore zarpó con la marea alta esta tarde hacia Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, a donde va a proteger los intereses estadounidenses en ese puerto.

Oakes Republican

The Republican prints the closing part of the Santiago dispatchEl Republican publica la parte final del despacho de Santiago

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The revolution which has broken out in the northern part of the republic of Santo Domingo and which has already resulted in the establishment of a provisional government at Puerto Plata under the presidency of General Morales, was caused, according to advices received here, by the numerous custom house frauds and the prevarications of the ministers of war and finance. The signal for the outbreak of the revolt was given at 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots. That same evening all the partisans of President Woss y Gil at Puerto Plata were arrested. The inhabitants of Monte Cristi, La Ligne and Moca united and attacked Santiago. Telegraphic communication between Santiago and La Vega has been severed. The revolutionary outbreak is extending and the general opinion is that the government of President Woss y Gil is lost. Vice President Deschamps has abandoned the government and has arrived here in a small sailboat from Monte Cristi. The Paris item says advices received from Cape Haytien, Hayti, confirm the reports that the revolutionary movement in the republic of Santo Domingo is in favor of former President Jiminez; the revolution is spreading, and, according to the dispatches received here, all the inhabitants of the region of Monte Cibao have joined the uprising against the government; and the election of General Morales as president of the provisional government is said to be a step taken pending the arrival of General Jiminez.

La revolución que ha estallado en la parte norte de la república de Santo Domingo y que ya ha resultado en el establecimiento de un gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata bajo la presidencia del general Morales, fue causada, según informes recibidos aquí, por los numerosos fraudes de aduanas y las prevaricaciones de los ministros de Guerra y de Hacienda. La señal para el estallido de la revuelta se dio a las 2 de la tarde del sábado con tres cañonazos. Esa misma noche todos los partidarios del presidente Woss y Gil en Puerto Plata fueron arrestados. Los habitantes de Monte Cristi, La Ligne y Moca se unieron y atacaron Santiago. La comunicación telegráfica entre Santiago y La Vega ha sido cortada. El estallido revolucionario se extiende y la opinión general es que el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil está perdido. El vicepresidente Deschamps ha abandonado el gobierno y ha llegado aquí en un pequeño bote de vela desde Monte Cristi. La nota de París dice que los informes recibidos desde Cabo Haitiano, Haití, confirman los reportes de que el movimiento revolucionario en la república de Santo Domingo es a favor del expresidente Jiménez; la revolución se extiende, y, según los despachos recibidos aquí, todos los habitantes de la región de Monte Cibao se han unido al levantamiento contra el gobierno; y se dice que la elección del general Morales como presidente del gobierno provisional es un paso dado a la espera de la llegada del general Jiménez.

También denunciaba un impuesto al petróleo de 8 centavos por galón, un préstamo de 120.000 dólares, un anticipo de 40.000 dólares de la Hamburg-American Line y un proyecto para declarar neutrales las aguas dominicanas y libres los puertos en caso de guerra.

New-York Tribune

Cartridges for San DomingoCartuchos para Santo Domingo

November 1, 19031 de noviembre de 1903

It is asserted by exporters in this city, doing business with San Domingo, that the insurrection will be suppressed before it makes much headway. General Carlos Morales, who heads the movement, has been governor of Puerto Plata under the present government, having fought against President Vasquez to place General Woss y Gil in power; in fact, General Morales has rebelled against every government under which he has at various times served; he was formerly a priest, but renounced the church for politics, and it has long been his ambition to be president. General Juan Isidro Jiminez, ex-president of San Domingo, who left this city a short time ago and is now at Mayaguez, Porto Rico, is also anxious to get back to power; President Woss y Gil appointed him fiscal agent to the United States and Europe, with a salary of $12,000 a year, to keep him quiet, it is said; he resigned this office, however, that he might take part in the new revolutionary movement; and it is not considered likely that he will be selected as chief executive, even in the event of rebel success, which is regarded as improbable.
