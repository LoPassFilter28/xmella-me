A General Attack Expected—Minister Powell Refuses to Recognize the Insurgents—The New York Fired On.SITUACIÓN EN SANTO DOMINGO LOS INSURGENTES BOMBARDEAN LA CIUDAD. Se espera un ataque general—El ministro Powell se niega a reconocer a los insurgentes—El New York tiroteado

November 20, 190320 de noviembre de 1903

The Messenger prints the closing part of a San Domingo dispatch: a general attack is expected within the next few days; General Woss y Gil refuses to capitulate and it is believed the fighting will be severe; the situation is desperate and fighting in the streets is likely to occur at any moment; the United States cruiser Baltimore has been compelled to leave to re-coal; United States Minister Powell is endeavoring to protect American interests with the limited means at his disposal; a German naval vessel is ready to land troops at a moment's notice; the insurgents endeavored to have Minister Powell recognize them, but the minister refused to do so; the revolutionists fired on the Clyde Line steamer New York as she was entering the port of Samana, but the vessel was uninjured; the Dominican government has appointed Minister of Foreign Relations Galvan and Judge George Gray of Delaware as commissioners to arbitrate the Santo Domingo Improvement matter, as the result of Minister Powell's effort to compel the carrying out of the terms of the protocol, and Commissioner Galvan will leave for the United States tomorrow, though it is possible the insurgents may endeavor to capture him.

El Messenger publica la parte final de un despacho de San Domingo: se espera un ataque general dentro de los próximos días; el general Woss y Gil se niega a capitular y se cree que la lucha será severa; la situación es desesperada y la lucha en las calles puede ocurrir en cualquier momento; el crucero estadounidense Baltimore se ha visto obligado a partir a recargar carbón; el ministro estadounidense Powell se esfuerza por proteger los intereses estadounidenses con los limitados medios a su disposición; un buque de guerra alemán está listo para desembarcar tropas en cualquier momento; los insurgentes trataron de que el ministro Powell los reconociera, pero el ministro se negó a hacerlo; los revolucionarios dispararon contra el vapor New York, de la línea Clyde, cuando entraba al puerto de Samaná, pero el buque no sufrió daños; el gobierno dominicano ha nombrado al ministro de Relaciones Exteriores Galván y al juez George Gray, de Delaware, como comisionados para arbitrar el asunto de la Santo Domingo Improvement, como resultado del esfuerzo del ministro Powell por obligar al cumplimiento de los términos del protocolo, y el comisionado Galván partirá mañana hacia los Estados Unidos, aunque es posible que los insurgentes traten de capturarlo.

Evening Capital

The Capital prints a feature under the head 'San Domingo's Queer Soldiers'El Capital publica un reportaje bajo el titular «San Domingo's Queer Soldiers»

November 20, 190320 de noviembre de 1903

The Capital prints a feature under the head 'San Domingo's Queer Soldiers': some of the queerest soldiers in the world are to be found in San Domingo, or the Dominican Republic, as it is officially called; an Englishman who ran a steamship business in San Domingo City says there were four or five generals among the hundred coal carriers he employed, who coaled the steamers by carrying big baskets on their heads at 25 cents a day, and now and then would take a day off to review their armies, conduct manoeuvres or fight revolutionists; one of these generals, who had no uniform, stole a pair of gold-laced 'pants' from a shop, was caught with the goods and punished by being paraded around town on a donkey, face toward the tail, with the 'pants' tied around his neck; the soldiers are not volunteers, for when the government wants troops it simply sends into the country districts and seizes the laborers in the fields and the travelers on the roads, and though at first naturally discontented, they like the new job when they find that soldiers have no work to do; they are furnished with a suit of clothes every year, but it does not last them long, for they are inveterate gamblers and after they have lost all their money they gamble away their uniform, keeping nothing save their rifles, an old pair of trousers and a ragged shirt.

El Capital publica un reportaje bajo el titular «San Domingo's Queer Soldiers»: algunos de los soldados más extraños del mundo se encuentran en San Domingo, o República Dominicana, como se le llama oficialmente; un inglés que dirigía un negocio de vapores en la ciudad de Santo Domingo dice que había cuatro o cinco generales entre los cien carboneros que empleaba, quienes acarreaban el carbón a los vapores llevando grandes cestas sobre la cabeza a 25 centavos al día, y de vez en cuando tomaban un día libre para pasar revista a sus ejércitos, dirigir maniobras o combatir a los revolucionarios; uno de estos generales, que no tenía uniforme, robó de una tienda un par de «pantalones» con galones dorados, fue sorprendido con la mercancía y castigado haciéndolo desfilar por el pueblo montado en un burro, de cara a la cola, con los «pantalones» atados al cuello; los soldados no son voluntarios, pues cuando el gobierno quiere tropas simplemente envía a los distritos del campo y se apodera de los labradores en los campos y de los viajeros en los caminos, y aunque al principio están naturalmente descontentos, les gusta el nuevo trabajo cuando descubren que los soldados no tienen nada que hacer; se les entrega un traje cada año, pero no les dura mucho, porque son jugadores inveterados y después de perder todo su dinero apuestan hasta el uniforme, sin guardar nada más que sus fusiles, un viejo par de pantalones y una camisa harapienta.

November 20, 190320 de noviembre de 1903

Associated Press dispatches from San Domingo Tuesday said that, yielding to the continued vigorous action of Minister Powell, the Dominican government had appointed Minister Galvan and Judge George Gray of Delaware to arbitrate the difficulties between that government and the San Domingo Improvement Company, an American concern, that Minister Galvan was to leave immediately for the United States, and that on his departure the insurgents might make an effort to capture him; the Improvement Company was operating under several concessions obtained from the Dominican government, when an attempt was made to withdraw the privileges granted under these concessions. Bajo el titular «Recognition Refused Revolutionists by United States», el periódico informa que los revolucionarios de San Domingo solicitaron el martes al Departamento de Estado el reconocimiento por los Estados Unidos, siendo presentada la solicitud por J. The Providence News

The News prints part of an editorial on Samana Bay: in spite of the factEl News publica parte de un editorial sobre la bahía de Samaná: a pesar de que este país está en posesión de San Juan, de Culebra y de Guantánamo

November 23, 190323 de noviembre de 1903

The News prints part of an editorial on Samana Bay: in spite of the fact that this country is in possession of San Juan, of Culebra and of Guantanamo, it would be in a stronger and more impregnable position than ever if it acquired Samana Bay also, which besides being easily defended affords a harbor in which the whole American navy could ride in safety, without encountering the dangers of being bottled up as was Cervera's fleet at Santiago; should this country conclude to make the purchase, the fact will in no way affect the sovereignty of the Dominican republic, while we will be relieved of the incessant trouble and annoyance that would inevitably otherwise result; a man of great experience and keen observation has declared Samana Bay to be 'one of those maritime positions that are encountered in only two or three places in the world; it is not only the military key but the commercial key to this part of the world'; the advantages of Samana, the paper says, are as fully realized at Washington as anywhere else.

El News publica parte de un editorial sobre la bahía de Samaná: a pesar de que este país está en posesión de San Juan, de Culebra y de Guantánamo, estaría en una posición más fuerte y más inexpugnable que nunca si adquiriera también la bahía de Samaná, que además de ser fácilmente defendida ofrece un puerto en el que toda la marina estadounidense podría permanecer en seguridad, sin correr el peligro de quedar embotellada como lo fue la flota de Cervera en Santiago; si este país decidiera hacer la compra, el hecho en nada afectaría la soberanía de la república dominicana, y nos libraríamos de los incesantes problemas y molestias que inevitablemente resultarían en caso contrario; un hombre de gran experiencia y aguda observación ha declarado que la bahía de Samaná es «una de esas posiciones marítimas que se encuentran en solo dos o tres lugares en el mundo; no es solo la llave militar sino la llave comercial de esta parte del mundo»; las ventajas de Samaná, dice el periódico, se comprenden en Washington tan plenamente como en cualquier otro lugar.

The Tribune prints part of an item on the New York's arrival: she came in fully a day ahead of her best schedule time, because she had so little cargo to pick up from the ports outside of San Domingo which are in the hands of the insurgents, and brought only one passenger and about one-third her usual cargo; Captain Morimon and the other officers of the New York say they do not think that the Cherokee will have any trouble in making the five ports which the Dominican government has declared 'closed,' as they say no attempt at blockading them was in evidence when they left; they made all these ports, discharging cargo and mails and landing passengers unmolested, and the only passenger brought to this city was from Puerto Plata, one of the five 'closed' ports; on the trip down the New York was fired on by the insurgents as she tried to enter Samana Bay at 4 o'clock on the morning of November 11; 'It was just growing light,' said Captain Morimon, 'and we were heading in toward the entrance of the bay, when a shell from one of the forts passed close under our stern; I sounded three whistles, and no more shots were fired; later an apology was made, and the explanation offered that we were mistaken for a government gunboat'; the captain said his ship was much larger than the gunboats, and that it was bright moonlight when the shot was fired; the purser's coat was cut by a shell as he was going to the custom house.

El Tribune publica parte de una nota sobre la llegada del New York: el vapor llegó con un día completo de adelanto sobre su mejor horario, porque tenía muy poca carga que recoger de los puertos fuera de San Domingo que están en manos de los insurgentes, y trajo solo un pasajero y alrededor de un tercio de su carga habitual; el capitán Morimon y los demás oficiales del New York dicen que no creen que el Cherokee tenga ningún problema para hacer los cinco puertos que el gobierno dominicano ha declarado «cerrados», pues dicen que ningún intento de bloquearlos era evidente cuando partieron; hicieron todos esos puertos, descargando carga y correo y desembarcando pasajeros sin ser molestados, y el único pasajero traído a esta ciudad procedía de Puerto Plata, uno de los cinco puertos «cerrados»; en el viaje de ida el New York fue atacado a tiros por los insurgentes cuando trataba de entrar a la bahía de Samaná a las 4 de la mañana del 11 de noviembre; «Apenas comenzaba a clarear», dijo el capitán Morimon, «y nos dirigíamos hacia la entrada de la bahía, cuando un proyectil de uno de los fuertes pasó cerca, bajo nuestra popa; toqué tres silbatos, y no se hicieron más disparos; más tarde se presentó una disculpa, y la explicación ofrecida fue que nos habían confundido con una cañonera del gobierno»; el capitán dijo que su buque era mucho más grande que las cañoneras, y que había una brillante luna cuando se hizo el disparo; el abrigo del sobrecargo fue cortado por un proyectil cuando se dirigía a la aduana.

The Star prints part of the profile of General Deschamps, an official of the Dominican republic in the government of President Alejandro Woss y Gil up to a few days ago, when he resigned his position to join his former chief, ex-President Juan Isidro Jiminez: General Deschamps is one of the most popular men in the Dominican republic; his popularity is not, however, due to military prowess, but to the fact that he has always been the people's friend, is a great lover of progress, has always had the tact to espouse the right cause, and above all because he has been the victim of the most terrible persecution; due to his bold opposition to President Heureaux's sanguinary and corrupted policy he was forced to leave Santo Domingo and live abroad, where he had to suffer many hardships; although living in exile he did not slacken in his attacks upon President Heureaux's policy, and about seven years ago, while residing in Ponce, Porto Rico, an assassin paid by President Heureaux to assassinate him drove a dagger into his back, very nearly killing him; the assassin escaped and Deschamps slowly recovered, but he has never succeeded in making the wound heal thoroughly, and it is a constant source of annoyance to him; at another time his wife, who had accompanied him in exile, embarked in a small schooner at the British island of Grand Turk for Puerto Plata, but on her arrival at that port she was not permitted to disembark, and though she was accompanied by her children and the eldest boy took ill on board, still she was not permitted to disembark.

El Star publica parte del perfil del general Deschamps, un funcionario de la república dominicana en el gobierno del presidente Alejandro Woss y Gil hasta hace pocos días, cuando renunció a su cargo para unirse a su antiguo jefe, el expresidente Juan Isidro Jiménez: el general Deschamps es uno de los hombres más populares de la república dominicana; su popularidad no se debe, sin embargo, a sus proezas militares, sino al hecho de que siempre ha sido amigo del pueblo, es un gran amante del progreso, siempre ha tenido el tacto de abrazar la causa correcta y, sobre todo, porque ha sido víctima de la más terrible persecución; debido a su audaz oposición a la política sanguinaria y corrupta del presidente Heureaux se vio obligado a salir de Santo Domingo y vivir en el extranjero, donde tuvo que sufrir muchas penurias; aunque vivía en el exilio no aflojó en sus ataques contra la política del presidente Heureaux, y hace unos siete años, mientras residía en Ponce, Puerto Rico, un asesino pagado por el presidente Heureaux para asesinarlo le clavó un puñal en la espalda, casi matándolo; el asesino escapó y Deschamps se recuperó lentamente, pero nunca ha logrado que la herida sane por completo, y es para él una fuente constante de molestia; en otra ocasión su esposa, que lo había acompañado en el exilio, se embarcó en una pequeña goleta en la isla británica de Gran Turca rumbo a Puerto Plata, pero a su llegada a ese puerto no se le permitió desembarcar, y aunque la acompañaban sus hijos y el niño mayor enfermó a bordo, aun así no se le permitió desembarcar.

The Star prints a sketch of General Ramon Caceres, the slayer of President Heureaux on June 26, 1899 and ex-governor of Santiago de los Caballeros: he is one of the chief leaders of the present revolution, ranks next to Jiminez, and will most likely be appointed to the vice presidency should the revolution triumph; General Caceres is a man of great energy and courage and has set his heart upon the highest position in the republic, but he is unfortunately a man of the sword and not of the pen, and although he might make an excellent president for the quelling of revolutions, he would certainly not know how to set about extracting the republic from its present financial difficulties, which are many and pressing; if the present revolution triumphs it will have many claims upon its hands: there is a Cuban claim for preventing the Cuban steamer Maria Herrera to enter the port of Puerto Plata to land her passengers, mail and cargo without there being any notification whatever of the existence of a regular blockade; there is just such another claim to be made by the Clyde Line of steamers, there is a German claim, and there is said to be a Spanish claim for the death of the chancellor of the Spanish consulate who was killed by a bullet while writing in his office during the last revolution, eight months ago, and others; meanwhile the resources of the country have been completely paralyzed, and sugar, which is the chief product, is lacking a market, the American market being the only one it ever had.

The Sun prints a New York item: the Clyde Line steamer New York was exposed at the capital, Santo Domingo, to the firing between the government and insurgent forces; entering the bay of Samana at 4 o'clock in the morning, she was fired on close to the ship from the insurgent fort; Captain Marmion of the New York said: 'We were entering the west channel about 4 o'clock in the morning, and I was standing on the bridge when I heard a report and a shell whistled over my head; the channel is very narrow and the shore rises into a high bluff; I never knew that bluff to be fortified before, in all my experience with Dominican revolutions, but learned later that the insurgents had two guns up there; it was impossible for them to hit us because they were so far above.'
