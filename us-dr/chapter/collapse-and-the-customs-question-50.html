United States Minister Powell's reports to the State Department for the last six months have been a series of accounts; at Samana, last month, a band of insurgents attacked the United States consulate and removed two refugees who had sought shelter there; at Santo Domingo, the insurgents fired deliberately upon American seamen and killed Machinist Johnson; telephone and telegraph lines belonging to Americans have been cut in various parts of the island, and Americans who have ranches in the Dominican Republic have been persecuted by insurgents, who have killed cattle and offered every possible insult to American subjects.

Deseret Evening News

The News prints a signed description of Santo Domingo, its harborsEl News publica una descripción firmada de Santo Domingo, sus puertos

March 12, 190412 de marzo de 1904

There are a number of small cities in the republic: Santo Domingo the capital, and Puerto Plata the principal port, at about the same number of inhabitants; La Vega about 10,000, Santiago 8,000 and Samana 2,000. The oldest settlement made in the New World was on this island, Columbus himself having founded it, and here the great discoverer was buried for a number of years; it was the capital of the Spanish possessions in the New World. At the end of the eighteenth century Spain ceded the eastern half of the island to France, which held the western half; soon after, the description says, the whole country under Toussaint Louverture gained its independence, and there followed the rebellion of the eastern portion of the island, resulting in the formation of the present Dominican Republic. Outside of the commercial element, there is said to be much opposition to the idea of annexation to the United States.

Existen varias poblaciones pequeñas en la república: Santo Domingo, la capital, y Puerto Plata, el puerto principal, con más o menos el mismo número de habitantes; La Vega, unos 10,000; Santiago, 8,000, y Samaná, 2,000. El asentamiento más antiguo del Nuevo Mundo estuvo en esta isla, fundado por el propio Colón, y aquí estuvo enterrado el gran descubridor durante varios años; fue la capital de las posesiones españolas en el Nuevo Mundo. A fines del siglo XVIII, España cedió la mitad oriental de la isla a Francia, que poseía la mitad occidental; poco después, dice la descripción, todo el país bajo Toussaint Louverture obtuvo su independencia, y siguió la rebelión de la porción oriental de la isla, que dio por resultado la formación de la actual República Dominicana. Fuera del elemento comercial, se dice que existe mucha oposición a la idea de la anexión a los Estados Unidos.

Essex County Herald

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinionEl Herald publica el final de un examen de opinión

March 25, 190425 de marzo de 1904

The Philadelphia Ledger says that a 'military occupation of Santo Domingo, purely as an exercise of international police power, is the measure suggested by present conditions.' The Philadelphia Record thinks that if we attempt to police the island it will gradually become a ward of our government: 'A government cannot play the role of a big policeman within the limits of another state without becoming the protector of the latter. The sovereignty of a region resides where the power to preserve order resides, and when one takes over the sovereignty the territory usually goes with it.' Russia understood this fully, the Record says, when she undertook to establish settled conditions in and suppress the banditti of Manchuria. A military administrator put 'temporarily' in charge of the administration of Santo Domingo would be likely to undergo a slow and gradual transformation into a subordinate of the bureau of insular affairs in the war department. The Brooklyn Eagle thinks that there is little hope for any of the West Indies 'except as wards of this nation', and the St. Louis Globe-Democrat, which has advocated annexation of Santo Domingo for some time, says that nothing short of American supervision, which will amount to a virtual protectorate, will end the misrule of the island's ambitious politicians and their followers.

El Herald publica el final de un examen de la opinión editorial, y el Atlanta Journal, el Buffalo Express, el Macon Telegraph, el Philadelphia Ledger, el New Orleans Times-Democrat y el New York Globe denuncian todos la idea de la anexión. El Philadelphia Ledger dice que una «ocupación militar de Santo Domingo, puramente como ejercicio de poder policial internacional, es la medida que sugieren las condiciones presentes». El Philadelphia Record cree que si intentamos vigilar la isla, esta se convertirá gradualmente en pupila de nuestro gobierno: «Un gobierno no puede desempeñar el papel de gran policía dentro de los límites de otro Estado sin convertirse en protector de este último. La soberanía de una región reside donde reside el poder de preservar el orden, y cuando uno asume la soberanía, el territorio suele ir con ella». Rusia lo entendió plenamente, dice el Record, cuando emprendió establecer condiciones de orden y suprimir a los bandidos de Manchuria. Un administrador militar puesto «temporalmente» a cargo de la administración de Santo Domingo probablemente sufriría una transformación lenta y gradual hasta convertirse en subordinado de la oficina de asuntos insulares del Departamento de Guerra. El Brooklyn Eagle cree que existe poca esperanza para cualquiera de las Antillas «excepto como pupilas de esta nación», y el St. Louis Globe-Democrat, que ha abogado por la anexión de Santo Domingo desde hace algún tiempo, dice que nada menos que una supervisión estadounidense, que equivaldrá a un protectorado virtual, pondrá fin al desgobierno de los políticos ambiciosos de la isla y sus seguidores.

The New-York Tribune recounted political maneuvers during President Buenaventura Báez's historical efforts to annex the Dominican Republic to the United States. The Advertiser prints an editorial survey of the island's pastEl Advertiser publica un examen editorial del pasado de la isla

April 8, 19048 de abril de 1904

The republic has a population of somewhat more than 610,000; suffrage is universal in theory, at least, and a president is supposed to be elected once every four years, but the editorial asserts that the people are absolutely incapable of self-rule, and that the president is any man who is able to seize the government by force and to hold it. General Jiminez sailed from Monte Cristi last week, after the city had surrendered to the government forces. He alleges he is in the United States to rest and is not here for the purpose of buying arms and ammunition to be used in resisting the authority of President Morales. Statements made by General Jiminez in New York yesterday do not agree with reports made to the State Department concerning the conditions in Santo Domingo: it has been reported officially and unofficially many times that President Morales has the situation well under control and is far the strongest man in the republic, a leader who has the support of practically all factions.
