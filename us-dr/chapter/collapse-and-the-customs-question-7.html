Evening Times-Republican

The Washington officials are not crossing any bridges until they comeLos funcionarios de Washington no se preocupan por puentes que aún no llegan

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

The Evening Times-Republican reported that Washington officials were prepared to intervene and establish a protectorate over the Dominican Republic if violent conflict erupted over the succession to the assassinated President Heureaux. Cuban newspapers debated remarks by General Maximo Gomez's secretary suggesting foreign intervention would be required to maintain order on the island. Meanwhile, Dominican Vice President Wenceslao Figuereo reportedly accepted the presidency to maintain constitutional continuity.

El Evening Times-Republican informó que las autoridades en Washington estaban preparadas para intervenir y establecer un protectorado sobre la República Dominicana si estallaba un conflicto violento por la sucesión del asesinado presidente Heureaux. Periódicos cubanos debatieron declaraciones del secretario del general Máximo Gómez en las que sugería que se requeriría una intervención extranjera para preservar el orden en la isla. Entretanto, según se informó, el vicepresidente dominicano Wenceslao Figuereo asumió la presidencia para asegurar la continuidad constitucional.

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

I want to emphasize this prophecy — that the United States will eventually be compelled to interfere to establish peace in the island, just as this country did in Cuba. I favor the policy of peace. I shall be glad to return to my country and support such a policy if it is inaugurated by Gen. Figuereo, the vice-president, who succeeds to the head of the government. Pero si no se hace algo rápidamente para establecer el orden y la paz, seguirán más expediciones revolucionarias, como la del gen. Jiminez y el gen. The Times

The Times prints part of an editorial on Santo Domingo and annexationEl Times publica parte de un editorial sobre Santo Domingo y la anexión

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

The Times prints part of an editorial on Santo Domingo and annexation: the country might have been had without bloodshed and heavy expenditure through the forces of 'benevolent assimilation,' which would have been set in motion; what we foolishly refused to accept then as a free offering we may have to fight for in the not distant future; nothing has occurred since 1871 to kill the spirit of genuine Americanism which animated the request of the Dominicans for annexation to this country — it represents one of those impulses of a people which can be chilled but not slain by indifference; but the natural enemies of the project have grown stronger through organization than they were twenty-eight years ago, and it will now require an effort on our part, either through the slow processes of diplomacy or the undue aggressions of war, to compose the differences in the little Republic before we can set up our authority there; our solemn obligation to Porto Rico and Cuba admonishes us of our duty in Santo Domingo; if we permit disorder to reign there we may expect to see the infectious spirit of anarchy communicated to the nearby islands, and if we allow old Maximo Gomez and the other leaders driven from the island by the despotic Heureaux to return and ply their trade, we may expect to see rebellion break out in our own new possessions; and this can be prevented in only one way, and that is a very simple way — run up the Stars and Stripes at San Domingo City and take possession of the country.

El Times publica parte de un editorial sobre Santo Domingo y la anexión: el país podría haberse obtenido sin derramamiento de sangre y sin gran gasto a través de las fuerzas de la «asimilación benévola», que se habrían puesto en movimiento; lo que tontamente rehusamos aceptar entonces como una oferta gratuita puede que tengamos que pelear por ello en un futuro no distante; nada ha ocurrido desde 1871 que mate el espíritu de genuino americanismo que animó la solicitud de los dominicanos de anexión a este país — representa uno de esos impulsos de un pueblo que pueden ser enfriados pero no muertos por la indiferencia; pero los enemigos naturales del proyecto se han hecho más fuertes a través de la organización que lo que eran hace veintiocho años, y ahora requerirá un esfuerzo de nuestra parte, ya a través de los lentos procesos de la diplomacia o de las indebidas agresiones de la guerra, para componer las diferencias en la pequeña República antes de que podamos establecer nuestra autoridad allí; nuestra solemne obligación hacia Puerto Rico y Cuba nos amonesta de nuestro deber en Santo Domingo; si permitimos que el desorden reine allí podemos esperar ver el espíritu infeccioso de la anarquía comunicado a las islas cercanas, y si permitimos que el viejo Máximo Gómez y los otros líderes expulsados de la isla por el despótico Heureaux regresen y ejerzan su oficio, podemos esperar ver estallar la rebelión en nuestras propias nuevas posesiones; y esto puede prevenirse de una sola manera, y es una manera muy simple — izar las Barras y Estrellas en la ciudad de Santo Domingo y tomar posesión del país.

New York Journal and Advertiser

The Journal prints part of an editorial urging the acquisition of Santo DomingoEl Journal publica parte de un editorial que urge la adquisición de Santo Domingo

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

The Journal prints part of an editorial urging the acquisition of Santo Domingo as a more desirable acquisition than Porto Rico, for, with over five times the area, it has only two-thirds the population, so that the opportunities remaining open for development are much greater; it has 18,045 square miles, almost exactly the area of New Hampshire and Vermont combined, with something over 600,000 inhabitants; unlike Haiti, the black republic on the same island, it does not have a majority of negroes; the bulk of its land is available for cultivation; it is rich in all varieties of tropical woods and plants; it is solvent, and its standard of value is the United States gold dollar; with the acquisition of Santo Domingo we should almost complete our cordon of defence of the Nicaragua Canal; of course nobody would urge the forcible annexation of an unwilling people, but if the former sentiments of the Dominicans toward the United States still exist, or are likely to revive, we should be ready to take advantage of them; and we ought to have a couple of war ships always at hand, with instructions to meet any friendly overtures in the proper spirit before the opportunity goes by.

El Journal publica parte de un editorial que urge la adquisición de Santo Domingo como una adquisición más deseable que Puerto Rico, pues, con más de cinco veces el área, tiene solo dos tercios de la población, de modo que las oportunidades que permanecen abiertas para el desarrollo son mucho mayores; tiene 18,045 millas cuadradas, casi exactamente el área de New Hampshire y Vermont combinados, con algo más de 600,000 habitantes; a diferencia de Haití, la república negra en la misma isla, no tiene una mayoría de negros; el grueso de su tierra está disponible para el cultivo; es rica en todas las variedades de maderas y plantas tropicales; es solvente, y su estándar de valor es el dólar de oro de los Estados Unidos; con la adquisición de Santo Domingo casi completaríamos nuestro cordón de defensa del Canal de Nicaragua; por supuesto nadie urgiría la anexión forzosa de un pueblo no dispuesto, pero si los sentimientos anteriores de los dominicanos hacia los Estados Unidos todavía existen, o es probable que revivan, debemos estar listos para aprovecharnos de ellos; y debemos tener un par de buques de guerra siempre a mano, con instrucciones de recibir cualquier insinuación amistosa en el espíritu apropiado antes de que la oportunidad pase.

The Wichita Daily Eagle

The Eagle prints part of a sketch of the late President HeureauxEl Eagle publica parte de un bosquejo del difunto presidente Heureaux

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

The Eagle prints part of a sketch of the late President Heureaux, a man of unbending will, endowed with much intellectual force, and possessed of great courage, he strove honestly for the people of his fatherland, but with methods which found their only counterpart in the middle ages; in the less than fifty years of his life, he rose from a peasant to the dictatorship of San Domingo, and in the thirteen years of his administration he materially advanced the country in civilization, welding out of a mass of brigands an army of considerable strength, and building up a financial system which brought prosperity to his people; his people looked upon him either with a devotion akin to idolatry, or with horror and fear, for his vigorous personality admitted of no lukewarm sentiment — whether they loved or hated him, they obeyed him without question; thirty years ago, an ill-clad, half-starved boy from the hills of the interior, he joined as a private the so-called army, a lawless, impotent mob controlled by corrupt politicians and incompetent generals; despite a withered arm, which was almost useless, Heureaux developed into a good soldier, and soon rose from the ranks to an under officership; his genius for organization developed, and by rapid promotions the young officer advanced until he became commander in chief; then came the turbulent days of 1885-'86, and in the disorder of the time Heureaux found his opportunity — with his power as head of the army, he forced his election as president.

El Eagle publica parte de un bosquejo del difunto presidente Heureaux, un hombre de voluntad inflexible, dotado de mucha fuerza intelectual y poseedor de gran valor, que se esforzó honestamente por el pueblo de su patria, pero con métodos que encontraron su único contrapeso en la edad media; en los menos de cincuenta años de su vida, se elevó de campesino a la dictadura de San Domingo, y en los trece años de su administración avanzó materialmente el país en civilización, soldando de una masa de bandidos un ejército de fuerza considerable, y construyendo un sistema financiero que trajo prosperidad a su pueblo; su pueblo lo miraba ya con una devoción parecida a la idolatría, o con horror y miedo, pues su vigorosa personalidad no admitía ningún sentimiento tibio — lo amaran o lo odiaran, le obedecían sin cuestión; hace treinta años, un muchacho mal vestido y medio hambriento de las lomas del interior, se unió como soldado raso al llamado ejército, una muchedumbre sin ley e impotente controlada por políticos corruptos y generales incompetentes; a pesar de un brazo marchito, que era casi inútil, Heureaux se desarrolló en un buen soldado y pronto se elevó de las filas a una suboficialidad; su genio para la organización se desarrolló, y por rápidas promociones el joven oficial avanzó hasta llegar a ser comandante en jefe; entonces vinieron los días turbulentos de 1885-86, y en el desorden de la época Heureaux encontró su oportunidad — con su poder como jefe del ejército, forzó su elección como presidente.

The Wichita Daily Eagle

The Eagle prints part of a sketch of President Heureaux and his courageEl Eagle publica parte de un bosquejo del presidente Heureaux y su valor

August 1, 18991 de agosto de 1899

The Eagle prints part of a sketch of President Heureaux and his courage: it was difficult to secure from Dominicans expressions of opinion on any subject touching the president or his policy, but when it was possible to find a Dominican who was willing to talk of the life of Heureaux, the stories told had the flavor of old-time romance; one of the striking instances of the president's personal courage is to be found in the story of his suppression of the 'Santiago' rebellion a few years ago — Santiago is inland from the famous Samana Bay, and at a distance from the capital which made it possible for the residents to foment trouble with the possibility of escaping immediate punishment; here a band of malcontents from all parts of the island gathered and soon succeeded in enlisting the sympathies of almost the entire population of the town, and, encouraged by the fact that the terrible president was several days' journey distant, the ringleaders finally became bold enough to issue a manifesto declaring Heureaux a monstrous tyrant, and urging an uprising, and great posters were printed bearing the picture of a coffin and calling on all patriots to aid in ridding the fatherland of its oppressor; the government secret service soon apprised the president of the affair, and in his usual picturesque manner he proceeded with preparations for revenge: he ordered made a tough black coffin, and, putting it aboard his little armoured yacht, he took a detail of soldiers and sailed around the coast for Santiago, and, landing with thirty soldiers, he started on the twenty-five-mile horseback journey.

El Eagle publica parte de un bosquejo del presidente Heureaux y su valor: era difícil conseguir de los dominicanos expresiones de opinión sobre cualquier tema que tocara al presidente o su política, pero cuando era posible encontrar un dominicano dispuesto a hablar de la vida de Heureaux, las historias contadas tenían el sabor del romance de antaño; uno de los ejemplos notables del valor personal del presidente se encuentra en la historia de su supresión de la rebelión de «Santiago» hace algunos años — Santiago está tierra adentro desde la famosa Bahía de Samaná, y a una distancia de la capital que hacía posible para los residentes fomentar problemas con la posibilidad de escapar al castigo inmediato; allí una banda de descontentos de todas partes de la isla se reunió y pronto logró enlistar las simpatías de casi la población entera del pueblo, y, alentados por el hecho de que el terrible presidente estaba a varios días de jornada, los cabecillas finalmente se volvieron suficientemente atrevidos para emitir un manifiesto declarando a Heureaux un tirano monstruoso, y urgiendo un levantamiento, y se imprimieron grandes carteles con la figura de un ataúd llamando a todos los patriotas a ayudar a librar a la patria de su opresor; el servicio secreto del gobierno pronto informó al presidente del asunto, y en su manera pintoresca usual procedió con preparativos de venganza: ordenó hacer un ataúd negro y resistente, y, poniéndolo a bordo de su pequeño yate acorazado, tomó un destacamento de soldados y navegó alrededor de la costa hacia Santiago, y, desembarcando con treinta soldados, emprendió la jornada a caballo de veinticinco millas.

The Spy prints an editorial on the island and the Dominican Republic: the conflict between Hippolyte and Legitime furnished the most constant and exciting items of news from the West Indies, and all the force and ability of the extraordinary Heureaux were required to restore order in the republic; the condition of the island is of vastly more importance to the United States now than it was a dozen years ago, and the affairs of the islands therefore deserve the more consideration; the island of Hispaniola, Hayti or Santo Domingo, as it has been at various times called, is divided into two republics — the western third of the territory is occupied by the republic of Hayti, which dates back to 1800, and is of French character, even the language being a French patois; the remaining two-thirds, representing an area of some 19,000 square miles, or somewhat more than the combined areas of New Hampshire and Vermont, constitute the Dominican republic; the language is Spanish, and the people are of mixed French, Spanish, English and African blood; the soil is wonderfully fertile, bearing tobacco, cotton, sugar cane, cocoa, coffee, fruits and valuable woods; the scenery is mountainous and picturesque, and under proper government the country would be a pleasant place of residence; and while the island has not been remarkable for prosperity, there is a certain rich class which indulges in educational aspirations, and not a few of its sons have been sent to this country or to Europe to study, and two sons of the late President Heureaux are said to be studying law and medicine in Paris.

El Spy publica un editorial sobre la isla y la República Dominicana: el conflicto entre Hippolyte y Legitime suministró las notas de noticias más constantes y emocionantes de las Indias Occidentales, y toda la fuerza y habilidad del extraordinario Heureaux fueron requeridas para restaurar el orden en la república; la condición de la isla es de mucho más importancia para los Estados Unidos ahora que lo que era hace una docena de años, y los asuntos de las islas por tanto merecen la mayor consideración; la isla de Hispaniola, Hayti o Santo Domingo, como ha sido llamada en varios tiempos, está dividida en dos repúblicas — el tercio occidental del territorio está ocupado por la república de Hayti, que data de 1800, y es de carácter francés, siendo el lenguaje mismo un patois francés; los restantes dos tercios, que representan un área de unas 19,000 millas cuadradas, o algo más que las áreas combinadas de New Hampshire y Vermont, constituyen la república dominicana; el lenguaje es español, y el pueblo es de sangre mezclada francesa, española, inglesa y africana; el suelo es maravillosamente fértil, produciendo tabaco, algodón, caña de azúcar, cacao, café, frutas y maderas valiosas; el escenario es montañoso y pintoresco, y bajo un gobierno apropiado el país sería un lugar agradable de residencia; y aunque la isla no ha sido notable por prosperidad, hay una cierta clase rica que se entrega a aspiraciones educacionales, y no pocos de sus hijos han sido enviados a este país o a Europa a estudiar, y se dice que dos hijos del difunto presidente Heureaux están estudiando derecho y medicina en París.

President Heureaux was a soldier during his entire lifetime: one of his arms was paralyzed from a wound he received by a rifle ball in battle; he is described by Americans familiar with his career as a man without fear, and when there was any disturbance or revolution in any part of the republic, he mounted his horse and went pell-mell himself to the front as leader, and learned all the details of the revolution as well as the causes leading to it; he went as a soldier, not as President or General, and thus was unknown by his people; he generally traveled on horseback and made frequent excursions through the country, and when on such trips he avoided hotels and private houses and slept along the roadside; he had a wonderful career, and was held in high regard by the majority of his people; and he participated in many battles, and always with success.

El presidente Heureaux fue un soldado durante toda su vida: uno de sus brazos estaba paralizado por una herida que recibió de una bala de rifle en batalla; es descrito por americanos familiarizados con su carrera como un hombre sin miedo, y cuando había cualquier disturbio o revolución en cualquier parte de la república, montaba su caballo e iba precipitadamente él mismo al frente como líder, y aprendía todos los detalles de la revolución así como las causas que la motivaron; iba como soldado, no como Presidente o General, y así era desconocido por su pueblo; generalmente viajaba a caballo e hacía frecuentes excursiones a través del país, y cuando en tales viajes evitaba hoteles y casas privadas y dormía a la orilla del camino; tuvo una carrera maravillosa y era tenido en alta estima por la mayoría de su pueblo; y participó en muchas batallas, y siempre con éxito.

August 2, 18992 de agosto de 1899

The Herald prints an editorial on the talk of revolution in the Dominican Republic: the revolts there have been so many in the past quarter of a century that no one would be surprised if a rising should occur at this time; the assassination of its President shows that the conditions which ordinarily lead to revolts are at hand; there is a comparatively large number of Europeans and Americans in the Dominican Republic, and almost all of them, it is understood, favor annexation to the United States; if that annexation project of thirty years ago which President Grant favored, and which the people of the island approved, had been carried out, it would undoubtedly have been a benefit to all parties; the present disturbance may lead to another annexation movement — this time it would succeed; and the country is a little wiser than it was then.

El Herald publica un editorial sobre la plática de revolución en la República Dominicana: las revueltas allí han sido tantas en el pasado cuarto de siglo que nadie estaría sorprendido si un levantamiento ocurriera en este tiempo; el asesinato de su Presidente muestra que las condiciones que ordinariamente conducen a revueltas están a mano; hay un número comparativamente grande de europeos y americanos en la República Dominicana, y casi todos ellos, se entiende, favorecen la anexión a los Estados Unidos; si ese proyecto de anexión de hace treinta años que el presidente Grant favoreció, y que el pueblo de la isla aprobó, hubiera sido llevado a cabo, indudablemente habría sido un beneficio para todas las partes; la presente perturbación puede conducir a otro movimiento de anexión — esta vez triunfaría; y el país es un poco más sabio que lo que era entonces.

The Censor prints a dispatch from Santo Domingo and a sketch of President Heureaux: the former reports that immediately upon the announcement of the president's death, Vice President Gen. Heureaux was born in Porto Plata in 1846, and served in the war against Spain from 1868 to 1874; later he was put at the head of the advanced Dominicans, who sought to improve their country by inducing foreign capital to locate on the island; as he advanced, the wealthy inhabitants joined his forces, and when he arrived at the gates of the historic city of Santo Domingo he was chosen president of the republic, and served his term of four years; at the expiration of every four years an electoral college assembles in the city to vote for a president for the following term, but owing to Gen. Heureaux's popularity and ability he has always been unanimously re-elected, and was serving his fifth term.

El Censor publica un despacho de Santo Domingo y un bosquejo del presidente Heureaux: el primero reporta que inmediatamente después del anuncio de la muerte del presidente, el vicepresidente gen. The Tribune prints the closing part of a statement on the situation in Santo Domingo: there is no fear that the insurrectionists will interfere in any way with the property of foreigners on the island; notwithstanding the many revolutions which have taken place in Santo Domingo since it attained its independence, in no instance has either life been taken or property molested of any foreigner, with the single exception of the action of Heureaux, who opened the vaults of the French bank because of a personal quarrel with one of its directors; women and children are safe at all times, as the Dominicans never stoop to war with the helpless, nor do they ever sack or pillage, but fight for what they believe to be right in the open, as men and soldiers should do; Mr. Jiminez and General Gomez are warm personal friends — in fact, both reside in the same house in Havana; as Heureaux was a usurper, the Vice-President is equally so, but bloodshed and insurrection would cease at once if the latter would announce that a legal, public election would take place on August 16, which is the Independence day of the country; the entire country is aroused to the necessity of a change to a government where life is secure, and it recognizes that the former Vice-President and the present pretending President is but one of the accomplices of Heureaux, and as such has no confidence in him; and there is but one man who at this time represents the people and their best interests, and that is Jiminez, and if an opportunity is given to the people they will prove it by their ballots — but if none is afforded in that way, then force will be used.
