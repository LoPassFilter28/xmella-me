Passaic City Record

The Record prints a geographical description of Santo DomingoEl Record publica una descripción geográfica de Santo Domingo

July 18, 189118 de julio de 1891

The Bay of Samana is beyond doubt the grandest harbor of the Antilles, extending some thirty miles from east to west by nine or twelve from north to south; the peninsula that forms it is a grand promontory of steep, irregular hills ranging from a few hundred to 2,000 feet, covered with dense underbrush, vines and timber and watered by mountain streams that sometimes fall in beautiful cascades. The predominant rock is limestone, with schist, quartz and mica slate also occurring, and bituminous coal is found of exceeding richness; the harbor's entrance, for vessels drawing more than eight feet, is contracted to about 2,000 yards by a broad coral reef, north of which lie five small islands, the largest of about 100 acres.

La bahía de Samaná es sin duda el puerto más grandioso de las Antillas, con unas treinta millas de este a oeste por nueve o doce de norte a sur; la península que la forma es un gran promontorio de colinas escarpadas e irregulares que van de unos pocos cientos a 2,000 pies, cubiertas de densa maleza, enredaderas y bosques y regadas por arroyos de montaña que a veces caen en hermosas cascadas. La roca predominante es la caliza, con esquistos, cuarzo y mica también presentes, y se encuentra carbón bituminoso de extraordinaria riqueza; la entrada del puerto, para buques de más de ocho pies de calado, se estrecha a unas 2,000 yardas por un amplio arrecife de coral, al norte del cual hay cinco islotes, el mayor de unas 100 acres.

But in 1877 a new vault was discovered in San Domingo, near that opened in 1795, and the coffin found in it bore inscriptions showing that the mortal remains of Christopher Columbus were certainly contained in it; the body previously exhumed was that of his son Diego. The Sun

The Sun argues the strategic case for Samana Bay on expert testimonyEl Sun argumenta el caso estratégico de la bahía de Samaná sobre testimonio pericial

August 1, 18911 de agosto de 1891

The two great nautical routes between Europe and the Gulf of Mexico and Central America, the Sun continues, are the Mona Pass, on the south side of the island of San Domingo, and the Mole St. Nicolas route, or Windward Passage, on the northern or Haytian side of the island; the power that occupies these two points and fortifies them will control the principal waterways between Europe and the Gulf of Mexico. Were an interoceanic canal built across Central America, the occupation of both the Mole St. Nicolas and Samana Bay would prove of invaluable importance to the United States, and the Mole lies barely thirty miles from the Cuban coast.

Las dos grandes rutas náuticas entre Europa y el golfo de México y la América Central, continúa el Sun, son el paso de la Mona, en el lado sur de la isla de Santo Domingo, y la ruta del Môle St. Nicolas, o paso de los Vientos, en el lado norte o haitiano de la isla; la potencia que ocupe esos dos puntos y los fortifique controlará las principales vías acuáticas entre Europa y el golfo de México. Si se construyera un canal interoceánico a través de la América Central, la ocupación tanto del Môle St. Nicolas como de la bahía de Samaná resultaría de inestimable importancia para los Estados Unidos, y el Môle dista apenas treinta millas de la costa cubana.
