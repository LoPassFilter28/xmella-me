November 2, 18972 de noviembre de 1897

Yet the strategic importance of the bay, from its opening upon the Mona Passage, makes its future an object of watchfulness for the United States Government, and since the collection of the export and import duties of San Domingo is to pass from an American to an English syndicate, American authorities, after Mr. Powell's letter, will naturally await assurances that no political or territorial control can thereby pass to the British Government.

Sin embargo, la importancia estratégica de la bahía, por su apertura sobre el paso de la Mona, hace de su futuro un objeto de vigilancia para el gobierno de los Estados Unidos, y como el cobro de los derechos de exportación e importación de San Domingo va a pasar de un sindicato americano a un sindicato inglés, las autoridades americanas, tras la carta del Sr. Powell, esperarán naturalmente seguridades de que ningún control político o territorial pueda pasar con ello al gobierno británico.

December 10, 189710 de diciembre de 1897

It has long been the custom of the United States to make the money of several South American countries, and as an act of comity and good will the work is done at the actual cost of labor and material; the present order from Santo Domingo also includes 800,000 half dollars, 375,000 quarter dollars and 750,000 dimes. The mint bureau has just filled an order from Costa Rica for a large quantity of gold coins, and Costa Rica, so pleased with the coinage, has ordered through its minister at Washington 40,000 more coins of gold; and the first work of this kind done by the United States was in 1876, when $100,000 worth of minor coins were turned out for the government of Venezuela.

The San Francisco Call

To Display the American FlagPara exhibir la bandera americana

December 24, 189724 de diciembre de 1897

The San Francisco Call reported that the warship Detroit was ordered to Samana Bay, where U.S. officials had previously negotiated for a coaling station. While naval authorities claimed the visit was merely to display the American flag, the deployment was also expected to expedite the arbitration of a contract claim by American citizen Nathaniel McKay against the Dominican government. The report also mentioned pending arbitration proposals regarding American claims in Haiti.

El San Francisco Call informó que el buque de guerra Detroit recibió órdenes de dirigirse a la bahía de Samaná, donde funcionarios estadounidenses habían negociado previamente una estación carbonera. Si bien las autoridades navales afirmaron que la visita solo tenía por objeto mostrar la bandera estadounidense, se esperaba también que el despliegue acelerara el arbitraje de una reclamación contractual del ciudadano estadounidense Nathaniel McKay contra el gobierno dominicano. El informe mencionó igualmente propuestas pendientes de arbitraje respecto a reclamaciones estadounidenses en Haití.

The Saint Paul Globe

About thirty years ago an attempt was made by the GrantHace unos treinta años la administración de Grant hizo un intento de

December 31, 189731 de diciembre de 1897

The Saint Paul Globe published a letter comparing the proposed annexation of Hawaii to President Grant's unsuccessful effort to annex Santo Domingo in 1869 and 1870. The writer recalled that despite strategic arguments, joint resolution efforts, and a favorable commission report, the annexation treaty failed in the Senate. The author argued that past fears of foreign powers absorbing Santo Domingo proved unfounded and hoped Hawaii would avoid annexation.

The Saint Paul Globe publicó una carta en la que comparaba la proyectada anexión de Hawái con la infructuosa tentativa del presidente Grant de anexionar Santo Domingo en 1869 y 1870. El autor recordaba que, a pesar de los argumentos estratégicos, de los esfuerzos por lograr una resolución conjunta y de un informe favorable de la comisión, el tratado de anexión fracasó en el Senado. El autor sostuvo que los temores pasados de que potencias extranjeras absorbieran a Santo Domingo resultaron infundados y expresó su deseo de que Hawái evitara la anexión.

The Sun

An American company held land in the neighborhood many years AgoUna compañía estadounidense tenía tierras en la vecindad hace muchos años

April 7, 18987 de abril de 1898

The Sun highlighted the strategic value of Samana Bay as a coaling station along the Mona Passage between Santo Domingo and Puerto Rico, citing its sheltered anchorage and defensible geography. The article reviewed the rejection of President Grant's annexation project 28 years prior, which led the Dominican Republic to refuse any sale, cession, or lease of its territory. It emphasized that current American interest did not contemplate territorial annexation or permanent fortifications.

The Sun destacó el valor estratégico de la bahía de Samaná como estación carbonera en el paso de la Mona, entre Santo Domingo y Puerto Rico, señalando su fondeadero protegido y su ventajosa geografía defensiva. El artículo repasó el rechazo al proyecto de anexión del presidente Grant ocurrido 28 años atrás, lo que llevó a la República Dominicana a negarse a cualquier venta, cesión o arrendamiento de su territorio. Subrayó que el interés estadounidense en ese momento no contemplaba anexiones territoriales ni fortificaciones permanentes.

The Mona Passage, only sixty miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico; St. John's is an ancient town, its old buildings curious and interesting, especially the palace erected by Ponce de Leon, who conquered the island for Spain; Ponce, the principal city on the southern coast, has 40,000 inhabitants, situated three miles inland, and Mayaguez, on the west coast, contains 30,000 people; the island's harbors are rapidly filling up with silt and are dangerous to heavy vessels; and in the group of the Virgin Islands, to the east, only the two Danish islands, St. Thomas and St. Croix, are probable — the passage is cut off there.

There are quite a million people on the island, but they dally on the way to civilization; there is no army, so Hayti's only use to the United States would be in lending it a harbor: there are no harbors on the north coast, but Port au Prince, in the republic of Hayti, is a well-protected port lying behind the island of Gonave; in the Dominican Republic there are the harbors of Santo Domingo and Puerto Plata, which would make convenient bases for operations in the Caribbean. The Mona Passage, only sixty miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico; St. John's is an ancient town, its old buildings curious and interesting, especially the palace erected by Ponce de Leon, who conquered the island for Spain, and Ponce, the principal city on the southern coast, has 40,000 inhabitants, situated three miles inland, while Mayaguez, on the west coast, contains 30,000 people, separated from its port by a river spanned by an iron bridge — there is a barracks here, but no fort.
