The Indianapolis Journal

A Haitian deputy, who professes to be well informed on allUn diputado haitiano, que profesa estar bien informado sobre todo

May 16, 189816 de mayo de 1898

The Indianapolis Journal reported from Haiti regarding proposed constitutional changes concerning foreign property rights. Haitian Foreign Minister Brutus St. Victor denied claims that the government intended to repeal Article 6 of the constitution, which prohibited alien ownership of real estate, stating the public was not prepared for such reform. St. Victor also declined to disclose specific government plans to address the rising premium on paper currency.

The Indianapolis Journal informó desde Haití sobre las propuestas de reforma constitucional relativas a los derechos de propiedad de los extranjeros. El ministro de Asuntos Exteriores haitiano, Brutus St. Victor, desmintió las versiones de que el gobierno pretendiera derogar el artículo 6 de la constitución, que prohibía a los extranjeros la propiedad de bienes inmuebles, afirmando que la opinión pública no estaba preparada para tal cambio. St. Victor declinó asimismo revelar planes gubernamentales específicos para hacer frente al creciente sobreprecio del papel moneda.

Twice-A-Week Plain Dealer

The Plain Dealer prints part of a West Indies survey in wartimeEl Plain Dealer publica parte de un estudio de las Indias Occidentales en tiempo de guerra

May 17, 189817 de mayo de 1898

Toussaint L'Ouverture's successful struggle against France and his capture by treachery, followed by the complete expulsion of the French, is one of liberty's favorite tales; the Spanish invaded San Domingo in 1861, and their defeat left the islanders free; the Haytians have experimented with kings and discarded them, and the treasure of one of these despots, Christophe, remains buried under the rank vegetation among the ruins of a stronghold he built, whose architect and all the workmen were thrown from the parapets so that the secrets of the citadel they had just completed should never be divulged. There are quite a million people on the island; there is no army, or Hayti's only use to us would be in lending us a harbor: there are no harbors on the north coast, but Port au Prince, in the republic of Hayti, is a well-protected port lying behind the island of Gonaive, while in the Dominican republic there are the harbors of Santo Domingo and Porto Plata, which would make convenient bases for operations in the Caribbean; and the Mona passage, only 60 miles wide, separates Hayti from Porto Rico.

La exitosa lucha de Toussaint L'Ouverture contra Francia y su captura por traición, seguida de la completa expulsión de los franceses, es uno de los relatos favoritos de la libertad; los españoles invadieron Santo Domingo en 1861, y su derrota dejó libres a los isleños; los haitianos han experimentado con reyes y los han descartado, y el tesoro de uno de esos déspotas, Christophe, permanece enterrado bajo la exuberante vegetación entre las ruinas de una fortaleza que construyó, cuyo arquitecto y todos los obreros fueron arrojados desde las almenas para que los secretos de la ciudadela que acababan de completar nunca fueran revelados. Hay cerca de un millón de personas en la isla; no hay ejército, o el único uso de Haití para nosotros sería prestarnos un puerto: no hay puertos en la costa norte, pero Puerto Príncipe, en la república de Haití, es un puerto bien protegido situado detrás de la isla de Gonaive, mientras que en la república dominicana están los puertos de Santo Domingo y Puerto Plata, que serían bases convenientes para las operaciones en el Caribe; y el paso de la Mona, de solo 60 millas de ancho, separa a Haití de Puerto Rico.

June 3, 18983 de junio de 1898

Under the head 'Expedition Captured,' the New Mexican prints a dispatch to the Herald from Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic: a revolutionary expedition was landed at Monte Cristi from Hayti under the leadership of Senor Jimenez, formerly a merchant in New York; the government had been advised of the movement, troops were waiting for the party, and all were captured; the leader, Jimenez, was reserved for examination, but General Augustin was shot without delay; President Heureaux will arrive at Monte Cristi tomorrow, and it is almost certain that the whole party will be executed.

Bajo el título «Expedition Captured», el New Mexican publica un despacho al Herald desde Puerto Plata, República Dominicana: una expedición revolucionaria fue desembarcada en Monte Cristi procedente de Haití bajo el liderazgo del señor Jimenez, antes comerciante en Nueva York; el gobierno había sido avisado del movimiento, las tropas estaban esperando al grupo, y todos fueron capturados; el líder, Jimenez, fue reservado para ser interrogado, pero el general Augustin fue fusilado sin demora; el presidente Heureaux llegará a Monte Cristi mañana, y es casi seguro que todo el grupo será ejecutado.

June 4, 18984 de junio de 1898

The preparations for the revolution are said to have been very elaborate, and there is a strong belief there that the revolutionists are sure of success; but, on the other hand, the Dominican consul there says he has a report that the revolutionists have been defeated at Monte Cristi, a report not generally believed. The advices received there on the subject say that the Fanita arrived at Monte Cristi at 3 o'clock yesterday afternoon, and that the revolutionists had no difficulty in taking the town; the most circumstantial reports have it that the revolutionists are now in considerable force, and that under Generals Morales and Villanueva they are marching on Puerto Plata, while another detachment is moving against Santiago de los Caballeros, on the Yaque river, one hundred miles east of there; General Garcia, another important Dominican leader, is said to be taking part in the revolution, and it is expected there will be a rising at Santo Domingo, the capital of the republic; and General Jimenez is a merchant who has business houses there, at Monte Cristi, at Puerto Plata and at Hamburg, in his youth he served in the army, and for many years he has been very popular in the Dominican Republic.
