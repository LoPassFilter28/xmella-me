New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints part of its correspondent's account of President HeureauxEl Tribune publica parte del relato de su corresponsal sobre el presidente Heureaux

June 24, 189824 de junio de 1898

One of the notable traits of Heureaux's conversation while in the port of Cape Haytien the second time, last Thursday, the 9th, was his contempt, not the least bit concealed, for the Haytians; nevertheless, on this visit he ordered the regulation salute of twenty-one guns to be fired, and it was returned after the usual lazy fashion by the Haytians from the two or three small field-pieces of ancient type which constitute about the whole of the serviceable artillery there. Under the head 'Night Excursions in Disguise,' the account adds that Heureaux is a man of mystery and of mystification, as well as of promptitude and blood, and that after each of his departures from Cape Haytien it was reported he had been ashore in the evening in disguise, and thus, for purposes of his own, had made a tour of the city.

Uno de los rasgos notables de la conversación de Heureaux durante su segunda visita al puerto de Cabo Haitiano, el jueves pasado, día 9, fue su desprecio, nada disimulado, por los haitianos; sin embargo, en esta visita ordenó disparar la salva reglamentaria de veintiún cañonazos, que fue contestada en la forma perezosa acostumbrada por los haitianos desde las dos o tres pequeñas piezas de campaña de tipo antiguo que constituyen casi toda la artillería útil de allí. Bajo el título «Night Excursions in Disguise», el relato añade que Heureaux es un hombre de misterio y de mistificación, así como de prontitud y de sangre, y que después de cada una de sus salidas de Cabo Haitiano se reportó que había estado en tierra por la noche disfrazado, y así, para fines suyos, había hecho un recorrido de la ciudad.

June 24, 189824 de junio de 1898

It recounts how Heureaux employs a large number of spies, many of them women, yet is his own best spy, telling of him disguised as a laborer learning of a conspiracy, confronting conspirators in their own meeting, and pardoning a hired assassin who revealed who sent him, while thousands of dollars have secretly been offered for his assassination and the common people believe he leads a charmed life.

Un reportaje sobre la política dominicana bajo el presidente Heureaux describe a Emmanuel Jimines como un joven de modales agradables que despierta mucha simpatía, pero califica su intento de Monte Cristi como uno de los peor planeados y más débiles que se hayan conocido. Relata cómo Heureaux emplea un gran número de espías, muchos de ellos mujeres, aunque él mismo es su mejor espía, y cuenta que disfrazado de obrero se enteró de una conspiración, que enfrentó a los conspiradores en su propia reunión y que perdonó a un asesino a sueldo que reveló quién lo había enviado, mientras se han ofrecido en secreto miles de dólares por su asesinato y el pueblo común cree que lleva una vida protegida por un encanto.

The president is about five feet eight inches in height, of athletic mold, though his right arm is still half disabled from the effects of two old wounds near the elbow; his movements are usually deliberate, as if all that he did in public was nicely calculated, yet he is capable of great animation, volubility and vivacity, spices his talk with satire and irony, and occasionally laughs broadly at his own joke or epigram, and it is impossible not to be impressed by the strong grasp of his mind and by the subtlety of his cunning. All the time the conversation went on, something over an hour, first with the correspondent and at intervals with visitor after visitor who came to pay his respects, there were three or four poor fellows down in the bowels of the ship, revolutionists taken with arms in their hands, who were waiting the word of this imperturbable dictator to determine whether they should live or die.

The Herald

The Herald's account gives Heureaux's views on Hayti and the United StatesEl relato del Herald recoge las opiniones de Heureaux sobre Haití y los Estados Unidos

June 26, 189826 de junio de 1898

Some day, he declared, all of the greater Antilles would either belong to him or be under the protectorate of the American republic, and there was but one sensible course for Santo Domingo or for Hayti — to cultivate the best political and commercial relations with the United States. On the subject of neutrality General Heureaux was somewhat ambiguous: he was indisposed, he said, to offend either Spain or the United States, the enmity of the former being as disastrous to his government as that of the latter, but he allowed it to be understood that, if it came to choice, he must decide to stand with the Americans. It was when the conversation turned to the late abortive uprising at Monte Cristo that the real opportunity to study the character of the dictator presented itself, and some of the Dominicans and prominent Haytiens who came aboard to pay homage to Heureaux had been hand in glove with the conspirators.

Algún día, declaró, todas las Antillas mayores o le pertenecerían a él o estarían bajo el protectorado de la república americana, y no había sino un camino sensato para Santo Domingo o para Haití: cultivar las mejores relaciones políticas y comerciales con los Estados Unidos. Sobre el tema de la neutralidad, el general Heureaux fue algo ambiguo: no estaba dispuesto, dijo, a ofender ni a España ni a los Estados Unidos, pudiendo la enemistad de la primera ser tan desastrosa para su gobierno como la de los segundos, pero dejó entender que, si llegaba a tener que escoger, debía decidirse a estar con los americanos. Fue cuando la conversación giró hacia el reciente alzamiento abortado en Monte Cristi que se presentó la verdadera oportunidad de estudiar el carácter del dictador, y algunos de los dominicanos y prominentes haitianos que subieron a bordo a rendir homenaje a Heureaux habían estado en íntima connivencia con los conspiradores.

The writer was sent to San Domingo for the express purpose of sifting the existing evidence and making a personal examination, and bore letters of recommendation from the then Secretary of State, Mr. Blaine, to the president of the Dominican republic, and every opportunity was afforded him for a rigid investigation: the minister of public affairs himself accompanied him to the cathedral, and by his command the revered relics were brought from their place of deposit, in an ancient cedar chest within a dark cell near the presbytery, and exposed to the light of day. Ober was sent to San Domingo for the express purpose of sifting the existing evidence and making a personal examination, and bore letters of recommendation from the then secretary of state, Mr. Blaine, to the president of the Dominican republic, and every opportunity was afforded him for a rigid investigation: the minister of public affairs himself accompanied him to the cathedral, and by his command the revered relics were brought from their place of deposit, in an ancient cedar chest within a dark cell near the presbytery, and exposed to the light of day. Photographs were then taken of the vault, the original place of sepulture being pointed out, the two vaults opened and their relative position shown, and also of the casket at present holding the bones, and of the crystal and satinwood case in which this is contained. In his report to this government, Mr. Ober said in substance that the conclusion reached by the Spanish academy is that the remains of Columbus are in the cathedral of Havana, in the shadow of the glorious banner of Castile, and that it is most fitting that over his sepulcher waves the same flag that sailed with him from Palos.

The Providence News

The News, commenting on the finances of San Domingo, reportsEl News, comentando las finanzas de Santo Domingo, reporta

September 21, 189821 de septiembre de 1898

Incidentally, interest is aroused in San Domingo's financial machinery by the revenues being collected at its ports and handled in New York; from all accounts, the policy of the San Domingo government of hiring expert New York financiers to run its money affairs works well, and might be imitated with advantage by other nations, though there is probably something in it too for the syndicate, especially as its agent doubtless has the first hand on the revenues.

Incidentalmente, el interés se despierta en la maquinaria financiera de Santo Domingo por el hecho de que las rentas se cobran en sus puertos y se manejan en Nueva York; según todos los informes, la política del gobierno de Santo Domingo de contratar financieros expertos de Nueva York para manejar sus asuntos de dinero funciona bien, y podría ser imitada con ventaja por otras naciones, aunque probablemente hay también algo en ello para el sindicato, especialmente porque su agente tiene sin duda la primera mano en las rentas.
