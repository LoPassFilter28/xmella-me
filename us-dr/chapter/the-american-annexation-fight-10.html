According to the newspaper, the American government made a previous effort to obtain the bay and peninsula of Samana approximately fifteen years prior under President Franklin Pierce and Secretary of State Wm. L. Marcy. In June 1854, then-Captain George B. McClellan was sent on a secret expedition to the West Indies aboard the frigate \1 to select a convenient harbor and coaling station for the U.S. Navy. McClellan returned in September with maps, sketches, and information, resulting in the selection of the bay and peninsula of Samana for the proposed station.

Según el diario, el gobierno estadounidense realizó una tentativa anterior para adquirir la bahía y la península de Samaná unos quince años atrás, bajo la presidencia de Franklin Pierce y con Wm. L. Marcy como secretario de Estado. En junio de 1854, el entonces capitán George B. McClellan fue comisionado en misión reservada a las Antillas a bordo de la fragata \1 con objeto de localizar una bahía adecuada y estación carbonera para la marina de los Estados Unidos. McClellan regresó en septiembre con planos, croquis e informes periciales, determinando la elección de la bahía y península de Samaná para el proyectado establecimiento naval.

A description of the Bay of Samaná, whose opening lines are cutUna descripción de la bahía de Samaná, cuyas líneas iniciales están cortadas

January 5, 18705 de enero de 1870

On the north shore is the small town of Santa Bárbara, which lies in a land-locked bay and has natural facilities for repairing vessels, a dilapidated Roman Catholic church, a neat Wesleyan chapel and a custom house; the peninsula of Samaná is thirty-two miles long, its highest peak, Sugar Loaf Hill, 1,936 feet, and Devil's Hill 1,309 feet above the level of the sea, its soil extremely fertile and to a great extent covered with timber suited both for ship-building and cabinet-work, and containing gold, copper and bituminous coal, its population at the last census 1,721. The New-York Tribune warned that using violence against San Domingo would provoke widespread alarm and hatred throughout South America. The article also described the island's history—including its discovery by Columbus, its division between Haiti and San Domingo, Spain's 1861 reannexation and subsequent 1863–1865 defeat, and Dominican independence—while noting that Buenaventura Baez has served as President since 1867 and is one of the negotiators of the annexation treaty.

El New-York Tribune advirtió que el empleo de la fuerza contra Santo Domingo concitaría honda alarma y animadversión en toda la América del Sur. El artículo reseñó asimismo la trayectoria histórica de la isla —desde su descubrimiento por Colón y su división entre Haití y Santo Domingo, hasta la reanexión española de 1861, su posterior derrota entre 1863 y 1865 y la reconquista de la soberanía dominicana—, haciendo constar que Buenaventura Báez presidía la nación desde 1867 y figuraba como uno de los negociadores del tratado de anexión.

The islands of the Gulf have no destiny beyond the foldsLas islas del Golfo no tienen destino más allá de los pliegues

January 13, 187013 de enero de 1870

The New-York Tribune reported on January 13, 1870, that while the islands of the Gulf and San Domingo naturally gravitated toward the United States through trade and statesmanship, immediate annexation might be fraught with peril, though leasing Samana Bay remained a good idea. The article noted that the U.S. government might encounter competing claims or leases regarding Samana Bay, highlighting a "sharp colored American" mariner who bought land there five years prior and now owns a considerable portion of the available water frontage, coal lands, and valuable property where a naval station would likely be located.

El New-York Tribune expuso el 13 de enero de 1870 que, si bien las islas del golfo y Santo Domingo gravitaban de forma natural hacia los Estados Unidos por imperativos mercantiles y geopolíticos, una anexión inmediata entrañaba graves riesgos, considerándose no obstante acertado el arrendamiento de la bahía de Samaná. El escrito prevenía que el gobierno estadounidense podría afrontar títulos contrapuestos o derechos de arriendo en la zona, destacando a un «sagaz marino estadounidense de color» que adquirió tierras allí un lustro antes y retenía una porción considerable de la ribera marítima, terrenos carboníferos y valiosas heredades donde previsiblemente se asentaría el apostadero naval.

All the inhabitants in the interior, who suffered so much from Spanish domination, consider intervention in the affairs of this country, no matter whence it may come, as a real calamity; among the different populations, as also in the principal towns, there are only a few merchants or business men who favor an alienation of territory, and these have an interest in the speculation, while all the remainder of the people, and even many foreigners, as is the case particularly in Puerto Plata, are privately or openly opposed to the sale of Samaná. One fact will enable the reader to form an idea of the great opposition to the annexation scheme: the Senate is composed of men wholly devoted to the service of Báez — as, for example, his brother Carlos — but even this Senate vacillates when it sees the agitation which reigns throughout the Republic, and does not know whether or not to give its approbation to the President's now favorite project; the Government's attempt to make believe that this agitation is produced by English gold is, the letter says, clearly a falsehood. The Tribune's narrative of Báez's career: Pimentel elected PresidentLa narración del Tribune sobre la carrera de Báez: Pimentel electo presidente

February 14, 187014 de febrero de 1870

The Tribune's narrative of Báez's career: Pimentel elected President, then forced to give way to Cabral when a pronunciamiento broke out in San Domingo City on the 4th of August, Cabral being commissioned Protector of the Republic; Báez presented himself as a candidate in the following December and, though not a military hero and notwithstanding that his sympathies and support were given to the Spaniards during their invasion of the island, by his tact and consummate management defeated Cabral and became President; Pimentel and Cabral entered his Cabinet, but in 1866 a revolution headed by Pimentel drove him out of San Domingo, and another revolution the same year brought him back into power; Cabral succeeded him, but in the latter part of 1867 the office was again vacated, and after Cabral and his Cabinet fled at night from the capital, Báez was formally proclaimed President in 1868; Cabral remained in the field, with Polanco, Luperón, Moreno, Castillo, Ogando and Adó, opposed to the President with arms or otherwise, and Salnave lent Báez a helping hand in those trying times, without which he could never have maintained his position — the window closing mid-sentence, on the President's notoriety for shrewdness.

La narración del Tribune sobre la carrera de Báez: Pimentel electo presidente, luego obligado a ceder el paso a Cabral cuando estalló un pronunciamiento en la ciudad de Santo Domingo el 4 de agosto, quedando Cabral nombrado Protector de la República; Báez se presentó como candidato en diciembre siguiente y, aunque no era un héroe militar y a pesar de que sus simpatías y su apoyo estuvieron con los españoles durante su invasión de la isla, derrotó a Cabral con su tacto y consumada habilidad y se convirtió en presidente; Pimentel y Cabral entraron a su gabinete, pero en 1866 una revolución encabezada por Pimentel lo expulsó de Santo Domingo, y otra revolución, ese mismo año, lo devolvió al poder; Cabral lo sucedió, pero a fines de 1867 el cargo quedó de nuevo vacante y, después de que Cabral y su gabinete huyeron de noche de la capital, Báez fue proclamado formalmente presidente en 1868; Cabral se mantuvo en campaña, con Polanco, Luperón, Moreno, Castillo, Ogando y Adó, opuesto al presidente con las armas o de otro modo, y Salnave le tendió una mano a Báez en aquellos tiempos difíciles, sin la cual nunca habría podido mantenerse en su posición — y la ventana se cierra a media frase, en la notoriedad de astucia del presidente.
