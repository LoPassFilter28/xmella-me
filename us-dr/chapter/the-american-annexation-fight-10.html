All the inhabitants in the interior, who suffered so much from Spanish domination, consider intervention in the affairs of this country, no matter whence it may come, as a real calamity; among the different populations, as also in the principal towns, there are only a few merchants or business men who favor an alienation of territory, and these have an interest in the speculation, while all the remainder of the people, and even many foreigners, as is the case particularly in Puerto Plata, are privately or openly opposed to the sale of Samaná. One fact will enable the reader to form an idea of the great opposition to the annexation scheme: the Senate is composed of men wholly devoted to the service of Báez — as, for example, his brother Carlos — but even this Senate vacillates when it sees the agitation which reigns throughout the Republic, and does not know whether or not to give its approbation to the President's now favorite project; the Government's attempt to make believe that this agitation is produced by English gold is, the letter says, clearly a falsehood.
