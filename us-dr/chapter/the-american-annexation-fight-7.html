The Herald, in an editorial extract that begins in mid-discussion

June 9, 18699 de junio de 1869

The Herald, in an editorial extract that begins in mid-discussion with a Mr. Hunt, who has orders to proceed to the Dominican republic and report upon its condition at an early day, and whether he has higher secret instructions the paper is not informed, surveys the new movements and combinations which are going on in the Antilles: Cuba is convulsed with the questions of revolution, independence and annexation; in St. Domingo a policy of seeking admission to the Union has been discussed by President Báez, which has awakened efforts against him by Cabral, his former antagonist; in Hayti General Domingue, who is making a powerful resistance to Salnave, months ago wrote to Secretary Seward, seeking a protectorate for the territory he ruled; in Porto Rico a violent explosion against the Spanish rule is only kept down by a force of bayonets; and in Jamaica the British government is exhibiting a sudden energy in restoring the harbor of Kingston. This movement, the editorial says, is an important one in many respects, and if the administration contemplates aright the combinations going on in the Antilles it may be productive in the highest degree of good to American political and material interests.

El Herald, en un extracto editorial que comienza en mitad de una discusión con un señor Hunt, que tiene órdenes de proceder a la República Dominicana e informar sobre su situación en fecha temprana, y del cual la redacción ignora si tiene instrucciones secretas de mayor alcance, pasa revista a los nuevos movimientos y combinaciones que están ocurriendo en las Antillas: Cuba está convulsionada con las cuestiones de revolución, independencia y anexión; en Santo Domingo el presidente Báez ha discutido una política de buscar la admisión en la Unión, lo cual ha despertado esfuerzos contra él por parte de Cabral, su antiguo antagonista; en Haití el general Domingue, que ofrece una poderosa resistencia a Salnave, escribió hace meses al secretario Seward en busca de un protectorado para el territorio que gobernaba; en Puerto Rico una violenta explosión contra el dominio español solo se contiene con una fuerza de bayonetas; y en Jamaica el gobierno británico muestra una súbita energía en restaurar el puerto de Kingston. Este movimiento, dice el editorial, es importante en muchos aspectos, y si la administración contempla acertadamente las combinaciones que ocurren en las Antillas, puede ser productivo en el más alto grado de bien para los intereses políticos y materiales estadounidenses.

The New York Herald

The Herald argues, in a companion editorial extract, that under a late policy

June 9, 18699 de junio de 1869

The Herald argues, in a companion editorial extract, that under a late policy all that was required was that an agent should present himself with a bag of gold in one hand and the American flag in the other, and that he had only to hoist the one as soon as the other had been accepted. In the pursuit of this petty and mischievous policy, it says, a signal rebuke was received from the Dominican republic in the Samaná question, and from the Senate of the United States in that of St. Thomas; both were merited, for the principles on which the course was founded were of the meanest and most contemptible kind. Questions of national importance merit a higher and a nobler style of treatment. Fifty years ago a policy was announced by President Monroe which acquired an extended influence in American politics and European cabinets, because it expressed the sentiment of the American people; during two generations that policy has been the pole star of national development on this continent, and it was adapted to its era and has consummated its work. It is no longer a question of founding new colonies or extending European influence over old ones; the colonies have become nations, and national development has brought new needs and new demands, and the extract ends as it turns to the desire developing in the countries south of the United States from their failure to attain the same prosperity.

El Herald argumenta, en un extracto editorial complementario, que todo lo que se requería bajo una política reciente era que un agente se presentara con una bolsa de oro en una mano y la bandera estadounidense en la otra, y que solo tenía que izar la una tan pronto como la otra hubiera sido aceptada. En la prosecución de esta política mezquina y perniciosa, dice, se recibió una señalada reprensión de la República Dominicana en la cuestión de Samaná, y del Senado de los Estados Unidos en la de Santo Tomás; ambas fueron merecidas, pues los principios en que se fundaba ese proceder eran de la clase más ruin y despreciable. Las cuestiones de importancia nacional merecen un trato de estilo más elevado y noble. Hace cincuenta años un presidente, Monroe, anunció una política que adquirió una extensa influencia en la política estadounidense y en los gabinetes europeos, porque expresaba el sentimiento del pueblo estadounidense; durante dos generaciones esa política ha sido la estrella polar del desarrollo nacional en este continente, y estaba adaptada a su época y ha consumado su obra. Ya no se trata de fundar nuevas colonias ni de extender la influencia europea sobre las antiguas; las colonias se han convertido en naciones, y el desarrollo nacional ha traído nuevas necesidades y nuevas demandas, y el extracto termina cuando pasa al deseo que se desarrolla en los países al sur de los Estados Unidos por su fracaso en alcanzar la misma prosperidad.

June 26, 186926 de junio de 1869

The Herald argues, in an editorial extract, that the islands of the Archipelago are to-day in a crisis of great political changes, the incipient point of which lies in the abolition of African slavery by the United States: Cuba is undergoing an armed revolution, with emancipation and annexation inscribed upon its banner; the Dominican republic is agitated with the proposition of a peaceful admission to the American Union; Hayti is divided into three hostile camps, in one of which at least the idea of an American protectorate is entertained; and Porto Rico is reported to have caught the fire in a way which greatly alarms the colonial officers of the Spanish monarchy. The various parties involved in all these movements have their active partisans in this country, who are engaged in pushing their respective views in every feasible way: the co-operation of the press is assiduously sought, diplomatic and extra-diplomatic communications are pressed upon the government and the people, arms and munitions of war are pushed forward through every possible channel, and steamers are being built, chartered or purchased and prepared for naval armament in behalf of several of the combatants. So numerous are these operations, and so conflicting is the information in regard to them which is laid before the administration, the extract ends, that the government thus far has not been able to convict or even detain those engaged in them.

El Herald sostiene, en un extracto editorial, que las islas del archipiélago están hoy en una crisis de grandes cambios políticos, cuyo punto inicial reside en la abolición de la esclavitud africana por los Estados Unidos: Cuba está atravesando una revolución armada, con la emancipación y la anexión inscritas en su bandera; la República Dominicana está agitada con la proposición de una admisión pacífica en la Unión Americana; Haití está dividido en tres bandos hostiles, en uno de los cuales al menos se acaricia la idea de un protectorado estadounidense; y se informa de que Puerto Rico ha cogido la candela de una manera que alarma grandemente a los oficiales coloniales de la monarquía española. Los diversos partidos envueltos en todos estos movimientos tienen sus partidarios activos en este país, empeñados en impulsar sus respectivos puntos de vista por todos los medios factibles: se busca asiduamente la cooperación de la prensa, se presionan comunicaciones diplomáticas y extradiplomáticas sobre el gobierno y el pueblo, se envían armas y municiones de guerra por todos los canales posibles, y se construyen, fletan o compran vapores, preparándolos para armamento naval en favor de varios de los combatientes. Tan numerosas son estas operaciones, y tan contradictoria la información sobre ellas que se presenta ante la administración, concluye el extracto, que el gobierno hasta ahora no ha podido condenar ni siquiera detener a quienes participan en ellas.

The Herald's correspondent, writing with the Tybee expedition

July 2, 18692 de julio de 1869

The Herald's correspondent, writing with the Tybee expedition, opens mid-narrative with the steamer taking on board for transportation about forty soldiers designed to reinforce the patriotic inhabitants who had already turned their town against Luperón and driven him back from it. Gregorio Luperón is an outlaw from St. Domingo, in consequence of his turbulent conduct and his repeated and fruitless attempts at raising an insurrection; under any other than the humane administration of Báez, the writer says, the renegade would have been executed. Going to St. Thomas, with the assistance of parties leagued with him through large promises of lands and other advantages, he secured a steamer in which he succeeded in reaching Samaná bay; without any shadow of belligerent rights, under various flags, he assumed the privilege of attacking a legitimate government and interfering with rightful trade, and the steamer Telégrafo bore the American flag when she set out on her piratical cruise. After passing over the bar at the mouth of the Ozama the Tybee took in tow the Alta Gracia, eighty tons, an armed schooner belonging to the Dominican republic; the Capotillo, eighty tons, also an armed sister schooner, had been sent ahead the night before, both having in view an attack upon the pirate at Samaná. The extract ends as the Tybee overtook the Capotillo and fastened her on behind the Alta Gracia.

El corresponsal del Herald, que escribe con la expedición del Tybee, abre a mitad de la narración con el vapor embarcando para su transporte a unos cuarenta soldados destinados a reforzar a los habitantes patriotas que ya habían vuelto su pueblo contra Luperón y lo habían hecho retroceder de él. Gregorio Luperón es un proscrito de Santo Domingo, a consecuencia de su conducta turbulenta y de sus repetidos e infructuosos intentos de levantar una insurrección; bajo cualquier administración que no fuera la humana administración de Báez, dice el autor, el renegado habría sido ejecutado. Yendo a Santo Tomás, con la ayuda de personas aliadas con él mediante grandes promesas de tierras y otras ventajas, consiguió un vapor en el que logró llegar a la bahía de Samaná; sin sombra alguna de derechos de beligerante, bajo varias banderas, se arrogó el privilegio de atacar a un gobierno legítimo y entorpecer el comercio legítimo, y el vapor Telégrafo enarbolaba la bandera estadounidense cuando salió en su crucero pirático. Tras pasar la barra en la desembocadura del Ozama, el Tybee tomó a remolque la Alta Gracia, de ochenta toneladas, una goleta armada perteneciente a la República Dominicana; la Capotillo, de ochenta toneladas, también una goleta hermana armada, había sido enviada por delante la noche anterior, teniendo ambas en vista un ataque contra el pirata en Samaná. July 4, 18694 de julio de 1869

A correspondent of the steamer Tybee, writing from Santo Domingo City on June 22, 1869, describes five lively days loading an assorted cargo of exports and visiting the capital, which he calls the oldest city in the western hemisphere, its site selected by Christopher Columbus. Un corresponsal del vapor Tybee, que escribe desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo el 22 de junio de 1869, describe cinco días animados cargando un surtido cargamento de exportaciones y visitando la capital, a la que llama la ciudad más antigua del hemisferio occidental, cuyo sitio fue escogido por Cristóbal Colón. Describe las viejas murallas y fortificaciones, la capilla construida por Colón y su reloj de sol, la primera catedral con su altar mayor dorado y sus enseres de plata, y los muchos edificios españoles antiguos todavía en uso, en una ciudad cuya población es ahora cerca de la mitad de los 20,000 habitantes que tuvo una vez.

In the Herald's account of the Tybee party's entertainment at St. Domingo

July 4, 18694 de julio de 1869

In the Herald's account of the Tybee party's entertainment at St. Domingo, the dinner was an elegant affair and heartily enjoyed by all the party. During the course of the entertainment Mr. Fabens, prefacing his remarks with a few appropriate expressions, proposed the health of the President of the Dominican Republic, which was drank standing. After a few moments President Báez rose and thanked Mr. Fabens and the party for their kind sentiments: there was a spirit of more than friendship existing between St. Domingo and the United States, and he hoped this would increase; he felt honored with the entertainment and proposed the health of the President of the United States, which was also drank standing. President Báez was then compelled to leave the table sooner than courtesy would seem to demand, owing to the severe pain he was suffering in the head from a renewed attack of neuralgia. The letter goes on to complain of the uncertainty of the mails between this city and the United States: letters written in March last via Havana had just reached the city upon the party's arrival, and letters sent in advance to announce the proposed departure of the Tybee had not been received; the Spanish steamers run semi-monthly, alternating on their way to Havana between this city and Puerto Plata, and returning on the same schedule to St. Thomas.

En el relato del Herald sobre el agasajo a la comitiva del Tybee en Santo Domingo, la cena fue un asunto elegante y disfrutada de corazón por toda la comitiva. The Herald's correspondent reports the commerce of the bay of Samaná with Turk's Island, shipping provisions to that barren spot, and receiving in return quantities of salt. The small town of Santa Barbara is situated on the slopes bordering a small bight on the north side of the bay, about six miles northwest of Cayo Levantado; it was founded in 1750 by Don Francisco Rubio y Peñaranda, then Governor of St. Domingo. On the southern shore, near the mouth of the Yuna, is the village of Savanna La Mar, containing a few hundred inhabitants. The bay of Samaná was one of the earliest attractions in the physical features of ancient Hispaniola, and its convenience of geographical position from a strategic point of view, and the accommodations afforded by its well sheltered and placid waters, have associated it with all the warlike operations of the Antilles. After his discovery of the country, which he named Hispaniola, now St. Domingo, Columbus set out on his return to old Spain; in January 1493 he passed a high headland, which he named Cabo del Enamorado, the Lover's Cape, now Cape Cabron; further eastward he saw another headland, now Cape Samaná, which formed the most eastern point of the peninsula of the same name, where the extract ends.

Fue fundado en 1750 por don Francisco Rubio y Peñaranda, a la sazón gobernador de Santo Domingo.
