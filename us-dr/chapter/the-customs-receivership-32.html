Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser

The Evening Star prints an item: a new State has been formed

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Evening Star prints an item: a new State has been formed in the rebellious northern territory by the secessionists, creating an unprecedented situation in the West Indian republic, according to advices received by Dominicans in this city today. The news which they consider of the greatest importance is that Ricardo Limardo, minister of public works and communications in the Bordas government, has resigned and joined the forces of the secession movement in the north. The secessionists in Puerto Plata have formed a provisional government with Horacio Vasquez at the head. According to a cable despatch received by F. Deetjen, the consul general of Santo Domingo in New York, the Dominican war vessel Jacaqua has left Santo Domingo for Puerto Plata with between five and six hundred of the best trained soldiers in the country on board.

The Evening Star publica una nota: formado por los secesionistas un nuevo Estado en el territorio rebelde del norte, se ha creado una situación sin precedentes en la república antillana, según informes recibidos por los dominicanos de esta ciudad hoy. La noticia que consideran de la mayor importancia es que Ricardo Limardo, ministro de Obras Públicas y Comunicaciones del gobierno de Bordas, ha renunciado y se ha unido a las fuerzas del movimiento secesionista del norte. Los secesionistas de Puerto Plata han formado un gobierno provisional con Horacio Vásquez a la cabeza. Según un despacho por cable recibido por F. Deetjen, cónsul general de Santo Domingo en Nueva York, el buque de guerra dominicano Jacaqua ha salido de Santo Domingo hacia Puerto Plata con entre quinientos y seiscientos de los soldados mejor entrenados del país a bordo.

The Watchman and Southron

The Watchman and Southron prints a dispatch: the cruiser Des Moines

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Watchman and Southron prints a dispatch: the cruiser Des Moines was coaling today and was expected to sail tonight directly for Puerto Plata. The cruiser had been preparing to sail from Guantanamo, Cuba, for Santo Domingo since reports of the latest revolution there were received several days ago. In the meantime communication between Puerto Plata and the outside world has been broken and no word has come to the State Department since the brief message early today announcing the bombardment. In order to make certain the re-establishment of communication with the arrival of the Des Moines, the Treasury Department was asked to dispatch the revenue cutter Algonquin from San Juan, Puerto Rico, to Mona Passage, a point on the coast midway between Puerto Plata and Santo Domingo City. The cutter will reach there tomorrow and will serve as a wireless relay station between the Des Moines and the commercial station at Santo Domingo. Long conferences were held today by President Wilson, Secretary Bryan and Assistant Secretary Osborne with James M. Sullivan, the newly appointed American minister to the Dominican Republic. The new minister will sail Friday or Saturday for Santo Domingo City with assurances for President Bordas that he has the moral support of the United States government in his efforts to suppress the Puerto Plata rebellion.

The Watchman and Southron publica un despacho: el Des Moines carboneaba hoy y se esperaba que zarpara esta noche directamente hacia Puerto Plata. El crucero se había estado preparando para zarpar de Guantánamo, Cuba, hacia Santo Domingo desde que se recibieron, hace varios días, los informes de la última revolución allí. Entretanto, la comunicación entre Puerto Plata y el mundo exterior se había interrumpido y no había llegado al Departamento de Estado ninguna noticia desde el breve mensaje de temprano hoy que anunció el bombardeo. Para asegurar el restablecimiento de la comunicación con la llegada del Des Moines, se pidió al Departamento del Tesoro que enviara el guardacostas Algonquin desde San Juan, Puerto Rico, al Pasaje de la Mona, un punto de la costa a mitad de camino entre Puerto Plata y la ciudad de Santo Domingo. El guardacostas llegará allí mañana y servirá de estación de relevo inalámbrica entre el Des Moines y la estación comercial de Santo Domingo. Hoy se celebraron largas conferencias del presidente Wilson, el secretario Bryan y el subsecretario Osborne con James M. Sullivan, el recién nombrado ministro estadounidense en la República Dominicana. El nuevo ministro zarpará el viernes o el sábado hacia la ciudad de Santo Domingo con la seguridad, para el presidente Bordas, de que cuenta con el apoyo moral del Gobierno de los Estados Unidos en sus esfuerzos por suprimir la rebelión de Puerto Plata.

The Colorado Statesman

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance, inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican Republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for wiping out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 in 1905 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.

The Statesman publica un reportaje bajo el título 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, de Nueva Jersey, ha sido nombrado por el presidente Wilson receptor general de las aduanas de la república de Santo Domingo, continuándose así un experimento novedoso en las finanzas nacionales, inaugurado en 1905. Es el primer caso en que una nación en bancarrota ha aceptado que otra nación actúe como receptora de sus rentas y se interponga entre ella y sus acreedores durante la liquidación de sus obligaciones pendientes. En la actualidad, la República Dominicana tiene más dinero para gastar en fines apropiados que nunca antes, al tiempo que se está proveyendo ampliamente para extinguir su deuda en fecha temprana. La deuda de $30,000,000 en 1905 se ha reducido a $17,000,000, mientras las cuotas aduaneras se han reducido, el comercio ha aumentado, el desgobierno ha llegado a su fin y las bendiciones de la paz se han establecido en el país antes turbulento.

The Chickasha Daily Express

The Daily Express prints a dispatch: the Clyde steamer Seminole

September 17, 191317 de septiembre de 1913

The Daily Express prints a dispatch: the Clyde steamer Seminole was stopped as she was nearing port Monday and boarded by an officer from one of the gunboats and told she could not enter the harbor. The captain of the Seminole replied he would enter anyway. Upon being asked what he would do if he were ordered to remain outside, the captain replied that he would go in and take the boarding officer with him if he did not leave the ship. The officer hurriedly returned to the gunboat and the Seminole entered the port. The blockading boats steamed away. The Seminole had cleared from New York before the blockade of Puerto Plata had been declared, and having no munitions of war aboard and relying upon the presence of the Des Moines, she entered the port without fear of molestation. The captain of the Clyde line steamer Algonquin, on arriving here Tuesday, reported everything quiet in the southern ports which are ostensibly in the hands of the government. With the exception of Monte Cristi and Santiago, the northern part of the island is in the hands of rebels under Gen. Horacio Vasquez, at one time president of the republic. The rebel towns of Sanchez and Samana are barricaded and the inhabitants are ready for a fight. Monte Cristi is reported quiet, though the people there are much excited. It is reported that the revolution has spread to Seibo.

El oficial regresó apresuradamente al cañonero y el Seminole entró en el puerto. Los buques del bloqueo se alejaron. Evening Star

The Star prints an item: Mr. Sullivan reports his safe arrival

September 17, 191317 de septiembre de 1913

The Star prints an item: Mr. Sullivan reports his safe arrival at Puerto Plata, the center of the latest Dominican revolution; he is the American minister to the Dominican Republic. He was accompanied by Consul Hathaway, who had been in the United States on leave. Mr. Sullivan, in his endeavor to reconcile the warring factions, will follow his instructions from President Wilson and tell the rebels that the United States has no sympathy with the present attempt to overthrow the Bordas government. No official report has been received here of the breaking of the blockade at Puerto Plata by the Clyde liner Seminole with the apparent sanction of the American minister and the commander of the Des Moines. Puerto Plata is in possession of the rebels under Gen. Vasquez, and a blockade was attempted by two Dominican gunboats.

The Star publica una nota: el Sr. Sullivan informa de su llegada a salvo a Puerto Plata, centro de la última revolución dominicana; es el ministro estadounidense en la República Dominicana. Le acompañaba el cónsul Hathaway, que había estado en los Estados Unidos de licencia. El Sr. Sullivan, en su empeño por reconciliar a las facciones en lucha, seguirá sus instrucciones del presidente Wilson y dirá a los rebeldes que los Estados Unidos no simpatizan con el actual intento de derrocar el gobierno de Bordas. Aquí no se ha recibido informe oficial alguno sobre la ruptura del bloqueo de Puerto Plata por el vapor Seminole, de la Clyde, con la aparente sanción del ministro estadounidense y del comandante del Des Moines. Puerto Plata está en posesión de los rebeldes bajo el general Vásquez, y dos cañoneros dominicanos intentaron un bloqueo.

September 19, 191319 de septiembre de 1913

The Sun reported that President Wilson adopted a firm policy against Dominican revolutionists in Puerto Plata, viewing their rebellion against President Bordas as driven merely by a quest for government offices and revenue. Backed by the cruiser Des Moines, Minister Sullivan warned insurgent leaders that Washington would refuse to recognize insurgent regimes or disburse customs funds to them. U.S. officials believed that withholding customs revenue served as a decisive weapon to end Caribbean insurrections.

The Sun informó que el presidente Wilson adoptó una enérgica postura frente a los revolucionarios dominicanos en Puerto Plata, considerando que su alzamiento contra el presidente Bordas respondía meramente a un afán de cargos públicos y rentas. Con el respaldo del crucero Des Moines, el ministro Sullivan advirtió a los jefes insurgentes que Washington se negaría a reconocer regímenes rebeldes o a entregarles caudales de las aduanas. Los diplomáticos estadounidenses consideraban que la retención de los ingresos aduaneros constituía un arma decisiva para sofocar las rebeliones en el Caribe.

The Washington Herald

The Herald comments editorially: information laid before President Wilson

September 19, 191319 de septiembre de 1913

The Herald comments editorially: information laid before President Wilson by State Department officials convinced him that the revolution in Santo Domingo was purely one of greed upon the part of a small group of malcontents, and that the rebellion against the Bordas government was not established on any principle other than that of a desire for office and the proceeds thereof. Consequently, it was determined almost at the outset that everything should be done to discourage the revolutionists. They were at once informed of the disfavor with which the Washington government viewed their attempt to bring on another political upheaval. This declaration of disapproval was soon followed by the double threat of nonrecognition and withholding funds in case the rebels should succeed by force in gaining control of the government authority. Minister Sullivan, recently appointed minister to Santo Domingo, has arrived at Puerto Plata, the stronghold of the revolutionists, and is reiterating to the rebel leaders the determined position of President Wilson. He is backed up by the gunboat Des Moines.

The Herald comenta editorialmente: la información presentada al presidente Wilson por funcionarios del Departamento de Estado lo convenció de que la revolución en Santo Domingo era puramente una de codicia por parte de un pequeño grupo de descontentos, y de que la rebelión contra el gobierno de Bordas no se fundaba en principio alguno que no fuera el deseo de cargos y de sus provechos. En consecuencia, se determinó casi desde el principio que debía hacerse todo lo posible por desalentar a los revolucionarios. Se les informó de inmediato del desagrado con que el Gobierno de Washington veía su intento de provocar otro trastorno político. Esta declaración de desaprobación fue seguida pronto por la doble amenaza del no reconocimiento y de la retención de fondos en caso de que los rebeldes lograran, por la fuerza, hacerse con el control de la autoridad gubernamental. El ministro Sullivan, recién nombrado ministro en Santo Domingo, ha llegado a Puerto Plata, baluarte de los revolucionarios, y está reiterando a los jefes rebeldes la determinada posición del presidente Wilson. Lo respalda el cañonero Des Moines.
