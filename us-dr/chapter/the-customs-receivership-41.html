Con la renuncia del ministro Sullivan y del recaudador Vick, el gobierno de los EE. While President Bordas of the Dominican republic, with his scanty army, is besieging the northern rebels at Puerto Plata, his hold upon the capital, San Domingo, in the south is weakening, and according to today's advices to the State Department the southern insurgents were already in a position to dictate terms, which may mean the abdication or flight of Bordas. American Charge d'Affaires White, in the absence of Minister Sullivan, has been very active in trying to prevent a clash near the capital, as it would be disastrous to foreigners and the many noncombatants who have flocked into the city. The insurgent general Vidal, having occupied San Cristobal and Bani after driving out the small government garrisons, was marching toward the capital when met by Charge White. The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Charge White had taken counselThe Evening Star publica: El encargado White había tomado consejo

July 19, 191419 de julio de 1914

The fact that Mr. White has behind him two powerful American warships, with their marine contingents, probably had its effect upon the insurgent leader. At any rate, he agreed to an armistice for a few days while the federal officers in Santo Domingo City communicated his demands to President Bordas. The wireless outfits of the American naval vessels were used in transmitting this information to President Bordas. They afford the only means of quick communication between the north and the south at present, owing to the destruction of the telegraph wires in the interior. At Puerto Plata, Capt. Eberle of the cruiser Washington submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. Originally it was intended no forward movement should be made by these marines until reinforced by 400 additional men from the Philadelphia navy yard, but as these cannot reach there for two weeks it is probable, if a crisis is reached, the Hancock will be dispatched immediately to Puerto Plata or Santo Domingo City. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan.

El hecho de que el Sr. White tiene detrás de sí dos poderosos buques de guerra americanos, con sus contingentes de infantes de marina, probablemente tuvo su efecto sobre el jefe insurgente. En todo caso, aceptó un armisticio por unos días mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de Santo Domingo comunicaban sus demandas al presidente Bordas. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques navales americanos fueron utilizados para transmitir esta información al presidente Bordas. Ofrecen el único medio de comunicación rápida entre el norte y el sur en la actualidad, debido a la destrucción de los cables telegráficos en el interior. En Puerto Plata, el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. Originalmente se pretendía que ningún movimiento de avance se hiciera con estos infantes de marina hasta ser reforzados por 400 hombres adicionales del arsenal naval de Filadelfia, pero como estos no pueden llegar allí por dos semanas es probable que, si se llega a una crisis, el Hancock sea despachado inmediatamente a Puerto Plata o a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan.

The Anderson Intelligencer

The Anderson Intelligencer prints a dispatch: Two powerful American warshipsThe Anderson Intelligencer publica una nota: El encargado White

July 21, 191421 de julio de 1914

The adjutant general, Vidal, has occupied San Cristobal and Bani after a successful uprising at La Romana, about 50 miles east of the capital, last week, and he was marching toward the capital when met by Charge White. The wireless outfits of the American vessels were used and at Puerto Plata Captain Eberle, of the cruiser Washington, submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. So far Mr. White has received no answer, but it is believed that Bordas will either make a desperate effort to capture Puerto Plata or to abandon the siege and return south to protect his own capital. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan regarding the Dominican situation. It will be determined at this conference whether the Hancock's marines shall be landed and a virtual, though temporary, protectorate established.

El ayudante general, Vidal, ha ocupado San Cristóbal y Baní después de un exitoso levantamiento en La Romana, a unas 50 millas al este de la capital, la semana pasada, y marchaba hacia la capital cuando fue encontrado por el encargado White. Los equipos inalámbricos de los buques americanos fueron utilizados y en Puerto Plata el capitán Eberle, del crucero Washington, presentó los mensajes al presidente Bordas en su campamento fuera de la ciudad. Hasta ahora el Sr. White no ha recibido respuesta, pero se cree que Bordas hará un esfuerzo desesperado por capturar Puerto Plata o abandonará el sitio y regresará al sur para proteger su propia capital. El transporte naval de los Estados Unidos Hancock está por llegar a Guantánamo con casi 300 infantes de marina de Veracruz. Serán enviados a la República Dominicana en caso de emergencia. El ministro americano Sullivan ha llegado a Washington y espera conferenciar con el presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan sobre la situación dominicana. En esta conferencia se determinará si los infantes de marina del Hancock serán desembarcados y se establecerá un protectorado virtual, aunque temporal.

Evening Times-Republican

Evening Times-Republican prints: San Juan, Porto Rico, July 22.Evening Times-Republican publica: Procedente de Puerto Plata

July 22, 191422 de julio de 1914

The United States revenue cutter Algonquin arrived here last night from Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic, with 186 refugees, mostly Porto Ricans, on board. One woman died during the voyage from the effects of starvation. The condition of those remaining in Santo Domingo was reported to be pitiable, as it was almost impossible to obtain foodstuffs, the prices of which were prohibitive. A report was brought here by the Algonquin that an American bluejacket on board the gunboat Machias had been shot in the leg by a bullet from the shore.

Una mujer murió durante la travesía por los efectos del hambre. Se reportó que la condición de los que permanecen en Santo Domingo era lastimosa, pues era casi imposible obtener alimentos, cuyos precios eran prohibitivos. Un informe fue traído aquí por el Algonquin de que un marino americano a bordo de la cañonera Machias había sido herido en la pierna por una bala desde la costa.
