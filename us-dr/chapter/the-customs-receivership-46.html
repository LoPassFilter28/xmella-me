The State Department, at Mr. Phelan's request, asked Mr. Sullivan whether he desired to appear in person or be represented by counsel at hearings which Mr. Phelan said he thought would start early in January. January 15, 191515 de enero de 1915

This loan, by arrangement with the United States State and War Departments, was being paid off at the rate of $30,000 a month out of the government customs receipts. Farnham said he knew only by hearsay of the reputation of the Banco Nacional and the Jarvis interests controlling it, and that he had no relations with Sullivan. He said that Sir Alexander Baird, representing English capitalists, in the promotion of the Samana railroad in the republic, had approached the National City Bank, seeking financial aid, but that the bank had declined to interest itself in the road. The only relations the bank had with Michelena, he said, was to ask him to present the bank proposition to the government. Farnham denied that there were any plans contemplated by the bank to exploit the island in any way. It was Secretary of State Knox who approved the loan, the witness said. Knox cabled the bank proposal to the then American minister, who presented it to the Dominican government. A copy went to Michelena. The minister and Michelena reported that the government favored their proposition in preference to propositions of other bankers.

Este préstamo, por arreglo con los Departamentos de Estado y de Guerra de los Estados Unidos, se estaba pagando a razón de $30,000 al mes de los ingresos de aduanas del gobierno. Farnham dijo que solo sabía de oídas sobre la reputación del Banco Nacional y los intereses Jarvis que lo controlaban, y que no tenía relaciones con Sullivan. Dijo que Sir Alexander Baird, en representación de capitalistas ingleses, en la promoción del ferrocarril de Samana en la república, se había acercado al National City Bank en busca de ayuda financiera, pero que el banco se había negado a interesarse en el ferrocarril. Las únicas relaciones que el banco tenía con Michelena, dijo, eran pedirle que presentara la proposición del banco al gobierno. Farnham negó que hubiera planes contemplados por el banco para explotar la isla en modo alguno. Fue el secretario de Estado Knox quien aprobó el préstamo, dijo el testigo. Knox cablegrafió la proposición del banco al entonces ministro americano, que la presentó al gobierno dominicano. Una copia fue a Michelena. The Montgomery Advertiser

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Bryne's affidavitThe Montgomery Advertiser publica una nota: La declaración jurada

January 17, 191517 de enero de 1915

I know all the officials of the State Department have been advised of the true conditions in Santo Domingo by many other Americans whose standing and integrity cannot be questioned. The American people, the President of the United States, and the Democratic party have been discredited and dishonored in Santo Domingo by the conduct of Minister Sullivan. The net result in Santo Domingo is that sincere and honest American business men who have invested their money in legitimate enterprises for the development of the Dominican Republic have suffered great loss.' Supplementing his affidavit, Bryne testified that he had asked Secretary Bryan 'most urgently to clean out the situation before it got public and created a scandal.' 'Secretary Bryan said to me,' testified the witness, 'I wish you'd write this out and sign your name to it and leave it here.' Then, keeping his eyes on me, he said, 'Perhaps you wouldn't care to do this.' I said, 'Mr. Bryan, I wouldn't have come here if I wasn't willing to stand by what I've told you.' I said I would be glad to write out a statement, and then he said it would not be necessary. I did everything I could to get Mr. Bryan to take the matter up.' Bryne testified that as far as he knew the alleged conspiracy had not matured, chiefly because of Timothy J. Sullivan's frankness in discussing it.

Sé que todos los funcionarios del Departamento de Estado han sido informados de las verdaderas condiciones en Santo Domingo por muchos otros americanos cuya reputación e integridad no pueden ser cuestionadas. El pueblo americano, el presidente de los Estados Unidos y el Partido Demócrata han sido deshonrados y desacreditados en Santo Domingo por la conducta del ministro Sullivan. El resultado neto en Santo Domingo es que hombres de negocios americanos sinceros y honestos que han invertido su dinero en empresas legítimas para el desarrollo de la República Dominicana han sufrido grandes pérdidas». Complementando su declaración jurada, Bryne declaró que había pedido al secretario Bryan «con la mayor urgencia que limpiara la situación antes de que se hiciera pública y creara un escándalo». «El secretario Bryan me dijo», declaró el testigo, «desearía que escribieras esto, lo firmaras con tu nombre y lo dejaras aquí». Luego, manteniendo su mirada en mí, dijo: «Quizás no querrías hacer esto». Dije: «Sr. Bryan, no habría venido aquí si no estuviera dispuesto a sostener lo que le he dicho». Dije que con gusto escribiría una declaración, y entonces él dijo que no sería necesario. Hice todo lo que pude para lograr que el Sr. Bryan se ocupara del asunto. Bryne declaró que, hasta donde él sabía, la supuesta conspiración no había madurado, principalmente por la franqueza de Timothy J. Sullivan al discutirla.

Alleged political activities of Congressman James A. Hamill, of New Jersey, W. Beer of New York, and John O. Gray, of Wilmington, Del., in procuring the appointment of James M. Sullivan as minister to the Dominican republic were described today at the investigation which is being conducted by Senator-elect Phelan, of California, into Sullivan's fitness to hold office. W. G. McGunness, of Washington, who described himself as a personal friend of President McKinley and a 'Republican politician,' testified that Beer and Sullivan had promised him the position of director of posts in Santo Domingo in return for his assistance in promoting Sullivan's candidacy. The position was to be a 'sinecure,' said the witness. He described meetings which he said were held between Beer, Hamill and Sullivan in Washington, previous to Sullivan's appointment, the subject of which was ways and means to get the ear of Secretary Bryan. Beer, it has been brought out in previous testimony, was attorney for the Banco Nacional interests, Santo Domingo, who are charged with schemes to exploit public works contracts with the aid of Sullivan.

El puesto iba a ser una «sinecura», dijo el testigo. Se describió a sí mismo como amigo personal del presidente McKinley y un «político republicano». Las supuestas actividades políticas del congresista James A. Hamill, de Nueva Jersey, W. Beer de Nueva York, y John O. Gray, de Wilmington, Delaware, para procurar el nombramiento de James M. Sullivan como ministro en la República Dominicana fueron descritas hoy en la investigación que conduce el senador electo Phelan, de California, sobre la aptitud de Sullivan para ocupar el cargo. Describió reuniones que dijo se celebraron entre Beer, Hamill y Sullivan en Washington, antes del nombramiento de Sullivan, cuyo tema fue los medios para ganarse el oído del secretario Bryan. Beer, se ha revelado en testimonios anteriores, era abogado de los intereses del Banco Nacional de Santo Domingo, a quienes se acusa de maquinaciones para explotar contratos de obras públicas con la ayuda de Sullivan.
