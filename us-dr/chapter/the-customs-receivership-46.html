The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: The appointment of James D. Phelan

December 23, 191423 de diciembre de 1914

The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints: The appointment of James D. Phelan, senator-elect from California, as a special commissioner to investigate charges against American Minister Sullivan in the Dominican republic was received today. President Wilson and Secretary Bryan have given Mr. Phelan full authority to conduct the investigation without further orders. He will work first in the insular bureau of the War Department and later make an investigation in Santo Domingo. Washington, Dec. 23.

The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser publica: El nombramiento de James D. Phelan, senador electo de California, como comisionado especial para investigar acusaciones contra el ministro americano Sullivan en la república dominicana fue recibido hoy. El presidente Wilson y el secretario Bryan han dado al Sr. Phelan plena autoridad para conducir la investigación sin más órdenes. Trabajará primero en la oficina insular del Departamento de Guerra y más tarde hará una investigación en Santo Domingo. Washington, 23 de diciembre.

December 26, 191426 de diciembre de 1914

Evening Star

The Evening Star prints an item under the head 'MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO': J. D. Phelan

December 27, 191427 de diciembre de 1914

The Evening Star prints an item under the head 'MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO': J. D. Phelan, senator-elect from California, appointed several days ago to investigate charges against James M. Sullivan, American minister to the Dominican republic, announced yesterday he might visit the island in connection with his inquiry. The State Department, at Mr. Phelan's request, asked Mr. Sullivan whether he desired to appear in person or be represented by counsel at hearings which Mr. Phelan said he thought would start early in January. Persons familiar with affairs in the Dominican republic have been asked to be present at the hearing. Their appearance will be voluntary, as there is no authority to subpoena witnesses or to compel them to testify. Some of the hearings will be here and some in New York. In accordance with a suggestion of Secretary Bryan, in his letter authorizing Mr. Phelan to make the inquiry, the hearings will be public.

The Evening Star publica una nota bajo el título «MAY VISIT SANTO DOMINGO»: J. D. Phelan, senador electo de California, nombrado hace varios días para investigar acusaciones contra James M. Sullivan, ministro americano en la república dominicana, anunció ayer que podría visitar la isla en relación con su investigación. El Departamento de Estado, a petición del Sr. Phelan, preguntó al Sr. Sullivan si deseaba comparecer en persona o ser representado por un abogado en audiencias que el Sr. Phelan dijo que creía que comenzarían a principios de enero. Se ha pedido a personas familiarizadas con los asuntos de la república dominicana que estén presentes en la audiencia. Su comparecencia será voluntaria, pues no hay autoridad para citar testigos ni para obligarlos a declarar. Algunas de las audiencias serán aquí y algunas en Nueva York. De acuerdo con una sugerencia del secretario Bryan, en su carta que autorizaba al Sr. Phelan a hacer la investigación, las audiencias serán públicas.

January 15, 191515 de enero de 1915

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer prints an item: The continuation of the inquiry to determine the fitness of James M. Sullivan, American minister to the republic, heard Farnham, of the National City Bank, as its first witness today before Senator-elect Phelan, of California. The National City Bank, it has been testified, was represented in negotiating the loan by Santiago Michelena, a Santo Domingo banker whose institution was depository of the republic until they were transferred, at the instance of Sullivan, it is alleged, to the Banco Nacionale. Michelena was not in favor with Secretary of State Bryan, it was testified, because Mr. Bryan 'was obsessed with the idea that Michelena represented the money trust.' Farnham began his testimony by saying the National City Bank had no interest in Santo Domingo in any way other than lending $1,500,000 to the government two years ago. This loan, by arrangement with the United States State and War Departments, was being paid off at the rate of $80,000 a month out of the government customs receipts. Farnham said he knew only by hearsay of the reputation of the Banco Nacional and the Jarvis interests controlling it, and that he had no relations with Sullivan. 'There has been talk of the existence of a money trust in New York,' said Commissioner Phelan. 'What do you know about that?' 'The National City Bank does not know that a money trust exists,' remarked Charles H. Strong, the commissioner's attorney.

New Britain Herald

The New Britain Herald prints an item: Farnham began his testimony

January 15, 191515 de enero de 1915

The New Britain Herald prints an item: Farnham began his testimony by saying that the National City Bank had no interest in Santo Domingo in any way, other than in lending $1,500,000 to the government two years ago. This loan, by arrangement with the United States State and War Departments, was being paid off at the rate of $30,000 a month out of the government customs receipts. Farnham said he knew only by hearsay of the reputation of the Banco Nacional and the Jarvis interests controlling it, and that he had no relations with Sullivan. He said that Sir Alexander Baird, representing English capitalists, in the promotion of the Samana railroad in the republic, had approached the National City Bank, seeking financial aid, but that the bank had declined to interest itself in the road. The only relations the bank had with Michelena, he said, was to ask him to present the bank proposition to the government. Farnham denied that there were any plans contemplated by the bank to exploit the island in any way. It was Secretary of State Knox who approved the loan, the witness said. Knox cabled the bank proposal to the then American minister, who presented it to the Dominican government. A copy went to Michelena. The minister and Michelena reported that the government favored their proposition in preference to propositions of other bankers.

The New Britain Herald publica una nota: El testimonio de Farnham comenzó diciendo que el National City Bank no tenía interés en Santo Domingo en modo alguno, que no fuera en prestar $1,500,000 al gobierno hace dos años. Este préstamo, por arreglo con los Departamentos de Estado y de Guerra de los Estados Unidos, se estaba pagando a razón de $30,000 al mes de los ingresos de aduanas del gobierno. Farnham dijo que solo sabía de oídas sobre la reputación del Banco Nacional y los intereses Jarvis que lo controlaban, y que no tenía relaciones con Sullivan. Dijo que Sir Alexander Baird, en representación de capitalistas ingleses, en la promoción del ferrocarril de Samana en la república, se había acercado al National City Bank en busca de ayuda financiera, pero que el banco se había negado a interesarse en el ferrocarril. Las únicas relaciones que el banco tenía con Michelena, dijo, eran pedirle que presentara la proposición del banco al gobierno. Farnham negó que hubiera planes contemplados por el banco para explotar la isla en modo alguno. Fue el secretario de Estado Knox quien aprobó el préstamo, dijo el testigo. Knox cablegrafió la proposición del banco al entonces ministro americano, que la presentó al gobierno dominicano. Una copia fue a Michelena. El ministro y Michelena informaron que el gobierno favorecía su proposición con preferencia a las proposiciones de otros banqueros.

Ese préstamo, por arreglo con el gobierno de los Estados Unidos, se estaba pagando a $30,000 al mes de los ingresos de aduanas. Farnham dijo que no tenía relaciones con Sullivan. El secretario Knox aprobó el plan, dijo el testigo. Knox cablegrafió la proposición del banco al entonces ministro americano, que la presentó al gobierno dominicano. Una copia fue a Michelena. El ministro y Michelena informaron que el gobierno favorecía su proposición con preferencia a las de otros bancos. Conroy, dijo Vick, admitió ante él que nunca había recibido un título de médico. Vick, que instigó la presente investigación, dijo que a Sullivan generalmente se le atribuía la responsabilidad de la revolución dominicana de 1914, porque apoyó al gobierno de Bordas contra los Legalistas.

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Bryne's affidavit

January 17, 191517 de enero de 1915

The Montgomery Advertiser prints an item: Bryne's affidavit continued: 'I told all these things in substance as set forth in this affidavit to Secretary Bryan. Supplementing his affidavit, Bryne testified that he had asked Secretary Bryan 'most urgently to clean out the situation before it got public and created a scandal.' 'Secretary Bryan said to me,' testified the witness, 'I wish you'd write this out and sign your name to it and leave it here.' Then, keeping his eyes on me, he said, 'Perhaps you wouldn't care to do this.' I said, 'Mr. Bryan, I wouldn't have come here if I wasn't willing to stand by what I've told you.' I said I would be glad to write out a statement, and then he said it would not be necessary. I did everything I could to get Mr. Bryan to take the matter up.' Bryne testified that as far as he knew the alleged conspiracy had not matured, chiefly because of Timothy J. Sullivan's frankness in discussing it.

The Montgomery Advertiser publica una nota: La declaración jurada de Bryne continuaba: «Dije todas estas cosas en sustancia, como se expone en esta declaración jurada, al secretario Bryan. Dije que con gusto escribiría una declaración, y entonces él dijo que no sería necesario. Hice todo lo que pude para lograr que el Sr. Bryan se ocupara del asunto. The Washington Herald

The Washington Herald prints an item: William S. McGinnis testified

January 20, 191520 de enero de 1915

The Washington Herald prints an item: William S. McGinnis testified today before Commissioner Phelan, who is investigating charges against James Mark Sullivan, United States Minister to the Dominican republic, that Beer had captured the finances of Santo Domingo, and was planning to put its officers into Nicaragua. Sullivan was appointed by the efforts of Beer, McGinnis said, and he called him the 'big chief.' When the two met in a Washington hotel, after Sullivan's appointment as Minister, the latter told him: 'Things are going fine, Bill. The big chief will soon be not only financial agent of Santo Domingo, but of Nicaragua as well.' When McGinnis repeated the conversation to Beer, the 'big chief' said, 'for God's sake, keep quiet about that.' On another occasion Beer told him in Washington, 'This Santo Domingo deal has been the dream of my life. There's millions in it. I've been waiting for it for years.' Representative James A. Hamill, of New Jersey, according to the witness, said: 'Beer gets me so worked up over Nicaragua that every morning I wake up thinking I'm going to get $999,000.' McGinnis, formerly assistant superintendent of the railway mail service, told in detail the final steps to put Sullivan into office, even after Secretary Tumulty had told Representative Hamill that the White House heard Beer was interested in the Banco Nacionale of Santo Domingo.

He described meetings which he said were held between Beer, Hamill and Sullivan in Washington, previous to Sullivan's appointment, the subject of which was ways and means to get the ear of Secretary Bryan. Beer, it has been brought out in previous testimony, was attorney for the Banco Nacional interests, Santo Domingo, who are charged with schemes to exploit public works contracts with the aid of Sullivan.
