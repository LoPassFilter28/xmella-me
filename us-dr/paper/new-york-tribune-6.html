The Tribune reports the clearance of the American steamer Madrid

February 17, 188917 de febrero de 1889

The Tribune reports the clearance of the American steamer Madrid, now the Dominican gunboat Conserva: Minister Preston received word from Acting Collector McClelland that the question of giving the vessel clearance had been referred to President Cleveland, and Consul L. Julia then appeared with his certificate, dated New York, February 15, 1889, that he had bought the American steamer Madrid, of New Orleans, of the burden of 560 58-100 tons, for account and on behalf of the Dominican Republic, that she had been legally transferred to him as his Government's representative, and that her name was changed to Conserva, sailing now under the Dominican flag. The United States Attorney advised that clearance could not be legally withheld, and it was granted at once; her cargo is 400 tons of coal, ballast and stores for ship's use, under Captain Ankers with a crew of twenty-three. The report adds that the steamer Carondelet, loaded with arms and ammunition from keelson to main deck, waits in an inlet along the coast to transfer her cargo to the Conserva, while word has been sent to Legitime's navy to watch for her coming to Samana.

El Tribune informa del despacho del vapor estadounidense Madrid, ahora la cañonera dominicana Conserva: el ministro Preston recibió aviso del colector interino McClelland de que la cuestión de dar despacho al buque había sido remitida al presidente Cleveland, y el cónsul L. Julia compareció entonces con su certificado, fechado en Nueva York el 15 de febrero de 1889, de que había comprado el vapor estadounidense Madrid, de Nueva Orleans, de 560 58-100 toneladas de arqueo, por cuenta y en nombre de la República Dominicana, de que le había sido legalmente transferido como representante de su Gobierno y de que su nombre fue cambiado a Conserva, navegando ahora bajo bandera dominicana. El informe añade que el vapor Carondelet, cargado de armas y municiones desde la quilla hasta la cubierta mayor, espera en una ensenada de la costa para transferir su cargamento a la Conserva, mientras se ha avisado a la marina de Légitime que vigile su llegada a Samaná.

A schooner's captain tells the Tribune how a strange war vessel

April 11, 188911 de abril de 1889

A schooner's captain tells the Tribune how a strange war vessel fired on his vessel and chased her: a shot struck the water a few yards under their stern, and the low, rakish, three-masted steamer of about 500 tons, apparently of French or Spanish build with her decks crowded with men, followed them some two hours before giving up and steaming toward land, though they were far outside any jurisdiction on the high seas with the American flag flying at their peak.
