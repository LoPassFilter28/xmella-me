Under the head 'Only Nine Heroes in Sight,' the Tribune prints

May 21, 189921 de mayo de 1899

Under the head 'Only Nine Heroes in Sight,' the Tribune prints Juan I. Jimenes's explanation of why his last revolution failed: Jimenes, who has desired for years to overthrow President Heureaux of the Dominican Republic, has issued an 'appeal to the Dominican people,' which, for fluency in the use of adjectives and power of vituperation, is hard to beat, and the translation into English, which he is distributing in this country, makes interesting, if not lucid, reading. Of his last attempt at a revolution, which ended in a fizzle, he writes: 'The proof of my determination was made evident on my landing in the harbor of Monte Cristi for the purpose of taking that city, with the nine men who accompanied me; upon seeing that my plan had failed, by which the city was to be surrendered to me by those expected to storm the place, in spite of this disappointment, I took the city and made its Governor my prisoner; but for the fact that the chief of the fortress managed to escape and give the alarm amongst the few soldiers asleep in the fortress, the latter would have fallen more easily, and the revolution would have continued its successful course; the uprisings that were to take place in other sections of the country did not occur, because of the instructions I had given to the effect that such uprisings must be carried out only after the city was in my possession, or not at all should I fail to take it.'

Bajo el título «Only Nine Heroes in Sight», el Tribune publica la explicación de Juan I. Jimenes de por qué fracasó su última revolución: Jimenes, que ha deseado por años derrocar al presidente Heureaux de la República Dominicana, ha emitido un «llamado al pueblo dominicano», que, por fluidez en el uso de adjetivos y poder de vituperio, es difícil de superar, y la traducción al inglés, que él está distribuyendo en este país, hace una lectura interesante, si no lúcida. De su último intento de revolución, que terminó en un fiasco, escribe: «La prueba de mi determinación se hizo evidente en mi desembarco en el puerto de Monte Cristi con el propósito de tomar esa ciudad, con los nueve hombres que me acompañaron; al ver que mi plan había fracasado, por el cual la ciudad había de serme entregada por los que se esperaba asaltaran la plaza, a pesar de esta decepción, tomé la ciudad e hice prisionero a su Gobernador; si no hubiera sido porque el jefe de la fortaleza logró escapar y dar la alarma entre los pocos soldados dormidos en la fortaleza, esta habría caído más fácilmente, y la revolución habría continuado su curso exitoso; los levantamientos que habían de tener lugar en otras secciones del país no ocurrieron, por causa de las instrucciones que yo había dado al efecto de que tales levantamientos debían llevarse a cabo solo después de que la ciudad estuviera en mi posesión, o no hacerse en absoluto si yo fracasaba en tomarla».

The Tribune prints a story accusing General Maximo Gomez of plotting to overthrow the government of San Domingo: 'Having abandoned the hope of becoming the head of the Cuban Republic, Gomez is conferring with discontented Dominicans, among whom is General Bautista, who is now in Havana, with a view of organizing an expedition composed of former American and Cuban soldiers, to attack San Domingo, kill President Heureaux and proclaim Gomez Dictator.' The Tribune prints an article on the Caracas government's effort

July 14, 189914 de julio de 1899

The Tribune prints an article on the Caracas government's effort to secure the body of Alonzo de Ojeda: it is probably upon the invitation of Dr. Jesus Munoz Tebar, governor of the Venezuelan state of Zulia, formerly Minister of Public Works, who has resided for nearly two years in New-York, where he wrote a book on the political philosophy of Latin America, that the government at Caracas has tried to secure the body. The Dominican President, General Ulysses Heureaux, is also a worshipper of all memories and relics relative to the history of his country; he caused recently the building of a handsome mausoleum at Santo Domingo, where were deposited last December the alleged remains of Columbus — the real ones, according to the Dominicans, who say that it is only the ashes of Diego, the son of Columbus, which have been transported by the Spaniards to the Seville cathedral; and the newspapers of Santo Domingo and public opinion there approve the intention of the government to decline the request of Venezuela and to keep the ashes of Ojeda in the lands of Cibao and San Juan, his last abode on earth.

El Tribune publica un artículo sobre el esfuerzo del gobierno de Caracas por asegurar el cuerpo de Alonzo de Ojeda: probablemente por invitación del Dr. Jesus Munoz Tebar, gobernador del estado venezolano de Zulia, antes ministro de Obras Públicas, que ha residido por casi dos años en Nueva York, donde escribió un libro sobre la filosofía política de América Latina, el gobierno de Caracas ha tratado de asegurar el cuerpo. El presidente dominicano, el general Ulysses Heureaux, es también un adorador de todas las memorias y reliquias relativas a la historia de su país; causó recientemente la construcción de un hermoso mausoleo en Santo Domingo, donde fueron depositados en diciembre pasado los supuestos restos de Colón — los verdaderos, según los dominicanos, que dicen que son solo las cenizas de Diego, el hijo de Colón, las que han sido transportadas por los españoles a la catedral de Sevilla; y los periódicos de Santo Domingo y la opinión pública allí aprueban la intención del gobierno de declinar la petición de Venezuela y de guardar las cenizas de Ojeda en las tierras del Cibao y San Juan, su última morada en la tierra.

The Tribune prints an editorial on the assassination and the standing of the republic

July 28, 189928 de julio de 1899

The Tribune prints an editorial on the assassination and the standing of the republic among nations: there were not a few who, on hearing the news of General Heureaux's assassination, cynically remarked, 'What, another?' as though such tragedies were common in that land; the fact is, however, that it is unique: two of our own Presidents have been murdered, and one of France's; recent years have witnessed the murder of a Russian Czar and an Austrian Empress; there were attempts upon the life of the first German Emperor, and even the Queen of England has not escaped such outrage; but now, for the first time, Santo Domingo mourns a murdered Chief of State, and it is for other nations not to rail at her, but to sympathize. Nor is the little republic thus brought into prominence in other respects a fitting target for our scorn: it has had numerous revolutions, but we think it has fared no worse in that respect than many others, certainly not if we take into account the disabilities under which it has labored — a republic of mixed races, by the side of a negro State and isolated from all others and treated with scorn and selfishness by all its betters, is not a likely candidate for the title of Utopia; nevertheless, this one has of late years been making steady progress, peace has prevailed, and industrial and commercial prosperity has been maintained and increased.

El Tribune publica un editorial sobre el asesinato y la posición de la república entre las naciones: no fueron pocos los que, al oír la noticia del asesinato del general Heureaux, remarcaron cínicamente, «¿Qué, otro?», como si tales tragedias fueran comunes en esa tierra; el hecho es, sin embargo, que es único: dos de nuestros propios Presidentes han sido asesinados, y uno de Francia; años recientes han presenciado el asesinato de un Zar ruso y de una Emperatriz austríaca; hubo intentos contra la vida del primer Emperador alemán, y hasta la Reina de Inglaterra no ha escapado de tal ultraje; pero ahora, por primera vez, Santo Domingo llora a un Jefe de Estado asesinado, y corresponde a las otras naciones no burlarse de ella, sino simpatizar. Ni es la pequeña república así traída a la prominencia en otros respectos un blanco apropiado para nuestro desprecio: ha tenido numerosas revoluciones, pero pensamos que no ha salido peor en ese respecto que muchas otras, ciertamente no si tomamos en cuenta las desventajas bajo las cuales ha trabajado — una república de razas mezcladas, al lado de un Estado negro y aislada de todos los demás y tratada con desprecio y egoísmo por todos sus superiores, no es una candidata probable para el título de Utopía; sin embargo, esta ha estado en años recientes haciendo un progreso constante, la paz ha prevalecido, y la prosperidad industrial y comercial ha sido mantenida y aumentada.

The Tribune continues its editorial on the assassination and the standing of the republic

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Tribune continues its editorial on the assassination and the standing of the republic: President Heureaux had been an eminently popular Chief Magistrate, commanding the support of all parties and credited by all with having secured for the country a stability of government and a general prosperity never known before; and the argument is strengthened by the fact that neither before the murder were there any symptoms of revolution nor have there been any since; Dominicans may not be versed in the highest culture of political revolutionists, but they probably know that a revolution is not to be effected by killing just one man and doing nothing more. There will doubtless be, as indeed there already is, a flocking to the scene of all manner of discontented and ambitious men eager to profit from whatever disturbance of affairs there may be; some professional revolutionists may try to stir up trouble; it is even rumored that General Gomez, having finished his work in Cuba, will make some effort to secure the Dominican Presidency — a rumor which it is difficult to credit. All such attacks upon the established order in Santo Domingo will be deplorable from every point of view, and will especially meet with the reprobation of Americans; in past years individual citizens of this country have more than once contributed to disorder and to revolt in the lands of tropical America, but they have never done so with the sympathy or countenance of the United States Government, and it is to be hoped none will be so unworthy as to join in such movements now.

El Tribune continúa su editorial sobre el asesinato y la posición de la república: el presidente Heureaux había sido un Magistrado Jefe eminentemente popular, que comandaba el apoyo de todos los partidos y a quien todos acreditaban haber asegurado para el país una estabilidad de gobierno y una prosperidad general nunca conocidas antes; y el argumento se fortalece por el hecho de que ni antes del asesinato hubo síntomas de revolución ni los ha habido desde entonces; los dominicanos pueden no estar versados en la más alta cultura de los revolucionarios políticos, pero probablemente saben que una revolución no se efectúa matando a un solo hombre y no haciendo nada más. Habrá sin duda, como de hecho ya la hay, una afluencia a la escena de toda clase de hombres descontentos y ambiciosos deseosos de aprovechar cualquier perturbación de los asuntos que pueda haber; algunos revolucionarios profesionales pueden tratar de agitar problemas; se rumora incluso que el general Gómez, habiendo terminado su labor en Cuba, hará algún esfuerzo para asegurarse la Presidencia dominicana — un rumor que es difícil de creer. Todos tales ataques sobre el orden establecido en Santo Domingo serán deplorables desde todo punto de vista, y encontrarán especialmente la reprobación de los americanos; en años pasados ciudadanos individuales de este país han contribuido más de una vez al desorden y a la revuelta en las tierras de la América tropical, pero nunca lo han hecho con la simpatía o el consentimiento del Gobierno de los Estados Unidos, y es de esperar que ninguno sea tan indigno como para unirse ahora a tales movimientos.

The Tribune completes its Havana item on General Gomez and the succession, and opens a Columbus item on Consul-General Maxwell: Gomez's actions indicated his contempt for the stories, yet in the clubs and cafés he is seriously accused of conspiring to bring both Hayti and Cuba under the dominion of the United States; in alluding to the café conspirators, Gomez made use of a contemptuous term which is used among Cubans to signify cowards, and said he did not believe that many men belonging to the army would have anything to do with such people, who, he declared, do not represent Cuba, yet cause much misrepresentation. The Tribune prints the views of Charles W. Wells on the situation

July 30, 189930 de julio de 1899

The Tribune prints the views of Charles W. Wells on the situation in the republic: the Santo Domingo Improvement Company, of this city, whose offices are in the Empire Building, own the railroad system in the republic, and are trustees for the Government bonds, as security for which they have charge of the collection of the revenues of the country. Mr. Wells, president of the company, has often visited in Santo Domingo; he returned from there about a month ago, after having spent several weeks with President Heureaux and other State officials, and was to have met the President in August; he is thoroughly familiar with the political, social and industrial condition of the republic, and, having large interests at stake himself, his views on the situation should be of comfort to other foreigners who hold Dominican securities: 'I am not in the least worried about Santo Domingo. To be sure, the republic has lost an able man and a good President by the death of Senor Heureaux, but one man does not make or even hold together a nation. In the United States it seems to be the prevailing opinion that the inhabitants of Santo Domingo are a lot of ignorant, uneducated savages. On the other hand, they are, as a whole, well bred, intelligent and refined. They devote a great deal of attention to education. Fathers whose means will permit of it educate their children.'

El Tribune publica las opiniones de Charles W. Wells sobre la situación en la república: la Santo Domingo Improvement Company, de esta ciudad, cuyas oficinas están en el Empire Building, posee el sistema ferroviario en la república, y es fideicomisaria de los bonos del Gobierno, como garantía de los cuales tiene a su cargo el cobro de las rentas del país. El señor Wells, presidente de la compañía, ha visitado a menudo Santo Domingo; regresó de allí hace cerca de un mes, después de haber pasado varias semanas con el presidente Heureaux y otros funcionarios del Estado, y había de haberse reunido con el Presidente en agosto; él está profundamente familiarizado con la condición política, social e industrial de la república, y, teniendo grandes intereses en juego él mismo, sus opiniones sobre la situación deben ser de consuelo para otros extranjeros que poseen valores dominicanos: «No estoy en lo más mínimo preocupado por Santo Domingo. Es cierto que la república ha perdido un hombre capaz y un buen Presidente con la muerte del señor Heureaux, pero un solo hombre no hace ni siquiera mantiene unida a una nación. En los Estados Unidos parece ser la opinión prevaleciente que los habitantes de Santo Domingo son un montón de salvajes ignorantes y sin educación. Por el contrario, son, en su conjunto, bien educados, inteligentes y refinados. Dedican gran atención a la educación. Los padres cuyos medios lo permiten educan a sus hijos».

The Tribune prints part of an editorial on the course of the government

August 2, 18992 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints part of an editorial on the course of the government after the assassination: the assassination of President Heureaux, it is proposed, shall not be made the pretext for a demonstration of naval force by foreign Powers, such as has usually attended the outbreak of the chronic happenings of a revolutionary character in the independent republics, so called, in the West Indies; this action of the Government, without awaiting the usual formulary of a request by the United States representative at the point of disturbance, is regarded as a public notice of an intention on the part of this Government to command order among these turbulent people; and aside from the 'sphere of influence' of the United States on the American Hemisphere, as set forth by President Monroe and repeatedly backed up by his long line of successors in the Presidential office.

El Tribune publica parte de un editorial sobre el curso del gobierno después del asesinato: el asesinato del presidente Heureaux, se propone, no será hecho el pretexto para una demostración de fuerza naval por Potencias extranjeras, como ha usualmente asistido el estallido de los sucesos crónicos de un carácter revolucionario en las repúblicas independientes, así llamadas, en las Indias Occidentales; esta acción del Gobierno, sin esperar el formulario usual de una solicitud por el representante de los Estados Unidos en el punto de disturbio, es considerada como un aviso público de una intención de parte de este Gobierno de imponer orden entre esa gente turbulenta; y aparte de la «esfera de influencia» de los Estados Unidos en el Hemisferio Americano, como establecida por el presidente Monroe y repetidamente respaldada por su larga línea de sucesores en el oficio Presidencial.

The Tribune prints the closing part of a statement on the situation

August 2, 18992 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints the closing part of a statement on the situation in Santo Domingo: there is no fear that the insurrectionists will interfere in any way with the property of foreigners on the island; notwithstanding the many revolutions which have taken place in Santo Domingo since it attained its independence, in no instance has either life been taken or property molested of any foreigner, with the single exception of the action of Heureaux, who opened the vaults of the French bank because of a personal quarrel with one of its directors; women and children are safe at all times, as the Dominicans never stoop to war with the helpless, nor do they ever sack or pillage, but fight for what they believe to be right in the open, as men and soldiers should do; Mr. Jiminez and General Gomez are warm personal friends — in fact, both reside in the same house in Havana; as Heureaux was a usurper, the Vice-President is equally so, but bloodshed and insurrection would cease at once if the latter would announce that a legal, public election would take place on August 16, which is the Independence day of the country; the entire country is aroused to the necessity of a change to a government where life is secure, and it recognizes that the former Vice-President and the present pretending President is but one of the accomplices of Heureaux, and as such has no confidence in him; and there is but one man who at this time represents the people and their best interests, and that is Jiminez, and if an opportunity is given to the people they will prove it by their ballots — but if none is afforded in that way, then force will be used.

El Tribune publica la parte final de una declaración sobre la situación en Santo Domingo: no hay temor de que los insurreccionistas interfieran en ninguna manera con la propiedad de los extranjeros en la isla; a pesar de las muchas revoluciones que han tenido lugar en Santo Domingo desde que alcanzó su independencia, en ningún caso ha sido tomada vida ni molestada propiedad de ningún extranjero, con la sola excepción de la acción de Heureaux, que abrió las bóvedas del banco francés por una querella personal con uno de sus directores; las mujeres y los niños están seguros en todo tiempo, pues los dominicanos nunca se rebajan a guerrear con los indefensos, ni saquean ni pillan jamás, sino que pelean por lo que creen correcto a campo abierto, como hombres y soldados deben hacer; el señor Jiminez y el general Gómez son amigos personales cálidos — de hecho, ambos residen en la misma casa en La Habana; como Heureaux fue un usurpador, el vicepresidente es igualmente eso, pero el derramamiento de sangre y la insurrección cesarían de inmediato si este último anunciara que una elección legal y pública tendría lugar el 16 de agosto, que es el día de la Independencia del país; el país entero está despertado a la necesidad de un cambio a un gobierno donde la vida sea segura, y reconoce que el anterior vicepresidente y el presente presidente pretendiente es solo uno de los cómplices de Heureaux, y como tal no tiene confianza en él; y hay un solo hombre que en este tiempo representa al pueblo y sus mejores intereses, y ese es Jiminez, y si una oportunidad es dada al pueblo ellos lo probarán por sus votos — pero si ninguna es ofrecida de esa manera, entonces se usará la fuerza.

The dispatch also opens the latest story of the assassination of President Heureaux received here, which agrees with previously published accounts in nearly all particulars: according to advices just at hand, President Heureaux was with the Administrator of Finances in Moca, when a beggar presented himself and requested a gratuity, and the President went to the door of the house in answer to the appeal.

The Tribune prints part of a letter written in defense of General Gomez

August 2, 18992 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints part of a letter written in defense of General Gomez: in it, he strove to obtain independence for the island for thirty years, and his efforts have been little recognized; the sweat on the horse is hardly dry when the rage of those who did not dare to take the field by his side falls upon him; insults will not affect him more than if they were directed against the Czar of Russia by a person with whom he had no connecting links; what, however, is irritating and painful, and has no logical explanation, is the desire to injure him, to despise the Dominican, to insult the stranger who believes he has done much for Cuba — they would insult the old Dominican Republic, which has brilliantly served the Cuban cause more than any Latin people, with money, love and blood; the Dominicans are called savages because their President was killed by the angered hands of vengeance — why then not call other nations savages on account of assassinations committed in them? The Tribune continues its description of the republic's products

August 6, 18996 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune continues its description of the republic's products and climate: honey, molasses, cocoanuts, dyewoods and many other articles are exported, the records showing that about £5,000,000 worth of various products leave the republic every year. As to the climate, a writer says: 'It must be healthful when one may see by the records that the death rate is only eight to the thousand inhabitants. There are no densely populated places, yellow fever is unknown, and intermittent fevers may be avoided, as in Europe, with the smallest precautions. There is scarcely a section of the republic where the trade winds do not blow all day long; the mountain ranges run east and west, and the valleys between them, as well as the mountain sides, receive the trade winds, which blow continually from the east in that latitude. The city of Puerto Plata, on the north coast, and the interior cities of La Vega, Moca and Santiago, in the Royal Plain (Vega Real) of Columbus, are kept healthful and comfortable all the year by this wind, which removes miasmatic emanations. On the south the great sugar-raising plains are between the mountains and the Caribbean Sea. Although Santo Domingo city, the capital, is situated only 18½ degrees from the equator, and Puerto Plata, the northern port, 20 degrees, and the temperature in winter is quite mild, the thermometer does not rise high in midsummer.'

El Tribune continúa su descripción de los productos de la república y el clima: se exportan miel, melaza, cocos, maderas tintóreas y muchos otros artículos, y los registros muestran que cada año salen de la república productos por valor de unas 5.000.000 de libras. En cuanto al clima, un escritor dice: 'Debe de ser saludable cuando se puede ver en los registros que la tasa de mortalidad es de solo ocho por cada mil habitantes. No hay lugares densamente poblados, la fiebre amarilla es desconocida, y las fiebres intermitentes pueden evitarse, como en Europa, con las más pequeñas precauciones. Apenas hay una sección de la república donde los vientos alisios no soplen durante todo el día; las cordilleras corren de este a oeste, y los valles entre ellas, así como las laderas de las montañas, reciben los vientos alisios, que soplan continuamente desde el este en esa latitud. La ciudad de Puerto Plata, en la costa norte, y las ciudades del interior de La Vega, Moca y Santiago, en la Vega Real de Colón, se mantienen saludables y confortables todo el año gracias a este viento, que elimina las emanaciones miasmáticas. En el sur, las grandes llanuras azucareras están entre las montañas y el mar Caribe. Aunque la ciudad de Santo Domingo, la capital, está situada a solo 18½ grados del ecuador, y Puerto Plata, el puerto del norte, a 20 grados, y la temperatura en invierno es bastante suave, el termómetro no sube mucho en pleno verano'.
