Nor is the little republic thus brought into prominence in other respects a fitting target for our scorn: it has had numerous revolutions, but we think it has fared no worse in that respect than many others, certainly not if we take into account the disabilities under which it has labored — a republic of mixed races, by the side of a negro State and isolated from all others and treated with scorn and selfishness by all its betters, is not a likely candidate for the title of Utopia; nevertheless, this one has of late years been making steady progress, peace has prevailed, and industrial and commercial prosperity has been maintained and increased.

The Tribune continues its editorial on the assassination and the standing of the republicEl Tribune continúa su editorial sobre el asesinato y la posición de la república

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

There will doubtless be, as indeed there already is, a flocking to the scene of all manner of discontented and ambitious men eager to profit from whatever disturbance of affairs there may be; some professional revolutionists may try to stir up trouble; it is even rumored that General Gomez, having finished his work in Cuba, will make some effort to secure the Dominican Presidency — a rumor which it is difficult to credit. All such attacks upon the established order in Santo Domingo will be deplorable from every point of view, and will especially meet with the reprobation of Americans; in past years individual citizens of this country have more than once contributed to disorder and to revolt in the lands of tropical America, but they have never done so with the sympathy or countenance of the United States Government, and it is to be hoped none will be so unworthy as to join in such movements now.

Habrá sin duda, como de hecho ya la hay, una afluencia a la escena de toda clase de hombres descontentos y ambiciosos deseosos de aprovechar cualquier perturbación de los asuntos que pueda haber; algunos revolucionarios profesionales pueden tratar de agitar problemas; se rumora incluso que el general Gómez, habiendo terminado su labor en Cuba, hará algún esfuerzo para asegurarse la Presidencia dominicana — un rumor que es difícil de creer. Todos tales ataques sobre el orden establecido en Santo Domingo serán deplorables desde todo punto de vista, y encontrarán especialmente la reprobación de los americanos; en años pasados ciudadanos individuales de este país han contribuido más de una vez al desorden y a la revuelta en las tierras de la América tropical, pero nunca lo han hecho con la simpatía o el consentimiento del Gobierno de los Estados Unidos, y es de esperar que ninguno sea tan indigno como para unirse ahora a tales movimientos.

The Tribune prints the views of Charles W. Wells on the situationEl Tribune publica las opiniones de Charles W. Wells sobre la situación

July 30, 189930 de julio de 1899

Mr. Wells, president of the company, has often visited in Santo Domingo; he returned from there about a month ago, after having spent several weeks with President Heureaux and other State officials, and was to have met the President in August; he is thoroughly familiar with the political, social and industrial condition of the republic, and, having large interests at stake himself, his views on the situation should be of comfort to other foreigners who hold Dominican securities: 'I am not in the least worried about Santo Domingo. To be sure, the republic has lost an able man and a good President by the death of Senor Heureaux, but one man does not make or even hold together a nation. The Tribune prints part of an editorial on the course of the government after the assassination: the assassination of President Heureaux, it is proposed, shall not be made the pretext for a demonstration of naval force by foreign Powers, such as has usually attended the outbreak of the chronic happenings of a revolutionary character in the independent republics, so called, in the West Indies; this action of the Government, without awaiting the usual formulary of a request by the United States representative at the point of disturbance, is regarded as a public notice of an intention on the part of this Government to command order among these turbulent people; and aside from the 'sphere of influence' of the United States on the American Hemisphere, as set forth by President Monroe and repeatedly backed up by his long line of successors in the Presidential office.

Declaración de los revolucionarios aquí. Acusan al difunto presidente de gobernar aterrorizando: un alegato por Jiminez, que vivió aquí

August 2, 18992 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints the closing part of a statement on the situation in Santo Domingo: there is no fear that the insurrectionists will interfere in any way with the property of foreigners on the island; notwithstanding the many revolutions which have taken place in Santo Domingo since it attained its independence, in no instance has either life been taken or property molested of any foreigner, with the single exception of the action of Heureaux, who opened the vaults of the French bank because of a personal quarrel with one of its directors; women and children are safe at all times, as the Dominicans never stoop to war with the helpless, nor do they ever sack or pillage, but fight for what they believe to be right in the open, as men and soldiers should do; Mr. Jiminez and General Gomez are warm personal friends — in fact, both reside in the same house in Havana; as Heureaux was a usurper, the Vice-President is equally so, but bloodshed and insurrection would cease at once if the latter would announce that a legal, public election would take place on August 16, which is the Independence day of the country; the entire country is aroused to the necessity of a change to a government where life is secure, and it recognizes that the former Vice-President and the present pretending President is but one of the accomplices of Heureaux, and as such has no confidence in him; and there is but one man who at this time represents the people and their best interests, and that is Jiminez, and if an opportunity is given to the people they will prove it by their ballots — but if none is afforded in that way, then force will be used.

El Tribune publica la parte final de una declaración sobre la situación en Santo Domingo: no hay temor de que los insurreccionistas interfieran en ninguna manera con la propiedad de los extranjeros en la isla; a pesar de las muchas revoluciones que han tenido lugar en Santo Domingo desde que alcanzó su independencia, en ningún caso ha sido tomada vida ni molestada propiedad de ningún extranjero, con la sola excepción de la acción de Heureaux, que abrió las bóvedas del banco francés por una querella personal con uno de sus directores; las mujeres y los niños están seguros en todo tiempo, pues los dominicanos nunca se rebajan a guerrear con los indefensos, ni saquean ni pillan jamás, sino que pelean por lo que creen correcto a campo abierto, como hombres y soldados deben hacer; el señor Jiminez y el general Gómez son amigos personales cálidos — de hecho, ambos residen en la misma casa en La Habana; como Heureaux fue un usurpador, el vicepresidente es igualmente eso, pero el derramamiento de sangre y la insurrección cesarían de inmediato si este último anunciara que una elección legal y pública tendría lugar el 16 de agosto, que es el día de la Independencia del país; el país entero está despertado a la necesidad de un cambio a un gobierno donde la vida sea segura, y reconoce que el anterior vicepresidente y el presente presidente pretendiente es solo uno de los cómplices de Heureaux, y como tal no tiene confianza en él; y hay un solo hombre que en este tiempo representa al pueblo y sus mejores intereses, y ese es Jiminez, y si una oportunidad es dada al pueblo ellos lo probarán por sus votos — pero si ninguna es ofrecida de esa manera, entonces se usará la fuerza.

As to the climate, a writer says: 'It must be healthful when one may see by the records that the death rate is only eight to the thousand inhabitants. There are no densely populated places, yellow fever is unknown, and intermittent fevers may be avoided, as in Europe, with the smallest precautions. There is scarcely a section of the republic where the trade winds do not blow all day long; the mountain ranges run east and west, and the valleys between them, as well as the mountain sides, receive the trade winds, which blow continually from the east in that latitude. The city of Puerto Plata, on the north coast, and the interior cities of La Vega, Moca and Santiago, in the Royal Plain (Vega Real) of Columbus, are kept healthful and comfortable all the year by this wind, which removes miasmatic emanations. On the south the great sugar-raising plains are between the mountains and the Caribbean Sea. Although Santo Domingo city, the capital, is situated only 18½ degrees from the equator, and Puerto Plata, the northern port, 20 degrees, and the temperature in winter is quite mild, the thermometer does not rise high in midsummer.'

En cuanto al clima, un escritor dice: 'Debe de ser saludable cuando se puede ver en los registros que la tasa de mortalidad es de solo ocho por cada mil habitantes. No hay lugares densamente poblados, la fiebre amarilla es desconocida, y las fiebres intermitentes pueden evitarse, como en Europa, con las más pequeñas precauciones. Apenas hay una sección de la república donde los vientos alisios no soplen durante todo el día; las cordilleras corren de este a oeste, y los valles entre ellas, así como las laderas de las montañas, reciben los vientos alisios, que soplan continuamente desde el este en esa latitud. La ciudad de Puerto Plata, en la costa norte, y las ciudades del interior de La Vega, Moca y Santiago, en la Vega Real de Colón, se mantienen saludables y confortables todo el año gracias a este viento, que elimina las emanaciones miasmáticas. En el sur, las grandes llanuras azucareras están entre las montañas y el mar Caribe. Aunque la ciudad de Santo Domingo, la capital, está situada a solo 18½ grados del ecuador, y Puerto Plata, el puerto del norte, a 20 grados, y la temperatura en invierno es bastante suave, el termómetro no sube mucho en pleno verano'.

Under the heading "Plans of the Revolutionists," an August 11 Kingston, Jamaica, dispatch relays an August 3 mail report from San Domingo via Hayti describing prominent civil and military men taking the field behind Jiminez, who is expected shortly from Cuba with munitions of war, while Generals Ramon Pacheco and Pablo Reyes march westward with armed followers including regular soldiers to cut off Monte Christi from reinforcements and join insurgents from Hayti. The dispatch adds that the revolution owes its popularity to Gomez's name and that if he declines the presidency or Jiminez finally opposes his election, leaders are considering a plebiscite to renew the 1871 vote for American annexation or an American protectorate rather than elect another dictator.

Bajo el título "Plans of the Revolutionists", un parte desde Kingston, Jamaica, del 11 de agosto transmite un informe por correo desde San Domingo del 3 de agosto vía Hayti que describe a hombres prominentes civiles y militares saliendo a campaña tras Jiminez, a quien se espera pronto desde Cuba con pertrechos de guerra, mientras los generales Ramon Pacheco y Pablo Reyes marchan hacia el oeste con seguidores armados que incluyen soldados regulares para aislar a Monte Christi de refuerzos y unirse a los insurgentes de Hayti. El despacho añade que la revolución debe su popularidad al nombre de Gomez y que, si este declina la presidencia o Jiminez se opone finalmente a su elección, los líderes contemplan un plebiscito para renovar el voto de 1871 por la anexión estadounidense o un protectorado estadounidense antes que elegir a otro dictador.

Regarding Pedro Lluberas, former Minister of the Interior and Police under the late President Heureaux, Jiminez states that despite Lluberas's claims of friendship he doubts his loyalty and considers him to have a bad record as a member of the Heureaux clique.
