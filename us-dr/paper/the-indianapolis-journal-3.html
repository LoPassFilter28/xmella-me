The State Department was sending written instructions to Minister Powell through the Navy Department, to go forward on the Baltimore, which sailed from Hampton Roads directly to San Domingo. A manifesto issued by the revolutionists, dated Puerto Plata, October 21, accused President Wos y Gil of arbitrariness and unjust persecutions, said the prisons of the capital were filled and many persons exiled, and charged that revenues had fallen by a million dollars in three months while nothing had been paid to national or foreign creditors since February. Un manifiesto de los revolucionarios, fechado en Puerto Plata el 21 de octubre, acusaba al presidente Wos y Gil de arbitrariedad y persecuciones injustas, afirmaba que las cárceles de la capital estaban llenas y muchas personas exiliadas, y denunciaba que los ingresos habían caído un millón de dólares en tres meses mientras desde febrero no se había pagado nada a los acreedores nacionales ni extranjeros. También denunciaba un impuesto al petróleo de 8 centavos por galón, un préstamo de 120.000 dólares, un anticipo de 40.000 dólares de la Hamburg-American Line y un proyecto para declarar neutrales las aguas dominicanas y libres los puertos en caso de guerra.

The Indianapolis Journal reported that insurgent artillery bombarded Santo Domingo City after notifying foreign diplomats of their intent to take the capital by any means necessary. Los diplomáticos extranjeros se reunieron y declararon que mantendrían relaciones oficiales únicamente con el gobierno legalmente constituido. El asedio se recrudeció con la llegada del general Paul Cabrera y 800 rebeldes pertrechados con artillería, mientras destacamentos navales alemanes protegían la propiedad extranjera y escoltaban a la navegación mercante.

The Journal prints the closing part of General Woss y Gil's San Juan interviewEl Journal publica la parte final de la entrevista del general Woss y Gil en San Juan

November 30, 190330 de noviembre de 1903

The Journal prints the closing part of General Woss y Gil's San Juan interview: he preferred to come to San Juan, and the German officials kindly offered him transportation; regarding the overthrow of his government, he said it was due to the impatience of the people, and that with his respect for the constitution this impatience caused the revolution; if the new government starts constitutionally his people will agree with it, and if not they will try another revolution; he had the chair through peace and does not want it through war, which is demoralizing to the country, and the future state of the Dominican republic devolves upon the recognition of the protocols signed by the Vasquez government with Minister Powell; the general will remain at San Juan two months; and when asked whether he was contemplating urging the United States government to establish a protectorate over Santo Domingo, his manner indicated the truth of the rumor that such a step was the object of Senor Galvan's mission to President Roosevelt, but he declined to speak concerning it.

El Journal publica la parte final de la entrevista del general Woss y Gil en San Juan: prefirió venir a San Juan, y los funcionarios alemanes amablemente le ofrecieron el transporte; en cuanto al derrocamiento de su gobierno, dijo que se debió a la impaciencia del pueblo, y que con su respeto a la constitución esa impaciencia causó la revolución; si el nuevo gobierno comienza constitucionalmente, su pueblo estará de acuerdo con él, y si no, intentarán otra revolución; ocupó la silla por la paz y no la quiere por la guerra, que es desmoralizadora para el país, y el estado futuro de la República Dominicana depende del reconocimiento de los protocolos firmados por el gobierno de Vásquez con el ministro Powell; el general permanecerá en San Juan dos meses; y al preguntársele si contemplaba instar al gobierno de los Estados Unidos a establecer un protectorado sobre Santo Domingo, su actitud indicó la verdad del rumor de que tal paso era el objeto de la misión del señor Galván ante el presidente Roosevelt, pero se negó a hablar sobre ello.

The Indianapolis Journal reported that the State Department withheld diplomatic recognition from Dominican factions despite claims by General Jimenes's followers that they controlled nearly the entire country outside the capital. A cable message was dispatched to Rear Admiral Wise, commanding the training squadron now at Guantanamo, instructing Admiral Wise to take immediate and effective steps for the adequate protection of American life and property in the Dominican Republic, limiting, of course, his action at all times to the recognized tenets of international law; upon the receipt of this dispatch Rear Admiral Wise is expected to proceed at once in his flagship, the Minneapolis, to Dominican waters and to make a personal investigation. The Columbia already has been ordered to return to San Domingo from Guantanamo, where she went for coal, and the Hartford will be ordered back from Guantanamo. The San Isidro plantation is owned by the Bartram brothers, of New York; it is managed by an American of the name of Rass; the plantation is situated on the Ozama river, about eleven miles inland; only steam tugs can navigate the river, and it is expected that Rear Admiral Wise, on his arrival, will dispatch one or more tugs with marines to guard the plantation. Admiral Wise is not instructed regarding the killing of Machinist Johnson pending the receipt of full particulars telegraphed for to-day by Secretary Moody. General Sanches, Dominican minister, has been informed of the firm policy adopted by the Washington government; he cabled his government to-night regarding the feeling in official circles.

El Diario publica la parte final de una nota de Washington sobre el almirante Wise: la parte inicial que sobrevive de la nota nombra al almirante Taylor, jefe de la Oficina de Navegación. War Minister General Raoul Cabrera, commanding the government troops, was killed, the revolutionists advanced on Navarrete, and U.S. warships Detroit and Newport were off Monte Cristi.

El periódico informa de una seria batalla en Esperanza, en el camino de Monte Cristi a Santiago, entre tropas gubernamentales dominicanas y revolucionarios, que resultaron victoriosos con muchos muertos o heridos en ambos bandos. El ministro de Guerra, general Raoul Cabrera, que comandaba las tropas gubernamentales, murió, los revolucionarios avanzaron sobre Navarrete, y los buques estadounidenses Detroit y Newport estaban frente a Monte Cristi.
