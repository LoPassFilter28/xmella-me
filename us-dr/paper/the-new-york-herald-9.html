The Herald prints the prospectus of the Samana Bay CompanyEl Herald publica el prospecto de la Compañía de la Bahía de Samaná

January 9, 18699 de enero de 1869

The passage opens in the middle of the Samaná affair, with word that Báez's own party is determined not to let him consummate anything of the kind. An opportunity is now being presented, the prospectus says, through the confidential agent of the Dominican Republic in New York, to secure a lease of the bay and peninsula of Samana, with all the eminent domain, rights and prerogatives of sovereignty now held and enjoyed by the republic, for a period of twenty years, paying for the same the sum of one hundred thousand dollars in gold annually in advance — it being understood that at the expiration of that period, or on the payment of the whole amount of the lease, to wit, two million dollars, the territory shall be finally transferred in fee and become the absolute property of the company. During the period of the lease the company will enjoy all privileges and immunities of sovereignty under the joint protection of the republics of St. Domingo and the United States; and in furtherance of the scheme the company proposes to raise at once the sum of one hundred thousand dollars in gold, and despatch its agent therewith to St. Domingo to tender the same to the Dominican authorities as the first instalment under the lease, and receive from them a formal ratification of the terms and conditions of the contract entered into with the Dominican agent in New York.

El pasaje se abre en mitad del asunto de Samaná, con la noticia de que el propio partido de Báez está decidido a no dejarle consumar nada semejante. Se presenta ahora una oportunidad, dice el prospecto, por medio del agente confidencial de la República Dominicana en Nueva York, de asegurar un arrendamiento de la bahía y la península de Samaná, con todo el dominio eminente, los derechos y las prerrogativas de soberanía que hoy posee y disfruta la república, por un período de veinte años, pagando por ello la suma de cien mil dólares en oro anuales por adelantado, entendiéndose que al expirar ese período, o al pagarse el importe total del arrendamiento, es decir, dos millones de dólares, el territorio será transferido finalmente en pleno dominio y pasará a ser propiedad absoluta de la compañía. Durante el período del arrendamiento, la compañía gozará de todos los privilegios e inmunidades de la soberanía bajo la protección conjunta de las repúblicas de Santo Domingo y de los Estados Unidos; y para llevar adelante el plan, la compañía se propone recaudar de inmediato la suma de cien mil dólares en oro y despachar a su agente con ella a Santo Domingo, para entregarla a las autoridades dominicanas como primera cuota del arrendamiento y recibir de ellas una ratificación formal de los términos y condiciones del contrato celebrado con el agente dominicano en Nueva York.

February 24, 186924 de febrero de 1869

That failed, the letter continues, and we now hear of annexation. Báez has purposely secured his loan in such a way as to keep up his government for two years, and by the end of that term he trusts to the development of the mining and industrial interests of the country for maintaining it without sacrificing Samana. The idea of annexation failed to alarm the people, the letter says, because it came in the form of a fraternal invitation, highly flattering to their self-respect, and if the project is followed up in that guise there will be no opposition worth mentioning to the measure. The country is quiet, though many arrests were made after the Seibo fiasco, and some twenty persons are held for examination on a charge of conspiracy; it is said that henceforth such attempts will be treated with the utmost rigor of the law, but before this system is put into effect Báez will proclaim a general amnesty, and only those who renew seditious efforts are to suffer the death penalty. The roads are bad beyond description; and before signing the drafts for the first $200,000 instalment of the loan, two engineers, brought from Europe for the purpose, made a scientific reconnaissance of the gold districts, to see whether there was sufficient promise of mineral wealth to secure the payment.

Aquello fracasó, continúa la carta, y ahora oímos hablar de anexión. Báez ha asegurado su empréstito deliberadamente de manera que su gobierno se mantenga dos años, y al final de ese plazo confía en el desarrollo de los intereses mineros e industriales del país para sostenerlo sin sacrificar Samaná. La idea de la anexión no alarmó al pueblo, dice la carta, porque vino en forma de una invitación fraternal, muy halagadora para su amor propio, y si el proyecto se sigue en esa forma no habrá oposición digna de mención a la medida. El país está tranquilo, aunque se hicieron muchos arrestos tras el fiasco de Seibo y unas veinte personas están detenidas para ser examinadas por un cargo de conspiración; se dice que en adelante tales intentos serán tratados con el máximo rigor de la ley, pero antes de que este sistema entre en vigor Báez proclamará una amnistía general, y solo quienes renueven los esfuerzos sediciosos sufrirán la pena de muerte. Las carreteras están malas más allá de toda descripción; y antes de firmar los giros de la primera cuota de $200.000 del empréstito, dos ingenieros, traídos de Europa con ese fin, hicieron un reconocimiento científico de los distritos auríferos, para ver si había suficiente promesa de riqueza mineral que asegurara el pago.

The Dominican Republic will be the great gainer by the step if it is ever taken, the editorial says, and we have no need to pay a high price for a very small article. As for Samaná, it has been in the market for sale under every administration St. Domingo has had since it separated from Hayti, and has not found a purchaser, and it has been before the world for settlement and population ever since Columbus struck the New World, in 1492, and its pestilential shores are as solitary now as they were three hundred and fifty years ago. La República Dominicana será la gran beneficiada por el paso, si alguna vez se da, dice el editorial, y no tenemos necesidad de pagar un precio alto por un artículo muy pequeño. En cuanto a Samaná, ha estado en venta bajo todas las administraciones que Santo Domingo ha tenido desde que se separó de Haití, y no ha encontrado comprador, y ha estado ante el mundo para su población y colonización desde que Colón llegó al Nuevo Mundo en 1492, y sus costas pestilentes están tan desiertas ahora como hace trescientos cincuenta años. Que los expoliadores dominicanos agiten en su país, concluye el editorial: no sacarán nada del Tesoro de los Estados Unidos bajo el nuevo régimen de honestidad que allí impera.

Mr. J. W. The debt, foreign and domestic, including the paper currency in circulation, figures at less than $1,000,000, while the schedule of public property to be transferred to the United States, including forts, arsenals, barracks, custom houses, vessels and munitions of war, is estimated at not less than a sum many times larger. Manifestoes have been received from Santiago de los Caballeros, Seibo and other points declaring for annexation, and clothing President Báez with full power to act. The State geological survey, under Professor William M. Gabb, of Philadelphia, is progressing; rich gold veins have been discovered. The country was never more quiet. Luperón's steamer Telégrafo has succeeded in reaching St. Marc, Hayti, where she is offered for sale, as Luperón has run out of funds.

He had written for certain official statistics to lay before the American people, but not receiving them in season, he came on in person and found them just ready to be forwarded to him. The papers thus prepared are in singular contradiction, the account says, to the estimate of the public debt made by Mr. Angenard: he estimated it at from $12,000,000 to $16,000,000, while it is in fact less than $1,000,000, and even if the French loan is accepted it will still be under that figure, and the republic has ten times that value in the public domain and gold fields, which are not burdened as yet by a single dollar of debt. There is not probably a single state in all America so free from debt and so rich in the means of securing her creditors as the Dominican Republic. Her whole revenue is some hundreds of thousands of dollars per annum, of which $400,000 is derived from her custom house receipts; these would inure to the United States Treasury in the event of annexation, but the Dominicans would have the desired equivalent in peace, protection, the development of her means and a vast enhancement of general prosperity.

Writing from St. Domingo under date of April 20, a Herald correspondentEscribiendo desde Santo Domingo con fecha 20 de abril, un corresponsal del Herald

May 13, 186913 de mayo de 1869

The heavy rains had continued six weeks later than usual this year, destroying half of the coffee crop and obstructing business by making the roads nearly impassable for bringing in products for exportation. There is but one way of travelling in the Dominican republic, he explains: on horseback, taking along a burden animal to carry hammocks, blankets and provisions for the trip; so equipped, with a native guide, three of the party started for a week's excursion, riding out through the suburb of San Carlos, which, according to tradition, occupies the site of the Sacred Palm Groves of the gentle tribes of Ozama, exterminated by the Spanish discoverers. For twelve miles they rode along a level road generally bordered by trees or thickets; for that distance from St. Domingo the Spanish forces made a complete desolation during their late occupation, dwellings, fences and fruit trees destroyed, and the extract ends as the riders near the city walls.

Las fuertes lluvias se habían prolongado este año seis semanas más de lo usual, destruyendo la mitad de la cosecha de café y obstruyendo el comercio al volver los caminos casi intransitables para sacar los productos de exportación. Solo hay una manera de viajar en la República Dominicana, explica: a caballo, llevando un animal de carga para las hamacas, las mantas y las provisiones del viaje; equipados así, con un guía nativo, tres del grupo partieron para una excursión de una semana, saliendo por el arrabal de San Carlos, que, según la tradición, ocupa el sitio de los Sagrados Palmares de las pacíficas tribus del Ozama, exterminadas por los descubridores españoles. Recorrieron doce millas por un camino llano bordeado generalmente de árboles o matorrales; en esa distancia desde Santo Domingo las fuerzas españolas hicieron una desolación completa durante su reciente ocupación, destruyendo viviendas, cercas y árboles frutales, y el extracto termina cuando los jinetes se acercan a las murallas de la ciudad.
