Among the Call's naval notes: the gunboat Presidente of San Domingo

January 25, 189725 de enero de 1897

Among the Call's naval notes: the gunboat Presidente of San Domingo has been refitted with new engines, and thoroughly refitted by Frame, Alston & Co., of London; on her recent trial trip the boat made 13½ knots, she is armed with seven Hotchkiss guns, and she is fitted up to accommodate the President and suite of the Dominican Republic.

Entre las notas navales del Call: la cañonera Presidente, de San Domingo, ha sido dotada de máquinas nuevas y completamente reacondicionada por Frame, Alston & Co., de Londres; en su reciente viaje de prueba la embarcación hizo 13½ nudos, está armada con siete cañones Hotchkiss y está acondicionada para alojar al presidente y a la comitiva de la República Dominicana.

The San Francisco Call reported that former minister Smythe negotiated the outline of a treaty with President Heureaux granting the United States a coaling station in Santo Domingo free of charge. El informe destacó la opinión de expertos navales sobre la imperiosa necesidad estratégica de adquirir estaciones carboneras en las Antillas.

The Call prints word of the arrest of Señor Jiminez at Inagua

June 7, 18987 de junio de 1898

The Call prints word of the arrest of Señor Jiminez at Inagua and an interview with President Heureaux: Señor Jiminez, the leader of the revolutionary expedition which recently landed in Santo Domingo from Hayti, on his arrival at Inagua from Monte Cristi, Santo Domingo, was arrested by the British authorities for a violation of the navigation laws in flying a Clyde line flag, though the Fanita, the vessel in which the expedition sailed, had been sold by the Clyde line company six months before; Chris Roberts, the pilot of the expedition, made a complaint on the above ground, and the American Consul also prosecuted Señor Jiminez, alleging that the expedition was illegal, inasmuch as the Fanita's papers were ostensibly for service in Cuban waters. Desde Puerto Plata, República Dominicana, el 6 de junio, el corresponsal informa de una entrevista con el presidente Heureaux, quien parte hacia Monte Cristi esta noche a bordo de un buque de guerra; declara que el país está tranquilo, y ha confiscado toda la propiedad de Jiminez.

The Call prints the Associated Press dispatch from Cape Haytien

June 10, 189810 de junio de 1898

The Call prints the Associated Press dispatch from Cape Haytien, June 10: President Heureaux, of the Dominican Republic, believes he has discovered proof that the steamer Fanita, formerly of the Clyde line, which was used by Señors Jiminez and Morales in their unsuccessful attempt to overthrow the Dominican Government, left the United States with the countenance of the American Government under a mere pretense of taking munitions of war to Cuba. President Heureaux arrived there to-day on the Dominican gunboat Restauracion, about noon, from Great Inagua, and was interviewed by the correspondent of the Associated Press; General Heureaux said he had been warned that Jiminez was conspiring against him by an official whom Jiminez offered a post, and he showed the correspondent notes he had made of the results of his investigation, and also a bundle of incriminating papers taken from prisoners and from the bodies of the slain at Monte Cristi.

El Call publica el despacho de la Associated Press desde Cabo Haitiano, del 10 de junio: el presidente Heureaux, de la República Dominicana, cree haber descubierto prueba de que el vapor Fanita, antes de la línea Clyde, que fue usado por los señores Jiminez y Morales en su infructuoso intento de derrocar el gobierno dominicano, salió de los Estados Unidos con la connivencia del gobierno americano bajo el mero pretexto de llevar municiones de guerra a Cuba. El presidente Heureaux llegó allí hoy en el cañonero dominicano Restauracion, cerca del mediodía, procedente de Gran Inagua, y fue entrevistado por el corresponsal de la Associated Press; el general Heureaux dijo que había sido advertido de que Jiminez conspiraba contra él por un oficial a quien Jiminez ofreció un puesto, y mostró al corresponsal notas que había hecho de los resultados de su investigación, y también un paquete de papeles incriminatorios tomados de prisioneros y de los cuerpos de los caídos en Monte Cristi.

The Call completes its Washington item on the assassination

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

The Call completes its Washington item on the assassination: Secretary Long said he could not recall that there were any ships of the United States navy now in Santo Domingo waters, and, should the developments of the next few days show a feeling of unrest and uncertainty regarding the future affairs of the republic, a United States man-of-war will be dispatched to that vicinity to look out for the protection of American interests. Under the head 'Ulises Heureaux's Checkered Career,' the Call prints a sketch of the dead president: when Ulises Heureaux was chosen president of San Domingo in 1883, the election taking place during the last three days of June, the defeated candidate, General Casimiro N. de Moya, rebelled in La Vega and Monte Cristi; on July 24 the country was put under martial law, and, after a series of bloody encounters, the rebels were finally defeated in August, the number of casualties on both sides exceeding 1,000 dead and wounded. Heureaux first saw the light of day in Puerto Plata in 1845; a soldier's life attracted him, and in 1862 he became a private, later becoming commandant in the District of Puerto Plata; he served continuously in the war against Spain, was twice exiled for political reasons, retired for two years from political life, and in 1878 again assumed command to put down a Spanish uprising; he was wounded many times.

El Call completa su nota de Washington sobre el asesinato: el secretario Long dijo que no podía recordar que hubiera ninguno de los buques de la marina de los Estados Unidos ahora en aguas de Santo Domingo, y que, si los acontecimientos de los próximos días muestran un sentimiento de inquietud e incertidumbre respecto a los asuntos futuros de la república, un buque de guerra de los Estados Unidos será despachado a esa vecindad para velar por la protección de los intereses americanos. Bajo el título «Ulises Heureaux's Checkered Career», el Call publica un esbozo del presidente muerto: cuando Ulises Heureaux fue elegido presidente de Santo Domingo en 1883, celebrándose la elección durante los últimos tres días de junio, el candidato derrotado, el general Casimiro N. de Moya, se rebeló en La Vega y Monte Cristi; el 24 de julio el país fue puesto bajo ley marcial, y, después de una serie de encuentros sangrientos, los rebeldes fueron finalmente derrotados en agosto, excediendo el número de bajas en ambos lados los 1,000 muertos y heridos. Heureaux vio la luz del día por primera vez en Puerto Plata en 1845; la vida de soldado lo atrajo, y en 1862 se hizo soldado raso, llegando luego a comandante en el Distrito de Puerto Plata; sirvió continuamente en la guerra contra España, fue dos veces exiliado por razones políticas, se retiró por dos años de la vida política, y en 1878 asumió de nuevo el mando para sofocar un levantamiento español; fue herido muchas veces.

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo

July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The Call prints a Washington item on the crisis in Santo Domingo and the foreign interests at stake: the vessels are being sent solely as a precautionary measure; the press dispatches indicate political intrigues which may result in revolution; and, as American interests in the little republic are paramount to those of any other country, it is the part of prudence to have ample force at hand to see that full protection is given to those interests. As to the possibility of annexation as an immediate outcome of the assassination, Secretary Hay and Secretary Long think that it is going too fast to expect such a result and rather discourage this kind of talk; they do not deny, however, that the United States may be forced to serious responsibilities in connection with the future government of San Domingo. While no detailed information has been received regarding the plot which resulted in the assassination of President Heureaux, it is believed to be probable that it was planned and executed by partisans of Jiminez, the revolutionary leader; it is known that Heureaux had lived in fear of assassination for many months, and had frankly announced that he proposed to continue the Presidency as long as he lived. German interests in this republic are considerable, and President Heureaux was strongly backed by them; the future of the republic may depend largely upon the course taken by the foreign element in this crisis, and the course of the German Government in the present crisis will be watched with considerable interest by the American Government.

El Call publica una nota de Washington sobre la crisis en Santo Domingo y los intereses extranjeros en juego: los buques están siendo enviados únicamente como medida precautoria; los despachos de prensa indican intrigas políticas que pueden resultar en revolución; y, como los intereses americanos en la pequeña república son primordiales frente a los de cualquier otro país, es propio de la prudencia tener amplia fuerza a mano para ver que se dé plena protección a esos intereses. En cuanto a la posibilidad de la anexión como resultado inmediato del asesinato, el secretario Hay y el secretario Long piensan que es ir demasiado rápido esperar tal resultado y más bien desalientan esta clase de conversación; ellos no niegan, sin embargo, que los Estados Unidos puedan verse forzados a serias responsabilidades en conexión con el gobierno futuro de Santo Domingo. Aunque no se ha recibido información detallada respecto al complot que resultó en el asesinato del presidente Heureaux, se cree probable que fue planeado y ejecutado por partidarios de Jiminez, el líder revolucionario; se sabe que Heureaux había vivido en temor de un asesinato por muchos meses, y había anunciado francamente que se proponía continuar la Presidencia mientras viviera. Los intereses alemanes en esta república son considerables, y el presidente Heureaux estaba fuertemente respaldado por ellos; el futuro de la república puede depender en gran medida del curso que tome el elemento extranjero en esta crisis, y el curso del Gobierno alemán en la crisis presente será vigilado con considerable interés por el Gobierno americano.

The Call completes a Washington item on the foreign navies and the errand of the men-of-war

July 30, 189930 de julio de 1899

The Call completes a Washington item on the foreign navies and the errand of the men-of-war: there is no foreign man-of-war in these waters to protect its citizens in the event of a revolution, the absence of such vessels being taken as an indication that other countries are satisfied to depend upon the United States to look after foreign interests in case of an uprising; Germany, the correspondent is told, has not a single man-of-war on this station, her North Atlantic squadron having recently been withdrawn to participate in the autumn maneuvers at Kiel; it was stated that a vessel was to be sent to Guatemala to demand the payment of debts due to certain German subjects, but the Navy Department has not been informed of her departure. The only possible fear of any undue interference in affairs in San Domingo has been from Germany: it is known that she has some interests there, and it has been stated that some of the German residents are in sympathy with Jiminez, who is said to be heading a movement antagonistic to the legitimate successor of Heureaux; while, keeping a careful watch that there is no foreign interference, the immediate work of the two men-of-war to be sent to San Domingo will be to protect American citizens, and if necessary marines will be landed to protect the American consulate and American property; the moral effect of the presence of these ships will, the authorities hope, be sufficient to put a damper on any revolutionary movements; and Secretary Long said to-day that he expected the Machias would reach San Domingo city by Monday, her repairs having been expedited so that she will be able to sail from St. Thomas to-morrow evening.

El Call completa una nota de Washington sobre las marinas extranjeras y la misión de los buques de guerra: no hay ningún buque de guerra extranjero en estas aguas para proteger a sus ciudadanos en el evento de una revolución, tomándose la ausencia de tales buques como una indicación de que otros países están satisfechos de depender de los Estados Unidos para cuidar los intereses extranjeros en caso de un levantamiento; Alemania, según se dice al corresponsal, no tiene un solo buque de guerra en esta estación, habiendo sido retirada recientemente su escuadra del Atlántico Norte para participar en las maniobras de otoño en Kiel; se afirmó que un buque iba a ser enviado a Guatemala a demandar el pago de deudas debidas a ciertos súbditos alemanes, pero el Departamento de Marina no ha sido informado de su partida. El único posible temor de cualquier interferencia indebida en los asuntos en San Domingo ha sido de Alemania: se sabe que ella tiene algunos intereses allí, y se ha afirmado que algunos de los residentes alemanes simpatizan con Jiminez, de quien se dice que encabeza un movimiento antagónico al sucesor legítimo de Heureaux; mientras, manteniendo una vigilancia cuidadosa de que no haya interferencia extranjera, la labor inmediata de los dos buques de guerra que serán enviados a San Domingo será proteger a los ciudadanos americanos, y si fuera necesario se desembarcarán infantes de marina para proteger el consulado americano y la propiedad americana; el efecto moral de la presencia de estos buques será, esperan las autoridades, suficiente para poner un freno a cualquier movimiento revolucionario; y el secretario Long dijo hoy que esperaba que el Machias llegaría a la ciudad de San Domingo para el lunes, habiéndose acelerado sus reparaciones para que pueda partir de St. Thomas mañana por la noche.

The Call prints the Port de France dispatch of July 29 on Monte Cristi

July 30, 189930 de julio de 1899

The Call prints the Port de France dispatch of July 29 on Monte Cristi and Moca: tranquility prevails at Monte Cristi, but all the people are anxiously awaiting the important events which it is expected will occur shortly; General Pichardo, the governor of the city, has taken all possible measures to repel any attack which may be made by the followers of Senor Juan Jiminez, one of the candidates for the presidency of the republic; it is said that General Pichardo has advised the Government to issue a decree proclaiming amnesty for all Dominican exiles, with a view to appeasing the people; at La Vega General Don Pedro Bobea has taken steps to suppress outbreaks should any occur; up to the present time perfect order has been maintained, but notwithstanding that fact all law-abiding citizens have been called upon to bear arms and hold themselves in immediate readiness for service; and at Moca, where the people criticize harshly the laxity of the governor, General Juan Gonzales, many versions are in circulation regarding the killing of the President — one has it that the assassin shot him with the muzzle of the weapon held close to Heureaux's body.

El Call publica el despacho de Port de France del 29 de julio sobre Monte Cristi y Moca: prevalece la tranquilidad en Monte Cristi, pero todo el pueblo espera ansiosamente los importantes eventos que se espera ocurrirán en breve; el general Pichardo, gobernador de la ciudad, ha tomado todas las medidas posibles para repeler cualquier ataque que pueda ser hecho por los seguidores del señor Juan Jiminez, uno de los candidatos a la presidencia de la república; se dice que el general Pichardo ha aconsejado al Gobierno emitir un decreto proclamando amnistía para todos los exiliados dominicanos, con miras a apaciguar al pueblo; en La Vega el general don Pedro Bobea ha tomado pasos para suprimir estallidos si alguno ocurriera; hasta el presente momento se ha mantenido un orden perfecto, pero no obstante ese hecho todos los ciudadanos respetuosos de la ley han sido llamados a tomar las armas y mantenerse en disposición inmediata para el servicio; y en Moca, donde el pueblo critica duramente la laxitud del gobernador, el general Juan Gonzales, circulan muchas versiones respecto a la muerte del Presidente — una sostiene que el asesino le disparó con la boca del arma pegada al cuerpo de Heureaux.

The Call prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution

August 4, 18994 de agosto de 1899

The Call prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolution in Santo Domingo — 'has joined the insurgents with several other generals, each commanding fairly important forces' — a Havana interview of August 3 with Juan Isidro Jiminez, and a Washington item of August 3: in conversation with a Herald correspondent to-day, Juan Isidro Jiminez, candidate for the dictatorship of San Domingo, said: 'For the last few years I have been working against President Heureaux. I have worked among foreign powers, I have landed filibustering expeditions, and at last my plans are successful. They were my agents who killed President Heureaux, and I am going to the United States in a day or so to organize another party who will go with me to San Domingo, and if necessary, take forcible possession of the Government. The people do not want Figuereo; they want me and they shall have me.' General Jiminez will be accompanied to the United States by Captain Piedra of the police force, and several prominent Cuban army men who sympathize with him; the correspondent saw cables to-day that General Jiminez sent to his agents in San Domingo, saying that unless the present Government retired before the 15th of this month he would lead a general uprising and take forcible possession of the island; his movement is attracting attention here because of the number of prominent men concerned in it, and Gomez is said to favor Jiminez. The Washington item says administration officials here are not disposed to take the Havana interview with Jiminez seriously.

El Call publica las palabras finales de un despacho sobre la revolución en Santo Domingo —«se ha unido a los insurrectos con varios otros generales, cada uno al mando de fuerzas bastante importantes»—, una entrevista de La Habana del 3 de agosto con Juan Isidro Jiménez y una nota de Washington del 3 de agosto: en conversación hoy con un corresponsal del Herald, Juan Isidro Jiménez, candidato a la dictadura de San Domingo, dijo: «Durante los últimos años he estado trabajando contra el presidente Heureaux. He trabajado entre las potencias extranjeras, he desembarcado expediciones filibusteras, y por fin mis planes han tenido éxito. Fueron mis agentes quienes mataron al presidente Heureaux, y voy a los Estados Unidos dentro de un día o dos para organizar otro grupo que irá conmigo a San Domingo, y si es necesario, tomará posesión del Gobierno por la fuerza. El pueblo no quiere a Figuereo; me quiere a mí y me tendrá». El general Jiménez será acompañado a los Estados Unidos por el capitán Piedra, de la policía, y varios prominentes militares cubanos que simpatizan con él; el corresponsal vio hoy cables que el general Jiménez envió a sus agentes en San Domingo, diciendo que a menos que el Gobierno actual se retire antes del 15 de este mes, él encabezará un levantamiento general y tomará posesión de la isla por la fuerza; su movimiento está atrayendo atención aquí por el número de hombres prominentes involucrados en él, y se dice que Gómez favorece a Jiménez. The Call prints a Havana dispatch of August 5 on the departure of General Jiminez: General Juan Isidro Jiminez, accompanied by six Cubans and four natives of San Domingo, is believed to have left here this afternoon for the Dominican republic; it has not yet been ascertained how the party left, but if, as is thought, they went on one of the Herrera line steamers for the north coast, expecting to obtain a schooner at Nuevitas, they will probably be arrested or prevented from continuing their journey; Jiminez claimed to be ill yesterday, but the many visitors whom he received this morning led to the belief that some move was on foot; one thing is certain, he has gone, taking his baggage with him, as did also his companions; and the authorities are making a close investigation in order to discover in which direction the party went. The San Francisco Call and the New York Herald, by special cable

August 25, 189925 de agosto de 1899

The San Francisco Call and the New York Herald, by special cable from Porto Plata dated August 24, report that the Jiminez revolution is expanding even though the uprising in Monte Christi was suppressed. Revolutionary chiefs Ramon Caceres, Horacio Vasquez, and Jose Brache—alleged to have taken part in the killing of President Heureaux—have captured San Francisco de Macoris, Lavega, and a third city under the war cry 'Vive Jiminez! Down with Paper Currency!' Rebels five miles outside Santiago plan to intercept Governor Pedro Pepin on his return from Monte Christi, while a strong government column from Cotui is advancing against the insurgents and the acting president has telegraphed that Americans in Havana arrested Jiminez. According to a Washington dispatch in The San Francisco Call

According to a Washington dispatch in The San Francisco Call, United States officials expressed satisfaction that American Minister Powell's request for naval protection in the Dominican Republic had been approved following the reported fall of Santo Domingo. Residents of Montedabo, Monte Cristi, La Ligna, and Loca joined

October 27, 190327 de octubre de 1903

Residents of Montedabo, Monte Cristi, La Ligna, and Loca joined the northern Dominican revolution, The San Francisco Call reports, attacking Santiago and cutting telegraph lines to La Vega. General Morales was designated president of the provisional government at Puerto Plata as an interim arrangement pending the arrival of General Jiminez, who is expected to assume the presidency if the uprising succeeds. Triggered Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots following widespread customhouse frauds and ministerial malfeasance in war and post offices, the revolt prompted the arrest of President Wos y Gil's supporters in Puerto Plata, with public sentiment considering his government displaced.

The San Francisco Call informa que habitantes de Montedabo, Monte Cristi, La Ligna y Loca se unieron a la revolución del norte dominicano, atacando Santiago y cortando las líneas telegráficas hacia La Vega. El general Morales fue designado presidente del gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata como medida transitoria a la espera de la llegada del general Jiminez, quien asumirá la presidencia si el alzamiento triunfa. Desatada el sábado por la tarde mediante tres cañonazos tras reiterados fraudes aduaneros y prevaricaciones ministeriales en guerra y correos, la revuelta condujo al arresto de los partidarios del presidente Wos y Gil en Puerto Plata, considerando la opinión pública que su gobierno está liquidado.
