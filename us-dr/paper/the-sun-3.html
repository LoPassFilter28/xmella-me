May 8, 19038 de mayo de 1903

The article asserts that the Dominican people would be entirely satisfied with the status currently held by the inhabitants of Porto Rico, where American authority would ensure similar prosperity. El artículo afirma que el pueblo dominicano estaría plenamente satisfecho con el estatus que actualmente tienen los habitantes de Puerto Rico, donde la autoridad estadounidense les garantizaría una prosperidad similar. Además, el periódico sostiene que bajo el control de los Estados Unidos alcanzarían un nivel de bienestar material superior al que los habitantes de Puerto Rico o incluso de Cuba podrían aspirar a lograr en la agricultura.

The Sun prints a New York item: the Clyde Line steamer New York was exposed at the capital, Santo Domingo, to the firing between the government and insurgent forces; entering the bay of Samana at 4 o'clock in the morning, she was fired on close to the ship from the insurgent fort; Captain Marmion of the New York said: 'We were entering the west channel about 4 o'clock in the morning, and I was standing on the bridge when I heard a report and a shell whistled over my head; the channel is very narrow and the shore rises into a high bluff; I never knew that bluff to be fortified before, in all my experience with Dominican revolutions, but learned later that the insurgents had two guns up there; it was impossible for them to hit us because they were so far above.'

October 13, 190513 de octubre de 1905

He said that by exalting the rank of its diplomatic establishment in this country his government wished to give expression to the intense sympathy which draws it to the American nation, whose friendship Santo Domingo earnestly desires to cultivate. In closing, Señor Joubert said he had been especially charged to convey the best wishes of the President of his country. President Roosevelt responded in the usual manner, thanking Señor Joubert as the representative of Santo Domingo for the action which had been taken and the earnest wishes expressed: 'I desire for my part to assure you of the great interest felt by this Government and people in the welfare of the Dominican Republic and to declare that it is the wish and intention of this Government to maintain firmly and cordially the bonds of friendship which now unite the two countries. It gives me pleasure, Mr. Minister, to congratulate you on your well deserved promotion.'

Dijo que al exaltar el rango de su establecimiento diplomático en este país su gobierno deseaba dar expresión a la intensa simpatía que lo atrae a la nación estadounidense, cuya amistad Santo Domingo desea encarecidamente cultivar. Al cerrar, el Sr. Joubert dijo que se le había encargado especialmente transmitir los mejores deseos del Presidente de su país. El presidente Roosevelt respondió de la manera acostumbrada, agradeciendo al Sr. Joubert como representante de Santo Domingo por la acción tomada y los deseos encarecidos expresados: «Deseo por mi parte asegurarle el gran interés que sienten este Gobierno y pueblo por el bienestar de la República Dominicana y declarar que es el deseo e intención de este Gobierno mantener firme y cordialmente los lazos de amistad que ahora unen a los dos países. Me da placer, Sr. Ministro, felicitarlo por su bien merecida promoción».
