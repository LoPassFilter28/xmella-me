The Waterbury Evening Democrat reported on the clandestine expedition of the steamer Fanita, which picked up exiled General Villanueva and pilot Chris Roberts at Great Inagua while concealing insurgent troops in its hold. Led by General Juan Isidro Jimenes, the expedition aimed to overthrow President Heureaux following extensive preparations. El Waterbury Evening Democrat informó sobre la expedición clandestina del vapor Fanita, que recogió en Gran Inagua al general exiliado Villanueva y al práctico Chris Roberts mientras ocultaba tropas insurgentes en su bodega. Encabezada por el general Juan Isidro Jimenes, la expedición tenía por objetivo derrocar al presidente Heureaux tras minuciosos preparativos. Aunque los primeros observadores auguraban el éxito, el cónsul dominicano informó que la fuerza insurgente había sido derrotada al desembarcar en Monte Cristi.

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

Details of the murder of President Heureaux are to the effect that he was assassinated while about to leave on horseback for Santiago de las Caballeros: the assassin, Raymond Caceres, surrounded by several accomplices, as he was talking with several friends, fired upon him twice with a revolver — the first bullet struck the left side and penetrated his heart, and the second bullet killed an old man who was standing near the president; the assassin and his friends then fled to escape the shots fired after them by the friends of the president; and the body of the president was taken to the palace of the governor of Moca, and the president's death has caused a panic.

Los detalles del asesinato del presidente Heureaux son al efecto de que fue asesinado mientras estaba a punto de partir a caballo para Santiago de los Caballeros: el asesino, Raymond Caceres, rodeado por varios cómplices, mientras él hablaba con varios amigos, disparó sobre él dos veces con un revólver — la primera bala alcanzó el costado izquierdo y le penetró el corazón, y la segunda bala mató a un anciano que estaba parado cerca del presidente; el asesino y sus amigos huyeron entonces para escapar de los disparos que les hicieron tras ellos los amigos del presidente; y el cuerpo del presidente fue llevado al palacio del gobernador de Moca, y la muerte del presidente ha causado un pánico.

Waterbury Evening Democrat

The Democrat completes its assassination dispatch: the remains of President HeureauxEl Democrat completa su despacho del asesinato: los restos del presidente Heureaux

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

Heureaux was 53 years old; he was born in Puerto Plata, the nearest city to the little town of Moca, where he was assassinated Tuesday; he became a common soldier, and by bravery, brains and dash soon won high command; all the West Indies at one time or another have fought Spain — Santo Domingo did, and Heureaux became a general in that war; he was a fighter, a fearless, reckless one, whom bullets could not stop, and he received several of them in his body, but did not mind them; finally, when warfare palled, he became a politician, and his success in that exciting game was so great that his military record was overshadowed; and in 1882 Heureaux was elected president of the republic.

Heureaux tenía 53 años; nació en Puerto Plata, la ciudad más cercana al pequeño pueblo de Moca, donde fue asesinado el martes; se hizo soldado raso, y por bravura, inteligencia y arrojo pronto ganó alto mando; todas las Indias Occidentales en un momento u otro han luchado contra España — Santo Domingo lo hizo, y Heureaux se hizo general en esa guerra; era un luchador, uno temerario y sin miedo, a quien las balas no podían detener, y recibió varias de ellas en su cuerpo, pero no les hacía caso; finalmente, cuando la guerra cansó, se hizo político, y su éxito en ese juego emocionante fue tan grande que su historial militar fue eclipsado; y en 1882 Heureaux fue elegido presidente de la república.

The Democrat prints the closing part of the interview with General Jiminez, opening with followings of 800 men: Dominican government paper, General Jiminez says, is unsalable; men of all colors in Santo Domingo are joining his cause, but most of his generals are colored; when questioned regarding a dispatch from Cape Haitien published by the Discusion, representing that the Haitian government is unfavorable to his pretensions and is prepared to prevent him from landing on Haitian territory, General Jiminez replied that the Haitian government was in a 'very rotten condition'; he estimates the revenue of the Dominican republic under a good government at not less than 2,500,000, an amount more than enough to meet governmental expenses and the interest on the public debt; when he attains to the presidency, he hopes to go ahead promptly with public works and to carry on other necessary improvements, but he insists that 'the first necessity of Santo Domingo is honest men in her administration'; and as to the possibility that the existing government may prevent his entering the country, he ridicules it, as, to quote his words, 'Santo Domingo is practically mine.'

It is also possible that in return for thus acting as Dominican receiver the United States may acquire the use of a coaling station on Samana. Under an arbitral award of July 14, 1904, the United States was empowered in the event of default of certain prescribed payments on American claims to assume control, through a fiscal agent, of the ports of Puerto Plata, Sanchez, Samana and Monte Cristi. Puerto Plata has already been taken, and the others will be in the near future. The only remaining ports of the republic are Santo Domingo city and Macoris, the receipts from which are pledged in payment of bonds held chiefly by French and Belgian citizens. The revolutions of such frequent occurrence in Santo Domingo are usually due to the efforts of the revolutionists to acquire possession of the ports which will soon be administered by the government, and American custody of these sources of revenue will therefore effectually prevent further revolution.

También es posible que a cambio de actuar así como receptor dominicano los Estados Unidos adquieran el uso de una estación carbonera en Samaná. Bajo un laudo arbitral del 14 de julio de 1904, los Estados Unidos quedaron facultados, en caso de incumplimiento de ciertos pagos prescritos sobre reclamaciones estadounidenses, para asumir el control, por medio de un agente fiscal, de los puertos de Puerto Plata, Sánchez, Samaná y Monte Cristi. Puerto Plata ya ha sido tomado, y los demás lo serán en un futuro cercano. Los únicos puertos restantes de la república son la ciudad de Santo Domingo y Macorís, cuyos ingresos están dados en garantía del pago de bonos poseídos principalmente por ciudadanos franceses y belgas. Las revoluciones de tan frecuente ocurrencia en Santo Domingo se deben usualmente a los esfuerzos de los revolucionarios por adquirir la posesión de los puertos que pronto serán administrados por el gobierno, y la custodia estadounidense de esas fuentes de ingresos impedirá por tanto efectivamente futuras revoluciones.
