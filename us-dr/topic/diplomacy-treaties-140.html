Facio said after a one-hour OAS meeting that war between Haiti and the Dominican Republic was distinctly possible, and that the council would remain in permanent session. Following his meeting with the military, Bosch ordered new tank and infantry units to the frontier and an anti-guerrilla battalion from Constanza to take up positions at Mao, a small village near the north coast a few miles from the Haitian town of Cap Haitien, and Dominican paratroop units were ordered to reinforce infantry troops in the Barahona area facing the road route to Port-au-Prince. Bosch told UPI, “We are moving additional forces to have complete control of the frontier.” A spokesman said the Dominican armed forces were in attack position the length of the border, a U.S. Navy task force at sea within 30 miles of Port-au-Prince stood ready to move at ten minutes' notice to evacuate some 1,000 American residents in the Haitian capital if fighting broke out, and the British sent a Royal Navy frigate to international waters just off Haiti to evacuate British nationals if that became necessary.

Tras su reunión con los militares, Bosch ordenó el envío de nuevas unidades de tanques e infantería a la frontera y de un batallón antiguerrillero de Constanza para tomar posiciones en Mao, una pequeña aldea cercana a la costa norte a pocas millas de la población haitiana de Cabo Haitiano, y unidades dominicanas de paracaidistas recibieron orden de reforzar a las tropas de infantería en la zona de Barahona, frente a la ruta terrestre hacia Puerto Príncipe.
