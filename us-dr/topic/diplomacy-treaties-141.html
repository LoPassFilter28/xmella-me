The Dominican defense minister, Maj. Gen. Victor Viñas Román, who apparently commanded the coup against Bosch, is regarded as largely owing his position in life to the Trujillos. U.S. officials were disappointed with Bosch, the country's first freely elected president in 31 years; they regarded him as well-intentioned and liberal, but ineffective and “drifting.” But these officials regarded the military coup as a much worse blow to U.S. policy to foster democracy in Latin America.
