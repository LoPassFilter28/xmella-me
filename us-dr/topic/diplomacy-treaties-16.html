The Evening Telegraph

A letter printed in the Telegraph argues the case of the GalvanicUna carta publicada en el Telegraph defiende el caso del Galvanic

April 7, 18697 de abril de 1869

An English possession with an English light-house upon it lay considerably north of the place of capture, the letter says, and far beyond Cuban waters; her papers were all in order, regularly made out for Puerto Plata, in the Dominican Republic; no arms were found on board, and if her passengers were Cubans, none had up to the time of the capture of the vessel engaged in acts of hostility against the Spanish government — and even if they had, the captain of the Galvanic had an unquestionable right to take them on board of his vessel, and carry them from Nassau to Puerto Plata. The trial of the captain and crew of the vessel the British representative justly deems of no weight, and farcical: the men were shown no justice or fairness, were denied all kind of assistance and defense, and were not even allowed to communicate in their emergencies with their Consul, and have the benefit of his presence during their trial. In view of the facts of the case, the demands of the British representative are therefore, the letter says, for rights and justice and not favors and concessions: they require that the Galvanic, a British vessel taken in British waters, be returned to her owners, and her captain, crew, and passengers set at liberty, and that damages be paid by the Spanish government. With such wide difference and discrepancies in the views of the British and Spanish authorities as to this case, the letter asks, may it not be well said to be fraught with perils to the peaceful relations of Great Britain and Spain?

Una posesión inglesa con un faro inglés sobre ella quedaba considerablemente al norte del lugar de la captura, dice la carta, y muy fuera de las aguas cubanas; sus documentos estaban todos en regla, extendidos regularmente para Puerto Plata, en la República Dominicana; no se encontraron armas a bordo, y si sus pasajeros eran cubanos, ninguno había participado hasta el momento de la captura del buque en actos de hostilidad contra el gobierno español, y aun si lo hubieran hecho, el capitán del Galvanic tenía un derecho incuestionable de llevarlos a bordo de su buque y transportarlos desde Nasáu hasta Puerto Plata. El juicio del capitán y la tripulación del buque, el representante británico lo considera con justicia de ningún peso, y una farsa: a los hombres no se les mostró justicia ni equidad, se les negó toda clase de asistencia y defensa, y ni siquiera se les permitió comunicarse en sus emergencias con su cónsul ni contar con el beneficio de su presencia durante el juicio. En vista de los hechos del caso, las demandas del representante británico son, por tanto, dice la carta, de derechos y justicia y no de favores ni concesiones: exigen que el Galvanic, buque británico apresado en aguas británicas, sea devuelto a sus dueños, que su capitán, tripulación y pasajeros sean puestos en libertad, y que el gobierno español pague daños y perjuicios. Con tan amplia diferencia y discrepancias en los criterios de las autoridades británicas y españolas sobre este caso, pregunta la carta, ¿no puede decirse con razón que está cargado de peligros para las relaciones pacíficas entre Gran Bretaña y España?

The newspaper reported that the Dominican Republic's paper currency was overprinted by various chiefs and ultimately repudiated, leaving the masses with a deep aversion to government money. Asimismo, el general Cabral incursionó en territorio dominicano manteniéndose en la franja fronteriza sin conseguir avanzar más ni ser desalojado, con partes contradictorios que reportaban escaramuzas aisladas que no alcanzaban la docena de bajas en ningún bando hasta el 5 de mayo.

July 4, 18694 de julio de 1869

During the course of the entertainment Mr. Fabens, prefacing his remarks with a few appropriate expressions, proposed the health of the President of the Dominican Republic, which was drank standing. After a few moments President Báez rose and thanked Mr. Fabens and the party for their kind sentiments: there was a spirit of more than friendship existing between St. Domingo and the United States, and he hoped this would increase; he felt honored with the entertainment and proposed the health of the President of the United States, which was also drank standing. President Báez was then compelled to leave the table sooner than courtesy would seem to demand, owing to the severe pain he was suffering in the head from a renewed attack of neuralgia. The letter goes on to complain of the uncertainty of the mails between this city and the United States: letters written in March last via Havana had just reached the city upon the party's arrival, and letters sent in advance to announce the proposed departure of the Tybee had not been received; the Spanish steamers run semi-monthly, alternating on their way to Havana between this city and Puerto Plata, and returning on the same schedule to St. Thomas.

Durante el transcurso del agasajo, el señor Fabens, precediendo sus palabras con algunas expresiones apropiadas, propuso un brindis por la salud del presidente de la República Dominicana, que se bebió de pie. Tras unos momentos el presidente Báez se levantó y agradeció al señor Fabens y a la comitiva sus amables sentimientos: existía un espíritu de más que amistad entre Santo Domingo y los Estados Unidos, y esperaba que este aumentara; se sentía honrado con el agasajo y propuso un brindis por la salud del presidente de los Estados Unidos, que también se bebió de pie. El presidente Báez se vio entonces obligado a retirarse de la mesa antes de lo que la cortesía parecería exigir, debido al severo dolor de cabeza que sufría por un renovado ataque de neuralgia. La carta pasa a lamentar la incertidumbre de los correos entre esta ciudad y los Estados Unidos: cartas escritas en marzo pasado vía La Habana acababan de llegar a la ciudad a la llegada de la comitiva, y cartas enviadas por adelantado para anunciar la partida propuesta del Tybee no se habían recibido; los vapores españoles corren dos veces al mes, alternando en su camino a La Habana entre esta ciudad y Puerto Plata, y regresando con el mismo itinerario a Santo Tomás.

The cattle of Dominica are celebrated; its mahogany and sandal wood are the best in the tropics; many American colored men have settled in this republic, and, but for the civil war, none would ever have quitted it. The bay of Samana, the feature continues, is the outlet of the Yuna River, and is fifty miles long and from fifteen to twenty miles wide; the ocean entrance to it is only a mile wide, and is commanded by an old fort, while high mountains enclose both shores. Twenty-five miles up the bay, upon an inlet to the north, is the town of Samana, the inlet very deep and almost land-locked; the bay has been compared both to the Bay of Naples and to the Chesapeake, to the former for its dazzling tropical and mountain scenery, to the latter for its innumerable wild fowl. This bay has been pronounced the key to the Gulf of Mexico; fine beds of coal surround it, and navigators say that the future route to Darien and Mexico will be by the Mona Passage, just outside of Samana. Samana town, the passage adds, is no town at all, a mere semi-detached peninsula with town vestiges upon it, and the site is swampy; Bonaparte planned a city near Samana, to be called Napoleon, and had the site surveyed.
