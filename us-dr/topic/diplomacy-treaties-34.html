The Herald

The Herald's account gives Heureaux's views on Hayti and the United StatesEl relato del Herald recoge las opiniones de Heureaux sobre Haití y los Estados Unidos

June 26, 189826 de junio de 1898

Some day, he declared, all of the greater Antilles would either belong to him or be under the protectorate of the American republic, and there was but one sensible course for Santo Domingo or for Hayti — to cultivate the best political and commercial relations with the United States. On the subject of neutrality General Heureaux was somewhat ambiguous: he was indisposed, he said, to offend either Spain or the United States, the enmity of the former being as disastrous to his government as that of the latter, but he allowed it to be understood that, if it came to choice, he must decide to stand with the Americans. It was when the conversation turned to the late abortive uprising at Monte Cristo that the real opportunity to study the character of the dictator presented itself, and some of the Dominicans and prominent Haytiens who came aboard to pay homage to Heureaux had been hand in glove with the conspirators.

Algún día, declaró, todas las Antillas mayores o le pertenecerían a él o estarían bajo el protectorado de la república americana, y no había sino un camino sensato para Santo Domingo o para Haití: cultivar las mejores relaciones políticas y comerciales con los Estados Unidos. Sobre el tema de la neutralidad, el general Heureaux fue algo ambiguo: no estaba dispuesto, dijo, a ofender ni a España ni a los Estados Unidos, pudiendo la enemistad de la primera ser tan desastrosa para su gobierno como la de los segundos, pero dejó entender que, si llegaba a tener que escoger, debía decidirse a estar con los americanos. Fue cuando la conversación giró hacia el reciente alzamiento abortado en Monte Cristi que se presentó la verdadera oportunidad de estudiar el carácter del dictador, y algunos de los dominicanos y prominentes haitianos que subieron a bordo a rendir homenaje a Heureaux habían estado en íntima connivencia con los conspiradores.

Some years ago Frederick A. Ober was sent to San Domingo for the express purpose of sifting the existing evidence and making a personal examination, and bore letters of recommendation from the then secretary of state, Mr. Blaine, to the president of the Dominican republic, and every opportunity was afforded him for a rigid investigation: the minister of public affairs himself accompanied him to the cathedral, and by his command the revered relics were brought from their place of deposit, in an ancient cedar chest within a dark cell near the presbytery, and exposed to the light of day. Photographs were then taken of the vault, the original place of sepulture being pointed out, the two vaults opened and their relative position shown, and also of the casket at present holding the bones, and of the crystal and satinwood case in which this is contained. In his report to this government, Mr. Ober said in substance that the conclusion reached by the Spanish academy is that the remains of Columbus are in the cathedral of Havana, in the shadow of the glorious banner of Castile, and that it is most fitting that over his sepulcher waves the same flag that sailed with him from Palos.

Heureaux first saw the light of day in Puerto Plata in 1845; a soldier's life attracted him, and in 1862 he became a private, later becoming commandant in the District of Puerto Plata; he served continuously in the war against Spain, was twice exiled for political reasons, retired for two years from political life, and in 1878 again assumed command to put down a Spanish uprising; he was wounded many times.

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

Gen. Ulysses Heureaux was born in 1838, when San Domingo was a Spanish colony, the republic not being established until 1845; he lived to fight for his country's independence, being one of the bravest leaders in the insurrection which ended the brief Spanish occupation during the civil war; he was first elected president in 1882, taking his seat in 1883, and was re-elected in 1884, 1888, 1892 and 1896, his first term being the unexpired term of his predecessor; he was a brave and determined man, and whenever there was a disturbance in his country he always proceeded to the scene in person, as a soldier, not as president of the country; during his lifetime he passed through many battles and escaped several attempts at assassination — no longer ago than May of last year he outwitted a band of revolutionists headed by Gen. Jiminez, and though another attempt was made to overturn the government by the hand of an assassin, he escaped unharmed; he was a man of medium height, a mulatto, and weighed about 170 pounds, and he spoke English fluently.

El general Ulysses Heureaux nació en 1838, cuando Santo Domingo era una colonia española, no habiéndose establecido la república hasta 1845; vivió para luchar por la independencia de su país, siendo uno de los líderes más bravos en la insurrección que terminó la breve ocupación española durante la guerra civil; fue elegido presidente por primera vez en 1882, tomando su asiento en 1883, y fue reelegido en 1884, 1888, 1892 y 1896, siendo su primer período el período no expirado de su predecesor; era un hombre bravo y determinado, y siempre que había un disturbio en su país procedía siempre a la escena en persona, como soldado, no como presidente del país; durante su vida pasó por muchas batallas y escapó de varios intentos de asesinato — no hace más que mayo del año pasado en que burló a una banda de revolucionarios encabezada por el general Jiminez, y aunque se hizo otro intento de derrocar el gobierno por la mano de un asesino, escapó ileso; era un hombre de estatura mediana, un mulato, y pesaba unas 170 libras, y hablaba inglés fluidamente.
