The Seattle Post-Intelligencer

Much Comment on Santo DomingoMuchos comentarios sobre Santo Domingo

August 6, 18996 de agosto de 1899

General Jiminez to-day displayed a cipher cablegram which he received from an agent in Santo Domingo, reading: 'Revolution commences to-day in Dajabon and La Vega. Send definite answer when you arrive. Must be as early as possible. — Franco.' Jiminez has replied: 'Be there by last of month with party. Delay so as to avoid bloodshed. Think present government will retire before then.' Jiminez declared that he had stands of arms in Cuba ready to be used, and that a party of Americans in the United States is preparing to sail at once; when asked if he did not think the United States would stop his expedition, he smiled and said: 'Has the United States enough vessels to guard the entire coast of Cuba and the Isle of Pines that can be spared? Never fear, we will have no trouble in sailing, and we will sail soon.' Jiminez is said to be backed by Gomez and other prominent Cuban army officers. The special closes with the report that General Jiminez, accompanied by six Cubans and four natives of Santo Domingo, is believed to have left Havana this afternoon for the Dominican republic.

El general Jiminez exhibió hoy un cablegrama en clave que recibió de un agente en Santo Domingo, que dice: 'La revolución comienza hoy en Dajabón y La Vega. Envíe respuesta definitiva sobre cuándo llega. Debe ser lo antes posible. — Franco'. Jiminez ha respondido: 'Estaré allí a fin de mes con mi grupo. Retrasen para evitar derramamiento de sangre. Creo que el gobierno actual se retirará antes de entonces'. Jiminez declaró que tenía armamentos en Cuba listos para ser usados y que un grupo de americanos en los Estados Unidos se prepara para partir de inmediato; al preguntársele si no creía que los Estados Unidos detendrían su expedición, sonrió y dijo: '¿Tienen los Estados Unidos buques suficientes para guardar toda la costa de Cuba y la Isla de Pinos que puedan prescindir de ellos? No tema, no tendremos problemas para partir, y partiremos pronto'. Se dice que Jiminez cuenta con el respaldo de Gómez y otros prominentes oficiales del ejército cubano. El especial cierra con el reporte de que se cree que el general Jiminez, acompañado de seis cubanos y cuatro naturales de Santo Domingo, partió esta tarde de La Habana con destino a la República Dominicana.

New York Journal and Advertiser

Revolt Spreads in San Domingo.La revuelta se extiende en San Domingo.

August 8, 18998 de agosto de 1899

The Puerto Plata dispatch says Minister of Finance Señor Alvarez and Senator Cordero, with 100 men, went to Monte Cristi yesterday, where they united with 700 men who had been assembled in behalf of the government for the restoration of order; American steamships are also watching the coast to prevent the landing of an insurrectionary expedition, should any such attempt be made; it is claimed the governor of Monte Cristi has 3,000 men under arms for the preservation of the peace, but the situation is considered very grave; and the United States gunboat Machias has left the port of Santo Domingo for Punta Macoris, but the cruiser New Orleans still remains here.

The Washington Weekly Post

The Weekly Post prints a Puerto Plata dispatch of August 7The Weekly Post publica un despacho de Puerto Plata del 7 de agosto

August 8, 18998 de agosto de 1899

The capital dispatch, headed 'Situation at the Capital', says the United States gunboat Machias has left here for Punta Macoris; news from the interior indicates that tranquillity prevails, but business continues depressed and the want of drafts is greatly felt; the government is endeavoring to ameliorate the financial condition; advices that a hurricane is approaching have been received from various parts of the republic of Dominica, and the barometer here is falling rapidly; the Dominican warships have sailed for Caldera, a port of refuge on the south coast; the United States cruiser New Orleans is still here, with steam up and ready to leave at any moment; the American steamer Carib, from New York July 28 for Turks Island, which is now at Samana, is anxiously expected; and as provisions are very scarce and prices high, the poor people are suffering severely, their earnings not being in proportion to the prices now asked for the necessaries of life.

El despacho de la capital, titulado 'Situation at the Capital', dice que el cañonero estadounidense Machias ha salido de aquí hacia Punta Macoris; noticias del interior indican que prevalece la tranquilidad, pero el comercio continúa deprimido y la falta de letras de cambio se siente mucho; el gobierno se esfuerza por mejorar la situación financiera; se han recibido avisos de que se acerca un huracán desde varias partes de la república de Dominica, y el barómetro aquí baja rápidamente; los buques de guerra dominicanos han zarpado hacia Caldera, un puerto de refugio en la costa sur; el crucero estadounidense New Orleans sigue aquí, con vapor levantado y listo para partir en cualquier momento; el vapor americano Carib, que salió de Nueva York el 28 de julio hacia la Isla Turca y que ahora está en Samaná, es esperado con ansiedad; y como las provisiones son muy escasas y los precios altos, la gente pobre sufre gravemente, pues sus ganancias no están en proporción con los precios que ahora se piden por los artículos de primera necesidad.

August 8, 18998 de agosto de 1899

The News prints a dispatch from Santo Domingo on the revolution: the revolutionary Dominicans have crossed the frontier and have taken possession of Dajabon; the garrison at that place retired to Fort Belair, a strategic position commanding the town; the foreign population and the Haytian consul left the place; it is considered that Jiminez, the leader of the revolution, will attempt to make a landing on the island shortly in order to join the revolutionary troops at Dajabon and take direction of the movement; the Haytian government is taking measures to prevent Jiminez from landing in its territory; it is very difficult at present to obtain information regarding the events transpiring in the Dominican Republic; and it is reported that the exiled Dominicans assembled at Ouanaminthe, Hayti, crossed the frontier of Santo Domingo and captured the town of Dajabon.

The Indianapolis Journal

Ties can be paid In American sold or national silverLos derechos pueden pagarse en oro americano o en plata nacional

August 9, 18999 de agosto de 1899

The Indianapolis Journal reported on Dominican fiscal measures requiring a portion of customs duties to be paid in paper currency, which would be publicly burned each month to retire the circulating paper notes. Concurrently, Dominican revolutionary forces crossed from Ouanaminthe, Haiti, and captured the border town of Dajabon, forcing the local garrison to retreat to Fort Belair. The report noted expectations that rebel leader General Jimenes would soon land to take direct command, despite Haitian efforts to prevent his transit.

El Indianapolis Journal informó sobre las medidas fiscales dominicanas que exigían el pago de una porción de los aranceles aduaneros en papel moneda, el cual se quemaría públicamente cada mes para retirar los billetes en circulación. Al mismo tiempo, fuerzas revolucionarias dominicanas cruzaron desde Ouanaminthe, Haití, y tomaron la localidad fronteriza de Dajabón, obligando a la guarnición local a replegarse hacia Fort Belair. El informe señalaba la expectativa de que el líder rebelde general Jimenes desembarcara en breve para asumir el mando directo, pese a los esfuerzos haitianos por impedir su paso.

August 10, 189910 de agosto de 1899

The Tribune prints part of an editorial item on the San Domingo Improvement Company: the American interest at stake in the republic, the item says, is due to a concession which a company incorporated in New Jersey and known as the 'San Domingo Improvement Company of New York' has acquired in the republic; in 1888 the fiscal agents of the Dominican republic entered into convention with an Amsterdam firm for a loan, and to meet the payments on the loan the Holland company was given the right to collect directly the receipts from all custom houses in the republic, and the indebtedness was made a mortgage upon all the revenues of the country; in 1893 the rights of the Amsterdam bankers were transferred to the New York company, and by act of the Santo Domingo Congress the right of the Americans to collect the customs and port dues was reaffirmed; and in the event of a revolution it will require the presence of several American warships to preserve the interest of the American company, and as a civil war would seriously reduce the revenues the administration may feel warranted to extend its protection to the extent of exerting force to maintain peace.

El Tribune publica parte de un artículo editorial sobre la San Domingo Improvement Company: el interés estadounidense en juego en la república, dice el artículo, se debe a una concesión que una compañía incorporada en Nueva Jersey y conocida como la «San Domingo Improvement Company of New York» ha adquirido en la república; en 1888 los agentes fiscales de la república dominicana celebraron un convenio con una firma de Ámsterdam para un préstamo, y para atender los pagos del préstamo se dio a la compañía holandesa el derecho de cobrar directamente los ingresos de todas las aduanas de la república, y la deuda quedó hipotecada sobre todas las rentas del país; en 1893 los derechos de los banqueros de Ámsterdam fueron transferidos a la compañía de Nueva York, y por ley del Congreso de Santo Domingo se reafirmó el derecho de los estadounidenses de cobrar las aduanas y los derechos portuarios; y en caso de una revolución se requerirá la presencia de varios buques de guerra estadounidenses para preservar el interés de la compañía americana, y como una guerra civil reduciría seriamente los ingresos, la administración podría sentirse justificada para extender su protección hasta ejercer la fuerza para mantener la paz.
