The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints the closing words of a dispatch on the revolutionEl Republic publica las palabras finales de un despacho sobre la revolución

October 28, 190328 de octubre de 1903

The Washington item says a full explanation of the stopping of the American mail steamer Cherokee at Porto Plata by a Dominican warship will to-morrow be demanded of the Dominican Republic by the State Department; an American cruiser or gunboat will meantime be ordered to Porto Plata and prevent further arbitrary interference with commerce, either by so-called blockades or otherwise. Up to the time a Republic dispatch stating the Cherokee had been turned back at Porto Plata was shown to a State Department official to-night the department had received no advices regarding this occurrence; the only advices received to-day came from Minister Powell at Santo Domingo City and stated that Porto Plata was in a state of revolution. From this it is inferred the Dominican government has instituted a blockade with the idea of cutting off the revolutionists' source of supplies. According to international law, however, a blockade, to be recognized by other powers, must be preceded by a formal notification of its institution. The tendency of the Central American and West Indian governments, particularly Haiti and Santo Domingo, has been so arbitrary in this regard that no blockade not preceded by formal notification long enough to exempt ships sailing before notice is given will be tolerated by the United States government.

La nota de Washington dice que mañana se exigirá a la República Dominicana, por conducto del Departamento de Estado, una explicación completa de la detención del vapor correo estadounidense Cherokee en Porto Plata por un buque de guerra dominicano; entretanto, un crucero o cañonera estadounidense será enviado a Porto Plata para impedir nuevas interferencias arbitrarias con el comercio, ya sea mediante supuestos bloqueos o de otro modo. Hasta el momento en que esta noche se mostró a un funcionario del Departamento de Estado un despacho del Republic que afirmaba que el Cherokee había sido obligado a retroceder en Porto Plata, el departamento no había recibido informes sobre este suceso; los únicos informes recibidos hoy provinieron del ministro Powell en la ciudad de Santo Domingo y afirmaban que Porto Plata estaba en estado de revolución. De ello se infiere que el gobierno dominicano ha instituido un bloqueo con la idea de cortar la fuente de suministros de los revolucionarios. Según el derecho internacional, sin embargo, un bloqueo, para ser reconocido por otras potencias, debe ir precedido de una notificación formal de su institución. La tendencia de los gobiernos centroamericanos y de las Indias Occidentales, particularmente Haití y Santo Domingo, ha sido tan arbitraria a este respecto que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos no tolerará ningún bloqueo que no haya sido precedido por una notificación formal con antelación suficiente para eximir a los buques que zarparon antes de que se diera el aviso.

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

Telegraphic communication with La Vega, San Domingo, has been restored. It is reported that the French cruiser Jurien de la Graviere, now at Port au Prince, Hayti, has been ordered to San Domingo. The San Domingo dispatch says that, owing to the rebellion in the northern part of the republic, the government has decreed the suspension of the constitutional guarantees and is enforcing martial law; this city and its vicinity is quiet; active measures are being taken to suppress the revolution; a warship with troops has been sent to Puerto Plata; and communication by telegraph over the land lines is partially interrupted. The Washington item says the State Department has received a cablegram from United States Minister Powell, dated at San Domingo to-day: Morales, the governor of Puerto Plata, has pronounced against this government; both the naval vessels, with troops, have been sent there; and it is currently reported that Moca is in insurrection.

The Clyde line steamer Cherokee from New York arrived to-day; she had been prevented by the Dominican government cruiser Independencia from calling at Puerto Plata, Samana, and Macoris; she reports that the whole of the northern part of the island is joined in the revolutionary movement.
