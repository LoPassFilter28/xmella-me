Grand Rapids Herald-Review

The Herald-Review prints the report of Captain James E. Miller

March 5, 19045 de marzo de 1904

The Herald-Review prints the report of Captain James E. Miller, commanding the cruiser Columbia, to the Navy Department, and a San Domingo dispatch of March 2: President Morales of the Dominican republic has been refused permission to blockade his own ports to keep out the revolutionists. 'This morning (Feb. 18) the American three-masted schooner W. R. Perkins came in from the eastward and our boarding boat went alongside. The captain informed me that he had been warned off Macoris and not allowed to enter by the gunboat Presidente, and was told that he must proceed to this port. Immediately upon receipt of this information I communicated with the minister, requesting him to communicate with President Morales and inform him that I would not recognize his right to blockade Macoris and refuse entrance to vessels flying the United States flag. The minister informs me that the president demurred at first, but finally agreed to the fact.' The report appears under the head 'No Blockade Allowed'. The dispatch, under the head 'Rebels Seize American Tug', reports that United States Minister Powell has been informed that the insurgents at San Pedro de Macoris have seized the tugboat Burro, belonging to the Clyde line of New York, and armed her; the Clyde line steamer Cherokee has gone to Azua de Compostela, convoyed by the United States training ship Hartford; many arrests have been made there, and several politicians were sent out of the country yesterday on board the steamer Julia of the Cuba line.

El Herald-Review publica el informe del capitán James E. Miller, comandante del crucero Columbia, al Departamento de Marina, y un despacho de Santo Domingo del 2 de marzo: al presidente Morales, de la República Dominicana, se le ha negado el permiso para bloquear sus propios puertos a fin de mantener fuera a los revolucionarios. «Esta mañana (18 de feb.) la goleta estadounidense de tres palos W. R. Perkins entró procedente del este y nuestra lancha de abordaje se puso a su costado. El capitán me informó que la cañonera Presidente le había cerrado el paso a Macorís y no le había permitido entrar, y que se le había dicho que debía dirigirse a este puerto. Inmediatamente después de recibir esta información me comuniqué con el ministro, pidiéndole que se comunicara con el presidente Morales y le informara que yo no reconocería su derecho a bloquear a Macorís y negar la entrada a los buques que enarbolan la bandera de los Estados Unidos. El ministro me informa que el presidente puso objeciones al principio, pero que finalmente aceptó el hecho». El informe aparece bajo el título «No Blockade Allowed». El despacho, bajo el título «Rebels Seize American Tug», informa que se ha comunicado al ministro estadounidense Powell que los insurgentes de San Pedro de Macorís se apoderaron del remolcador Burro, propiedad de la Clyde line de Nueva York, y lo armaron; el vapor Cherokee, de la Clyde line, ha salido hacia Azua de Compostela, escoltado por el buque escuela estadounidense Hartford; allí se han practicado muchas detenciones, y varios políticos fueron sacados ayer del país a bordo del vapor Julia, de la Cuba line.

Okolona Messenger

The Messenger prints an editorial on Santo Domingo: the United States

March 9, 19049 de marzo de 1904

The Messenger prints an editorial on Santo Domingo: the United States has many grievances, it says, and the result of the interference will doubtless look to establishing a stable and peaceful government, which will respect the rights of other nations. United States Minister Powell's reports to the State Department for the last six months have been a series of accounts; at Samana, last month, a band of insurgents attacked the United States consulate and removed two refugees who had sought shelter there; at Santo Domingo, the insurgents fired deliberately upon American seamen and killed Machinist Johnson; telephone and telegraph lines belonging to Americans have been cut in various parts of the island, and Americans who have ranches in the Dominican Republic have been persecuted by insurgents, who have killed cattle and offered every possible insult to American subjects.

El Messenger publica un editorial sobre Santo Domingo: los Estados Unidos tienen muchos agravios, dice, y el resultado de la intervención sin duda buscará establecer un gobierno estable y pacífico, que respete los derechos de las demás naciones. Los informes del ministro estadounidense Powell al Departamento de Estado durante los últimos seis meses han sido una serie de relatos; en Samaná, el mes pasado, una banda de insurgentes atacó el consulado de los Estados Unidos y se llevó a dos refugiados que habían buscado albergue allí; en Santo Domingo, los insurgentes dispararon deliberadamente contra marineros estadounidenses y mataron al maquinista Johnson; líneas telefónicas y telegráficas propiedad de estadounidenses han sido cortadas en varias partes de la isla, y los estadounidenses que tienen ranchos en la República Dominicana han sido perseguidos por los insurgentes, que han matado ganado y ofrecido todo insulto posible a súbditos estadounidenses.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints the closing part of an item on a naval visit

March 14, 190414 de marzo de 1904

The Call prints the closing part of an item on a naval visit, and a San Domingo dispatch of March 13: the item reports that the failure to visit caused considerable surprise at the time; there was fighting in progress in the vicinity on the day the party was there, and for that reason the admiral did not deem it advisable to make an official visit; during the forenoon there were several casualties in San Domingo City from the fighting then in progress, the shots crossing the river through which the boats must pass, and more fighting was expected during the afternoon; the admiral, however, sent his respects to President Morales by United States Minister Powell. The dispatch reports: 'After severe fighting, which lasted two days, San Pedro de Macoris has been recaptured by Government forces. President Morales returned here last night from San Pedro, leaving Luis Tejura to act as Governor of the town.'

El Call publica la parte final de una nota sobre una visita naval y un despacho de Santo Domingo del 13 de marzo: la nota informa que el hecho de que la visita no se realizara causó considerable sorpresa en su momento; había combates en curso en los alrededores el día en que la comitiva estuvo allí, y por esa razón el almirante no consideró aconsejable hacer una visita oficial; durante la mañana hubo varias bajas en la ciudad de Santo Domingo por los combates entonces en curso, con los disparos cruzando el río por el que deben pasar las embarcaciones, y se esperaban más combates durante la tarde; el almirante, sin embargo, envió sus saludos al presidente Morales por medio del ministro estadounidense Powell. El despacho informa: «Tras severos combates, que duraron dos días, San Pedro de Macorís ha sido recuperado por las fuerzas del Gobierno. El presidente Morales regresó aquí anoche desde San Pedro, dejando a Luis Tejura como gobernador en funciones de la población».

Essex County Herald

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinion

March 25, 190425 de marzo de 1904

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinion, and the Atlanta Journal, the Buffalo Express, the Macon Telegraph, the Philadelphia Ledger, the New Orleans Times-Democrat and the New York Globe all denounce the annexation idea. The Philadelphia Ledger says that a 'military occupation of Santo Domingo, purely as an exercise of international police power, is the measure suggested by present conditions.' The Philadelphia Record thinks that if we attempt to police the island it will gradually become a ward of our government: 'A government cannot play the role of a big policeman within the limits of another state without becoming the protector of the latter. The sovereignty of a region resides where the power to preserve order resides, and when one takes over the sovereignty the territory usually goes with it.' Russia understood this fully, the Record says, when she undertook to establish settled conditions in and suppress the banditti of Manchuria. A military administrator put 'temporarily' in charge of the administration of Santo Domingo would be likely to undergo a slow and gradual transformation into a subordinate of the bureau of insular affairs in the war department. The Brooklyn Eagle thinks that there is little hope for any of the West Indies 'except as wards of this nation', and the St. Louis Globe-Democrat, which has advocated annexation of Santo Domingo for some time, says that nothing short of American supervision, which will amount to a virtual protectorate, will end the misrule of the island's ambitious politicians and their followers.

El Herald publica el final de un examen de la opinión editorial, y el Atlanta Journal, el Buffalo Express, el Macon Telegraph, el Philadelphia Ledger, el New Orleans Times-Democrat y el New York Globe denuncian todos la idea de la anexión. El Philadelphia Ledger dice que una «ocupación militar de Santo Domingo, puramente como ejercicio de poder policial internacional, es la medida que sugieren las condiciones presentes». El Philadelphia Record cree que si intentamos vigilar la isla, esta se convertirá gradualmente en pupila de nuestro gobierno: «Un gobierno no puede desempeñar el papel de gran policía dentro de los límites de otro Estado sin convertirse en protector de este último. La soberanía de una región reside donde reside el poder de preservar el orden, y cuando uno asume la soberanía, el territorio suele ir con ella». Rusia lo entendió plenamente, dice el Record, cuando emprendió establecer condiciones de orden y suprimir a los bandidos de Manchuria. Un administrador militar puesto «temporalmente» a cargo de la administración de Santo Domingo probablemente sufriría una transformación lenta y gradual hasta convertirse en subordinado de la oficina de asuntos insulares del Departamento de Guerra. El Brooklyn Eagle cree que existe poca esperanza para cualquiera de las Antillas «excepto como pupilas de esta nación», y el St. Louis Globe-Democrat, que ha abogado por la anexión de Santo Domingo desde hace algún tiempo, dice que nada menos que una supervisión estadounidense, que equivaldrá a un protectorado virtual, pondrá fin al desgobierno de los políticos ambiciosos de la isla y sus seguidores.

End therewith he declared the conference At &n end Sure enough

March 27, 190427 de marzo de 1904

The New-York Tribune recounted political maneuvers during President Buenaventura Báez's historical efforts to annex the Dominican Republic to the United States. The account recalled how Báez jailed political opponents to guarantee passage of the treaty until an American politician persuaded him that a small dissenting vote would look less suspicious. It also detailed an earlier attempt to lease Samana Bay surveyed by Captain George B. McClellan, which collapsed when British and French warships arrived to oppose American acquisition.

El New-York Tribune relató las maniobras políticas durante las gestiones históricas del presidente Buenaventura Báez para anexionar la República Dominicana a los Estados Unidos. La crónica recordó cómo Báez encarceló a opositores políticos para asegurar la aprobación del tratado hasta que un político estadounidense lo convenció de que una pequeña votación disidente resultaría menos sospechosa. Detalló asimismo una tentativa anterior para arrendar la bahía de Samaná, inspeccionada por el capitán George B. McClellan, la cual fracasó al llegar buques de guerra británicos y franceses para oponerse a la adquisición estadounidense.

The Montgomery Advertiser

The Advertiser prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of April 3: a dispatch

April 4, 19044 de abril de 1904

The Advertiser prints a Cape Haytien dispatch of April 3: a dispatch received here from Monte Cristi, San Domingo, reports that General Epifanio Rodrigues and Ciena Navarro attacked that place, and that General Jiminez, being without ammunition, abandoned the town and embarked on the German steamer for St. Thomas; this news is confirmed by the Dominican consul here. New-York Tribune

The Tribune prints a New York item: General Sanchez sailed yesterday

April 8, 19048 de abril de 1904

The Tribune prints a New York item: General Sanchez sailed yesterday for San Domingo on the Clyde liner New York, on a special mission for his government; General Sanchez said that his mission to Washington had been entirely successful, the principal object of his trip north being to seek the formal recognition of President Morales by the United States, which was accorded. The Dominican diplomat was especially pleased over a tentative promise which, he said, he had obtained from Secretary Hay to assign a minister resident to San Domingo in the near future; at present Minister Powell, Minister to Hayti, with headquarters at Port-au-Prince, is also chargé d'affaires to the Dominican Republic. 'The sending of a minister to my country will develop closer relations with the United States,' said General Sanchez. 'The report that I came here to ask for the establishment of a protectorate over San Domingo was entirely without foundation. Mr. Loomis, on his recent tour of the republic, must have observed that President Morales is thoroughly capable of handling the situation.' In the hold of the steamer were 150,000 cartridges and rifles consigned to the government.

El Tribune publica una nota de Nueva York: el general Sánchez zarpó ayer hacia Santo Domingo en el vapor New York, de la Clyde line, en misión especial de su gobierno; el general Sánchez dijo que su misión a Washington había sido enteramente exitosa, siendo el objeto principal de su viaje al norte buscar el reconocimiento formal del presidente Morales por los Estados Unidos, que fue concedido. El diplomático dominicano estaba especialmente complacido por una promesa tentativa que, dijo, había obtenido del secretario Hay de asignar un ministro residente a Santo Domingo en un futuro cercano; en la actualidad el ministro Powell, ministro en Haití, con sede en Puerto Príncipe, es también encargado de negocios ante la República Dominicana. «El envío de un ministro a mi país desarrollará relaciones más estrechas con los Estados Unidos», dijo el general Sánchez. «El informe de que vine aquí a pedir el establecimiento de un protectorado sobre Santo Domingo carecía enteramente de fundamento. The Times prints an item reporting resistance in the interior of Santo Domingo

The Times prints an item reporting resistance in the interior of Santo Domingo to the control of the Dominican president: so declares Gen. Juan Isidro Jiminez, deposed president of the little republic, who arrived in New York yesterday on the Red D steamer Philadelphia. General Jiminez sailed from Monte Cristi last week, after the city had surrendered to the government forces. He alleges he is in the United States to rest and is not here for the purpose of buying arms and ammunition to be used in resisting the authority of President Morales. Statements made by General Jiminez in New York yesterday do not agree with reports made to the State Department concerning the conditions in Santo Domingo: it has been reported officially and unofficially many times that President Morales has the situation well under control and is far the strongest man in the republic, a leader who has the support of practically all factions.

The Union Times

The Union Times prints an editorial on the state of affairs

April 15, 190415 de abril de 1904

The Union Times prints an editorial on the state of affairs in Santo Domingo: Samana has been invaded by an armed force which dragged refugees from its protection; two American merchantmen, the Cherokee and the New York, have been repeatedly fired upon; the American training ship Yankee has been fired upon, a machinist aboard her being killed; the launch of the American man-of-war Newark has been fired upon, and the Newark has been forced to shell the woods lying across the river from the city of Santo Domingo. Such a state of affairs is simply intolerable. The great danger in which it places the peace of the world is illustrated by an incident that occurred a few weeks ago: supporters of President Morales boarded a German merchantman, then in the harbor of San Pedro de Macoris, and, seizing three refugees, carried them off as prisoners to a Dominican gunboat. Germany is occupied just at this time, but other such affronts, if they are permitted, may bring down upon the island the just wrath of Europe, and force the United States to assert the Monroe doctrine against some great European power. It is a mistake to suppose that Dominican revolutions are slight and bloodless affairs; as a rule, the editorial says, the slaughter is great.

El Union Times publica un editorial sobre el estado de cosas en Santo Domingo: Samaná ha sido invadida por una fuerza armada que arrastró a refugiados fuera de su protección; dos buques mercantes estadounidenses, el Cherokee y el New York, han recibido disparos repetidamente; se ha disparado contra el buque escuela estadounidense Yankee, resultando muerto un maquinista que iba a bordo; se ha disparado contra la lancha del buque de guerra estadounidense Newark, y el Newark se ha visto obligado a cañonear los bosques situados al otro lado del río desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo. Tal estado de cosas es sencillamente intolerable. El gran peligro en que pone la paz del mundo lo ilustra un incidente ocurrido hace unas semanas: partidarios del presidente Morales abordaron un buque mercante alemán, entonces en el puerto de San Pedro de Macorís, y, apoderándose de tres refugiados, se los llevaron prisioneros a una cañonera dominicana. Alemania está ocupada en estos momentos, pero otros ultrajes semejantes, si se permiten, pueden hacer caer sobre la isla la justa ira de Europa y obligar a los Estados Unidos a hacer valer la doctrina Monroe contra alguna gran potencia europea. Es un error suponer que las revoluciones dominicanas son asuntos leves e incruentos; por regla general, dice el editorial, la matanza es grande.

The Press prints a dispatch: United States Minister Powell

May 4, 19044 de mayo de 1904

The Press prints a dispatch: United States Minister Powell, who is to return to Port Au Prince, took leave of the provisional President Morales today. The minister, addressing the president, said the government and people of the United States had a profound interest in the future of San Domingo; neither the government nor the people of the United States had the least idea of annexation; they desired that Santo Domingo should remain a sovereign independent state, and in this the republic would have the support of the United States government. Mr. Powell said that his government would not consent to any portion of Dominican territory passing under the control of any foreign power. Mr. Powell leaves tomorrow; his departure is regretted by all classes. The United States cruiser Detroit returned last night from visiting Northern ports. The Dominican war ship Independence has left here with troops for Monte Cristi, which is still in the hands of the revolutionists.
