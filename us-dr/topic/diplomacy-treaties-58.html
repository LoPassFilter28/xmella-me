Vilas County News

The News prints a passage of the President's message on Santo DomingoEl News publica un pasaje del mensaje del Presidente sobre Santo Domingo

February 20, 190520 de febrero de 1905

The president then refers to the fact that loans of various kinds made to the Dominican government have become the nucleus of the various revolutions there, and continues: 'The ordinary resources of diplomacy and international arbitration are absolutely impotent to deal wisely and effectively with the situation in the Dominican republic, which can only be met by organizing its finances on a sound basis and by placing the custom house beyond the temptation of insurgent chieftains. Either we must abandon our duty under our traditional policy toward the Dominican people, who aspire to a republican form of government while they are actually drifting into a condition of permanent anarchy, in which case we must permit some other government to adopt its own measures in order to safeguard its own interests, or else we must ourselves take seasonable and appropriate action.' It is suggested by the president that if a plan could be adopted in regard to San Domingo similar to that in the Platt amendment for the government of Cuba it would be practicable and a solution of the problem. The message concludes as follows, under the head 'Simply Did His Duty': 'We on our part are simply performing in a peaceful manner, not only with the cordial acquiescence, but in accordance with the earnest request of the government concerned.'

El presidente se refiere luego al hecho de que préstamos de varias clases hechos al gobierno dominicano se han convertido en el núcleo de las varias revoluciones allí, y continúa: «Los recursos ordinarios de la diplomacia y el arbitraje internacional son absolutamente impotentes para tratar sabia y eficazmente con la situación en la república dominicana, la cual solo puede ser atendida organizando sus finanzas sobre una base sana y poniendo la aduana fuera de la tentación de los jefes insurgentes. O debemos abandonar nuestro deber bajo nuestra política tradicional hacia el pueblo dominicano, que aspira a una forma republicana de gobierno mientras está derivando en realidad hacia una condición de anarquía permanente, en cuyo caso debemos permitir que algún otro gobierno adopte sus propias medidas para salvaguardar sus propios intereses, o si no debemos tomar nosotros mismos acción oportuna y apropiada». Se sugiere por el presidente que si un plan pudiera adoptarse respecto a Santo Domingo similar al de la enmienda Platt para el gobierno de Cuba sería practicable y una solución al problema. El mensaje concluye como sigue, bajo el título «Simply Did His Duty»: «Nosotros, por nuestra parte, estamos simplemente desempeñando de una manera pacífica, no solo con la aquiescencia cordial, sino de acuerdo con la petición encarecida del gobierno interesado».

March 1, 19051 de marzo de 1905

The Times prints the exchange of notes between Mr. Hay and Minister Leger: Mr. Hay's note declares that the government of the United States of America has no intention of annexing either Haiti or Santo Domingo, and no desire of acquiring possession of them, either by force or by negotiation, and that even if the citizens of either of these republics should solicit incorporation into the American Union, there would be no inclination on the part of the National Government, nor in the sphere of public opinion, to agree to any such proposal: 'Our interests are in harmony with our sentiments in wishing you only continued peace, prosperity, and independence.' Minister Leger, in reply, wrote to Mr. Hay that he was deeply grateful for the sentiments expressed in Mr. Hay's note, and that it would give him great pleasure to convey to his government the views there contained.

El ministro Leger, en respuesta, escribió al Sr. Hay que estaba profundamente agradecido por los sentimientos expresados en la nota del Sr. Hay, y que le daría gran placer transmitir a su gobierno las opiniones allí contenidas.

March 29, 190529 de marzo de 1905

The Evening Times-Republican published President Roosevelt's directive instructing Minister Dawson to implement an interim modus vivendi for collecting Dominican customs revenues while the treaty awaited Senate approval. Under the executive plan, the Secretary of War would nominate port collectors, with unexpended revenues deposited in New York banks pending final legislative action. Según el plan ejecutivo, el secretario de Guerra designaría a los recaudadores de los puertos, y los ingresos no devengados se depositarían en bancos de Nueva York a la espera de la resolución legislativa definitiva.
