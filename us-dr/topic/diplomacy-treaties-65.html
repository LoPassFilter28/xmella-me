The Coalville Times

A consular report on mining and minerals in the Dominican RepublicUn informe consular sobre minería y minerales en la República Dominicana

December 31, 190931 de diciembre de 1909

The report identifies gold, silver, iron ore, copper ore, lignite, salt, and petroleum among the country's mineral resources, noting that gold is the only mineral that has been worked to any extent. According to old records cited from 1496, Indians paid tribute in gold under Columbus, with placer workings located in La Vega, the Cibao, San Cristóbal, and Buenaventura. Local residents continue to wash gold from the sands of the Yaque, Verde, and other rivers using primitive apparatus, reportedly securing two or three ounces per week, though modern prospectors report no quartz veins that would pay to work scientifically.

El informe identifica oro, plata, mineral de hierro, mineral de cobre, lignito, sal y petróleo entre los recursos minerales del país, señalando que el oro es el único que ha sido explotado en alguna medida. De acuerdo con antiguos registros citados correspondientes a 1496 (errata 'pear H90'), los indígenas pagaban tributo en oro en la época de Colón, con yacimientos de placer en La Vega, el Cibao, San Cristóbal y Buenaventura. Los pobladores locales continúan lavando oro en las arenas de los ríos Yaque, Verde y otros mediante aparatos primitivos, obteniendo supuestamente dos o tres onzas por semana, si bien los mineros modernos no han localizado vetas de cuarzo cuya explotación resulte rentable a escala científica.

Although opposition arose because she was not native-born, Miss Azar gained ardent Dominican champions, prompting torchlight processions, seventeen new cigarette brands named in her honor, and the naming of rum after her. March 23, 191023 de marzo de 1910

Objections that she lacked Spanish blood and was not native-born divided the local public and spread to Santo Domingo, where the army and police fractured and the cabinet boycotted President Ramon Caceres for staying neutral.
