The Graphic prints part of a survey of the West Indies in wartime: the passage opens with the closing words of the story of Christophe's citadel — that the secrets of the citadel they had just completed should never be divulged. The Mona Passage, only 60 miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico, which the survey calls a valuable possession and, taken altogether, the most important of all the West Indies after Cuba, its exports estimated at more than a quarter of a million dollars a few years ago and its staples sugar and coffee.

The Standard prints a survey of the West Indies in wartime

April 17, 189817 de abril de 1898

The Standard prints a survey of the West Indies in wartime: the island's two republics, it says, are the only countries in the West Indies whose form of government is like the United States' own, except, of course, the republic of Cuba. Toussaint L'Ouverture's successful struggle against France and his capture by treachery, followed by the complete expulsion of the French, is one of liberty's favorite tales; the Spanish invaded San Domingo in 1861, and their defeat left the islanders free. The Haytians have experimented with kings and discarded them; the treasure of one of these despots, Christophe, remains buried under the rank vegetation among the ruins of a stronghold he built, whose architect and workmen, the story goes, were thrown from the parapets so that the secrets of the citadel they had just completed should never be divulged. There are quite a million people on the island, but they dally on the way to civilization; there is no army or fortification worthy the name, and Hayti's only use to the United States would be in lending it a harbor: there are no harbors on the north coast, but Port au Prince, in the republic of Hayti, is a well-protected port lying behind the island of Gonave; in the Dominican Republic there are the harbors of Santo Domingo and Puerto Plata, which would make convenient bases for operations in the Caribbean. The Mona Passage, only 60 miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico, a valuable possession which Spain risks losing on a turn of the war cards.

The Salt Lake Herald

Among the Herald's war dispatches: a copyrighted cablegram of the World

May 9, 18989 de mayo de 1898

Among the Herald's war dispatches: a copyrighted cablegram of the World from its correspondent George Bronson Rea, dated Puerto Plata, San Domingo, May 8: Dominican officials report heavy cannonading off the north of Monte Cristi, about 50 miles from here, and it is believed there that Rear Admiral Sampson's squadron is engaging the Spanish Cape Verde fleet; the firing began about 9 o'clock this morning and was terrific. Sampson came here, the cablegram continues, with orders to find the fleet and reduce it, and after destroying the Spanish ships will begin a strict blockade and, if necessary, will bombard and capture Porto Rico; and a St. Thomas dispatch of May 8, printed in the same columns, reports that the American fleet under Rear Admiral Sampson has not yet arrived off Porto Rico and no fighting has yet occurred in that quarter.

Entre los despachos de guerra del Herald: un cablegrama del World con derechos reservados, de su corresponsal George Bronson Rea, fechado en Puerto Plata, San Domingo, el 8 de mayo: funcionarios dominicanos informan de un intenso cañoneo frente al norte de Monte Cristi, a unas 50 millas de aquí, y se cree allí que la escuadra del contralmirante Sampson está combatiendo con la flota española de Cabo Verde; el fuego comenzó hacia las 9 de esa mañana y fue terrible. Sampson vino aquí, continúa el cablegrama, con órdenes de encontrar la flota y destruirla, y tras destruir los buques españoles comenzará un bloqueo estricto y, si es necesario, bombardeará y capturará Puerto Rico; y un despacho de Santo Tomás del 8 de mayo, publicado en las mismas columnas, informa que la flota americana al mando del contralmirante Sampson aún no ha llegado frente a Puerto Rico y que no ha ocurrido combate alguno en ese frente.
