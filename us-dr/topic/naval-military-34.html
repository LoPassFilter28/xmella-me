The Washington Times

WOULD CEDE REPUBLIC TO THE UNITED STATES Santo Domingo President Credited With Scheme—The Pevolutionists Protest.Cedería la república a los Estados Unidos El presidente de Santo Domingo acusado de un plan—Los revolucionistas protestan.

November 19, 190319 de noviembre de 1903

Santo Domingo is still holding out against the attacks of the Dominican insurgents, but dispatches from the capital indicate that it cannot withstand the bombarding of the attacking forces much longer; all cities of importance, with the exception of Santo Domingo, are said to be under the control of the insurgents; the United States cruiser Baltimore is now at San Juan coaling, and in its absence from Santo Domingo the Newport went from Puerto Plata to look after American interests. The Clyde liner Cherokee, which forced the blockade at Dominican ports two weeks ago, left New York yesterday for another visit to Santo Domingo; Consul General Galvan, the Dominican representative in New York, refused to issue clearance papers for the vessel to enter any ports excepting Santo Domingo and Azua, but in spite of the refusal the Cherokee will attempt to enter Macoris, Puerto Plata, and other ports at which Santo Domingo is attempting to establish a blockade; the State Department does not recognize this blockade as effective; C. L. Maxwell, the United States consul general at Santo Domingo, is on the Cherokee and will advise the captain concerning his entrance into harbors where the blockade is supposed to be maintained.

Santo Domingo aún resiste los ataques de los insurgentes dominicanos, pero despachos desde la capital indican que no podrá resistir por mucho más tiempo el bombardeo de las fuerzas atacantes; se dice que todas las ciudades de importancia, con la excepción de Santo Domingo, están bajo el control de los insurgentes; el crucero estadounidense Baltimore está ahora en San Juan cargando carbón, y en su ausencia de Santo Domingo el Newport fue desde Puerto Plata a velar por los intereses estadounidenses. El buque Cherokee, de la línea Clyde, que forzó el bloqueo en puertos dominicanos hace dos semanas, salió ayer de Nueva York para otra visita a Santo Domingo; el cónsul general Galván, representante dominicano en Nueva York, se negó a expedir papeles de despacho para que el buque entrara en cualquier puerto excepto Santo Domingo y Azua, pero a pesar de la negativa el Cherokee intentará entrar en Macorís, Puerto Plata y otros puertos en los que Santo Domingo trata de establecer un bloqueo; el Departamento de Estado no reconoce este bloqueo como efectivo; C. L. Maxwell, cónsul general de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo, va en el Cherokee y asesorará al capitán sobre su entrada en puertos donde se supone que se mantiene el bloqueo.

New-York Tribune

The Clyde Line steamer Cherokee sailed from New York for San Domingo ports, with United States Consul General Charles J. Maxwell aboardEl vapor Cherokee, de la Línea Clyde, zarpó de Nueva York hacia puertos de San Domingo, con el Cónsul General de los Estados Unidos Charles J. Maxwell a bordo

November 19, 190319 de noviembre de 1903

Maxwell said he did not think the Dominican gunboats would fire, and that a blockade is effective only when warships are actually and permanently on the spot to prevent vessels entering. According to General Morales's advices, President Gil had offered to cede the republic to the United States, and negotiations looking toward this end were under way between the two governments; Senor Giordani, the revolutionary government's confidential agent in New York, said he would protest against it. Senor Galvan said yesterday that a large shipment of war material was expected to arrive at San Domingo City yesterday from Germany for the government.

El vapor Cherokee de la Clyde Line zarpó de Nueva York hacia puertos de Santo Domingo, con el cónsul general de los Estados Unidos Charles J. Maxwell a bordo para asesorar al capitán Archibald, después de que el gobierno dominicano bloqueó Sánchez, Samaná, Puerto Plata, Macorís y Monte Cristi y amenazó con hundir el barco si entraba en ellos. Maxwell dijo que no creía que las cañoneras dominicanas dispararan, y que un bloqueo solo es efectivo cuando los buques de guerra están real y permanentemente en el lugar para impedir la entrada de barcos. Según los informes del general Morales, el presidente Gil había ofrecido ceder la república a los Estados Unidos, y las negociaciones con ese fin estaban en curso entre los dos gobiernos; el señor Giordani, agente confidencial del gobierno revolucionario en Nueva York, dijo que protestaría contra ello. El señor Galván dijo ayer que se esperaba la llegada ayer a la ciudad de Santo Domingo de un gran cargamento de material de guerra procedente de Alemania para el gobierno.

Se espera un ataque general—El ministro Powell se niega a reconocer a los insurgentes—El New York tiroteado

November 20, 190320 de noviembre de 1903

The Messenger prints the closing part of a San Domingo dispatch: a general attack is expected within the next few days; General Woss y Gil refuses to capitulate and it is believed the fighting will be severe; the situation is desperate and fighting in the streets is likely to occur at any moment; the United States cruiser Baltimore has been compelled to leave to re-coal; United States Minister Powell is endeavoring to protect American interests with the limited means at his disposal; a German naval vessel is ready to land troops at a moment's notice; the insurgents endeavored to have Minister Powell recognize them, but the minister refused to do so; the revolutionists fired on the Clyde Line steamer New York as she was entering the port of Samana, but the vessel was uninjured; the Dominican government has appointed Minister of Foreign Relations Galvan and Judge George Gray of Delaware as commissioners to arbitrate the Santo Domingo Improvement matter, as the result of Minister Powell's effort to compel the carrying out of the terms of the protocol, and Commissioner Galvan will leave for the United States tomorrow, though it is possible the insurgents may endeavor to capture him.

The Tribune prints part of an item on the New York's arrival: she came in fully a day ahead of her best schedule time, because she had so little cargo to pick up from the ports outside of San Domingo which are in the hands of the insurgents, and brought only one passenger and about one-third her usual cargo; Captain Morimon and the other officers of the New York say they do not think that the Cherokee will have any trouble in making the five ports which the Dominican government has declared 'closed,' as they say no attempt at blockading them was in evidence when they left; they made all these ports, discharging cargo and mails and landing passengers unmolested, and the only passenger brought to this city was from Puerto Plata, one of the five 'closed' ports; on the trip down the New York was fired on by the insurgents as she tried to enter Samana Bay at 4 o'clock on the morning of November 11; 'It was just growing light,' said Captain Morimon, 'and we were heading in toward the entrance of the bay, when a shell from one of the forts passed close under our stern; I sounded three whistles, and no more shots were fired; later an apology was made, and the explanation offered that we were mistaken for a government gunboat'; the captain said his ship was much larger than the gunboats, and that it was bright moonlight when the shot was fired; the purser's coat was cut by a shell as he was going to the custom house.

The Star prints part of the profile of General Deschamps, an official of the Dominican republic in the government of President Alejandro Woss y Gil up to a few days ago, when he resigned his position to join his former chief, ex-President Juan Isidro Jiminez: General Deschamps is one of the most popular men in the Dominican republic; his popularity is not, however, due to military prowess, but to the fact that he has always been the people's friend, is a great lover of progress, has always had the tact to espouse the right cause, and above all because he has been the victim of the most terrible persecution; due to his bold opposition to President Heureaux's sanguinary and corrupted policy he was forced to leave Santo Domingo and live abroad, where he had to suffer many hardships; although living in exile he did not slacken in his attacks upon President Heureaux's policy, and about seven years ago, while residing in Ponce, Porto Rico, an assassin paid by President Heureaux to assassinate him drove a dagger into his back, very nearly killing him; the assassin escaped and Deschamps slowly recovered, but he has never succeeded in making the wound heal thoroughly, and it is a constant source of annoyance to him; at another time his wife, who had accompanied him in exile, embarked in a small schooner at the British island of Grand Turk for Puerto Plata, but on her arrival at that port she was not permitted to disembark, and though she was accompanied by her children and the eldest boy took ill on board, still she was not permitted to disembark.

November 25, 190325 de noviembre de 1903

Dispatches received at Cape Haytien from Puerto Plata say that the city of San Domingo was surrendered to the revolutionists this morning and that President Woss y Gil and his ministers took refuge on board a German warship, and that great enthusiasm prevails there. In a cablegram received at Washington from Minister Powell, dated San Domingo, November 23, he announced that the president of San Domingo had agreed to surrender the city to the revolutionists, that the articles of capitulation are being drawn up, and that the surrender will occur today. A San Domingo item of Monday, November 23, says President Woss y Gil requested the ministers of the United States, Belgium, Hayti and Spain to intervene in behalf of the government, and that the insurgents refused to accept the terms offered and demanded the immediate capitulation of the city.

Despachos recibidos en Cabo Haitiano procedentes de Puerto Plata dicen que la ciudad de San Domingo fue entregada a los revolucionarios esta mañana y que el presidente Woss y Gil y sus ministros se refugiaron a bordo de un buque de guerra alemán, y que reina un gran entusiasmo allí. En un cablegrama recibido en Washington del ministro Powell, fechado en San Domingo el 23 de noviembre, anunció que el presidente de San Domingo había aceptado entregar la ciudad a los revolucionarios, que se están redactando los artículos de capitulación y que la entrega ocurrirá hoy.
