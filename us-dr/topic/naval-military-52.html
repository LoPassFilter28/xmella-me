July 19, 191419 de julio de 1914

Mr. Sullivan came on the Clyde liner Seminole and left for Washington, where he will meet officials of the State Department either today or tomorrow and explain some phases of the revolution in Santo Domingo. His activities in influencing the American custom receivership in Santo Domingo have led, it is said, to strong anti-American feeling in the island. The food situation at Puerto Plata has been relieved by the arrival of large quantities of produce and cattle. Desultory fighting still continues about Puerto Plata. Two British schooners have come to Puerto Plata to take off eighty British subjects, residents of Turks Island.

El Sr. Sullivan vino en el vapor Seminole, de la línea Clyde, y partió hacia Washington, donde se reunirá con funcionarios del Departamento de Estado hoy o mañana y explicará algunas fases de la revolución en Santo Domingo. Sus actividades influyendo en la receptoría de aduanas americana en Santo Domingo han conducido, se dice, a un fuerte sentimiento antiamericano en la isla. La situación alimentaria en Puerto Plata ha sido aliviada por la llegada de grandes cantidades de productos y ganado. La lucha esporádica continúa aún alrededor de Puerto Plata. Dos goletas británicas han venido a Puerto Plata para llevarse a ochenta súbditos británicos, residentes de Turks Island.

The fact that Mr. White has behind him two powerful American warships, with their marine contingents, probably had its effect upon the insurgent leader. At any rate, he agreed to an armistice for a few days while the federal officers in Santo Domingo City communicated his demands to President Bordas. The wireless outfits of the American naval vessels were used in transmitting this information to President Bordas. They afford the only means of quick communication between the north and the south at present, owing to the destruction of the telegraph wires in the interior. At Puerto Plata, Capt. Eberle of the cruiser Washington submitted the messages to President Bordas in his camp outside the city. The United States naval transport Hancock is about due at Guantanamo with nearly 300 marines from Vera Cruz. They are to be sent to the Dominican Republic in case of emergency. Originally it was intended no forward movement should be made by these marines until reinforced by 400 additional men from the Philadelphia navy yard, but as these cannot reach there for two weeks it is probable, if a crisis is reached, the Hancock will be dispatched immediately to Puerto Plata or Santo Domingo City. American Minister Sullivan has arrived in Washington and expects to confer with President Wilson and Secretary Bryan.

El hecho de que el Sr. White tiene detrás de sí dos poderosos buques de guerra americanos, con sus contingentes de infantes de marina, probablemente tuvo su efecto sobre el jefe insurgente. En todo caso, aceptó un armisticio por unos días mientras los oficiales federales en la ciudad de Santo Domingo comunicaban sus demandas al presidente Bordas. State department officials declined to say whether foreign representations had advanced to the point where American action in Haiti was demanded to force some control of the island government's finances that would insure liquidation of its obligations. If intervention were ordered in Haiti, it was suggested that the United States would attempt to establish a financial trusteeship over the republic, by which American officials not only would collect taxes, but also would require proper appropriation of funds. If intervention in Haiti becomes necessary, it is possible that the American troops might be used to put down the long revolt that has harassed the Dominican government. Such action, it was suggested, might be accompanied by extension of the authority which the United States now exercises in the collection of Dominican customs. Representations that Haiti's credit was good and that the government had met international obligations have been made to President Wilson by Minister Menos. United States Must Act Soon in Haiti CaseEstados Unidos debe actuar pronto en el caso de Haiti

July 29, 191429 de julio de 1914

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrasted unrest in the Caribbean, noting that while Dominican disturbances were likely to prompt U.S. armed intervention, the country presented fewer military and diplomatic difficulties than neighboring Haiti. European nations maintained smaller financial interests in Santo Domingo, whereas Haitian instability threatened foreign defaults. The Wilson administration sought guarantees that Haitian customs revenues would be managed to satisfy foreign debts in a manner analogous to the Dominican receivership.

The New Hampshire Farmer and Weekly Union contrastó las revueltas en el Caribe, observando que, si bien era probable que las perturbaciones dominicanas provocaran una intervención militar estadounidense, dicho país planteaba menores dificultades bélicas y diplomáticas que el vecino Haití. Las naciones europeas conservaban intereses financieros más reducidos en Santo Domingo, al tiempo que la inestabilidad haitiana ponía en riesgo moratorias con el exterior. La administración Wilson demandaba garantías de que los ingresos de las aduanas de Haití fueran administrados para cancelar deudas foráneas de modo análogo a la receptoría dominicana.

Custer Weekly Chronicle

MORE WATCHFUL WAITING.Más espera vigilante.

August 1, 19141 de agosto de 1914

'Pressed by European powers,' the dispatches say, 'the U. S. has about lost patience with Haiti and San Domingo.' The policy in Santo Domingo of course is 'watchful waiting.' Puerto Plata, one of the chief seaports, is held by the rebels and has been declared under blockade by the Dominican government. Secretary Bryan has recognized the blockade, pronounced the port closed to American vessels and warned commanders of U. S. warships to wait watchfully for the outcome. As a result, American trade has almost ceased and American property interests are in constant danger. Meanwhile, European nations are pursuing a vigorous policy. A short time ago a German merchant vessel steamed into the 'blockaded' port and landed her cargo under protection of the guns of a German cruiser. No one can charge that the Wilson administration is responsible for the revolution, but it is undeniable that some of the deplorable conditions now existing are due to the prostitution of the diplomatic service, under the present regime, to the basest uses of partisan politics.

UU. que esperaran vigilantes el resultado.
