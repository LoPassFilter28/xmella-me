Evening Star

The Evening Star prints: this government, it is officially declared

June 30, 191430 de junio de 1914

The Evening Star prints: this government, it is officially declared, will not actually intervene so long as the lives and property of foreigners are not recklessly endangered by the contending factions. Administration officials indicated today that they thought the warring forces in the island republic had been sufficiently warned when the American gunboat Machias used her four-inch guns to silence the artillery of President Bordas, which was bombarding the rebel town of Puerto Plata. There is sufficient American naval force in Dominican waters to carry out this government's policy. The big battleship South Carolina, with the equivalent of a regiment of soldiers aboard in her personnel, is alongside the gunboat Machias in the harbor at Puerto Plata. The gunboat Castine is at Santo Domingo City and the gunboat Marietta is at Sanchez, on the northeast coast of the island, with other warships in adjacent waters. Unofficial reports to the State and Navy Departments have long been to the effect that the present political situation may be protracted for several weeks or even months, so long as the government is in receipt of its regular stipend from the American customs collections and the insurgents are in possession of ports that are not blockaded, through which they easily receive all the munitions of war and supplies for which they are able to pay.

The Evening Star publica: este gobierno, se declara oficialmente, no intervendrá realmente mientras las vidas y la propiedad de los extranjeros no sean puestas en peligro imprudentemente por las facciones contendientes. Los funcionarios de la administración indicaron hoy que pensaban que las fuerzas en guerra en la república isleña habían sido suficientemente advertidas cuando la cañonera americana Machias usó sus cañones de cuatro pulgadas para silenciar la artillería del presidente Bordas, que estaba bombardeando la ciudad rebelde de Puerto Plata. Hay fuerza naval americana suficiente en aguas dominicanas para llevar a cabo la política de este gobierno. El gran acorazado South Carolina, con el equivalente de un regimiento de soldados a bordo en su personal, está junto a la cañonera Machias en el puerto de Puerto Plata. La cañonera Castine está en la ciudad de Santo Domingo y la cañonera Marietta está en Sanchez, en la costa nordeste de la isla, con otros buques de guerra en aguas adyacentes. Los informes no oficiales a los Departamentos de Estado y de Marina han sido durante mucho tiempo en el sentido de que la situación política actual puede prolongarse por varias semanas o incluso meses, mientras el gobierno esté recibiendo su estipendio regular de las recaudaciones de las aduanas americanas y los insurgentes estén en posesión de puertos que no están bloqueados y por los cuales reciben fácilmente todas las municiones de guerra y suministros por los que son capaces de pagar.

The Oskaloosa Herald

The Oskaloosa Herald prints a dispatch: Washington, June 30.

July 2, 19142 de julio de 1914

The Oskaloosa Herald prints a dispatch: Washington, June 30. 'Very muddled' was the characterization applied to the San Dominican situation by President Wilson. He said that it was difficult to know just what to do in the Dominican republic, because of the many elements entering into the situation. He had received word of the firing by an American gunboat to stop a bombardment by the federal troops, but did not disclose what he expects to do finally. The bombardment of the rebel city of Puerta Plata by President Bordas of Santo Domingo was silenced by fire from the main battery of the American gunboat Machias. Only a few shots were required for the task.

Había recibido aviso del fuego de una cañonera americana para detener un bombardeo de las tropas federales, pero no reveló lo que espera hacer finalmente. El bombardeo de la ciudad rebelde de Puerta Plata por el presidente Bordas de Santo Domingo fue silenciado por el fuego de la batería principal de la cañonera americana Machias. Daily Kennebec Journal

Daily Kennebec Journal prints a dispatch: at the state department

July 3, 19143 de julio de 1914

Daily Kennebec Journal prints a dispatch: at the state department, it is said, the question discussed was how the United States could enter Hayti and manage her finances as the finances of the Dominican Republic are handled. After the departure Tuesday night of the new American minister to Hayti, Mr. Railly-Blanchard, a diplomat of long training and experience, the report was believed that the United States is trying to find a way to have Hayti ask this government to manage her finances. In the Dominican Republic the United States appoints the collector of customs, who takes charge of all revenues, payments on the national debt and maintenance of the customs force. The balance is then turned over to the island government. Despite a series of revolts in Santo Domingo recently, her revenues have not been disturbed, because of the presence of the Americans there. Hayti is now torn by rebellion and her credit is at a low mark.

Daily Kennebec Journal publica un despacho: la cuestión discutida en el Departamento de Estado, se dice, fue cómo los Estados Unidos podrían entrar en Haití y manejar sus finanzas como se manejan las finanzas de la República Dominicana. Después de la partida el martes por la noche del nuevo ministro americano en Haití, el Sr. Railly-Blanchard, un diplomático de larga formación y experiencia, se creyó el informe de que los Estados Unidos están tratando de encontrar una manera de hacer que Haití pida a este gobierno que maneje sus finanzas. En la República Dominicana los Estados Unidos nombran al recaudador de aduanas, que se hace cargo de todos los ingresos, los pagos de la deuda nacional y el mantenimiento de la fuerza aduanera. El saldo se entrega entonces al gobierno de la isla. A pesar de una serie de revueltas en Santo Domingo recientemente, sus ingresos no han sido perturbados, a causa de la presencia de los americanos allí. Haití está ahora desgarrado por la rebelión y su crédito está en un nivel bajo.

Fergus County Democrat

Lately the gunboat used her main battery of four-lueli guns to silence President Bordas' batteries

July 9, 19149 de julio de 1914

The U.S. gunboat had lately used her main battery of four-inch guns to silence President Bordas's batteries, which were bombarding the rebel garrison holding the city in violation of the agreement that the lives of foreigners and other non-combatants would not be endangered by artillery fire. A junta commission of legalistas, representing the revolutionists, boarded the vessel and gave assurances of their desire to avoid endangering the safety of Americans, after scattering rebel rifle fire had passed over the ship and some shots struck her. A cable report said Santo Domingo City was still besieged by rebels and that other towns had joined the revolutionist cause, with four separate revolutions raging in the country. From Washington, the little republic was causing grave anxiety: the American minister was returning home after several months of fruitless efforts to restore peace, and the day's reports allowed no improvement in conditions. Bordas, obliged to maintain himself in office by extra-official decrees and partial elections, had been leading his troops in person against the rebellious generals in the northern part of the republic without being able to overcome them, while revolutions had also broken out at Azua, where the government navy found it necessary to destroy the customs house, along the railway between Sánchez and Samaná Bay, at Montecristi and at La Romana, where the agents of extensive American-owned plantations appeared at the State Department seeking protection for their property. Official opinion in Washington was slowly crystallizing into a conviction that only powerful outside influence, probably that of the United States, could terminate the chaotic conditions and set up a stable government: the revolutionists appeared unable to overcome the Bordas government, supported by the monthly proportion of customs receipts handed over by American collectors, but Bordas had not diminished the number of his enemies after a six months' campaign. Mr. Vick, the American collector of customs, had resigned, and Minister Sullivan was returning to the United States, leaving Secretary White in charge of the American legation. The State Department was still reluctant to intervene forcibly, though it was expected that some strong man would be sent to win the confidence of all elements, and meanwhile the navy maintained a considerable fleet in Dominican waters, including the South Carolina at Puerto Plata, the Marietta at Sánchez and the Castine at Azua.

El cañonero estadounidense había empleado recientemente su batería principal de cañones de cuatro pulgadas para silenciar las baterías del presidente Bordas, que bombardeaban a la guarnición rebelde que ocupaba la ciudad, en violación del acuerdo de no poner en peligro con fuego de artillería las vidas de extranjeros y otros no combatientes. Una comisión de la junta de los legalistas, en representación de los revolucionarios, abordó la nave y dio seguridades de su deseo de no poner en peligro la seguridad de los estadounidenses, después de que fuego disperso de fusilería rebelde pasara sobre el buque y algunos disparos lo alcanzaran. Un despacho por cable decía que la ciudad de Santo Domingo seguía sitiada por los rebeldes y que otras poblaciones se habían unido a la causa revolucionaria, con cuatro revoluciones separadas en el país. Desde Washington, la pequeña república causaba grave preocupación: el ministro estadounidense regresaba a su país tras varios meses de infructuosos esfuerzos por restablecer la paz, y los informes del día no permitían mejora alguna en las condiciones. Bordas, obligado a mantenerse en el cargo mediante decretos extraoficiales y elecciones parciales, llevaba en persona sus tropas contra los generales rebeldes en el norte de la república sin poder vencerlos, mientras también habían estallado revoluciones en Azua, donde la marina gubernamental tuvo que destruir la aduana, a lo largo del ferrocarril entre Sánchez y la bahía de Samaná, en Montecristi y en La Romana, donde los agentes de extensas plantaciones de propiedad estadounidense acudieron al Departamento de Estado en busca de protección para sus bienes. La opinión oficial en Washington cristalizaba lentamente en la convicción de que solo una poderosa influencia exterior, probablemente la de Estados Unidos, podría terminar con las caóticas condiciones y establecer un gobierno estable: los revolucionarios parecían incapaces de vencer al gobierno de Bordas, sostenido por la proporción mensual de los ingresos aduaneros entregada por los colectores estadounidenses, pero Bordas no había reducido el número de sus enemigos tras una campaña de seis meses. El Sr. Vick, colector estadounidense de aduanas, había renunciado, y el ministro Sullivan regresaba a Estados Unidos, dejando al secretario White a cargo de la legación americana. El Departamento de Estado seguía renuente a intervenir por la fuerza, aunque se esperaba el envío de algún hombre fuerte que ganara la confianza de todos los elementos, y entretanto la marina mantenía una flota considerable en aguas dominicanas, incluidos el South Carolina en Puerto Plata, el Marietta en Sánchez y el Castine en Azua.

The Richmond Virginian prints: the sending of 700 marines to Guantanamo, Cuba, was announced tonight by the administration at Washington on July 13, recognizing the necessity of taking some action with regard to conditions in Hayti and Santo Domingo. New Britain Herald prints a report: plans are being perfected

July 22, 191422 de julio de 1914

New Britain Herald prints a report: plans are being perfected by navy department officials, confident that peace can be restored on the island only by the active intervention of the United States. State department officials declined to say whether foreign representations had advanced to the point where American action in Haiti was demanded to force some control of the island government's finances that would insure liquidation of its obligations. If intervention were ordered in Haiti, it was suggested that the United States would attempt to establish a financial trusteeship over the republic, by which American officials not only would collect taxes, but also would require proper appropriation of funds. If intervention in Haiti becomes necessary, it is possible that the American troops might be used to put down the long revolt that has harassed the Dominican government. Such action, it was suggested, might be accompanied by extension of the authority which the United States now exercises in the collection of Dominican customs. Representations that Haiti's credit was good and that the government had met international obligations have been made to President Wilson by Minister Menos.
