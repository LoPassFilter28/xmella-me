April 13, 191513 de abril de 1915

The Omaha Daily Bee prints a dispatch: Washington, April 12. On reports from Minister Sullivan of a new revolutionary outbreak in the Dominican republic, the Navy Department today ordered the cruiser Des Moines to Santo Domingo City from Progreso, Mexico. The gunboat Nashville already is off the Dominican capital. Because the Nashville's presence was necessary at Santo Domingo City to cooperate with the Des Moines in protecting American interests, its orders to go to Port-au-Prince, Haiti, to take American Minister Bailly-Blanchard to Santiago, Cuba, were cancelled. The gunboat Wheeling, on its way to Vera Cruz from Guantanamo, Cuba, has been turned back to get Mr. Bailly-Blanchard, who is coming to Washington via Cuba to confer with officials here on Haitian political and financial affairs.

July 20, 191520 de julio de 1915

The Washington Herald reports that Secretary of the Navy Daniels has announced the gunboat Nashville was ordered from San Juan, Porto Rico, to San Domingo City to guard American interests in the event of political disturbances in the Dominican Republic. To replace the Nashville in guarding neutrality at San Juan—where two German vessels, including the Odenwald, are in port—the gunboat Paducah was ordered from Guantanamo Bay. The Washington Herald informa que el secretario de la Marina, Daniels, anunció que el cañonero Nashville recibió la orden de trasladarse desde San Juan de Puerto Rico hacia la ciudad de Santo Domingo para proteger los intereses estadounidenses en caso de disturbios políticos en la República Dominicana. Para reemplazar al Nashville en la custodia de la neutralidad en San Juan —donde dos buques alemanes, entre ellos el Odenwald, se encuentran en puerto—, el cañonero Paducah fue ordenado desde la bahía de Guantánamo. The Washington Times

Reviewing the findings of Senator Phelan's investigation, The Washington Times reports

July 26, 191526 de julio de 1915

Reviewing the findings of Senator Phelan's investigation, The Washington Times reports that while no evidence showed Minister Sullivan directly received money for official favors in Santo Domingo or knew of it, evidence demonstrated that a proposal was made to a New York contractor to pay money to a representative of Sullivan's backers to obtain favorable contracts. The report cited specific misconduct by Sullivan, including his relations with the Banco Nacional, hostility toward other banks, unauthorized promises to Dominican revolutionists, participation in Dominican politics, unpaid personal debts, and unauthorized statements to Dominican deputies regarding a proposed loan of $.100,000 in 1914. Senator Phelan concluded that the Dominican government's dependence on the United States for control over Dominican finances was probably responsible for the Dominican Republic's failure to demand Sullivan's recall.

Al revisar las conclusiones de la investigación del senador Phelan, The Washington Times informa que, aunque ninguna prueba demostró que el ministro Sullivan recibiera directamente dinero por favores oficiales en Santo Domingo o estuviera al tanto de ello, sí hubo pruebas de que se propuso a un contratista de Nueva York pagar dinero a un representante de los patrocinadores de Sullivan para conseguir contratos favorables. El informe señaló faltas específicas de Sullivan, incluidas sus relaciones con el Banco Nacional, hostilidad hacia otros bancos, promesas no autorizadas a revolucionarios dominicanos, participación en la política dominicana, deudas personales impagas y declaraciones no autorizadas a diputados dominicanos sobre un préstamo propuesto de $.100,000 en 1914. El senador Phelan concluyó que la dependencia del gobierno dominicano respecto a Estados Unidos para el control de sus finanzas fue probablemente la causa de que la República Dominicana no exigiera la destitución de Sullivan.

The Sun

In the last I twenty years I'nlttil States naval ves-.upon any

August 1, 19151 de agosto de 1915

The Sun commented on chronic instability in Haiti and Santo Domingo, asserting that recurring revolutions over twenty years had cost the United States more in naval patrols and interventions than direct administrative control would have required. The article stated that constant strife had depressed population numbers and blocked foreign capital from developing the island's fertile resources. It noted that the Monroe Doctrine had repeatedly blocked European powers from intervening to impose order, leaving the island underdeveloped.

The Sun comentó la inestabilidad crónica imperante en Haití y Santo Domingo, aseverando que las incesantes revueltas a lo largo de veinte años habían costado a los Estados Unidos más en patrullajes navales e intervenciones de lo que habría demandado un control administrativo directo. El artículo sostenía que la pugna ininterrumpida había mermado el crecimiento demográfico e impedido la afluencia de capital extranjero para el aprovechamiento de los fértiles recursos insulares. Hizo notar que la Doctrina Monroe había impedido repetidamente a las potencias europeas intervenir para imponer el orden, sumiendo a la isla en el atraso.
