One of the notable traits of Heureaux's conversation while in the port of Cape Haytien the second time, last Thursday, the 9th, was his contempt, not the least bit concealed, for the Haytians; nevertheless, on this visit he ordered the regulation salute of twenty-one guns to be fired, and it was returned after the usual lazy fashion by the Haytians from the two or three small field-pieces of ancient type which constitute about the whole of the serviceable artillery there. The Herald

The Herald's account gives Heureaux's views on Hayti and the United StatesEl relato del Herald recoge las opiniones de Heureaux sobre Haití y los Estados Unidos

June 26, 189826 de junio de 1898

Some day, he declared, all of the greater Antilles would either belong to him or be under the protectorate of the American republic, and there was but one sensible course for Santo Domingo or for Hayti — to cultivate the best political and commercial relations with the United States. On the subject of neutrality General Heureaux was somewhat ambiguous: he was indisposed, he said, to offend either Spain or the United States, the enmity of the former being as disastrous to his government as that of the latter, but he allowed it to be understood that, if it came to choice, he must decide to stand with the Americans. It was when the conversation turned to the late abortive uprising at Monte Cristo that the real opportunity to study the character of the dictator presented itself, and some of the Dominicans and prominent Haytiens who came aboard to pay homage to Heureaux had been hand in glove with the conspirators.

Algún día, declaró, todas las Antillas mayores o le pertenecerían a él o estarían bajo el protectorado de la república americana, y no había sino un camino sensato para Santo Domingo o para Haití: cultivar las mejores relaciones políticas y comerciales con los Estados Unidos. Sobre el tema de la neutralidad, el general Heureaux fue algo ambiguo: no estaba dispuesto, dijo, a ofender ni a España ni a los Estados Unidos, pudiendo la enemistad de la primera ser tan desastrosa para su gobierno como la de los segundos, pero dejó entender que, si llegaba a tener que escoger, debía decidirse a estar con los americanos. Fue cuando la conversación giró hacia el reciente alzamiento abortado en Monte Cristi que se presentó la verdadera oportunidad de estudiar el carácter del dictador, y algunos de los dominicanos y prominentes haitianos que subieron a bordo a rendir homenaje a Heureaux habían estado en íntima connivencia con los conspiradores.

Under the head 'Ulises Heureaux's Checkered Career,' the Call prints a sketch of the dead president: when Ulises Heureaux was chosen president of San Domingo in 1883, the election taking place during the last three days of June, the defeated candidate, General Casimiro N. de Moya, rebelled in La Vega and Monte Cristi; on July 24 the country was put under martial law, and, after a series of bloody encounters, the rebels were finally defeated in August, the number of casualties on both sides exceeding 1,000 dead and wounded. Heureaux first saw the light of day in Puerto Plata in 1845; a soldier's life attracted him, and in 1862 he became a private, later becoming commandant in the District of Puerto Plata; he served continuously in the war against Spain, was twice exiled for political reasons, retired for two years from political life, and in 1878 again assumed command to put down a Spanish uprising; he was wounded many times.

Heureaux was 53 years old; he was born in Puerto Plata, the nearest city to the little town of Moca, where he was assassinated Tuesday; he became a common soldier, and by bravery, brains and dash soon won high command; all the West Indies at one time or another have fought Spain — Santo Domingo did, and Heureaux became a general in that war; he was a fighter, a fearless, reckless one, whom bullets could not stop, and he received several of them in his body, but did not mind them; finally, when warfare palled, he became a politician, and his success in that exciting game was so great that his military record was overshadowed; and in 1882 Heureaux was elected president of the republic.

Heureaux tenía 53 años; nació en Puerto Plata, la ciudad más cercana al pequeño pueblo de Moca, donde fue asesinado el martes; se hizo soldado raso, y por bravura, inteligencia y arrojo pronto ganó alto mando; todas las Indias Occidentales en un momento u otro han luchado contra España — Santo Domingo lo hizo, y Heureaux se hizo general en esa guerra; era un luchador, uno temerario y sin miedo, a quien las balas no podían detener, y recibió varias de ellas en su cuerpo, pero no les hacía caso; finalmente, cuando la guerra cansó, se hizo político, y su éxito en ese juego emocionante fue tan grande que su historial militar fue eclipsado; y en 1882 Heureaux fue elegido presidente de la república.

New York Journal and Advertiser

Santo Domingo's President Who Was an Autocrat and Despot Slain.El presidente de Santo Domingo, que era un autócrata y déspota, asesinado.

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

Secret assassination was another of his favorite methods of heading off plots, and insurrections were nipped in the bud by almost indiscriminate executions; men were sent on journeys of state and never reached their destinations, and others were arrested and nothing more heard of their fate. Three years ago General Castillo, Minister of War, thought the opportunity favorable for starting a revolution for his own benefit: there was some trouble in the province, and Castillo got 1,000 rifles from the President on the plea of sending them to the province to put down the rebellion — of course, Castillo saw that they fell into the hands of his friends. Heureaux was for a time fooled, but discovered what was going on; he ordered General Estay, governor of the province, to execute Castillo, but the order was not carried out; by a neat stratagem the President enticed Castillo to the capital and arrested him, and the Minister was put aboard a war ship and taken to the province, where he was shot, and General Estay, surprised and protesting, was executed also because he had not obeyed orders. During the past two years Heureaux had lively times with the Jimenes insurrection that would not completely down, although he succeeded in driving Jimenes and General Morales out of the country; despotism was wearing on the people, and Heureaux was waning a bit in popular favor, and besides, Heureaux was piling up the national debt of the country.

El asesinato secreto era otro de sus métodos favoritos de cortar las conspiraciones, y las insurrecciones eran cortadas de raíz por ejecuciones casi indiscriminadas; los hombres eran enviados a viajes de estado y nunca llegaban a sus destinos, y otros eran arrestados y nada más se supo de su suerte. Hace tres años el general Castillo, ministro de Guerra, creyó favorable la oportunidad para comenzar una revolución para su propio beneficio: había algún disturbio en la provincia, y Castillo consiguió 1,000 rifles del Presidente con el pretexto de enviarlos a la provincia para sofocar la rebelión — por supuesto, Castillo se aseguró de que cayeran en manos de sus amigos. Heureaux estuvo por un tiempo engañado, pero descubrió lo que estaba pasando; ordenó al general Estay, gobernador de la provincia, ejecutar a Castillo, pero la orden no se llevó a cabo; por una estratagema ingeniosa el Presidente atrajo a Castillo a la capital y lo arrestó, y el Ministro fue puesto a bordo de un buque de guerra y llevado a la provincia, donde fue fusilado, y el general Estay, sorprendido y protestando, fue ejecutado también porque no había obedecido las órdenes. Durante los últimos dos años Heureaux tuvo tiempos animados con la insurrección de Jimenes que no se dejaba sofocar por completo, aunque logró expulsar a Jimenes y al general Morales del país; el despotismo estaba cansando al pueblo, y Heureaux estaba perdiendo un poco de favor popular, y además, Heureaux estaba acumulando la deuda nacional del país.

The Evening Times

The Times prints a dispatch on the assassination and the State Department's confirmation of itEl Times publica un despacho sobre el asesinato y la confirmación del Departamento de Estado

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

The State Department received this morning from Campbell L. Maxwell, American consul general at Santo Domingo, an official confirmation of the assassination of President Heureaux; and through the State Department, President McKinley sent a cablegram of condolence to President Figuereo: 'Lake Champlain, via Washington, July 27, 1899. His Excellency Wenceslao Figuereo, President of the Dominican Republic, Santo Domingo: In the name of the American people, and in my own, I offer to your Excellency and the Dominican Nation most sincere condolences, by reason of the death of President Heureaux. William McKinley.' It was said at the State Department this afternoon that the assassination of President Heureaux will probably not disturb the diplomatic relations between that country and the United States, and that, unless it shall appear that American business interests on the island are in danger by a possible revolution or political agitation growing out of the tragedy, no action will be taken by this Government beyond the official expression of sympathy with the inhabitants of the Republic.

El Departamento de Estado recibió esta mañana de Campbell L. Maxwell, cónsul general americano en Santo Domingo, una confirmación oficial del asesinato del presidente Heureaux; y a través del Departamento de Estado, el presidente McKinley envió un cablegrama de condolencia al presidente Figuereo: «Lake Champlain, vía Washington, 27 de julio de 1899. Su Excelencia Wenceslao Figuereo, Presidente de la República Dominicana, Santo Domingo: En nombre del pueblo americano, y en el mío propio, ofrezco a su Excelencia y a la Nación Dominicana mis más sinceras condolencias, por motivo de la muerte del presidente Heureaux. William McKinley». Se dijo en el Departamento de Estado esta tarde que el asesinato del presidente Heureaux probablemente no perturbará las relaciones diplomáticas entre ese país y los Estados Unidos, y que, a menos que resulte que los intereses comerciales americanos en la isla están en peligro por una posible revolución o agitación política que surja de la tragedia, no se tomará ninguna acción por este Gobierno más allá de la expresión oficial de simpatía con los habitantes de la República.
