The Daily Journal

The Journal prints the Washington items and the Fort de France dispatchEl Journal publica las notas de Washington y el despacho de Fort de France

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

A Washington item of July 27 says the State Department has received confirmation of the death of the president of San Domingo. The Fort de France dispatch of July 27 says General Ulysses Heureaux, president of the Dominican republic, was assassinated at Moca, Santo Domingo, at 4 o'clock last evening; the name of the murderer is Ramon Caceros; he succeeded in making his escape, but an energetic pursuit was at once begun, and it is probable that he will soon be captured; Vice-President General Wenceslao Figuereo, immediately upon the announcement of the president's assassination, assumed the direction of affairs; at present, calmness prevails in the republic; and the remains of President Heureaux will probably be taken to Santo Domingo for funeral services. The Washington item says no information was received up to 10 o'clock last night from the consular representatives of the United States in San Domingo, regarding the assassination of President Heureaux, and that, pending official advices of the assassination, no formal action will be taken by this government: Hon. William F. Powell, the minister to Hayti, is also chargé d'affaires to Santo Domingo, while this government is directly represented in the republic in the person of Campbell T. Maxwell, who is consul-general, and John A. Read, who is vice-consul; and officials here recall that attempts have been made heretofore on the life of Mr. Heureaux.

Una nota de Washington del 27 de julio dice que el Departamento de Estado ha recibido confirmación de la muerte del presidente de Santo Domingo. El despacho de Fort de France del 27 de julio dice que el general Ulysses Heureaux, presidente de la República Dominicana, fue asesinado en Moca, Santo Domingo, a las 4 de la tarde de ayer; el nombre del asesino es Ramon Caceros; logró hacer su escape, pero una persecución enérgica se comenzó de inmediato, y es probable que pronto sea capturado; el vicepresidente general Wenceslao Figuereo, inmediatamente después del anuncio del asesinato del presidente, asumió la dirección de los asuntos; al presente, prevalece la calma en la república; y los restos del presidente Heureaux serán probablemente llevados a Santo Domingo para los servicios fúnebres. La nota de Washington dice que no se recibió hasta las 10 de anoche ninguna información de los representantes consulares de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo, respecto al asesinato del presidente Heureaux, y que, pendientes los avisos oficiales del asesinato, no se tomará ninguna acción formal por este gobierno: el honorable William F. Powell, el ministro en Haití, es también encargado de negocios en Santo Domingo, mientras el gobierno está directamente representado en la república en la persona de Campbell T. Maxwell, que es cónsul general, y John A. Read, que es vicecónsul; y los funcionarios aquí recuerdan que se han hecho antes intentos contra la vida del señor Heureaux.

Alexandria Gazette

The Gazette completes its assassination dispatch: the remains of President HeureauxEl Gazette completa su despacho del asesinato: los restos del presidente Heureaux

July 27, 189927 de julio de 1899

President Heureaux was a soldier during his entire lifetime: one of his arms was paralyzed from a wound he received by a rifle ball in battle; he is described by Americans familiar with his career as a man without fear, and when there was any disturbance or revolution in any part of the republic, he mounted his horse and went pellmell himself to the front as leader, and learned all the details of the revolution as well as the causes leading to it; President Heureaux was about fifty-four years of age, he spoke English fluently, and treated everybody who approached him with courtesy. The Gazette also prints a Puerto Plata item of July 27: it is learned that President Heureaux met his death at the hands of three men as he was entering a friend's house: the three fired at Heureaux with revolvers, and one shot entered the heart, causing instant death; friends of the President returned the fire, but the assassins fled and their whereabouts are not known; Heureaux was at Moca on one of his periodical trips to investigate affairs on the island; and the assassination is believed to be due to the personal animosity of Caceres, the chief assassin, whose father was executed by orders of Heureaux in 1884.

El presidente Heureaux fue un soldado durante toda su vida: uno de sus brazos estaba paralizado por una herida que recibió por una bala de rifle en batalla; es descrito por americanos familiarizados con su carrera como un hombre sin miedo, y cuando había algún disturbio o revolución en cualquier parte de la república, montaba su caballo y iba precipitadamente él mismo al frente como líder, y aprendía todos los detalles de la revolución así como las causas que la motivaron; el presidente Heureaux tenía unos cincuenta y cuatro años, hablaba inglés fluidamente, y trataba a todos los que se le acercaban con cortesía. El Gazette también publica una nota de Puerto Plata del 27 de julio: se sabe que el presidente Heureaux encontró su muerte a manos de tres hombres cuando estaba entrando en la casa de un amigo: los tres dispararon a Heureaux con revólveres, y un tiro le entró en el corazón, causándole la muerte instantánea; los amigos del Presidente devolvieron el fuego, pero los asesinos huyeron y no se sabe su paradero; Heureaux estaba en Moca en uno de sus viajes periódicos para investigar los asuntos en la isla; y se cree que el asesinato se debe a la animosidad personal de Caceres, el asesino principal, cuyo padre fue ejecutado por órdenes de Heureaux en 1884.

The Register prints a sketch of Heureaux's career: he was born in Puerto Plata, San Domingo, in 1846; he became a private soldier in 1862 and was soon after appointed commandant in the district of Puerto Plata; he served continuously in the war against Spain from 1868 to 1874; twice he was exiled for political reasons; for two years he retired to private life, and again in 1878 assumed command to put down a Spanish uprising; he had been wounded many times and narrowly escaped several plots to kill him; during the recent Spanish-American war a filibustering expedition, alleged to have been fitted out in a port in the southern part of the United States, made an attempt to land in San Domingo, but was met and checked by Heureaux's prompt action, and at that time his assassination was rumored and had undoubtedly been planned; Gen. Heureaux was re-elected president of San Domingo at a general election, November 1, 1898, and was serving his fourth term; Gen. Heureaux was first elected president, September 1, 1882, to succeed Fernando A. Merino, when the national party, of which he was an active representative, was powerful; General Maximo Gomez, former president of the Cuban insurrectionists, who is a native of San Domingo, also aspires to the presidency; the father of Caceros, the assassin, was put to death by order of President Heureaux in 1884; and a financial crisis having arisen in Santo Domingo, and an insurrection being feared owing to the number of malcontents, President Heureaux went to the north and west of the island to prepare for all eventualities.

The Times

Assassination of HeureauxAsesinato de Heureaux

July 28, 189928 de julio de 1899

"I had hoped that this rumor, like many others I have heard in the course of the past year, would prove false." Consul General Gil then expressed the opinion that the president's death would not have a serious effect upon the republic. Mr. York, of the Santo Domingo Improvement Company, was very positive that no serious results would follow the president's death: "The affairs of the country are in a sound, healthy condition and things will go on as usual. The fact that the President was assassinated does not mean that there is any general discontent among the citizens of the country. That would be a false assumption. Discontented persons with murderous instincts are to be found in every country, but as a rule they do not represent the great body of the people. You will appreciate the extent of my confidence in the strength of the Government when I tell you my wife and children sail for Santo Domingo today on the Clyde line steamship Carib. I am sorry to hear of General Heureaux's death, very sorry, but it will surely not affect the country seriously." Moca is the principal town of the Espaillat province; it is situated in the mountains on the north coast of the island, and has no railroad communication.

«Había esperado que este rumor, como muchos otros que he oído en el curso del año pasado, resultara falso». El cónsul general Gil expresó entonces la opinión de que la muerte del presidente no tendría un efecto serio sobre la república. El señor York, de la Santo Domingo Improvement Company, estuvo muy positivo de que no seguirían resultados serios a la muerte del presidente: «Los asuntos del país están en una condición sana y saludable y las cosas seguirán como de costumbre. El hecho de que el presidente fue asesinado no significa que haya ningún descontento general entre los ciudadanos del país. Esa sería una suposición falsa. Personas descontentas con instintos asesinos se encuentran en todo país, pero por regla general no representan al gran cuerpo del pueblo. Usted apreciará la extensión de mi confianza en la fuerza del gobierno cuando le diga que mi esposa e hijos zarpan hoy para Santo Domingo en el vapor Carib de la línea Clyde. Siento oír de la muerte del general Heureaux, lo siento mucho, pero seguramente no afectará seriamente al país». Moca es el pueblo principal de la provincia Espaillat; está situado en las montañas en la costa norte de la isla, y no tiene comunicación por ferrocarril.

Richmond Dispatch

Death of Heureaux.La muerte de Heureaux.

July 28, 189928 de julio de 1899

President Heureaux's death has caused a panic: one Juan Isidoro Jimenez, who took part in the attempted insurrection in June, 1868, is said to be a candidate for President; General Maximo Gomez, former President of the Cuban insurrection, who is a native of Santo Domingo, also aspires to the position; and the father of Caceres, the assassin, was put to death by order of President Heureaux in 1884.
