The New York Herald

A correspondent's letter from the City of Santo Domingo describes a military commission that sentenced General…

June 23, 185523 de junio de 1855

A correspondent's letter from the City of Santo Domingo describes a military commission that sentenced General Pelletier, General Aybar, Francisco Ruiz and others accused of conspiracy to death, with others sentenced to expatriation, imprisonment or banishment. After the condemned men's children pleaded for grace, the prisoners were led to the execution ground on May 21, knelt while their sentence was read and faced the firing party, until General Santana cried halt and had a pardon for the three read aloud; Ruiz rose from his knees having lost his reason under the horror. The letter adds that the pardoned men received passports to leave the country and that upwards of fifty individuals were banished, including women and children and the sisters of ex-President Baez.

La carta de un corresponsal desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo describe una comisión militar que condenó a muerte al general Pelletier, al general Aybar, a Francisco Ruiz y a otros acusados de conspiración, mientras otros fueron condenados a expatriación, prisión o destierro. Después de que los hijos de los condenados suplicaran gracia, los prisioneros fueron llevados al lugar de ejecución el 21 de mayo, se arrodillaron mientras se leía su sentencia y quedaron frente al pelotón de fusilamiento, hasta que el general Santana gritó alto e hizo leer en voz alta un indulto para los tres; Ruiz se levantó de rodillas habiendo perdido la razón por el horror. La carta añade que los indultados recibieron pasaportes para salir del país y que más de cincuenta personas fueron desterradas, incluidas mujeres y niños y las hermanas del expresidente Báez.

The New York Herald

The paper says the rumor of the defeat of the Haitians by the Dominicans, received by way of Boston, has been…

January 23, 185623 de enero de 1856

The paper says the rumor of the defeat of the Haitians by the Dominicans, received by way of Boston, has been confirmed by telegraph from Philadelphia, with advices from Port-au-Prince to January 1 stating that Emperor Faustin had taken to his heels in the first brush. It says a reward of ten thousand doubloons had been offered for his head, and the excitement against him among his own troops was so great that it was expected he would be shot if he fell into their hands.

El periódico dice que el rumor de la derrota de los haitianos por los dominicanos, recibido vía Boston, ha sido confirmado por telégrafo desde Filadelfia, con avisos de Puerto Príncipe hasta el 1 de enero que afirman que el emperador Faustin había huido al primer choque. Dice que se había ofrecido una recompensa de diez mil doblones por su cabeza, y que la excitación contra él entre sus propias tropas era tan grande que se esperaba que fuera fusilado si caía en sus manos.

The New York Herald

The British steamer Buzzard brought news from Haiti, dated at Jacmel on January 16, to Jamaica, confirming the…

January 30, 185630 de enero de 1856

The British steamer Buzzard brought news from Haiti, dated at Jacmel on January 16, to Jamaica, confirming the defeat of Soulouque and his army by the Dominicans. It says that after two of his general officers were killed, Soulouque ran away and had not been heard of after, and that, acting on the supposition that he was dead, a provisional government of the empire had been inaugurated.

El vapor británico Buzzard llevó a Jamaica noticias de Haití, fechadas en Jacmel el 16 de enero, que confirman la derrota de Soulouque y su ejército por los dominicanos. Dice que después de que dos de sus oficiales generales fueron muertos, Soulouque huyó y no se había sabido de él después, y que, actuando bajo la suposición de que estaba muerto, se había inaugurado un gobierno provisional del imperio.

New-York Dispatch

The paper narrates Soulouque's rise after he was firmly seated in the presidential chair, saying he intrigued…

February 3, 18563 de febrero de 1856

The paper narrates Soulouque's rise after he was firmly seated in the presidential chair, saying he intrigued through the army, the rival factions and the two branches of the Legislature to transform the state into an empire. It says that after two years the imperial crown was placed on his head by the vote of the Senate and Representatives, making him Faustin the First, Emperor of Haiti and its dependent isles.

El periódico narra el ascenso de Soulouque después de que se afianzó en la silla presidencial, diciendo que intrigó a través del ejército, las facciones rivales y las dos cámaras de la Legislatura para transformar el Estado en un imperio. Dice que después de dos años la corona imperial fue colocada en su cabeza por voto del Senado y de los Representantes, convirtiéndolo en Faustin Primero, emperador de Haití y sus islas dependientes.

New-York Daily Tribune

A letter dated May 10 from a prominent inhabitant of Aux Cayes reports that an insurrectionary movement against the…

June 4, 18564 de junio de 1856

A letter dated May 10 from a prominent inhabitant of Aux Cayes reports that an insurrectionary movement against the existing government in Haiti has actually commenced there. The writer says he learned that a body of 2,000 to 3,000 insurgents had taken possession of a post between the town and Pingrey overnight with a view to attacking the town that day, the alarm drums were beaten, and every male in the town was ordered out.

Una carta fechada el 10 de mayo de un habitante prominente de Les Cayes informa que un movimiento insurreccional contra el gobierno existente en Haití ha comenzado realmente allí. El autor dice que supo que un cuerpo de 2.000 a 3.000 insurgentes había tomado un puesto entre la ciudad y Pingrey durante la noche con intención de atacar la ciudad ese día, que se tocaron los tambores de alarma y que se ordenó salir a todos los varones de la ciudad.

The paper prints a letter from a prominent inhabitant in Aux Cayes reporting that, since the utter failure of the attack upon the Dominicans, Soulouque has rapidly lost popularity in some parts of the island, and a strong insurrectionary movement against the existing government has commenced there. The letter, dated May 10, describes being woken by alarm drums and learning that a body of 2,000 to 3,000 insurgents had taken possession of a post between the town and Pingrey overnight, with a view to attacking the town that day.

El periódico publica una carta de un habitante prominente de Les Cayes que informa que, desde el fracaso total del ataque contra los dominicanos, Soulouque ha perdido popularidad rápidamente en algunas partes de la isla, y que un fuerte movimiento insurreccional contra el gobierno existente ha comenzado allí. La carta, fechada el 10 de mayo, describe haber sido despertado por tambores de alarma y saber que un cuerpo de 2.000 a 3.000 insurgentes había tomado un puesto entre la ciudad y Pingrey durante la noche, con intención de atacar la ciudad ese día.

Alexandria Gazette

The Alexandria Gazette reports that a revolution is in progress

September 19, 185719 de septiembre de 1857

The Alexandria Gazette reports that a revolution is in progress in the Dominican Republic, on recent accounts from St. Domingo, aimed at removing President Báez and restoring Santana to power. According to the notice, the Báez faction, finding itself hard pressed by the insurgents, has formed an alliance with Emperor Soulouque of Haiti, who has dispatched assistance to his ally.

La Alexandria Gazette informa que hay una revolución en curso en la República Dominicana, según noticias recientes procedentes de Santo Domingo, con el propósito de destituir al presidente Báez y restituir a Santana en el poder. Según la nota, el bando de Báez, al verse fuertemente acosado, ha formado una alianza con el emperador Soulouque de Haití, quien ha enviado ayuda a su aliado.
