The San Francisco Call

The Call prints the closing part of a Washington item on San DomingoEl Call publica la parte final de una nota de Washington sobre San Domingo

December 5, 19035 de diciembre de 1903

The Call prints the closing part of a Washington item on San Domingo: the president is now on his way to the United States and is expected to land at New Orleans to-day or to-morrow, and he will come to Washington; advices reached Washington that there are now three revolutions in San Domingo, the revolution led by Morales, a revolution at Monte Cristi led by the followers of Jiminez, and a third and stronger revolution in the south of the island; it appears that Morales has secured control by an appeal to the Dominicans that the United States intended to annex the island, and it is also said that Morales has received many courtesies from European representatives in the island, who are also opposed to a step by the United States in the direction of asserting the supremacy of its interests in the island.

El Call publica la parte final de una nota de Washington sobre San Domingo: el presidente viene ahora hacia los Estados Unidos y se espera que desembarque en Nueva Orleans hoy o mañana, y vendrá a Washington; llegaron a Washington avisos de que ahora hay tres revoluciones en San Domingo, la revolución acaudillada por Morales, una revolución en Monte Cristi acaudillada por los partidarios de Jiménez, y una tercera y más fuerte revolución en el sur de la isla; parece que Morales ha logrado el control mediante un llamamiento a los dominicanos de que los Estados Unidos intentaban anexar la isla, y se dice también que Morales ha recibido muchas cortesías de los representantes europeos en la isla, que también se oponen a un paso de los Estados Unidos en el sentido de afirmar la supremacía de sus intereses en la isla.

The Atlanta Constitution

The Constitution prints the Washington item on the San Domingo Improvement Company arbitrationEl Constitution publica la nota de Washington sobre el arbitraje de la San Domingo Improvement Company

December 7, 19037 de diciembre de 1903

The Constitution prints the Washington item on the San Domingo Improvement Company arbitration in a degraded impression: Senor Galvan, secretary of finance under the Woss y Gil administration, was named as the Dominican representative in the arbitration, and the United States and San Domingo united in the selection of Judge George Gray as the umpire; the State Department will support Minister Powell in his refusal to yield to the desire of the Dominican government that the personnel of the arbitrators be changed so as to eliminate Senor Galvan; the State Department has been informed that already a new revolutionary movement has begun in San Domingo, before the provisional government that ousted Woss y Gil has been able to solidify itself into a permanent government, and these repeated uprisings, in the face of Minister Powell's warnings, have concentrated the attention of the Washington government upon the island, where much American capital is invested; and the arbitration commission held its first meeting today in the hearing room of the Interstate Commerce Commission, among those present being Gray, former Secretary of the Treasury Carlisle, and Senor Galvan.

El Constitution publica la nota de Washington sobre el arbitraje de la San Domingo Improvement Company en una impresión degradada: el señor Galván, secretario de Hacienda durante la administración de Woss y Gil, fue nombrado representante dominicano en el arbitraje, y los Estados Unidos y San Domingo se unieron en la selección del juez George Gray como árbitro dirimente; el Departamento de Estado apoyará al ministro Powell en su negativa a ceder al deseo del gobierno dominicano de que se cambie el personal de los árbitros para eliminar al señor Galván; el Departamento de Estado ha sido informado de que ya ha comenzado un nuevo movimiento revolucionario en San Domingo, antes de que el gobierno provisional que derrocó a Woss y Gil haya podido consolidarse en gobierno permanente, y estos repetidos levantamientos, ante las advertencias del ministro Powell, han concentrado la atención del gobierno de Washington sobre la isla, donde hay invertido mucho capital norteamericano; y la comisión de arbitraje celebró hoy su primera reunión en la sala de audiencias de la Comisión de Comercio Interestatal, estando entre los presentes Gray, el exsecretario del Tesoro Carlisle y el señor Galván.

The Pioneer prints the closing part of a Santo Domingo item and a Washington item of December 4: the first says that Minister Powell declined to grant a demand concerning the board appointed by the government of President Woss y Gil to arbitrate the claims of the Santo Domingo Improvement company, and supplemented his declination by the statement that the United States government could only recognize the provisional government of Santo Domingo when it agreed to strictly observe all the acts and engagements entered into by former governments with the United States; the Washington item says it is learned that John G. Carlisle has been named as a representative of the United States in the arbitration of the claims of the San Domingo Improvement company against the Dominican republic, which was arranged for by former President Woss y Gil as one of the last of his official acts, that Senor Galvan, secretary of finance under the Woss y Gil administration, was named as the Dominican representative in the arbitration, and that the United States and San Domingo united in the selection of Judge George Gray as the umpire; the State Department will support Minister Powell in his refusal to yield to the desire of the Dominican provisional government that the personnel of the arbitrators be changed so as to eliminate Senor Galvan; and the State Department has been informed that already a new revolutionary movement has begun in San Domingo, even before the provisional government that ousted Woss y Gil has been able to solidify itself into a permanent government.

El Pioneer publica la parte final de una nota de Santo Domingo y una nota de Washington del 4 de diciembre: la primera dice que el ministro Powell se negó a conceder una demanda relativa a la junta nombrada por el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil para arbitrar las reclamaciones de la Santo Domingo Improvement Company, y complementó su negativa con la declaración de que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos solo podría reconocer al gobierno provisional de Santo Domingo cuando este conviniera en observar estrictamente todos los actos y compromisos contraídos por los gobiernos anteriores con los Estados Unidos; la nota de Washington dice que se sabe que John G. Carlisle ha sido nombrado representante de los Estados Unidos en el arbitraje de las reclamaciones de la San Domingo Improvement Company contra la República Dominicana, que fue concertado por el expresidente Woss y Gil como uno de los últimos actos oficiales suyos, que el señor Galván, secretario de Hacienda durante la administración de Woss y Gil, fue nombrado representante dominicano en el arbitraje, y que los Estados Unidos y San Domingo se unieron en la selección del juez George Gray como árbitro dirimente; el Departamento de Estado apoyará al ministro Powell en su negativa a ceder al deseo del gobierno provisional dominicano de que se cambie el personal de los árbitros para eliminar al señor Galván; y el Departamento de Estado ha sido informado de que ya ha comenzado un nuevo movimiento revolucionario en San Domingo, aun antes de que el gobierno provisional que derrocó a Woss y Gil haya podido consolidarse en gobierno permanente.

The Loup City Northwestern

The Northwestern prints the closing part of the Washington itemEl Northwestern publica la parte final de la nota de Washington

December 11, 190311 de diciembre de 1903

The Northwestern prints the closing part of the Washington item on San Domingo and a San Domingo item on recognition: the president is now on his way to the United States and is expected to land at New Orleans today or tomorrow, and he will come to Washington; advices reached Washington that there are now three revolutions in San Domingo, the revolution led by Morales, a revolution at Monte Cristo led by the followers of Jiminez, and a third and stronger revolution in the south of the island, and that Morales has secured control by an appeal to the Dominicans that the United States intends to annex the island, and has received many courtesies from European representatives in the island, who are also opposed to a step by the United States in the direction of asserting the supremacy of its interests in the island; the San Domingo item says the provisional government has made an official request for recognition to the diplomatic and consular bodies, and gives United States Minister Powell's conditions on which the United States will recognize the new government: the government must agree to abide by and respect the agreements made by the preceding administrations, must recognize the manner of settlement arrived at in the case of the Santo Domingo Improvement company of New York and the Ros and Sala cases, must recognize and carry into full effect the concession to the steamship company, and must grant the privilege to the United States to establish at dangerous points on the coast lighthouses to aid maritime commerce.

The Roswell Daily Record

The Record prints part of an item about a letter and a Washington item of December 15El Record publica parte de una nota sobre una carta y una nota de Washington del 15 de diciembre

December 15, 190315 de diciembre de 1903

The Record prints part of an item about a letter and a Washington item of December 15: the letter, received by a fellow countryman in this city, formerly an official under the deposed president of the republic, Woss y Gil, states that the plot was so arranged that ex-President Jiminez, under whose leadership Woss y Gil was overthrown, should be killed about the same time; it was planned to kill Powell by 'accident' during a street melee engaged in by plotters; Jiminez was also the victim of the plan of assassination, and already two unsuccessful attempts have been made upon his person; the plot against Powell is said to have been abandoned because of the strong stand taken by members of the cabinet, although the diplomat is being carefully guarded now wherever he goes, and his opposition to the plans of certain officials caused him to be marked for assassination; the Washington item says United States Consul Simpson, at Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, has cabled the State Department under today's date that a revolution has broken out in Santiago and that the movement is extensive and serious, which is supposed to be the result of the failure of the provisional government to secure recognition at the hands of United States Minister Powell, a fact which operated to prevent the government from obtaining money to maintain itself through a foreign loan.

El Record publica parte de una nota sobre una carta y una nota de Washington del 15 de diciembre: la carta, recibida por un compatriota en esta ciudad, antes funcionario durante el gobierno del presidente depuesto de la república, Woss y Gil, dice que el complot estaba arreglado de tal manera que el expresidente Jiménez, bajo cuyo mando fue derrocado Woss y Gil, debía ser muerto por la misma época; se planeaba matar a Powell por «accident» durante una refriega callejera provocada por los complotados; Jiménez era también la víctima del plan de asesinato, y ya se habían hecho dos intentos infructuosos contra su persona; se dice que el complot contra Powell fue abandonado por la firme postura adoptada por miembros del gabinete, aunque el diplomático está ahora cuidadosamente custodiado dondequiera que va, y su oposición a los planes de ciertos funcionarios hizo que se le marcara para el asesinato; la nota de Washington dice que el cónsul de los Estados Unidos, Simpson, en Puerto Plata, Santo Domingo, ha cablegrafiado al Departamento de Estado con fecha de hoy que ha estallado una revolución en Santiago y que el movimiento es extenso y serio, lo que se supone resultado del fracaso del gobierno provisional en lograr el reconocimiento de manos del ministro de los Estados Unidos, Powell, hecho que operó para impedir que el gobierno obtuviera dinero para sostenerse por medio de un empréstito extranjero.

Evening Star

The Star prints part of Minister Powell's report on the revolutionEl Star publica parte del informe del ministro Powell sobre la revolución

December 28, 190328 de diciembre de 1903

Though over 80,000 rounds of ammunition have been used on both sides, and there has been constant firing by both sides night and day (in the attack on Santo Domingo city), the total loss on both sides numbers twenty-five, and these principally from stray bullets and accidents. The insurgent loss numbered two men. Nor was this loss greater in other sections. At Monte Cristi, none; at Puerto Plata, none; at Santiago, about seventy (this includes the loss on both sides); at Moca, none; at La Vega, none; at Macoris, none. The only places I have not heard from are Azua and Barahona.' Mr. Powell also describes the house in which he formerly resided, about one mile from the city: it suffered more than any of the surrounding buildings and is almost a wreck, as if both sides had used it as a target; several shells have burst inside and rifle balls have perforated it; many of the things he left there are ruined, and had they remained, the office would have been in need of another representative.

Aunque se han usado más de 80,000 cartuchos de munición entre ambos bandos, y ha habido fuego constante de ambos lados noche y día (en el ataque a la ciudad de Santo Domingo), la pérdida total de ambos lados suma veinticinco, y estos principalmente por balas perdidas y accidentes. La pérdida insurgente sumó dos hombres. Tampoco fue mayor la pérdida en otras secciones. En Monte Cristi, ninguna; en Puerto Plata, ninguna; en Santiago, unas setenta (esto incluye la pérdida de ambos lados); en Moca, ninguna; en La Vega, ninguna; en Macorís, ninguna. Los únicos lugares de los que no he tenido noticias son Azua y Barahona». El Sr. Powell describe también la casa en la que residía antes, a una milla aproximadamente de la ciudad: sufrió más que cualquiera de los edificios circundantes y está casi en ruinas, como si ambos bandos la hubieran usado como blanco; varias granadas han estallado dentro y las balas de fusil la han perforado; muchas de las cosas que dejó allí están arruinadas, y de haber permanecido allí, la oficina habría necesitado otro representante.
