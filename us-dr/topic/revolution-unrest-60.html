Minister Powell reported to the State Department today that he had received belated advices from Jean Villain, the vice consular agent at Samana, dated Feb. 9, to this effect: 'Insurgents entered our consulate at Samana with armed force and took two refugees out.' The State Department communicated this information to the Navy Department, with a request that proper action be taken by the naval authorities to protect the United States consulate; this was transmitted to Admiral Wise, and it is the belief that he will not only protect the consulate, but will demand the punishment of the perpetrators of this outrage, and if the provisional government is not able to do this he will employ the United States naval force to chastise the insurgents. The St. Louis Republic

The Republic prints two items: a message received from San DomingoEl Republic publica dos notas sobre el bombardeo y la anexión

February 18, 190418 de febrero de 1904

Under the head 'Eager for Annexation', special correspondence of the New York Herald and the Republic from San Juan, Porto Rico, dated February 10, reports that a strenuous effort is now being made by the most prominent merchants of Puerto Plata to bring about the annexation of Santo Domingo to the United States, with a view to extricating the country from its chaotic condition; annexation to the United States has long been the cherished dream of the Dominican commercial element, and not much thought is given to the particular form of government the United States would establish, it being immaterial to the business men whether it be annexation or a protectorate, so long as a firm and stable government be established, insuring peace and guaranteeing life and property. Should the American government be ultimately induced to interfere in Dominican affairs, it will be confronted with a situation that it has at no time had to deal with: Cuba, Porto Rico and the Philippines were ruled by an organized government when the United States took charge of them; no such thing will be found in Santo Domingo, as not even when in the enjoyment of undisturbed peace can that country boast of a form of government that could be styled organized or modern.

El Republic publica dos notas: un mensaje recibido de Santo Domingo, fechado el 17 de febrero, dice que un buque de guerra estadounidense bombardeó a los insurgentes en Pajarito, cerca de Santo Domingo, y luego desembarcó infantes de marina; los hombres, sin embargo, se reembarcaron después. Bajo el titular «Eager for Annexation», la correspondencia especial del New York Herald y el Republic desde San Juan, Puerto Rico, fechada el 10 de febrero, informa que los comerciantes más prominentes de Puerto Plata están haciendo ahora un esfuerzo enérgico por lograr la anexión de Santo Domingo a los Estados Unidos, con miras a sacar al país de su caótica situación; la anexión a los Estados Unidos ha sido durante mucho tiempo el sueño acariciado del elemento comercial dominicano, y no se piensa mucho en la forma particular de gobierno que los Estados Unidos establecerían, pues a los hombres de negocios les es indiferente que sea anexión o protectorado, siempre que se establezca un gobierno firme y estable, que asegure la paz y garantice la vida y los bienes. Si el gobierno estadounidense fuera inducido finalmente a intervenir en los asuntos dominicanos, se enfrentaría a una situación con la que nunca ha tenido que lidiar: Cuba, Puerto Rico y Filipinas eran gobernados por un gobierno organizado cuando los Estados Unidos se hicieron cargo de ellos; no se encontrará tal cosa en Santo Domingo, pues ni siquiera cuando disfruta de una paz sin perturbaciones puede ese país presumir de una forma de gobierno que pudiera calificarse de organizada o moderna.

A Washington item reports that drastic measures will be taken by the government to end the anarchy in San Domingo: continued violation of international law, the destruction of American property and disregard of foreign interests has convinced officials that affairs there can best be improved by force; its handling has been turned over to the navy, and Secretary Moody has been sending supplementary instructions to Rear Admiral Wise, commanding the training squadron now in Dominican waters, giving him wide latitude in the course he is to follow. A San Juan, P. R., item reports that the cruiser Dixie took coal here and sailed on rush orders to Santo Domingo, and that the cruisers Montgomery and Detroit, now at Culebra, have been ordered from there to Puerto Plata.

Una nota de San Juan, P. Reporter and Farmer

The Reporter and Farmer prints the official confirmation of the Pajarito bombardmentEl Reporter and Farmer publica la confirmación oficial del bombardeo de Pajarito

February 25, 190425 de febrero de 1904

According to Capt. Miller's dispatch the affair occurred on a river twenty miles from San Domingo, presumably at Pajarito, the place mentioned in the press dispatches as the locality where the bombardment occurred. The insurgents, who were stationed on the mainland, fired on a United States merchant vessel, believed to be the New York, which was lying in the river; the shots fell so dangerously near the war vessels that the acts of the insurgents were construed by Capt. Miller as an attack on the vessels, and a battery fire was opened on the revolutionists. This was followed by the debarkation of 400 marines and bluejackets from the Columbia and Newark, who made a successful landing ashore; they dislodged the insurgents from their position and chased them into the country; after accomplishing this the marines and bluejackets took again to their boats and returned to their ships. One man, a bugler named Painter, was seriously wounded by the accidental explosion of his firing piece; there were no other casualties. Authoritatively it is stated that the question of the annexation of Santo Domingo to the United States never has received the slightest consideration on the part of the administration.

Según el despacho del capitán Miller, el hecho ocurrió en un río a veinte millas de Santo Domingo, presumiblemente en Pajarito, el lugar mencionado en los despachos de prensa como la localidad donde ocurrió el bombardeo. Los insurgentes, que estaban apostados en tierra firme, dispararon contra un buque mercante de los Estados Unidos, que se cree era el New York, que estaba en el río; los disparos cayeron tan peligrosamente cerca de los buques de guerra que los actos de los insurgentes fueron interpretados por el capitán Miller como un ataque a los buques, y se abrió fuego de batería contra los revolucionarios. A esto siguió el desembarco de 400 infantes de marina y marineros del Columbia y el Newark, que efectuaron un desembarco exitoso en la costa; desalojaron a los insurgentes de su posición y los persiguieron hacia el interior; tras lograr esto, los infantes de marina y los marineros volvieron a sus botes y regresaron a sus buques. Un hombre, un corneta llamado Painter, resultó seriamente herido por la explosión accidental de su pieza de fuego; no hubo otras bajas. Se ha afirmado autorizadamente que la cuestión de la anexión de Santo Domingo a los Estados Unidos nunca ha recibido la más mínima consideración por parte de la administración.

The Tribune prints Señor Joubert's statement and Captain Wainwright's reportEl Tribune publica la declaración del señor Joubert y el informe del capitán Wainwright

February 25, 190425 de febrero de 1904

Señor Joubert added: 'President Morales will soon have the rebellion suppressed and the affairs will again assume their normal state. If the reports from San Domingo are true that the rebels have committed outrages against American interests, no one will regret it more keenly than President Morales. He is supported by the best element of the country, and this rebellion is a fight between them and the worst political element, as you would call them here. The President and his government are not only most friendly toward American interests, but it is his first care that all foreign property and life shall be respected and protected.' Under the head 'American Marines in San Domingo', a Washington item of February 20 reports that by order of Secretary Moody the report of Captain Richard Wainwright, commanding the Newark, regarding his recent reconnaissance about San Domingo, was made public at the Navy Department to-day: Captain Wainwright found on his arrival at Sanchez that the daughter and brother-in-law of the American consular agent had been wounded by the same bullet in the fighting there; as it was expected that the attack on February 8 would be repeated the following day, he wrote a letter to the commanders of the government and insurgent forces protesting against further firing in the streets of the city or toward the houses of the inhabitants.

El señor Joubert añadió: «El presidente Morales pronto habrá suprimido la rebelión y los asuntos asumirán de nuevo su estado normal. Si los informes de Santo Domingo son ciertos, de que los rebeldes han cometido ultrajes contra los intereses estadounidenses, nadie lo lamentará más vivamente que el presidente Morales. The insurgents, who were stationed on the mainland, fired on a United States merchant vessel, believed to be the New York, which was lying in the river; the shots fell so dangerously near the war vessels that the acts of the insurgents were construed by Capt. Miller as an attack on the vessels, and a battery fire was opened on the revolutionists. This was followed by the debarkation of 400 marines and bluejackets from the Columbia and Newark, who made a successful landing ashore; they dislodged the insurgents from their position and chased them into the country; after accomplishing this the marines and bluejackets took again to their boats and returned to their ships.
