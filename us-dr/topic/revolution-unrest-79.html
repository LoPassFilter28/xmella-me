Norwich Bulletin

The Bulletin prints an item: Washington, September 9. James M. SullivanThe Bulletin publica una nota de Washington, 9 de septiembre

September 10, 191310 de septiembre de 1913

The Bulletin prints an item: Washington, September 9. James M. Sullivan, the new American minister to Santo Domingo, and Charles M. Hathaway, consul at Puerto Plata, left Washington late today for Puerto Plata to tell the Dominican revolutionists there in emphatic terms that the United States has no sympathy with their efforts to overthrow President Bordas's government. They received final instructions at the State Department just before their departure. Mr. Sullivan will take steps to protect the customs houses in the revolutionary zone, which, like all those in the island republic, are operated under American supervision. The cruiser Des Moines was due at Puerto Plata late today, and should it be necessary there will be no hesitancy in the landing of marines, which besides guarding the customs houses would afford protection to the American consulate and all American interests. If hostilities still are in progress, an effort will probably be made to persuade the belligerents to do their fighting outside the city limits.

Recibieron las instrucciones finales en el Departamento de Estado justo antes de su partida. El Sr. Sullivan tomará medidas para proteger las aduanas de la zona revolucionaria, que, como todas las de la república isleña, funcionan bajo supervisión estadounidense. El crucero Des Moines debía llegar a Puerto Plata hoy a última hora y, de ser necesario, no se vacilará en desembarcar infantes de marina, lo que, además de custodiar las aduanas, brindaría protección al consulado estadounidense y a todos los intereses estadounidenses. Si las hostilidades continúan todavía, probablemente se hará un esfuerzo por persuadir a los beligerantes de que luchen fuera de los límites de la ciudad.

The Sun reported that President Wilson adopted a firm policy against Dominican revolutionists in Puerto Plata, viewing their rebellion against President Bordas as driven merely by a quest for government offices and revenue. Backed by the cruiser Des Moines, Minister Sullivan warned insurgent leaders that Washington would refuse to recognize insurgent regimes or disburse customs funds to them. U.S. officials believed that withholding customs revenue served as a decisive weapon to end Caribbean insurrections.

The Sun informó que el presidente Wilson adoptó una enérgica postura frente a los revolucionarios dominicanos en Puerto Plata, considerando que su alzamiento contra el presidente Bordas respondía meramente a un afán de cargos públicos y rentas. Con el respaldo del crucero Des Moines, el ministro Sullivan advirtió a los jefes insurgentes que Washington se negaría a reconocer regímenes rebeldes o a entregarles caudales de las aduanas. Los diplomáticos estadounidenses consideraban que la retención de los ingresos aduaneros constituía un arma decisiva para sofocar las rebeliones en el Caribe.

General Horatio Vasques, leader of the Dominican revolution, has agreed that the rebels shall suspend warfare, according to a dispatch received here from Puerto Plata last night.
