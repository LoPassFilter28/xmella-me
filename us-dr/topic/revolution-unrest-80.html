September 20, 191320 de septiembre de 1913

Gen. Horatio Vasquez, leader of the Dominican revolution, has agreed that the revolutionists shall suspend warfare, according to a dispatch received here from Puerto Plata. The proposal for the suspension of hostilities was made by James M. Sullivan, American minister to Santo Domingo. The gunboat Nashville arrived at Puerto Plata yesterday. When an understanding was reached with Gen. Vasquez assuring the end of the revolt against President Bordas, Mr. Sullivan went on board the gunboat and left for Santo Domingo City, the dispatch states.

La propuesta de suspensión de las hostilidades la hizo James M. Sullivan, ministro estadounidense en Santo Domingo. La cañonera Nashville llegó a Puerto Plata ayer. Cuando se llegó a un entendimiento con el general Vasquez que asegura el fin de la revuelta contra el presidente Bordas, el Sr. Sullivan subió a bordo de la cañonera y partió hacia la ciudad de Santo Domingo, afirma el despacho.

The Evening Star prints a dispatch: Santo Domingo, September 24.The Evening Star publica un despacho: Santo Domingo, 24 de septiembre

September 24, 191324 de septiembre de 1913

The revolution has been limited to Puerto Plata, Moca and Samana Bay, in the northern part of the republic. All other parts of the country are reported to be quiet. Foreign residents are in no danger, conducting their various businesses and traveling without fear of being molested. The blockade, however, continues at Puerto Plata and Samana Bay.

La revolución se ha limitado a Puerto Plata, Moca y la bahía de Samaná, en el norte de la república. Se informa que todas las demás partes del país están tranquilas. Los residentes extranjeros no corren peligro, atienden sus diversos negocios y viajan sin temor a ser molestados. El bloqueo, sin embargo, continúa en Puerto Plata y la bahía de Samaná.

Puerto Plata, the chief stronghold of the Dominican rebels on the north coast, surrendered to the government forces today, according to official advices received at the capital. East Oregonian : E.O

The East Oregonian prints a dispatch: Santo Domingo, October 9.The East Oregonian publica un despacho fechado en Santo Domingo

October 9, 19139 de octubre de 1913

The American minister to the Dominican republic, James M. Sullivan, has induced the warring factions in this country to sign a peace pact, and the revolution headed by General Horatio Vasquez against the government of the provisional president, Jose Bordas Valdez, is at an end. The American minister's action is the first successful application of the United States government's new Latin-American policy with regard to the Dominican Republic, which is to the effect that force of arms never again can settle any question in this country. Mr. Sullivan has made it known that the last civil war in this republic has been fought. In the future any uprising, he says, will be stamped as criminal without a conference between the opposing factions.

La acción del ministro estadounidense es la primera aplicación exitosa de la nueva política latinoamericana del gobierno de los Estados Unidos con respecto a la República Dominicana, cuyo sentido es que la fuerza de las armas nunca más podrá resolver cuestión alguna en este país. El Sr. Sullivan ha dado a conocer que la última guerra civil de esta república ya se ha librado. En el futuro, dice, todo levantamiento será tachado de criminal sin conferencia alguna entre las facciones opuestas.

The Montgomery Advertiser

The Montgomery Advertiser prints a dispatch: risings, he saysThe Montgomery Advertiser publica un despacho: los levantamientos

October 9, 19139 de octubre de 1913

The people of Santo Domingo are being instructed that the remedy for future grievances lies in the free ballot at a future election. The American policy as announced by Mr. Sullivan has been accepted joyously by all parties regardless of political affiliations. The American minister arrived at Santo Domingo City from the United States September 17 and immediately brought about an armistice between the rebels and the government troops fighting in the northern department. Since then negotiations had been in progress continuously, with the result that what promised to be the most sanguinary revolution in the history of the Dominican republic has been averted. The last battle was fought October 2, when the government forces in the vicinity of Puerto Plata attacked the rebels. As a result of the intervention of Commander Louis M. Nulton, of the United States cruiser Nashville, and Walter W. Vick, general receiver of customs, the revolutionary and governmental peace commissions which had reached a deadlock at Puerto Plata were urged to come to Santo Domingo City, where the rebels could treat direct with the government and with the friendly co-operation of the American minister. The commissioners were brought to the capital on the Nashville.

Se está instruyendo al pueblo de Santo Domingo de que el remedio para agravios futuros está en el voto libre en una elección futura. La política estadounidense, tal como la anunció el Sr. Sullivan, ha sido aceptada con alegría por todos los partidos sin importar las afiliaciones políticas. El ministro estadounidense llegó a la ciudad de Santo Domingo desde los Estados Unidos el 17 de septiembre e inmediatamente logró un armisticio entre los rebeldes y las tropas del gobierno que combatían en el departamento del norte. Desde entonces las negociaciones habían avanzado continuamente, con el resultado de que se ha evitado lo que prometía ser la revolución más sangrienta de la historia de la República Dominicana. La última batalla se libró el 2 de octubre, cuando las fuerzas del gobierno en las cercanías de Puerto Plata atacaron a los rebeldes. Como resultado de la intervención del comandante Louis M. Nulton, del crucero estadounidense Nashville, y de Walter W. Vick, receptor general de aduanas, se instó a las comisiones de paz revolucionaria y gubernamental, que habían llegado a un punto muerto en Puerto Plata, a venir a la ciudad de Santo Domingo, donde los rebeldes podían tratar directamente con el gobierno y con la amistosa cooperación del ministro estadounidense. Los comisionados fueron traídos a la capital en el Nashville.

October 9, 19139 de octubre de 1913

The Daily Missoulian reported that U.S. Minister Sullivan brokered a peace settlement between the Dominican government and northern insurgents, proclaiming that armed rebellion would no longer be tolerated and constitutional changes must occur via the ballot. After initial talks deadlocked at Puerto Plata following an October 2 clash, Commander Nulton of the Nashville and Customs Receiver Vick transported negotiating teams to Santo Domingo City. The ensuing conference produced a final peace agreement that averted further bloodshed.

The Daily Missoulian informó que el ministro de los EE. UU. Sullivan concertó un acuerdo de paz entre el gobierno dominicano y los insurgentes del norte, proclamando que las sublevaciones armadas no se tolerarían más y que los cambios constitucionales debían efectuarse a través de las urnas. Luego de que las conversaciones iniciales se empantanaran en Puerto Plata a raíz de un combate el 2 de octubre, el comandante Nulton del Nashville y el receptor aduanero Vick trasladaron a las comisiones negociadoras a la ciudad de Santo Domingo. La conferencia subsiguiente culminó en un acuerdo definitivo de paz que impidió mayores derramamientos de sangre.

October 9, 19139 de octubre de 1913

The American minister's action is the first successful application of the United States government's new Latin American policy with regard to the Dominican Republic, which is to the effect that the force of arms can never again settle any question in this country. Mr. Sullivan has made it known that the last civil war in this Republic has been fought. In the future any uprising, he says, will be stamped out as criminal without a conference being held between the opposing factions, the United States government supporting the constitutional authority against force regardless of the merits of the issue. The people of Santo Domingo are being instructed that the remedy for future grievances lies in the free ballot at a future election. The American policy, as announced by Mr. Sullivan, has been accepted joyously by all parties, regardless of political affiliations. The American minister arrived at Santo Domingo City from the United States on September 17 and immediately brought about an armistice between the rebels and the government troops fighting in the northern department. Since then negotiations had been in progress continuously with the result that what promised to be the most sanguinary revolution in the history of the Dominican Republic has been averted.
