In this dilemma, it relates, the Dominican cabinet entered into negotiation with the Paris banking house of Erlanger & Co.: the firm was to open a subscription in France for a loan of two millions and a half of dollars in gold, one tenth of the amount to be guaranteed by the house if the subscription had little success in Europe, its advances to be guaranteed in securities on the custom-house duties of the republic. El gobierno de Cabral se negó indignado a aceptar la proposición, aunque las dificultades financieras del país podrían aún obligar a la administración a llegar a un acuerdo.

The New York Herald

Conflicting reports of Cabral's defeat and Báez's proclamationInformes contradictorios sobre la derrota de Cabral y la proclamación de Báez

December 24, 186724 de diciembre de 1867

A press telegram from Santo Domingo of December 20 announces that Cabral's government has been defeated, owing to the defective organization of his troops; that a pronunciamiento of the people against him has been issued and Báez proclaimed President; and that a commission is going to Washington to complete arrangements for the transfer of the bay of Samana to the United States. Later advices from Santiago de Cuba report that well-informed persons there doubt the news of the defeat of General Cabral's forces, as well as the announcement of a pronunciamiento in favor of ex-President Báez. From Santo Domingo City the paper also reports that Señor Pujol, still at Santiago, was expected there and is to offer the lease of Samana for ninety-nine years; that in the Legislative Chamber the ten-million-franc loan recently negotiated in Paris was approved; and that Consul Savage, on a telegram from the American consul at Santiago de Cuba, has telegraphed Secretary Seward advising him to defer further proceedings in relation to the St. Thomas purchase.

Un telegrama de prensa de Santo Domingo, del 20 de diciembre, anuncia que el gobierno de Cabral ha sido derrotado, a causa de la defectuosa organización de sus tropas; que se ha emitido un pronunciamiento del pueblo en su contra y Báez ha sido proclamado presidente; y que una comisión va a Washington a ultimar los arreglos para la transferencia de la bahía de Samaná a los Estados Unidos. Avisos posteriores de Santiago de Cuba reportan que personas bien informadas allí dudan de la noticia de la derrota de las fuerzas del general Cabral, así como del anuncio de un pronunciamiento en favor del expresidente Báez. Desde la ciudad de Santo Domingo el periódico informa además que el señor Pujol, todavía en Santiago, era esperado allí y va a ofrecer el arrendamiento de Samaná por noventa y nueve años; que en la Cámara Legislativa fue aprobado el empréstito de diez millones de francos negociado recientemente en París; y que el cónsul Savage, a raíz de un telegrama del cónsul americano en Santiago de Cuba, ha telegrafiado al secretario Seward aconsejándole diferir las gestiones relativas a la compra de Santo Tomás.

The New York Herald

A Dominican mission has arrived at Washington from New YorkUna misión dominicana ha llegado a Washington procedente de Nueva York

January 6, 18686 de enero de 1868

The object of the visit is to lay before Secretary Seward, by instructions from President Cabral, a proposition to sell the peninsula and bay of Samana to the United States. The terms of the purchase are two million dollars cash down and three hundred thousand dollars per annum for fifty years, the government of St. Domingo reserving to itself one fifth of the proceeds from the various mines on the peninsula — a stipulation General Pujol has the option of waiving if the sale can be better facilitated by it. At the expiration of the fifty years the peninsula becomes the fee-simple property of the United States, the total cost being seventeen millions.

El objeto de la visita es presentar al secretario Seward, por instrucciones del presidente Cabral, una proposición para vender la península y la bahía de Samaná a los Estados Unidos. Los términos de la compra son dos millones de dólares en efectivo de contado y trescientos mil dólares anuales durante cincuenta años, reservándose el gobierno de Santo Domingo una quinta parte de los productos de las distintas minas de la península, estipulación que el general Pujol tiene la opción de renunciar si con ello se facilita mejor la venta. Al expirar los cincuenta años, la península pasará a ser propiedad plena y absoluta de los Estados Unidos, con un costo total de diecisiete millones.

The Ottawa Free Trader

The Free Trader recounts the history of the guano claims at Alto Velo.El Free Trader relata la historia de las reclamaciones por el guano de Alto Velo.

May 9, 18689 de mayo de 1868

Americans who had been taking guano from the island were ejected by the Dominican government, which asserted its jurisdiction, and the parties then sought a contract with that government for permission to ship guano and claimed damages. Secretary of State Jeremiah S. Black refused to notice the claim for damages until the United States agent at St. Domingo should have ascertained and reported the grounds upon which the Dominican government had asserted its jurisdiction; before the answer was received, Black had ceased to be Secretary of State and had been succeeded by Mr. Seward, and the rebellion and the war, various revolutions in the Dominican Republic, and war between that government and Hayti postponed any consideration of the claims. When the question was raised again, the department answered that two parties of Americans had claimed the island as discoverers, that St. Domingo had asserted a title and ejected the claimants, and that it declined to give any encouragement to Americans to resort there for guano without first obtaining the consent of St. Domingo.

Los estadounidenses que extraían guano de la isla fueron expulsados por el gobierno dominicano, que afirmó su jurisdicción, y los interesados buscaron entonces un contrato con ese gobierno para obtener permiso de embarcar guano, reclamando además daños y perjuicios. El secretario de Estado Jeremiah S. Black se negó a atender la reclamación de daños hasta que el agente de los Estados Unidos en Santo Domingo averiguara e informara sobre los fundamentos en que el gobierno dominicano había basado su jurisdicción; antes de que llegara la respuesta, Black había cesado como secretario de Estado y había sido sucedido por el señor Seward, y la rebelión y la guerra, varias revoluciones en la República Dominicana y la guerra entre ese gobierno y Haití pospusieron toda consideración de las reclamaciones. Cuando la cuestión se planteó de nuevo, el departamento respondió que dos grupos de estadounidenses habían reclamado la isla como descubridores, que Santo Domingo había afirmado un título y expulsado a los reclamantes, y que se negaba a dar estímulo alguno a los estadounidenses para acudir allí por guano sin obtener primero el consentimiento de Santo Domingo.

New-York Tribune

The Tribune's special correspondent writes from Puerto PlataEl corresponsal especial del Tribune escribe desde Puerto Plata

July 24, 186824 de julio de 1868

The correspondent reports the manifesto the malcontent generals have published, addressed to the people of St. Domingo, setting forth that the revolutionists are not animated by a spirit of vengeance, but that it is absolutely necessary to the peace and prosperity of the country that Báez should be removed from the presidency — that should he abdicate power, dissensions may speedily be healed, but if he resists their demands a general civil war is imminent. In the city of St. Domingo great disquiet prevails in consequence of the imminency of another desperate struggle; the prisons, it is said, are full, and one of the prisoners has been tried by a council of war and sentenced to death, but an appeal has been made to the Supreme Court, where it is expected the sentence will be reversed.

El corresponsal reproduce el manifiesto publicado por los generales descontentos, dirigido al pueblo de Santo Domingo, en el que exponen que los revolucionarios no están animados por un espíritu de venganza, pero que es absolutamente necesario para la paz y la prosperidad del país que Báez sea apartado de la presidencia: que si abdica del poder, las disensiones podrán curarse pronto, pero que si se resiste a sus demandas, una guerra civil general es inminente. En la ciudad de Santo Domingo reina gran inquietud ante la inminencia de otra lucha desesperada; se dice que las cárceles están llenas, y uno de los prisioneros ha sido juzgado por un consejo de guerra y sentenciado a muerte, pero se ha presentado una apelación ante la Corte Suprema, donde se espera que la sentencia sea revocada.

La dificultad en su camino, dice la versión, fue la exhibición de un excesivo afán de comprar, que indujo a Cabral, entonces al frente del gobierno dominicano, a mostrarse exorbitante, esperando obtener así una vasta riqueza con la que abandonar la isla. The Herald prints the prospectus of the Samana Bay CompanyEl Herald publica el prospecto de la Compañía de la Bahía de Samaná

January 9, 18699 de enero de 1869

The passage opens in the middle of the Samaná affair, with word that Báez's own party is determined not to let him consummate anything of the kind. An opportunity is now being presented, the prospectus says, through the confidential agent of the Dominican Republic in New York, to secure a lease of the bay and peninsula of Samana, with all the eminent domain, rights and prerogatives of sovereignty now held and enjoyed by the republic, for a period of twenty years, paying for the same the sum of one hundred thousand dollars in gold annually in advance — it being understood that at the expiration of that period, or on the payment of the whole amount of the lease, to wit, two million dollars, the territory shall be finally transferred in fee and become the absolute property of the company. During the period of the lease the company will enjoy all privileges and immunities of sovereignty under the joint protection of the republics of St. Domingo and the United States; and in furtherance of the scheme the company proposes to raise at once the sum of one hundred thousand dollars in gold, and despatch its agent therewith to St. Domingo to tender the same to the Dominican authorities as the first instalment under the lease, and receive from them a formal ratification of the terms and conditions of the contract entered into with the Dominican agent in New York.

Durante el período del arrendamiento, la compañía gozará de todos los privilegios e inmunidades de la soberanía bajo la protección conjunta de las repúblicas de Santo Domingo y de los Estados Unidos; y para llevar adelante el plan, la compañía se propone recaudar de inmediato la suma de cien mil dólares en oro y despachar a su agente con ella a Santo Domingo, para entregarla a las autoridades dominicanas como primera cuota del arrendamiento y recibir de ellas una ratificación formal de los términos y condiciones del contrato celebrado con el agente dominicano en Nueva York.

Public Ledger

The New Goverament de facto in Cuba and its Relations.El nuevo gobierno de facto en Cuba y sus relaciones

February 3, 18693 de febrero de 1869

In Mexico the question is already put forward as one of annexation of Cuba to the Mexican republic, the editorial says, while among the Dominicans, at home and in the revolutionary service in Cuba, it assumes the form of a West Indian confederation. No word of annexation to the United States is breathed by the Cuban revolutionists, and thus far President Céspedes seems to have carefully abstained from sending an authorized agent to Washington — a reserve the editorial traces to the opinion prevalent in Cuba that in former efforts to attain independence, whenever Cubans have confided their hopes and views to members of the Washington cabinet, from Fillmore's time down to that of Buchanan, they have been betrayed to the Spanish government. Whether the new combinations will crystallize into something effective and tangible, the Ledger concludes, yet remains to be seen.

En México la cuestión ya se plantea como la anexión de Cuba a la república mexicana, dice el editorial, mientras que entre los dominicanos, en el país y en el servicio revolucionario en Cuba, asume la forma de una confederación de las Indias Occidentales. Under the head 'What San Domingo Is,' the Spy describes the island of San DomingoBajo el encabezado «Lo que es Santo Domingo», el Spy describe la isla de Santo Domingo

February 9, 18699 de febrero de 1869

It has an area, with the smaller ones under its jurisdiction, of about 30,000 square miles, and is in extent about the size of Scotland. Every product of tropical and temperate climes can be grown there; it is known to be enormously rich in mineral wealth — for many years, while under the dominion of Spain, its annual tribute from the gold mines, consisting of one-fourth of the product, was about $5,000,000 — and in timber it is exceedingly rich, having every variety of cabinet woods; the flora includes the most valuable medicinal plants known to man. The Dominican territory could sustain in comfort at least ten million inhabitants, probably twice as many; there is an abundant water power; and it is geographically in the very centre of the Antilles. Commercially speaking, its position is worth everything to us, the writer says: we acquire Samana, one of the only half dozen harbors in the world in which the Great Eastern can ride, and secure the key and controlling position in the Gulf. The outlines given are, in the writer's judgment, what seems now the most honest in effort and most promising in result of all annexation schemes lately had before the public.

It is intimated, the editorial says, that evidence may be produced to show that the populations of the most desirable of the West India islands, including Cuba and Porto Rico, are eager to solve the troublesome and at present gloomy problem of their destiny by annexation to the United States, and it dwells on the richness of the island of St. Domingo in mineral and agricultural resources. It is not to be supposed, the Crescent argues, that if the Dominican republic were annexed, Hayti would be content to remain, or would in any case long remain, outside of American jurisdiction. Dominica occupies the larger part of the territory, and contains the greater portion of the population, of the island of St. Domingo, of which the whole area is 28,000 square miles and the whole population 1,250,000; next to Cuba in point of population, this island is also, next to Cuba, the most beautiful and productive of the West Indies. And the value to American commerce of the trade which would be developed by the opening of this island to American enterprise by annexation is only less, the editorial says, than that which would result from the annexation of Cuba. The Crescent has no idea that any definite programme for these ends will be carried in the present Congress, but it expects that the subject will be a subject of increasing agitation during the incoming administration.

Se sugiere, dice el editorial, que podrá producirse evidencia de que las poblaciones de las más deseables de las islas de las Indias Occidentales, incluidas Cuba y Puerto Rico, ansían resolver el problema trabajoso y actualmente sombrío de su destino mediante la anexión a los Estados Unidos, y se detiene en la riqueza de la isla de Santo Domingo en recursos minerales y agrícolas. New-York Tribune

The Dominican Question.La cuestión dominicana.

February 19, 186919 de febrero de 1869

In the mean time, the editorial says, there is the 'Samana Bay Company' and the 'Alta Vela Guano Company,' rich and powerful in both countries: while the ceding and giving away is going on, land grants, gold mines, and guano islands may be made over to these gentlemen under the seal of the nation, and the President, too, his country being so ungrateful, might seek an honest recompense for all his toils by becoming a partner in the speculations of these good people. Besides all this, one of the conditions of the transfer is understood to be, as international law requires, that the United States shall pay the public debt of St. Domingo, and so soon as the former is made liable, the gentlemen of the ring will turn up with certificates of indebtedness. When the vaunted evidence of the willingness of the people of St. Domingo to be annexed comes to be produced, the Tribune predicts, it will likely be found to consist of nothing but the mutual assurances of these high contracting parties — of the waning President and 'his friends' on the one hand, and of the waning Secretary of State on the other.

Mientras tanto, dice el editorial, están la «Compañía de la Bahía de Samaná» y la «Compañía del Guano de Alta Vela», ricas y poderosas en ambos países: mientras sigue la cesión y la entrega, podrán hacerse a estos caballeros, bajo el sello de la nación, traspasos de concesiones de tierras, minas de oro e islas de guano, y el propio presidente, su país siéndole tan ingrato, podría buscar una honesta recompensa por todos sus trabajos haciéndose socio de las especulaciones de esta buena gente. Además de todo esto, se entiende que una de las condiciones de la transferencia es, como exige el derecho internacional, que los Estados Unidos paguen la deuda pública de Santo Domingo, y en cuanto aquel quede obligado, los caballeros de la camarilla aparecerán con certificados de deuda. Cuando se presente la tan pregonada evidencia de la disposición del pueblo de Santo Domingo a ser anexado, predice el Tribune, probablemente se encontrará que consiste únicamente en las mutuas seguridades de estas altas partes contratantes: del presidente en decadencia y «sus amigos», por una parte, y del secretario de Estado en decadencia, por la otra.
