The New York Herald

A letter printed in the Herald gives an account, beginning in mid-courseUna carta publicada en el Herald ofrece un relato, que comienza a mitad de su curso

February 24, 186924 de febrero de 1869

That failed, the letter continues, and we now hear of annexation. Báez has purposely secured his loan in such a way as to keep up his government for two years, and by the end of that term he trusts to the development of the mining and industrial interests of the country for maintaining it without sacrificing Samana. The idea of annexation failed to alarm the people, the letter says, because it came in the form of a fraternal invitation, highly flattering to their self-respect, and if the project is followed up in that guise there will be no opposition worth mentioning to the measure. The country is quiet, though many arrests were made after the Seibo fiasco, and some twenty persons are held for examination on a charge of conspiracy; it is said that henceforth such attempts will be treated with the utmost rigor of the law, but before this system is put into effect Báez will proclaim a general amnesty, and only those who renew seditious efforts are to suffer the death penalty. The roads are bad beyond description; and before signing the drafts for the first $200,000 instalment of the loan, two engineers, brought from Europe for the purpose, made a scientific reconnaissance of the gold districts, to see whether there was sufficient promise of mineral wealth to secure the payment.

Aquello fracasó, continúa la carta, y ahora oímos hablar de anexión. Báez ha asegurado su empréstito deliberadamente de manera que su gobierno se mantenga dos años, y al final de ese plazo confía en el desarrollo de los intereses mineros e industriales del país para sostenerlo sin sacrificar Samaná. La idea de la anexión no alarmó al pueblo, dice la carta, porque vino en forma de una invitación fraternal, muy halagadora para su amor propio, y si el proyecto se sigue en esa forma no habrá oposición digna de mención a la medida. El país está tranquilo, aunque se hicieron muchos arrestos tras el fiasco de Seibo y unas veinte personas están detenidas para ser examinadas por un cargo de conspiración; se dice que en adelante tales intentos serán tratados con el máximo rigor de la ley, pero antes de que este sistema entre en vigor Báez proclamará una amnistía general, y solo quienes renueven los esfuerzos sediciosos sufrirán la pena de muerte. Las carreteras están malas más allá de toda descripción; y antes de firmar los giros de la primera cuota de $200.000 del empréstito, dos ingenieros, traídos de Europa con ese fin, hicieron un reconocimiento científico de los distritos auríferos, para ver si había suficiente promesa de riqueza mineral que asegurara el pago.

The New York Herald

An editorial in the Herald lays down conditions for annexationUn editorial del Herald establece condiciones para la anexión

March 9, 18699 de marzo de 1869

The Dominican Republic will be the great gainer by the step if it is ever taken, the editorial says, and we have no need to pay a high price for a very small article. As for Samaná, it has been in the market for sale under every administration St. Domingo has had since it separated from Hayti, and has not found a purchaser, and it has been before the world for settlement and population ever since Columbus struck the New World, in 1492, and its pestilential shores are as solitary now as they were three hundred and fifty years ago. Let the Dominican spoilsmen agitate at home, the editorial concludes: they can get nothing out of the United States Treasury under the new regime of honesty that rules there.

La República Dominicana será la gran beneficiada por el paso, si alguna vez se da, dice el editorial, y no tenemos necesidad de pagar un precio alto por un artículo muy pequeño. En cuanto a Samaná, ha estado en venta bajo todas las administraciones que Santo Domingo ha tenido desde que se separó de Haití, y no ha encontrado comprador, y ha estado ante el mundo para su población y colonización desde que Colón llegó al Nuevo Mundo en 1492, y sus costas pestilentes están tan desiertas ahora como hace trescientos cincuenta años. Que los expoliadores dominicanos agiten en su país, concluye el editorial: no sacarán nada del Tesoro de los Estados Unidos bajo el nuevo régimen de honestidad que allí impera.

He had written for certain official statistics to lay before the American people, but not receiving them in season, he came on in person and found them just ready to be forwarded to him. The papers thus prepared are in singular contradiction, the account says, to the estimate of the public debt made by Mr. Angenard: he estimated it at from $12,000,000 to $16,000,000, while it is in fact less than $1,000,000, and even if the French loan is accepted it will still be under that figure, and the republic has ten times that value in the public domain and gold fields, which are not burdened as yet by a single dollar of debt. Her whole revenue is some hundreds of thousands of dollars per annum, of which $400,000 is derived from her custom house receipts; these would inure to the United States Treasury in the event of annexation, but the Dominicans would have the desired equivalent in peace, protection, the development of her means and a vast enhancement of general prosperity.

The West Indies line was advertised by the firm of Spofford, Tileston & Co., No. 29 Broadway; the pioneer vessel was to be the first-class steamship Tybee, which the advertisement stated would leave Pier No. 4, North River, on Wednesday evening, going first to St. Thomas, proceeding thence to Santo Domingo City, Samaná, and Puerto Plata, and returning from the last-named place to New York. The vessel was advertised as for freight and passengers, and a notice on the steamship bulletin board at the General Post Office stated that a mail would be despatched by the Tybee. Meantime, suspicion having been aroused that the Tybee was intended to play the same role as the Perit, representations were made to Mr. Grinnell by the Spanish Consul in this city to the effect that the vessel's real destination was the Island of Cuba, whither she was about to convey munitions of war for the use of the insurgents, besides two hundred and fifty American volunteers for the Cuban army; in support of this it was stated that the Tybee had shipped several cases containing rifles. Acting upon this information, Collector Grinnell gave orders that the Tybee should be searched, and the extract ends as the search began yesterday forenoon.

La línea de las Indias Occidentales fue anunciada por la firma de Spofford, Tileston & Co., n.º 29 de Broadway; el buque pionero sería el vapor de primera clase Tybee, que según el anuncio saldría del Muelle n.º 4, North River, el miércoles por la noche, yendo primero a Santo Tomás, procediendo de allí a la ciudad de Santo Domingo, Samaná y Puerto Plata, y regresando desde este último lugar a Nueva York. El buque se anunciaba para carga y pasajeros, y un aviso en el tablón de vapores de la Oficina General de Correos indicaba que se despacharía correspondencia por el Tybee. Posteriormente la hora de salida del buque se cambió al sábado 6 de junio, a las 8 de la noche, pero, no habiéndose completado los arreglos de su partida para entonces, se retrasó aún más hasta ayer al mediodía. In the pursuit of this petty and mischievous policy, it says, a signal rebuke was received from the Dominican republic in the Samaná question, and from the Senate of the United States in that of St. Thomas; both were merited, for the principles on which the course was founded were of the meanest and most contemptible kind. Questions of national importance merit a higher and a nobler style of treatment. Fifty years ago a policy was announced by President Monroe which acquired an extended influence in American politics and European cabinets, because it expressed the sentiment of the American people; during two generations that policy has been the pole star of national development on this continent, and it was adapted to its era and has consummated its work. En la prosecución de esta política mezquina y perniciosa, dice, se recibió una señalada reprensión de la República Dominicana en la cuestión de Samaná, y del Senado de los Estados Unidos en la de Santo Tomás; ambas fueron merecidas, pues los principios en que se fundaba ese proceder eran de la clase más ruin y despreciable. Las cuestiones de importancia nacional merecen un trato de estilo más elevado y noble. Hace cincuenta años un presidente, Monroe, anunció una política que adquirió una extensa influencia en la política estadounidense y en los gabinetes europeos, porque expresaba el sentimiento del pueblo estadounidense; durante dos generaciones esa política ha sido la estrella polar del desarrollo nacional en este continente, y estaba adaptada a su época y ha consumado su obra. Ya no se trata de fundar nuevas colonias ni de extender la influencia europea sobre las antiguas; las colonias se han convertido en naciones, y el desarrollo nacional ha traído nuevas necesidades y nuevas demandas, y el extracto termina cuando pasa al deseo que se desarrolla en los países al sur de los Estados Unidos por su fracaso en alcanzar la misma prosperidad.
