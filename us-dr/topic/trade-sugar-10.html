San Domingo is divided into five provinces; there is an army of 20,000 troops of all arms, and a small navy; the language of the people is Spanish, and the religion Roman Catholic; the government is that of a president, with a Congress consisting of two branches. Hayti and Dominica were several times united, the item recounts, but were divided by revolution, and the country has been for twenty years in a state of chronic revolution, the Dominicans having long since concluded that there can be no peace until they are taken under the flag of the United States; for several years they have proposed to cede the Bay of Samana to the United States, and now offer the whole republic. Haití y Dominica estuvieron varias veces unidas, relata la nota, pero se dividieron por revolución, y el país ha estado durante veinte años en un estado de revolución crónica, habiendo concluido los dominicanos desde hace tiempo que no puede haber paz hasta ser tomados bajo la bandera de los Estados Unidos; durante varios años han propuesto ceder la bahía de Samaná a los Estados Unidos, y ahora ofrecen la república entera. Under the heading "Dominica and its people," the feature relates that the eastern, superior, and yet weaker half of the island contains the historical relics: the first colony founded in the New World was Isabella, on its north coast, near the present town of Puerto Plata, founded by Columbus himself; his brother, Bartholomew Columbus, founded the city of Santo Domingo in 1494, as the passage dates it; Columbus' son Diego built the cathedral here. He presented evidence that the majority of the people voted for annexation and that insurrectionary leaders, whose rebellion lacked strength after being cut off from Hayti, were not inherently opposed to it. Furthermore, the President expressed his belief that acquiring San Domingo would immediately benefit U.S. commerce and the Darien canal, while potentially leading to broader territorial control of the Gulf islands.

En conferencia con senadores el viernes, el presidente encareció la pronta ratificación del tratado para incorporar a Santo Domingo, advirtiendo de que, en caso contrario, dicho territorio podría ser brindado a una potencia europea en quebranto de la doctrina Monroe. Exhibió pruebas de que la generalidad de la ciudadanía sufragó a favor de la anexión y de que los caudillos insurrectos, cuyas fuerzas languidecían al quedar desasistidos desde Haití, no repudiaban la medida en esencia. Manifestó asimismo su convicción de que la anexión de Santo Domingo acarrearía inmediatos provechos al tráfico marítimo estadounidense y al proyectado canal del Darién, franquando de paso el control de las islas del Golfo.
