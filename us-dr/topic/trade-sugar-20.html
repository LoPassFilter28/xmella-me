November 2, 18972 de noviembre de 1897

The Sun reported that an English company was replacing an American syndicate in managing Dominican customs duties and railroad concessions to secure foreign loan payments. The article traced the concession back to an earlier multimillion-dollar loan by the Amsterdam firm Westendorp & Co., which had financed a railroad from Puerto Plata to the interior. It noted that customs revenues subsequently declined due to poor crops, administrative mismanagement, and widespread smuggling.

The Sun informó que una compañía inglesa estaba sustituyendo a un consorcio estadounidense en la administración de las aduanas y concesiones ferroviarias dominicanas para asegurar el pago de empréstitos extranjeros. El artículo remontó el origen de la concesión a un préstamo multimillonario previo de la firma de Ámsterdam Westendorp & Co., la cual había financiado un ferrocarril desde Puerto Plata hacia el interior. Señaló además que las rentas aduaneras disminuyeron con posterioridad debido a malas cosechas, mala gestión administrativa y un contrabando generalizado.

About thirty years ago an attempt was made by the GrantHace unos treinta años la administración de Grant hizo un intento de

December 31, 189731 de diciembre de 1897

The Saint Paul Globe published a letter comparing the proposed annexation of Hawaii to President Grant's unsuccessful effort to annex Santo Domingo in 1869 and 1870. The writer recalled that despite strategic arguments, joint resolution efforts, and a favorable commission report, the annexation treaty failed in the Senate. The author argued that past fears of foreign powers absorbing Santo Domingo proved unfounded and hoped Hawaii would avoid annexation.

The Saint Paul Globe publicó una carta en la que comparaba la proyectada anexión de Hawái con la infructuosa tentativa del presidente Grant de anexionar Santo Domingo en 1869 y 1870. El autor recordaba que, a pesar de los argumentos estratégicos, de los esfuerzos por lograr una resolución conjunta y de un informe favorable de la comisión, el tratado de anexión fracasó en el Senado. El autor sostuvo que los temores pasados de que potencias extranjeras absorbieran a Santo Domingo resultaron infundados y expresó su deseo de que Hawái evitara la anexión.

Toussaint L'Ouverture's successful struggle against France and his capture by treachery, followed by the complete expulsion of the French, is one of liberty's favorite tales; the Spanish invaded San Domingo in 1861, and their defeat left the islanders free. The Haytians have experimented with kings and discarded them; the treasure of one of these despots, Christophe, remains buried under the rank vegetation among the ruins of a stronghold he built, whose architect and workmen, the story goes, were thrown from the parapets so that the secrets of the citadel they had just completed should never be divulged. The Mona Passage, only 60 miles wide, separates Hayti from the beautiful island of Porto Rico, a valuable possession which Spain risks losing on a turn of the war cards.

The Indianapolis Journal reported from Haiti regarding proposed constitutional changes concerning foreign property rights. Haitian Foreign Minister Brutus St. Victor denied claims that the government intended to repeal Article 6 of the constitution, which prohibited alien ownership of real estate, stating the public was not prepared for such reform. St. Victor also declined to disclose specific government plans to address the rising premium on paper currency.

The Indianapolis Journal informó desde Haití sobre las propuestas de reforma constitucional relativas a los derechos de propiedad de los extranjeros. El ministro de Asuntos Exteriores haitiano, Brutus St. Victor, desmintió las versiones de que el gobierno pretendiera derogar el artículo 6 de la constitución, que prohibía a los extranjeros la propiedad de bienes inmuebles, afirmando que la opinión pública no estaba preparada para tal cambio. St. Victor declinó asimismo revelar planes gubernamentales específicos para hacer frente al creciente sobreprecio del papel moneda.

Under the head 'President Heureaux Shot for Revenge,' the Puerto Plata dispatch says President Heureaux was killed by the son of a man whom the president had put to death in 1884: the son, Ramon Caceres, vowed revenge for the execution of his father, who was merely suspected of joining in a rebel plot, and has never lost an opportunity to foment insurrection. During the past few weeks there has been much discontent, owing to the financial crisis, and a revolution was feared.

Bajo el título «President Heureaux Shot for Revenge», el despacho de Puerto Plata dice que el presidente Heureaux fue muerto por el hijo de un hombre a quien el presidente había ejecutado en 1884: el hijo, Ramon Caceres, juró venganza por la ejecución de su padre, que fue meramente sospechado de unirse a un complot rebelde, y nunca ha perdido una oportunidad de fomentar insurrección. July 29, 189929 de julio de 1899

The New York item of July 29 prints General Abelardo A. Moscoso, an exiled leader of the liberal party of Santo Domingo, now living in New York, who said Thursday night: 'The death of President Heureaux will, I think, surely be followed by disorder and revolution. I want to emphasize this prophecy that the United States will eventually be compelled to interfere to establish peace in the island, just as this country did in Cuba. If San Domingo must of necessity, I believe, ultimately become attached to this country.' General Moscoso has been an exile in New York for three years; he opposed the late president politically and had to flee to save his life.

The San Francisco Call

The Call prints the Port de France dispatch of July 29 on Monte CristiEl Call publica el despacho de Port de France del 29 de julio sobre Monte Cristi

July 30, 189930 de julio de 1899

The Call prints the Port de France dispatch of July 29 on Monte Cristi and Moca: tranquility prevails at Monte Cristi, but all the people are anxiously awaiting the important events which it is expected will occur shortly; General Pichardo, the governor of the city, has taken all possible measures to repel any attack which may be made by the followers of Senor Juan Jiminez, one of the candidates for the presidency of the republic; it is said that General Pichardo has advised the Government to issue a decree proclaiming amnesty for all Dominican exiles, with a view to appeasing the people; at La Vega General Don Pedro Bobea has taken steps to suppress outbreaks should any occur; up to the present time perfect order has been maintained, but notwithstanding that fact all law-abiding citizens have been called upon to bear arms and hold themselves in immediate readiness for service; and at Moca, where the people criticize harshly the laxity of the governor, General Juan Gonzales, many versions are in circulation regarding the killing of the President — one has it that the assassin shot him with the muzzle of the weapon held close to Heureaux's body.

El Call publica el despacho de Port de France del 29 de julio sobre Monte Cristi y Moca: prevalece la tranquilidad en Monte Cristi, pero todo el pueblo espera ansiosamente los importantes eventos que se espera ocurrirán en breve; el general Pichardo, gobernador de la ciudad, ha tomado todas las medidas posibles para repeler cualquier ataque que pueda ser hecho por los seguidores del señor Juan Jiminez, uno de los candidatos a la presidencia de la república; se dice que el general Pichardo ha aconsejado al Gobierno emitir un decreto proclamando amnistía para todos los exiliados dominicanos, con miras a apaciguar al pueblo; en La Vega el general don Pedro Bobea ha tomado pasos para suprimir estallidos si alguno ocurriera; hasta el presente momento se ha mantenido un orden perfecto, pero no obstante ese hecho todos los ciudadanos respetuosos de la ley han sido llamados a tomar las armas y mantenerse en disposición inmediata para el servicio; y en Moca, donde el pueblo critica duramente la laxitud del gobernador, el general Juan Gonzales, circulan muchas versiones respecto a la muerte del Presidente — una sostiene que el asesino le disparó con la boca del arma pegada al cuerpo de Heureaux.
