April 17, 190317 de abril de 1903

Among the papers is a report from Dr. Powell, American minister to the republic, recounting that Dominican authorities honored General Maximo Gomez during his visit to San Domingo by allowing him to inspect and handle the bones of Christopher Columbus. The relics reside in a casket measuring 18x10 inches and 10 inches deep, and this occasion was the fifth time they had ever been opened to public view. The note records that an unextracted bullet said to have been lodged in the discoverer's leg remains in the box as proof of authenticity, and Powell noted that he was likewise permitted to hold some of the relics.

Un despacho de Washington en el Greene County Herald reseña documentos diplomáticos que aparecerán en el próximo volumen de Relaciones Exteriores relativos a Cuba y a la República Dominicana. Entre los papeles figura un informe del doctor Powell, ministro estadounidense ante la república, relatando que las autoridades dominicanas homenajearon al general Máximo Gómez durante su visita a Santo Domingo permitiéndole inspeccionar y tocar los restos óseos de Cristóbal Colón. Las reliquias yacen en un cofre de 18x10 pulgadas y 10 pulgadas de profundidad, y esta ocasión constituyó apenas la quinta vez en que se exhibieron al público. La nota consigna que dentro del cofre reposa una bala nunca extraída que supuestamente estuvo incrustada en la pierna del descubridor como testimonio de autenticidad, y Powell señaló que a él también se le permitió sostener parte de las reliquias.

The St. Louis Republic

DOMINICANS STOP AMERICAN VESSEL. Blockade of Porto Plata Put in Force Without Notice as measure Against Rebels. REVOLUTION GAINS RAPIDLY. Leaders Well-Supplied With Sinews of War—United States Will Demand Explanation of Warship's Actions.Los dominicanos detienen un buque estadounidense. Bloqueo de Puerto Plata puesto en vigor sin aviso como medida contra los rebeldes. La revolución avanza rápidamente. Los líderes bien provistos de los recursos para la guerra: Estados Unidos exigirá una explicación de las acciones del buque de guerra.

October 28, 190328 de octubre de 1903

The Washington item says a full explanation of the stopping of the American mail steamer Cherokee at Porto Plata by a Dominican warship will to-morrow be demanded of the Dominican Republic by the State Department; an American cruiser or gunboat will meantime be ordered to Porto Plata and prevent further arbitrary interference with commerce, either by so-called blockades or otherwise. Up to the time a Republic dispatch stating the Cherokee had been turned back at Porto Plata was shown to a State Department official to-night the department had received no advices regarding this occurrence; the only advices received to-day came from Minister Powell at Santo Domingo City and stated that Porto Plata was in a state of revolution. From this it is inferred the Dominican government has instituted a blockade with the idea of cutting off the revolutionists' source of supplies. According to international law, however, a blockade, to be recognized by other powers, must be preceded by a formal notification of its institution. The tendency of the Central American and West Indian governments, particularly Haiti and Santo Domingo, has been so arbitrary in this regard that no blockade not preceded by formal notification long enough to exempt ships sailing before notice is given will be tolerated by the United States government.

La nota de Washington dice que mañana se exigirá a la República Dominicana, por conducto del Departamento de Estado, una explicación completa de la detención del vapor correo estadounidense Cherokee en Porto Plata por un buque de guerra dominicano; entretanto, un crucero o cañonera estadounidense será enviado a Porto Plata para impedir nuevas interferencias arbitrarias con el comercio, ya sea mediante supuestos bloqueos o de otro modo. Hasta el momento en que esta noche se mostró a un funcionario del Departamento de Estado un despacho del Republic que afirmaba que el Cherokee había sido obligado a retroceder en Porto Plata, el departamento no había recibido informes sobre este suceso; los únicos informes recibidos hoy provinieron del ministro Powell en la ciudad de Santo Domingo y afirmaban que Porto Plata estaba en estado de revolución. De ello se infiere que el gobierno dominicano ha instituido un bloqueo con la idea de cortar la fuente de suministros de los revolucionarios. Según el derecho internacional, sin embargo, un bloqueo, para ser reconocido por otras potencias, debe ir precedido de una notificación formal de su institución. La tendencia de los gobiernos centroamericanos y de las Indias Occidentales, particularmente Haití y Santo Domingo, ha sido tan arbitraria a este respecto que el gobierno de los Estados Unidos no tolerará ningún bloqueo que no haya sido precedido por una notificación formal con antelación suficiente para eximir a los buques que zarparon antes de que se diera el aviso.

Oakes Republican

The Republican prints the closing part of the Santiago dispatchEl Republican publica la parte final del despacho de Santiago

October 30, 190330 de octubre de 1903

The revolution which has broken out in the northern part of the republic of Santo Domingo and which has already resulted in the establishment of a provisional government at Puerto Plata under the presidency of General Morales, was caused, according to advices received here, by the numerous custom house frauds and the prevarications of the ministers of war and finance. The signal for the outbreak of the revolt was given at 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon by three cannon shots. That same evening all the partisans of President Woss y Gil at Puerto Plata were arrested. The inhabitants of Monte Cristi, La Ligne and Moca united and attacked Santiago. Telegraphic communication between Santiago and La Vega has been severed. The revolutionary outbreak is extending and the general opinion is that the government of President Woss y Gil is lost. Vice President Deschamps has abandoned the government and has arrived here in a small sailboat from Monte Cristi. The Paris item says advices received from Cape Haytien, Hayti, confirm the reports that the revolutionary movement in the republic of Santo Domingo is in favor of former President Jiminez; the revolution is spreading, and, according to the dispatches received here, all the inhabitants of the region of Monte Cibao have joined the uprising against the government; and the election of General Morales as president of the provisional government is said to be a step taken pending the arrival of General Jiminez.

La revolución que ha estallado en la parte norte de la república de Santo Domingo y que ya ha resultado en el establecimiento de un gobierno provisional en Puerto Plata bajo la presidencia del general Morales, fue causada, según informes recibidos aquí, por los numerosos fraudes de aduanas y las prevaricaciones de los ministros de Guerra y de Hacienda. La señal para el estallido de la revuelta se dio a las 2 de la tarde del sábado con tres cañonazos. Esa misma noche todos los partidarios del presidente Woss y Gil en Puerto Plata fueron arrestados. Los habitantes de Monte Cristi, La Ligne y Moca se unieron y atacaron Santiago. La comunicación telegráfica entre Santiago y La Vega ha sido cortada. El estallido revolucionario se extiende y la opinión general es que el gobierno del presidente Woss y Gil está perdido. El vicepresidente Deschamps ha abandonado el gobierno y ha llegado aquí en un pequeño bote de vela desde Monte Cristi. La nota de París dice que los informes recibidos desde Cabo Haitiano, Haití, confirman los reportes de que el movimiento revolucionario en la república de Santo Domingo es a favor del expresidente Jiménez; la revolución se extiende, y, según los despachos recibidos aquí, todos los habitantes de la región de Monte Cibao se han unido al levantamiento contra el gobierno; y se dice que la elección del general Morales como presidente del gobierno provisional es un paso dado a la espera de la llegada del general Jiménez.

Previous to the beginning of the bombardment of San Domingo the revolutionists notified the diplomatic corps and the consular officers that they had previously served notice on the Dominican government that the forces of the revolution intended to adopt all means, including a bombardment, in their efforts to capture the city; the representatives of the powers met to-day and decided that they could only hold communication with the legally established government of the country. Paul Cabrera, at the head of 800 revolutionists, with six pieces of artillery and a treasure chest containing $4,000, arrived yesterday before the city. The situation here is becoming very critical, and the presence of more warships at San Domingo is urgently required. The guard for the German consulate, which was landed from the cruisers of Germany now in port, is in constant communication with the latter by means of a signal station which has been erected over the consulate. The German steamer Athen, which was prevented some days ago from landing her cargo at San Pedro de Macoris, under the protection of the German cruiser succeeded in landing her freight at that port, and has proceeded to the northern ports of Santo Domingo, for which she has cargo and passengers; the Athen has a German escort with her in order to facilitate the discharging of her freight.

The Star prints a further part of its account of the Dominican exilesEl Star publica otra parte de su relato de los exiliados dominicanos

November 16, 190316 de noviembre de 1903

All the exiles are confident of the fall of the government, as they say that Puerto Plata and Santiago de los Caballeros together form the keystone of the republic, and these towns are asserted to be in the hands of the rebels. President Alejandro Woss y Gil is a man of considerable worth as a statesman and soldier; he is a well-educated man and has lived abroad for a considerable time, representing the Dominican republic at Washington for quite a number of years during the presidency of General Heureaux. Under the head 'Second Presidential Term,' the account continues: this is his second presidential term, having occupied that position for the first time when he was about twenty-six or twenty-seven years of age, being the first Dominican to occupy that high post at such an early age; he was the intimate friend of President Heureaux, and is said to be a man of his school, that is to say, a man who does not hesitate to give his troublesome adversaries six feet of good Dominican soil whenever he can get hold of them, and a tenacious fighter, who is not to be daunted by either the large number or superior strategic position of the enemy.

Todos los exiliados confían en la caída del gobierno, pues dicen que Puerto Plata y Santiago de los Caballeros juntas forman la piedra angular de la república, y se afirma que estas poblaciones están en manos de los rebeldes. El presidente Alejandro Woss y Gil es un hombre de considerable valor como estadista y soldado; es un hombre bien educado y ha vivido en el extranjero por un tiempo considerable, representando a la república dominicana en Washington durante bastantes años durante la presidencia del general Heureaux. Five Ports Closed.Cinco puertos cerrados.

November 17, 190317 de noviembre de 1903

Agents of the Clyde Line in New York declared that the steamer Cherokee would sail on Wednesday on her regular schedule, stopping at Samana Bay, Monte Christi, Sanchez, Puerto Plata and Macoria. Manuel De J. Galvan, consul general from San Domingo to New York, said the Cherokee would certainly be sunk if she attempted to enter any of those five harbors, with the gunboats El Presidente and Independence on guard to maintain the blockade of the ports, now in the hands of the rebels under Jiminez. Official advices at the San Domingo consulate in San Juan said General Jiminez was in supreme command of the revolutionists and that Vice President Deschamps was to join him with the best supporters of President Wos y Gil; the article also noted that merchant vessels must respect the blockade if enforced, and that no regular United States liner would be sunk so long as the Baltimore was in Dominican waters.

Los agentes de la Clyde Line en Nueva York declararon que el vapor Cherokee zarparía el miércoles en su itinerario regular, con escalas en la bahía de Samaná, Monte Cristi, Sánchez, Puerto Plata y Macoria. Manuel De J. Galván, cónsul general de Santo Domingo en Nueva York, dijo que el Cherokee sería hundido con seguridad si intentaba entrar en cualquiera de esos cinco puertos, con los cañoneros El Presidente e Independencia de guardia para mantener el bloqueo de los puertos, ahora en manos de los rebeldes de Jiminez. Avisos oficiales en el consulado de Santo Domingo en San Juan dijeron que el general Jiminez tenía el mando supremo de los revolucionarios y que el vicepresidente Deschamps se uniría a él con los mejores partidarios del presidente Wos y Gil; el artículo señaló además que los buques mercantes debían respetar el bloqueo si se hacía efectivo, y que ningún vapor regular estadounidense sería hundido mientras el Baltimore estuviera en aguas dominicanas.
