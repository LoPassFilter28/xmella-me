The Reporter and Farmer prints the official confirmation of the Pajarito bombardmentEl Reporter and Farmer publica la confirmación oficial del bombardeo de Pajarito

February 25, 190425 de febrero de 1904

According to Capt. Miller's dispatch the affair occurred on a river twenty miles from San Domingo, presumably at Pajarito, the place mentioned in the press dispatches as the locality where the bombardment occurred. The insurgents, who were stationed on the mainland, fired on a United States merchant vessel, believed to be the New York, which was lying in the river; the shots fell so dangerously near the war vessels that the acts of the insurgents were construed by Capt. Miller as an attack on the vessels, and a battery fire was opened on the revolutionists. This was followed by the debarkation of 400 marines and bluejackets from the Columbia and Newark, who made a successful landing ashore; they dislodged the insurgents from their position and chased them into the country; after accomplishing this the marines and bluejackets took again to their boats and returned to their ships. One man, a bugler named Painter, was seriously wounded by the accidental explosion of his firing piece; there were no other casualties. Authoritatively it is stated that the question of the annexation of Santo Domingo to the United States never has received the slightest consideration on the part of the administration.

Según el despacho del capitán Miller, el hecho ocurrió en un río a veinte millas de Santo Domingo, presumiblemente en Pajarito, el lugar mencionado en los despachos de prensa como la localidad donde ocurrió el bombardeo. Los insurgentes, que estaban apostados en tierra firme, dispararon contra un buque mercante de los Estados Unidos, que se cree era el New York, que estaba en el río; los disparos cayeron tan peligrosamente cerca de los buques de guerra que los actos de los insurgentes fueron interpretados por el capitán Miller como un ataque a los buques, y se abrió fuego de batería contra los revolucionarios. A esto siguió el desembarco de 400 infantes de marina y marineros del Columbia y el Newark, que efectuaron un desembarco exitoso en la costa; desalojaron a los insurgentes de su posición y los persiguieron hacia el interior; tras lograr esto, los infantes de marina y los marineros volvieron a sus botes y regresaron a sus buques. Essex County Herald

The Herald prints the end of a survey of editorial opinionEl Herald publica el final de un examen de opinión

March 25, 190425 de marzo de 1904

The Philadelphia Ledger says that a 'military occupation of Santo Domingo, purely as an exercise of international police power, is the measure suggested by present conditions.' The Philadelphia Record thinks that if we attempt to police the island it will gradually become a ward of our government: 'A government cannot play the role of a big policeman within the limits of another state without becoming the protector of the latter. The sovereignty of a region resides where the power to preserve order resides, and when one takes over the sovereignty the territory usually goes with it.' Russia understood this fully, the Record says, when she undertook to establish settled conditions in and suppress the banditti of Manchuria. A military administrator put 'temporarily' in charge of the administration of Santo Domingo would be likely to undergo a slow and gradual transformation into a subordinate of the bureau of insular affairs in the war department. The Brooklyn Eagle thinks that there is little hope for any of the West Indies 'except as wards of this nation', and the St. Louis Globe-Democrat, which has advocated annexation of Santo Domingo for some time, says that nothing short of American supervision, which will amount to a virtual protectorate, will end the misrule of the island's ambitious politicians and their followers.

El Herald publica el final de un examen de la opinión editorial, y el Atlanta Journal, el Buffalo Express, el Macon Telegraph, el Philadelphia Ledger, el New Orleans Times-Democrat y el New York Globe denuncian todos la idea de la anexión. El Philadelphia Ledger dice que una «ocupación militar de Santo Domingo, puramente como ejercicio de poder policial internacional, es la medida que sugieren las condiciones presentes». El Philadelphia Record cree que si intentamos vigilar la isla, esta se convertirá gradualmente en pupila de nuestro gobierno: «Un gobierno no puede desempeñar el papel de gran policía dentro de los límites de otro Estado sin convertirse en protector de este último. La soberanía de una región reside donde reside el poder de preservar el orden, y cuando uno asume la soberanía, el territorio suele ir con ella». Rusia lo entendió plenamente, dice el Record, cuando emprendió establecer condiciones de orden y suprimir a los bandidos de Manchuria. Un administrador militar puesto «temporalmente» a cargo de la administración de Santo Domingo probablemente sufriría una transformación lenta y gradual hasta convertirse en subordinado de la oficina de asuntos insulares del Departamento de Guerra. El Brooklyn Eagle cree que existe poca esperanza para cualquiera de las Antillas «excepto como pupilas de esta nación», y el St. Louis Globe-Democrat, que ha abogado por la anexión de Santo Domingo desde hace algún tiempo, dice que nada menos que una supervisión estadounidense, que equivaldrá a un protectorado virtual, pondrá fin al desgobierno de los políticos ambiciosos de la isla y sus seguidores.

Evening Star

The Star prints an item on the movements of the ex-presidentEl Star publica una nota sobre los movimientos del expresidente

April 18, 190418 de abril de 1904

General Jiminez remained in San Juan but a few hours, leaving by train for Mayaguez, where his wife and children have resided ever since the revolution began. After the defeat of his troops before the city of Santo Domingo and their terrible routing in San Pedro de Macoris and La Vega and other serious reverses, General Jiminez took a German steamer at Monte Cristi, the first to leave that port, for St. Thomas, for the purpose of coming to Porto Rico to try and raise funds for the purchase of arms and ammunition. Should he be unsuccessful in raising a sufficient amount here, he will visit the United States for that purpose. He is no longer the man that he was; the months that he has spent in camp in his disastrous campaign have been sufficient to make him twenty years older. He asserts that his defeat was exclusively due to the lack of arms and ammunition, and that if he can but obtain the necessary funds he may yet recuperate all that has been lost.

El general Jiménez permaneció en San Juan solo unas horas, saliendo en tren hacia Mayagüez, donde su esposa e hijos han residido desde que comenzó la revolución. Tras la derrota de sus tropas ante la ciudad de Santo Domingo y su terrible desbandada en San Pedro de Macorís y La Vega y otros graves reveses, el general Jiménez tomó un vapor alemán en Monte Cristi, el primero en salir de ese puerto, rumbo a St. Thomas, con el propósito de venir a Puerto Rico para tratar de recaudar fondos para la compra de armas y municiones. Since the formal proclamation of the Republic in 1844 there has been no end of dictators as well as presidents, some of them good, many the reverse; and tyrants have retained the reins of government longer than humane rulers. American material interests in San Domingo are very large; half of the sugar estates belong to Americans.

Desde la proclamación formal de la República en 1844 no ha habido fin de dictadores y de presidentes, algunos buenos, muchos lo contrario; y los tiranos han conservado las riendas del gobierno más tiempo que los gobernantes humanos. The oldest settlement made in the new world was on this island, Columbus himself having founded it; here the great discoverer was buried, and here for a number of years was the capital of the Spanish possessions in the new world. El asentamiento más antiguo del Nuevo Mundo estuvo en esta isla, fundado por el propio Colón; aquí fue enterrado el gran descubridor, y aquí estuvo durante varios años la capital de las posesiones españolas en el Nuevo Mundo. A fines del siglo XVIII, España cedió la mitad oriental de la isla a Francia, que poseía la mitad occidental; poco después, todo el país bajo Toussaint Louverture obtuvo su independencia. En 1844 vino la rebelión de la porción oriental de la isla, que dio por resultado la formación de la actual República Dominicana.

While production at the present time is comparatively small, and confined chiefly to sugar, coffee, cacao, bananas, mahogany and logwood, its possibilities of production are considered very great at such time as the interior may be opened by railways and roads. Marrion Wilcox, an authority on tropical America, is quoted that the island 'excels nearly all others in the world, certainly the neighboring members of the Greater Antilles (Cuba and Porto Rico), in natural fertility and diversity of climate', and that if such a system of highways and railways as the English have constructed in Jamaica were established in Santo Domingo, the long period of political disturbances and revolutions in the Dominican Republic and Republic of Haiti would gradually come to an end. At present the railways of the island have a total length of about 300 miles and are chiefly on the northern coast, extending inland from Samana Bay, one of the finest natural harbors of the West Indies, while another line extends westwardly from Santo Domingo, the capital of the republic, about 100 miles along the southern coast. Santo Domingo, the capital, has a population of 25,000, and is the oldest town of European origin in the New World, having been founded in 1496.

Mientras la producción en el tiempo presente es comparativamente pequeña, y se limita principalmente al azúcar, el café, el cacao, los bananos, la caoba y el palo de tinte, sus posibilidades de producción se consideran muy grandes cuando el interior sea abierto por ferrocarriles y carreteras.
