The Washington Herald

The Washington Herald reports that Receiver General W. C. Pulliam

October 26, 191226 de octubre de 1912

The Washington Herald reports that Receiver General W. C. Pulliam, head of the Dominican customs service, arrived in Washington yesterday for extended conferences with Acting Secretary of State Wilson. His advice was sought regarding measures to restrain Dominicans from reckless and wrongful expenditure of their revenues, as Dominicans are unwilling to accept direct United States supervision of their spending. Meanwhile, the transport Prairie, carrying 750 marines, arrived at Puerto Plata, where a rebel attack was feared at any moment, having stopped on its way to investigate Samana following reports of rebel bombardment.

The Washington Herald informa que el receptor general W. C. Pulliam, titular del servicio de aduanas dominicano, llegó ayer a Washington para mantener extensas conferencias con el secretario de Estado interino Wilson. Se solicitó su asesoramiento en relación con medidas para impedir que los dominicanos gasten sus rentas de forma imprudente e indebida, en vista de que no aceptarán una supervisión directa de sus desembolsos por parte de Estados Unidos. Entre tanto, el transporte Prairie, con 750 infantes de marina a bordo, arribó a Puerto Plata, donde se temía un asalto rebelde en cualquier momento, habiendo hecho escala previa en Samaná para investigar denuncias de bombardeos insurgentes.

The Sun

Advices forwarded to the State Department from Puerto Plata report

November 2, 19122 de noviembre de 1912

Advices forwarded to the State Department from Puerto Plata report that bombardment has been continuing since Sunday, prompting expectations that a United States warship has been sent there. The cruiser Baltimore sailed from Philadelphia at noon today for Santo Domingo City and the Yankton left New York yesterday for Puerto Plata, bringing the total United States naval presence in Dominican waters to four ships alongside the Prairie at Caldera Bay and the Wheeling at Santo Domingo City. Upon arrival, the Baltimore will embark a detachment of the 750 marines aboard the Prairie, while special commissioners continue negotiations with the Dominican government in Santo Domingo City.

Informes remitidos al Departamento de Estado desde Puerto Plata señalan que el bombardeo continúa desde el domingo, lo que hace presumir el envío de un buque de guerra estadounidense a dicho punto. El crucero Baltimore zarpó hoy al mediodía desde Filadelfia hacia la ciudad de Santo Domingo y el Yankton partió ayer de Nueva York rumbo a Puerto Plata, con lo que la presencia naval de Estados Unidos en aguas dominicanas ascenderá a cuatro naves junto al Prairie en la bahía de las Calderas y el Wheeling en la capital dominicana. A su llegada, el Baltimore embarcará a un contingente de los 750 infantes de marina que se encuentran a bordo del Prairie, mientras comisionados especiales continúan las negociaciones con el gobierno dominicano en la capital.

The Washington Times

The Washington Times reports that a crisis has arrived in Dominican

November 4, 19124 de noviembre de 1912

The Washington Times reports that a crisis has arrived in Dominican affairs, with expectations that rebels will decide to lay down their arms before the week ends to grant the country a period of peace. American commissioners and the Archbishop of Santo Domingo are proceeding aboard the gunboat Wheeling to confer with insurgent representatives at Samana Bay this week. Although Samana Bay and the adjoining district have been in rebel possession for some time, the insurgents are reported to have made little progress.

The Washington Times comunica que ha sobrevenido una crisis en los asuntos dominicanos, esperándose que los rebeldes decidan deponer las armas antes de que termine la semana para permitir al país un período de paz. Los comisionados estadounidenses y el arzobispo de Santo Domingo se trasladan a bordo del cañonero Wheeling para reunirse esta semana con representantes insurgentes en la bahía de Samaná. A pesar de que la bahía de Samaná y el distrito adyacente han estado bajo dominio rebelde durante algún tiempo, se informa que los insurgentes han logrado escasos avances.

The New Haven Union

The New Haven Union reports that voting on the election of Archbishop Nouel proceeded

December 2, 19122 de diciembre de 1912

The New Haven Union reports that voting on the election of Archbishop Nouel proceeded favorably in both houses, though Dominican law requires the House to vote three times with no more than one ballot per day, requiring three days to finalize the result. The election carries out a compromise program proposed by United States commissioners and accepted by the now-resigned Victoria administration and the revolutionists, with a permanent presidential election planned once normal conditions return. In a Rome dispatch dated Dec. 3, it is reported that the Pope cabled congratulations and his apostolic blessing to Archbishop Nouel on his election as provisional president of the Dominican Republic, expressing hope that it will strengthen ties with the Holy See and noting that the prelate will likely be created a cardinal upon completing his term.

The New Haven Union informa que la votación para la elección del arzobispo Nouel avanzó favorablemente en ambas cámaras, aunque la ley dominicana exige que la Cámara vote tres veces a razón de no más de un sufragio por día, por lo que se requerirán tres días para finalizar el resultado. La elección responde a un programa de compromiso propuesto por los comisionados de los Estados Unidos y aceptado tanto por el ya dimitido gobierno de Victoria como por los revolucionarios, previéndose una elección presidencial permanente tan pronto como se restablezcan las condiciones normales. En un despacho de Roma con fecha del 3 de diciembre, se informa que el Papa telegrafió sus felicitaciones y su bendición apostólica al arzobispo Nouel por su elección como presidente provisional de la República Dominicana, expresando la esperanza de que fortalezca los lazos con la Santa Sede y señalando que es probable que el prelado sea nombrado cardenal al concluir su mandato.

All pending disputes between former rebel leader Desidario Arias

January 29, 191329 de enero de 1913

All pending disputes between former rebel leader Desidario Arias and Archbishop Nouel, provisional president of the Dominican Republic, have been settled, the Evening Star reports under the headline "Peace in Santo Domingo," re-establishing permanent peace. La crónica añade que el prelado, en el ejercicio de sus funciones presidenciales, ya había pacificado con anterioridad a otros líderes rebeldes al visitar varios puertos a bordo de la nave estadounidense.

The Bridgeport Evening Farmer

The Farmer prints a sketch of the island of Santo Domingo: French buccaneers

August 16, 191316 de agosto de 1913

The Farmer prints a sketch of the island of Santo Domingo: French buccaneers gained a foothold in the island; in 1795 the flag of France was supreme, but in 1809 the combined Spanish and English forces captured the island and Spanish sovereignty was restored. In 1821 the people of the Spanish part of the island declared their independence, but President Boyer of Hayti was able to extend his dominions to include all of the island. In 1846 the first Dominican Republic was established, and continued until 1861, when the Dominicans, fearing foreign invasion, appealed to Spain for protection. Spain annexed the country, but on August 16, 1863, at Capotillo, a revolution was instituted which resulted in the restoration of the republic. Living side by side on the same island, and presenting many differences to the most casual observer, the people of the Dominican Republic and Hayti are compared by the paper: French is the language of Hayti, while the Dominicans speak Spanish; the Dominicans are better educated, more progressive, less grossly superstitious, and, on the whole, have shown greater capacity for self-government than their neighbors. The newspapers of a country afford the best index to the character and culture of a people, and in this respect the Dominicans are far ahead of the Haytians. There are half a dozen daily papers in the cities of Santo Domingo, Santiago and Puerto Plata, and Santo Domingo also has two literary weeklies, a humorous weekly, and several monthly, professional and literary magazines.

The Farmer publica un esbozo histórico de la isla de Santo Domingo, donde los bucaneros franceses se afianzaron en la isla; en 1795 la bandera de Francia era suprema, pero en 1809 las fuerzas combinadas españolas e inglesas capturaron la isla y se restauró la soberanía española. En 1821 el pueblo de la parte española de la isla declaró su independencia, pero el presidente Boyer, de Haití, pudo extender sus dominios hasta incluir toda la isla. En 1846 se estableció la primera República Dominicana, que continuó hasta 1861, cuando los dominicanos, temiendo una invasión extranjera, apelaron a España en busca de protección. España anexó el país, pero el 16 de agosto de 1863, en Capotillo, se inició una revolución que resultó en la restauración de la república. Viviendo lado a lado en la misma isla, y presentando muchas diferencias al observador más casual, los pueblos de la República Dominicana y de Haití son comparados por el periódico: el francés es el idioma de Haití, mientras que los dominicanos hablan español; los dominicanos están mejor educados, son más progresivos, menos groseramente supersticiosos y, en conjunto, han mostrado mayor capacidad de autogobierno que sus vecinos. Los periódicos de un país ofrecen el mejor índice del carácter y la cultura de un pueblo, y en este aspecto los dominicanos van muy por delante de los haitianos. Hay media docena de diarios en las ciudades de Santo Domingo, Santiago y Puerto Plata, y Santo Domingo también tiene dos semanarios literarios, un semanario humorístico y varias revistas mensuales, profesionales y literarias.

The Colorado Statesman

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick

September 13, 191313 de septiembre de 1913

The Statesman prints a feature under the head 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance, inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican Republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for wiping out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 in 1905 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.

The Statesman publica un reportaje bajo el título 'Receiver of Revenues of Santo Domingo': Walter W. Vick, de Nueva Jersey, ha sido nombrado por el presidente Wilson receptor general de las aduanas de la república de Santo Domingo, continuándose así un experimento novedoso en las finanzas nacionales, inaugurado en 1905. Es el primer caso en que una nación en bancarrota ha aceptado que otra nación actúe como receptora de sus rentas y se interponga entre ella y sus acreedores durante la liquidación de sus obligaciones pendientes. En la actualidad, la República Dominicana tiene más dinero para gastar en fines apropiados que nunca antes, al tiempo que se está proveyendo ampliamente para extinguir su deuda en fecha temprana. La deuda de $30,000,000 en 1905 se ha reducido a $17,000,000, mientras las cuotas aduaneras se han reducido, el comercio ha aumentado, el desgobierno ha llegado a su fin y las bendiciones de la paz se han establecido en el país antes turbulento.

The Birmingham Age-Herald

The Age-Herald prints a dispatch: ordered to Dominican waters

September 17, 191317 de septiembre de 1913

The Age-Herald prints a dispatch: ordered to Dominican waters by Washington to protect American interests, the cruiser Des Moines arrived at Puerto Plata today, where two Dominican gunboats were blockading the port. The steamer Seminole, of the Clyde line, was stopped as she was nearing port yesterday and boarded by an officer from one of the gunboats and told she could not enter the harbor. The captain of the Seminole replied he would enter anyway. Upon being asked what he would do if he were ordered to remain outside, the captain replied that he would go in and take the boarding officer with him if he did not leave the ship. The officer hurriedly returned to the gunboat and the Seminole entered the port. The blockading boats steamed away. The Seminole had cleared from New York before the blockade of Puerto Plata had been declared and having no munitions of war aboard and relying upon the presence of the Des Moines, she entered the port without fear of molestation. The captain of the Clyde line steamer Algonquin, on arriving here today, reported everything quiet in the southern ports which are ostensibly in the hands of the government. With the exception of Monte Cristi and Santiago, the northern part of the island is in the hands of rebels under Gen. Horacio Vasques, at one time president of the republic. The rebel towns of Sanches and Samana are barricaded and the inhabitants are ready for a fight. Monte Cristi is reported quiet, though the people there are much excited. It is reported the revolution has spread to Seybo.

Pinedale Roundup

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey

October 23, 191323 de octubre de 1913

The Pinedale Roundup prints a feature: Walter W. Vick, of New Jersey, has been appointed by President Wilson receiver-general of the customs of the republic of Santo Domingo, thus continuing a novel experiment in national finance inaugurated in 1905. It is the first case in which a bankrupt nation has accepted another nation to act as receiver of its revenues and stand between it and its creditors during the settlement of its outstanding obligations. At the present time the Dominican republic has more money to spend for proper purposes than ever before, while ample provision is being made for paying out its indebtedness at an early date. The debt of $30,000,000 has been reduced to $17,000,000, while customs dues have been reduced, trade increased, misrule brought to an end, and the blessings of peace established in the formerly turbulent country.

The Pinedale Roundup publica un reportaje: de Nueva Jersey, Walter W. Vick ha sido nombrado por el presidente Wilson receptor general de las aduanas de la república de Santo Domingo, continuando así un novedoso experimento en las finanzas nacionales inaugurado en 1905. Es el primer caso en que una nación en bancarrota ha aceptado que otra nación actúe como receptor de sus rentas y se interponga entre ella y sus acreedores durante la liquidación de sus obligaciones pendientes. En la actualidad, la república dominicana tiene más dinero para gastar en fines adecuados que nunca antes, mientras se provee ampliamente para pagar su deuda en una fecha temprana. La deuda de $30,000,000 se ha reducido a $17,000,000, mientras los derechos de aduana se han reducido, el comercio ha aumentado, el mal gobierno ha llegado a su fin, y las bendiciones de la paz se han establecido en el país antes turbulento.

The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints a dispatch of November 21

November 21, 191321 de noviembre de 1913

The Newark Evening Star and Newark Advertiser prints a dispatch of November 21: a five-fold increase in the foreign trade of San Domingo has been one of the beneficial results of the six years' operations of the 'Dominican convention' under which the United States supervises the customs collection of that country, Professor Jacob Hollander, of Johns Hopkins University, told the Clark University conference on Latin America today at Worcester, Mass. Professor Hollander was financial adviser of the Dominican republic from 1908 to 1910. 'The foreign trade of San Domingo for 1911-12, the latest fiscal year for which figures are available,' he said, 'aggregated nearly $20,600,900, as compared with some $6,000,000 for the year preceding the convention. Little short of a revolution, social, political and economic, has been wrought in the country. The people of the island, protected from rapine and bloodshed, are fairly on the way to becoming a decent peasantry, an industrious and stable one, as sub-tropical conditions are likely to evolve.' Professor Philip Brown, of Princeton, former minister to Honduras, advocated a union of the Central American republics and urged that the United States take the initiative in bringing it about. Professor Hiram Bingham, of Yale, and George K. Tucker spoke on the Monroe Doctrine.
