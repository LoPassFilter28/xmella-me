Paul Verna, a former Haitian diplomat representing the Union, said an unidentified island off the coast was used as a staging area for the invasion and that some of the invaders were former Haitian army officers who had been living in Santo Domingo; the rebels expected to form a military-civilian provisional government in north Haiti. Duvalier, who had given himself an illegal second term extending until 1967 after a rigged election in 1961, had built up a civilian militia of about 5,000 and a force of 5,000 Tonton Macoute while cutting the regular army to about 3,300 men, and the United States — which had cut off economic aid the year before and suspended diplomatic relations when Duvalier remained in office past his constitutional term — had resumed relations on June 3.

Mientras tanto, familias estadounidenses fueron evacuadas hacia Cap Haitien por orden del gobierno de Duvalier desde la plantación de sisal Dauphin, de propiedad estadounidense, situada a unas 5 millas al oeste de la frontera dominicana.
